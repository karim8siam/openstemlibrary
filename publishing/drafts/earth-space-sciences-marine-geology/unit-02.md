# Unit 2: Oceanic crust, ridges and plate boundaries

## Guiding question: What geological evidence reconstructs creation, movement and consumption of oceanic lithosphere?

**Synthetic case:** A synthetic ocean basin contains a spreading ridge, symmetrical magnetic bands, a transform offset and a trench. Different observers obtain bathymetry, magnetic intensity, earthquake locations and seafloor samples. The challenge is to reconcile these as independent constraints, not count correlated maps as independent proof.

## Geological framework: questions, scales and competing evidence

The ocean floor is continuously created and recycled. Ridge segmentation, reversals of the magnetic field, fracture-zone offsets, seismicity and crustal ages constrain plate motion over different intervals and with different kinds of errors. The same lineament can have tectonic, sedimentary or post-depositional origins. Students should not infer plate motion from morphology alone: several data streams must be placed in a self-consistent kinematic reconstruction. Full spreading rate is twice a half-rate only when the two sides are symmetric; rates inferred from specific markers average over the age interval represented by those markers. An ideal crustal column is a useful conceptual map, not a guarantee that drilling at a ridge will encounter an untouched layered sequence. Geochemical alteration and tectonic denudation make real sections more complicated.

## Five lessons with evidence notebooks

Each lesson joins a physical principle, an observable, a competing explanation, and a quantified classroom calculation. A numerical result is conditional on the stated synthetic inputs. Details such as local hazard probability, legal extraction requirements or physical-property inversion cannot be derived from the simple cases.

## Lesson 2.1 — Seafloor spreading and ocean crust structure

### Geological principle: what produces the pattern?

Partial mantle melting at divergent boundaries supplies magma to layered basaltic crust, while tectonic extension and alteration modify idealized stratigraphy. An oceanic crustal column may include pillows, sheeted dikes, gabbro and altered ultramafic rocks, but tectonic exposures are incomplete.

### Observation plan: what is actually measured?

Combine ridge bathymetry, seismic velocity, heat flow and dredged or drilled lithology. Use magnetic anomalies and rock ages where available while recognizing hydrothermal alteration and tectonic rotations.

### Alternative hypotheses and what would refute them

A high seismic velocity is not definitive evidence of a particular rock without physical-property context. Oceanic core complexes expose deeper lithologies and depart from a simple uniform crust model.

### Worked numerical investigation

**Synthetic problem — Crust generation.** A ridge spreads symmetrically at 32 mm/yr per side for 1 million years; find one-sided width.

**Equation and substitution:** `32*1e6/1000/1000`. The computed answer is **32 km**. The units in the input determine the units in the answer. Check the dimensional conversion before interpreting a scalar as a geological quantity. A physically meaningful result should also be examined at a second parameter value to determine whether the trend makes sense.

**Interpretation:** One-sided width is half the full newly formed corridor if spreading stays symmetrical. **What this does not establish:** Actual plate motion and ridge geometry can change.

### A focused field or laboratory investigation

Build and revise a crustal cross section using three independent data types and identify locations where drilling would test the proposed interpretation.

**Design choice:** Inspect sensitivity to processing. Imagine regridding an acoustic field, changing a lithology classification threshold or revising one age control. Determine which apparent geological boundary persists and which vanishes. Report a stable core of interpretation as well as the disputed region, rather than selecting the single map with the neatest outlines.

## Lesson 2.2 — Magnetic stripes and reversal chronology

### Geological principle: what produces the pattern?

Cooling volcanic crust locks in remanent magnetization as magnetic minerals pass blocking temperatures. Reversals of Earth's field may generate roughly symmetric magnetic lineations across spreading axes, allowing identification of polarity chrons and rates when the polarity timescale is constrained.

### Observation plan: what is actually measured?

Use profiles normal to a spreading ridge and compare pattern shapes after removing regional field components. Check ship heading effects, sample remanence and whether a polarity sequence has unique matches.

### Alternative hypotheses and what would refute them

Magnetization intensity and direction depend on mineral content, alteration and geometry; a magnetic low need not coincide directly with a single reversal boundary. Correlation by eye risks circular age assignment.

### Worked numerical investigation

**Synthetic problem — Half-spreading rate.** A dated magnetic boundary at 42 km from the ridge axis is 2.1 Ma old; find mean half-rate.

**Equation and substitution:** `42/2.1`. The computed answer is **20 km/Ma**. The units in the input determine the units in the answer. Check the dimensional conversion before interpreting a scalar as a geological quantity. A physically meaningful result should also be examined at a second parameter value to determine whether the trend makes sense.

**Interpretation:** One km per million years is one millimeter per year. **What this does not establish:** Age correlation, ridge jumps and plate-motion changes affect the estimate.

### A focused field or laboratory investigation

Test alternative chron correlations and compute a half-spreading rate from distance to a dated anomaly, stating both distance and time uncertainty.

**Design choice:** Separate event from background. Estimate whether a measured layer or acoustic feature records a short-lived high-intensity event or the integration of many smaller processes. A process can dominate preserved sediment while occupying little elapsed time. Search for event boundaries, independent dates and reworked materials before extending the inference beyond one location.

## Lesson 2.3 — Transform faults and fracture zones

### Geological principle: what produces the pattern?

Transform faults accommodate differential motion between offset ridge segments; their active portions are earthquake-bearing plate boundaries. Older fracture zones preserve fossil age contrasts and bathymetric steps beyond the active transform.

### Observation plan: what is actually measured?

Compare earthquake focal mechanisms with offsets in ridge axes, magnetic lineations and bathymetric fabric. Differentiate active strike-slip motion from inactive topographic scars.

### Alternative hypotheses and what would refute them

A long linear seabed valley need not be an active plate boundary. Flexure, inherited fabric and landslides can make similar lineaments; slip sense must come from kinematic evidence.

### Worked numerical investigation

**Synthetic problem — Transform slip distance.** Relative plate motion is 25 mm/yr over 0.8 Ma; compute displacement.

**Equation and substitution:** `25*0.8`. The computed answer is **20 km**. The units in the input determine the units in the answer. Check the dimensional conversion before interpreting a scalar as a geological quantity. A physically meaningful result should also be examined at a second parameter value to determine whether the trend makes sense.

**Interpretation:** Numerically, mm per year multiplied by Ma converts to kilometers. **What this does not establish:** Slip may occur on multiple strands rather than one fault.

### A focused field or laboratory investigation

Trace an idealized fracture zone across dated crust, predict relative plate-motion direction and identify segments expected to be seismically active.

**Design choice:** Apply a conservation or kinematic constraint. Define what enters and leaves the chosen region, and distinguish a material inventory from a flux. Where the subject is tectonic rather than depositional, check whether distances, ages and rate units are consistent. A residual indicates something unmeasured or a violated assumption; it does not automatically verify a preferred mechanism.

## Lesson 2.4 — Subduction trenches and accretionary prisms

### Geological principle: what produces the pattern?

At convergent margins, a descending plate bends into a trench and may transfer material to an accretionary wedge or carry it deeper. Seismicity, fault geometry, sediment thickness and pore-fluid state all influence wedge deformation.

### Observation plan: what is actually measured?

Interpret bathymetric trench profiles together with seismic reflection images and earthquake depth distributions. Separate the plate interface from seaward normal faults and shallow sediment deformation.

### Alternative hypotheses and what would refute them

A steep slope may be created by erosion or recent mass wasting without tectonic uplift. Trench fill can obscure relief, and the wedge toe may be poorly imaged beneath disturbed sediments.

### Worked numerical investigation

**Synthetic problem — Trench sediment supply.** An incoming sediment layer is 0.8 km thick, 15 km wide, and moves 0.04 km/ka; find bulk volume flux.

**Equation and substitution:** `0.8*15*0.04`. The computed answer is **0.48 km³/ka**. The units in the input determine the units in the answer. Check the dimensional conversion before interpreting a scalar as a geological quantity. A physically meaningful result should also be examined at a second parameter value to determine whether the trend makes sense.

**Interpretation:** A cross-sectional area multiplied by velocity yields bulk transport volume per time. **What this does not establish:** Not all incoming sediment is accreted, and compaction alters bulk volume.

### A focused field or laboratory investigation

Construct two end-member mass-balance scenarios for sediment accretion versus subduction and list data needed to constrain the real partition.

**Design choice:** Replicate the observation. For this topic, describe which parts of a figure are directly measured and which were inferred from calibration, interpolation, geological priors or visual classification. The observation may be convincing even if the preferred explanation remains uncertain. Design a check in which a second observer can reproduce the result using the same input files and clear processing parameters.

## Lesson 2.5 — Seamounts, hotspots and ocean islands

### Geological principle: what produces the pattern?

Seamount chains may record volcanism on moving lithosphere above mantle melting regions, but not all chains result from stationary hotspots. Erosion, subsidence and carbonate growth can modify volcanic shapes after eruption.

### Observation plan: what is actually measured?

Combine bathymetric geometry, rock radiometric ages, geochemistry, gravity anomalies and evidence of subaerial weathering. Check whether age progression remains monotonic within error.

### Alternative hypotheses and what would refute them

A linear volcanic chain alone does not uniquely recover plate speed. Changes in volcanic source position, multiple eruptive episodes and later tectonic motion complicate a single fixed-hotspot interpretation.

### Worked numerical investigation

**Synthetic problem — Seamount chain rate.** Two volcanic edifices are 310 km apart and differ in radiometric age by 5 Ma; estimate relative trend speed.

**Equation and substitution:** `310/5`. The computed answer is **62 km/Ma**. The units in the input determine the units in the answer. Check the dimensional conversion before interpreting a scalar as a geological quantity. A physically meaningful result should also be examined at a second parameter value to determine whether the trend makes sense.

**Interpretation:** Age-distance slope is a kinematic estimate in the chosen reference frame. **What this does not establish:** Hotspot movement and complex volcanism can affect the trend.

### A focused field or laboratory investigation

Estimate a hypothetical age-distance trend and compare the inferred rate with independent plate reconstructions before assigning a geodynamic mechanism.

**Design choice:** Choose a competing model. List two geologically distinct processes that produce a similar measured pattern, then predict a variable on which those explanations disagree. Prefer the prediction with a measurable sign, geometry, age ordering or composition rather than a vague expectation of greater complexity. Specify both an inexpensive screening observation and a decisive but costly validation.

### Research deep dive: resolving an apparent ridge migration

A set of magnetic stripes seems symmetric across an oceanic spreading ridge. The first temptation is to label every local peak as one polarity reversal and compute plate motion. A better procedure starts with the geomagnetic polarity timescale independently of the profile and asks which candidate correlations match the ordering and spacing of reversals. A strip of positive magnetic anomaly can be widened by magnetized crustal thickness or altered by sensor geometry. One peak should never be treated as an exact reversal age with no error interval.

Suppose two candidate dates yield half-rates of 18 and 25 mm/yr over a long interval. Both may fit noisy magnetics if one magnetic boundary is ambiguous. Test them with the predicted positions of additional anomaly reversals, fracture-zone traces or radiometric ages of recovered basalts. If independent evidence selects a rate, the answer is an average over the interval, not necessarily the rate of motion today. Kinematic rotations and discontinuous plate boundaries mean that a whole-ocean reconstruction is more than joining points on a single straight line.

Consider a fracture zone that extends far beyond the active transform fault. Earthquakes may cluster only on the segment linking ridge axes, while the inactive continuation records a fossil age contrast between plates. Calling the whole long feature an active transform would overstate present hazard and misunderstand the relationship between ridge geometry and plate motion. Likewise the unusual lithologies of oceanic core complexes show that faults can expose deeper crustal or mantle rocks at some spreading centers; they are not necessarily exceptions that should be erased to preserve a textbook diagram.

To defend a tectonic interpretation, place observations on a plate-motion diagram with error bars. Describe which boundaries must be active, which may be relics, and which may arise from sediment cover or sonar processing. The hypothesis becomes stronger when it predicts an observation not used to construct it, such as independent magnetic polarity or an earthquake focal mechanism. This is the difference between matching a pretty geometry and testing a plate-tectonic mechanism.

## Unit investigation: linking map, core and mechanism

**Aim.** Use the forty synthetic stations in `datasets/unit-02.csv` to test a question involving oceanic crust, ridges and plate boundaries. Treat the CSV as invented material for practicing statistical quality control, not as an actual seabed dataset. Plot water depth and acoustic sediment thickness along the transect; add markers for the `review` flags and record how the conclusions change after excluding them. The along-track separation is a schematic distance, not latitude or longitude, and therefore cannot establish the orientation or full area of a geological body.

**Design brief.** A synthetic ocean basin contains a spreading ridge, symmetrical magnetic bands, a transform offset and a trench. Different observers obtain bathymetry, magnetic intensity, earthquake locations and seafloor samples. The challenge is to reconcile these as independent constraints, not count correlated maps as independent proof. Specify one observation that you expect to see if your mechanism is correct and a different one expected under a plausible alternative. Identify three layers of evidence: a survey observation, a physical sample, and a geophysical or chronologic constraint. Give each evidence layer a separate confidence score and justification. Do not average those scores unless their dependence is explicitly justified. Two products derived from the same ship track are not fully independent corroboration.

**Procedure, Part A — reconstruct what was measured.** Load the CSV and confirm that depth, thickness and size values are positive where expected. Calculate the smallest and largest water depths, the range of recovered thicknesses, and the number of flagged rows. Make two alternative maps: one preserving all points with quality flags visible, another excluding flagged stations. Label this contrast a sensitivity study. The second view should not be automatically judged more correct; removal can itself bias the spatial pattern if poor observations cluster in difficult terrain.

**Procedure, Part B — inspect model dependency.** Examine the plotted gradient at an interval of two, five and ten stations, and identify which short features are averaged away. Estimate a simple slope in metres per kilometer using a defined pair of samples. State whether this is a geometric seabed slope or a sediment thickness gradient; the distinction affects interpretation. If you attempt uncertainty propagation, specify assumed error correlations rather than selecting a root-sum-square formula by habit.

**Procedure, Part C — compare competing histories.** Write two plausible geological narratives that fit the synthetic pattern. One should emphasize a structural or long-timescale explanation; the other should emphasize deposition, redistribution or erosional overprinting where meaningful. List predictions that differ: reflector geometry, sediment type, age ordering, fault offsets, pore-fluid signals or mineralogical provenance. Rank possible next observations by ability to distinguish those predictions, not by how dramatic their imagery would look.

**Procedure, Part D — communicate limitations.** Prepare a four-panel reproducible report: data provenance, primary measurement plot, competing explanation diagram and uncertainty ledger. In the ledger, list unknown vertical datum, unrealistically simplified synthetic values, unmeasured lithology, timing uncertainty and any relevant instrument bias. Explicitly describe which claims the report cannot make. For marine hazards, resource assessment and navigational uses, do not substitute this exercise for official data or specialist review.

**Deliverables and grading:** 20% clean reproducible analysis, 20% physically coherent interpretations, 20% alternative hypotheses, 20% quantitative unit and sensitivity checks, and 20% uncertainty communication. A technically polished visualization that hides the fact that the data are synthetic cannot receive full credit. Submit the processing steps, plots, an evidence matrix and a revision note explaining which conclusion changed after reviewing uncertainties.

## Unit assessment: reasoning rather than memorization

**Question A:** Design a way to disprove the leading hypothesis for oceanic crust, ridges and plate boundaries while spending no more than two new stations on the fictional cruise. Justify each station by the predicted difference between models.

**Question B:** Explain how an erroneous vertical datum, poor coring recovery, or inappropriate age tie point would affect at least two of the results presented in this unit. Distinguish changed numerical answers from changed geological conclusions.

**Question C:** Propose a figure caption for an unfamiliar scientific reader. It must name what was measured, the units, geographic or stratigraphic coverage, important transformations, and one explicit limitation.

**Question D:** Choose one of the unit models and identify which environmental or geological variable is intentionally held constant. Explain how failure of that constant would change the interpretation but not necessarily invalidate the model as a teaching aid.

## Further investigation and practical extension

To extend the fictional unit project into real research, start with the references and select a genuine open dataset with clear acquisition date, license, method, processing version and coordinate reference system. For oceanic crust, ridges and plate boundaries, seek at least one direct measurement that does not originate from the same inversion as the map. Compare two spatial resolutions before discussing morphology. Keep real observations strictly separated from the invented training CSV. A reproducible report should link each plotted feature to a raw record, show how missing values were handled, and make failed interpretations visible rather than deleting them from the final narrative.
