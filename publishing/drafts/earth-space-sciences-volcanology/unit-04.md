# Unit 4: Effusive Eruptions and Lava Emplacement

## Central question

**How do lava properties and topography shape volcanic flows and domes?**

**Synthetic scenario.** An invented basaltic fissure eruption crosses irregular terrain, while a second, more viscous lava body builds a dome near a valley head. This is an imagined classroom landscape; no measured eruption or real safety recommendation is implied.

## Concepts and study approach

The unit brings together **eruption discharge, lava rheology, cooling, surface crust and emplacement pathways**. Physical processes can be reconstructed from deposits, rock composition, geodetic observations or measured signals, but different materials survive and different instruments observe different parts of the system. The same observable feature may arise from distinct combinations of source conditions and later changes. The aim is to identify the evidence most capable of separating interpretations, not merely to attach a name to the most striking landform. The included case measurements and graphs were designed solely for teaching.

**Problem framing.** An invented basaltic fissure eruption crosses irregular terrain, while a second, more viscous lava body builds a dome near a valley head. Imagine that students must present a short technical brief. Before making a numerical model, determine the time span, which observations are independent, and which boundaries are directly mapped rather than estimated. Data may have unequal completeness and calibration. Distinguish an interpretation based on direct field material from one inferred from numerical fitting or a remote sensor. Where the evidence allows more than one answer, explain what added observation would be valuable.

## Integrated research studio: Unit 4

**Scenario.** An invented basaltic fissure eruption crosses irregular terrain, while a second, more viscous lava body builds a dome near a valley head. Assemble an evidence ledger with a row for each observation, its provenance, its measured units, and an explicit distinction between original observation and interpreted process. Add a third column for alternative hypotheses. Decide which items could be affected by the same source of error or sampling gap before computing averages, rates or correlations. A synthetic observation should be documented as such both in the file name and in any figure legend, because a decontextualized number can appear real after it is copied into another report.

**Research sequence.** First define a narrowly stated question. Second draw the relevant physical geometry or time axis. Third identify process variables that could be measured independently. Fourth check dimensional consistency of a simple model. Fifth create two alternative explanations and an observation capable of distinguishing them. Sixth record an uncertainty or sensitivity check. Seventh communicate the conclusion in a short paragraph aimed at technically informed readers and in another paragraph suited to nonspecialists, preserving the uncertainty in both.

**Practical assessment.** Use the explicitly synthetic CSV file for this unit to construct a two-panel visualization: one panel shows the fictional input series, and the other shows the result of a transparently specified numerical transformation. Add labeled axes and units; do not connect samples with a line if the observation units are unrelated. Explain whether outliers represent legitimate processes, data errors or unknowns. Describe how results change if the least complete rows are excluded, and archive the transformation steps.

**Scope and responsibility.** The educational concepts here do not generate official volcano warnings, physical access permissions or operational models of lava inundation, ash dispersal or lahar travel. Where a problem resembles an applied hazard calculation, the student must state why it lacks sufficient measurements for public-safety use. Official observatories and civil authorities are responsible for operational interpretations and decisions. For reproducible research, preserve the original dataset, all transformations, software environment information and the model assumptions.
