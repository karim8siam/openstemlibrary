## Concepts and study approach

The unit brings together **plate boundaries, magma transport, volcanic landforms, eruption chronology and geological evidence**. Physical processes can be reconstructed from deposits, rock composition, geodetic observations or measured signals, but different materials survive and different instruments observe different parts of the system. The same observable feature may arise from distinct combinations of source conditions and later changes. The aim is to identify the evidence most capable of separating interpretations, not merely to attach a name to the most striking landform. The included case measurements and graphs were designed solely for teaching.

**Problem framing.** A fictional island arc, an oceanic spreading center and an intraplate shield are represented by cross-sections with the same vertical exaggeration. Students compare what each setting can and cannot explain. Imagine that students must present a short technical brief. Before making a numerical model, determine the time span, which observations are independent, and which boundaries are directly mapped rather than estimated. Data may have unequal completeness and calibration. Distinguish an interpretation based on direct field material from one inferred from numerical fitting or a remote sensor. Where the evidence allows more than one answer, explain what added observation would be valuable.

### Lesson 1.2: Subduction-zone volcanic arcs

**Scientific foundation.** Fluids released as a subducting slab undergoes metamorphic reactions can promote melting in the overlying mantle wedge; mantle-derived melts subsequently evolve in the crust. The issue is not resolved by knowing a label alone: observations may be indirect, sampled at different scales or altered after the event. A defensible explanation specifies the process, its boundary conditions and a plausible competing mechanism.

**Evidence and method.** Compare trench position, slab dip, earthquake depths and the distribution of eruptive centers using schematic geophysical cross-sections. Define the instrument or mapping method, the measured physical quantity, the sampling interval and the possible biases. State what could be directly checked by another observer, without requiring them to accept your interpretation first.

**Common interpretive trap.** An arc volcano does not necessarily erupt liquid made directly from the subducted slab; that oversimplifies mantle wedge melting and crustal processing. The corresponding methodological safeguard is to compare observations that should differ between explanations. A failure to collect discriminating data is a limitation, not proof that the working mechanism is right.

#### Mechanisms and competing explanations

A causal model should connect source conditions, transport, transformation and the observable product. The central mechanism is: Fluids released as a subducting slab undergoes metamorphic reactions can promote melting in the overlying mantle wedge; mantle-derived melts subsequently evolve in the crust. This description predicts particular textures or signals, yet alternate processes might also produce parts of the same pattern. Use **An arc volcano does not necessarily erupt liquid made directly from the subducted slab; that oversimplifies mantle wedge melting and crustal processing.** as a test of an attractive but inadequate story. Set out two hypotheses with predictions that diverge at a practical sampling location. Retain the less dramatic hypothesis in the record until some observation actually rules it out; familiarity is not a substitute for comparison.

#### Contradictions worth testing

A scientific explanation is strengthened when it survives serious attempts at falsification. Here, a specific danger is: An arc volcano does not necessarily erupt liquid made directly from the subducted slab; that oversimplifies mantle wedge melting and crustal processing. Write the interpretation that a skeptical colleague would advance from the same field notebook. Then identify what the two explanations predict differently in rock fabric, chemistry, geophysical time series or deposit distribution. Record the proposed test before examining the answer so it cannot be adjusted after the fact. Where more than one hypothesis survives, report that ambiguity instead of choosing the version that tells the most coherent story.

#### Measurements and evidence quality

Implement the following discipline-specific measurement plan: Plot a conceptual mantle pressure-temperature path relative to a dry peridotite solidus and distinguish shallow volcanism from deep source conditions. Make sampling and calibration explicit. If an instrument observes several correlated points, avoid reporting them as fully independent replications. Carry the raw data and a record of exclusions into the final analysis. If a signal is close to the detection limit, show an interval or a qualified statement rather than an extra decimal place. Ask whether the same observational conclusion would emerge if the most uncertain record were removed or processed using another defensible method.

#### Worked quantitative reasoning

The numerical exercise uses fictional inputs and one declared equation, so it can be recalculated without external data. First determine the measured quantity, units, reference level and temporal averaging interval. Change the model input by a modest fraction and check whether the response is linear, nonlinear or bounded. Be careful not to infer that a good calculator result validates the underlying physics. For **ridges, rifts, and decompression melting**, numbers support an explanation only when the chosen equation applies to the process, not merely because the units can be made to cancel.

#### Interpretation, uncertainty, and safe limits

When presenting a result, separate the observation, the calculation and the implication in three sentences. In this topic, **A young-looking surface may be an eroded ancient flow or a weathered recent one; color and vegetation are unreliable absolute clocks.** remains an important constraint even when the exercise appears numerically neat. The scientific report should end by naming what would have to be independently observed before any consequential application.

## Integrated research studio: Unit 1

**Scenario.** A fictional island arc, an oceanic spreading center and an intraplate shield are represented by cross-sections with the same vertical exaggeration. Students compare what each setting can and cannot explain. Assemble an evidence ledger with a row for each observation, its provenance, its measured units, and an explicit distinction between original observation and interpreted process. Add a third column for alternative hypotheses. Decide which items could be affected by the same source of error or sampling gap before computing averages, rates or correlations. A synthetic observation should be documented as such both in the file name and in any figure legend, because a decontextualized number can appear real after it is copied into another report.

**Research sequence.** First define a narrowly stated question. Second draw the relevant physical geometry or time axis. Third identify process variables that could be measured independently. Fourth check dimensional consistency of a simple model. Fifth create two alternative explanations and an observation capable of distinguishing them. Sixth record an uncertainty or sensitivity check. Seventh communicate the conclusion in a short paragraph aimed at technically informed readers and in another paragraph suited to nonspecialists, preserving the uncertainty in both.

**Practical assessment.** Use the explicitly synthetic CSV file for this unit to construct a two-panel visualization: one panel shows the fictional input series, and the other shows the result of a transparently specified numerical transformation. Add labeled axes and units; do not connect samples with a line if the observation units are unrelated. Explain whether outliers represent legitimate processes, data errors or unknowns. Describe how results change if the least complete rows are excluded, and archive the transformation steps.

**Scope and responsibility.** The educational concepts here do not generate official volcano warnings, physical access permissions or operational models of lava inundation, ash dispersal or lahar travel. Where a problem resembles an applied hazard calculation, the student must state why it lacks sufficient measurements for public-safety use. Official observatories and civil authorities are responsible for operational interpretations and decisions. For reproducible research, preserve the original dataset, all transformations, software environment information and the model assumptions.
