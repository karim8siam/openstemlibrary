# Unit 6 — Sequence stratigraphy and basin architecture

## Central question

Accommodation, sediment supply and shoreline movement.

## Learning outcomes

Analyze stacking patterns without equating every facies shift with global sea-level change.

## Extended unit perspective

Sequence stratigraphy links surfaces and stacking patterns to the changing distribution of sediment and accommodation. A tempting mistake is to describe a sand-over-mud transition as universal sea-level change. The same vertical order may reflect sediment supply, delta-lobe switching, tectonic subsidence, compaction or shoreline migration. Relative sea level is a local quantity measured against land, not automatically an eustatic global signal. Surface terminology depends in part on the analytical scheme and the scale at which a package is defined. Students should first draw physical contacts, age constraints and facies geometry, and only then apply a sequence interpretation. Alternative models should make different predictions at the shelf edge, in proximal incised valleys or in distal marine beds. The exercises are conceptual reconstructions and cannot establish real subsurface geometry without field, core and seismic evidence.

## Five lessons and worked calculations

## Lesson 6.1 — Accommodation and sediment supply

### What is being explained?

Basin subsidence, relative sea-level change, compaction and sediment input jointly control available space and preserved stratigraphy. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Stratal geometries, shoreline trajectory and thickness maps can distinguish accumulation from bypass and erosion. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

A progradational wedge may result from higher supply at fixed sea level, lower accommodation or their combination. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Sketch time-varying accommodation and sediment supply scenarios that produce similar progradation but different shelf-edge patterns. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Field notebook and facies recognition.** Write a two-column field record headed *observation* and *inference*. Enter grains, laminae, colour, contact shape and any measurable geometry in the observation column; reserve terms describing ancient rivers, shorelines or deep water for the inference column. Choose an outcrop reference scale suitable for the problem: centimetres for a ripple foreset, metres for a channel body, and kilometres for a basin map. Mark beds that cannot be observed because of cover. The same feature may be meaningful at one scale and misleading at another. Draw the simplest facies association consistent with the visible relationships and list one expected lateral equivalent. In your report do not call an exposure representative merely because it is accessible; accessibility itself may produce sampling bias.

### Worked quantitative exercise 26: Sediment accumulation relative to space creation

**Hypothetical inputs:** 0.72 mm/yr sediment accumulation and 0.90 mm/yr accommodation production under simplified common datum. The transparent classroom expression is `0.72/0.9`. Evaluating this expression produces **0.8 dimensionless**. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Compaction and tectonic tilt may alter observed thickness and geometry after deposition. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Treat accommodation and supply as jointly inferred controls, not interchangeable labels.

### Practice and evidence checks

1. Describe **accommodation and sediment supply** using three observations that do not yet assume a depositional setting.
2. State the physical reason the proposed mechanism would produce one of those observations, then identify a contrary observation that would count against it.
3. Recalculate Exercise 26 after modifying one stated parameter, document units and explain sensitivity.
4. Design an independent test using the protocol above; state a clear predicted outcome for the leading model and for its rival.
5. Write an 80-word report that explains what is known, what remains uncertain and why the distinction matters when interpreting preserved strata.


## Lesson 6.2 — Transgression, regression and shoreline trajectories

### What is being explained?

Landward shoreline movement can stack deeper-water facies over shallower-water strata, while basinward progradation can build shallowing packages. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Ravinement surfaces, retrogradational parasequences, clinoform trajectories and shoreline facies shifts support different path histories. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

Local delta abandonment may mimic marine transgression without any basinwide relative sea-level rise. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Correlate multiple proximal-to-distal sections and test whether shoreline shift is regionally coherent. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Measurement and dimensional reasoning.** For the numerical component, write the governing relation and then translate every quantity into a unit consistent with that relation. State whether thickness is true stratigraphic thickness or apparent width, whether grain size is a mass-weighted or number-weighted distribution, and whether a flux represents sediment delivered or rock preserved. A point estimate may hide a large physical uncertainty. Recalculate after changing one assumed input by ten percent, and distinguish the mathematical sensitivity from the real variability of that input. Present units in the plotted axis titles rather than only in figure captions. A sensible arithmetic result is not sufficient evidence that the equation is valid at a particular outcrop. Discuss whether equilibrium, uniform flow, conserved grain mass or constant deposition has been assumed.

### Worked quantitative exercise 27: Apparent shoreline translation rate

**Hypothetical inputs:** 2400 m shoreline displacement in 1200 years, ideal net migration not event-scale movement. The transparent classroom expression is `2400/1200`. Evaluating this expression produces **2 m/year**. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Time-transgressive facies boundaries make a one-dimensional upward-deepening log incomplete evidence. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Reconstruct trajectories from mapped positions through time, not merely the vertical order of two facies.

## Lesson 6.3 — Unconformities, flooding and maximum-regression surfaces

### What is being explained?

Erosion or nondeposition generates hiatuses; flooding surfaces and sequence boundaries require explicit operational definitions. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Erosional truncation, onlap, abrupt facies shifts and independent age gaps help identify discontinuities. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

A sharp facies contact may be a rapid depositional transition rather than a major regional unconformity. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Compare contact geometry and age control between multiple sections before assigning a sequence boundary. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Hypothesis competition and counterexample.** Begin with a pair of incompatible explanations that both seem plausible from the first observation. Instead of collecting more features that merely illustrate the preferred story, identify the measurement on which the predictions disagree. Consider that one hypothesis may involve sediment transport while the other involves later erosion or cementation; these are not mutually exclusive unless the timing is explicitly constrained. Record the expected observation under each explanation before seeing any additional dataset. Evidence that fails to discriminate is still descriptive evidence, but it does not rank the competing models. Where a third process could imitate both, give that possibility a separate row in the reasoning table. The final verdict may be that present evidence supports only a family of environments or ages.

### Worked quantitative exercise 28: Duration of inferred hiatus

**Hypothetical inputs:** 8.6 Ma age below erosional surface and 6.9 Ma above, both hypothetical; uncertainty not included. The transparent classroom expression is `8.6-6.9`. Evaluating this expression produces **1.7 Myr**. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

A boundary may vary in erosional expression laterally; not every surface is synchronous. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Separate the observed physical surface from its interpretation within a preferred sequence-stratigraphic scheme.

## Lesson 6.4 — Systems tracts and stacking patterns

### What is being explained?

Packages of progradational, aggradational and retrogradational beds are interpreted in the context of shoreline and accommodation histories. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Parasequence stacking, clinoform downlap and regional flooding surfaces may constrain an interval-scale model. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

Falling-stage, lowstand and highstand labels differ between schemes and can be overused where chronology is weak. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Create two alternative systems-tract assignments for the same synthetic correlation panel and list required age evidence. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Sampling design and data integrity.** Prepare an explicit sampling protocol: what interval is the population of interest, how will representative sites be selected, which fraction of the sediment is included, and what precision is possible? Replicate critical counts because sedimentary deposits are patchy in both space and time. A sieve sample, thin section and borehole log observe different volumes and may not sample equivalent properties. Record locations and orientation without inventing precision that was not measured. For destructive sampling, respect collection permits and institution rules. Store the original readings separately from any normalized percentages so that the transformation can be inspected later. Treat missing data as missing, not as zeros, and write down every exclusion criterion before producing an attractive final plot.

Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Nonmarine and carbonate successions can respond differently from idealized shelf clastic examples. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

## Lesson 6.5 — Basin subsidence, compaction and backstripping

### What is being explained?

Burial compacts porous sediment; reconstructing older basin geometry requires assumptions about porosity-depth trends and palaeowater depth. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Well logs, decompacted thicknesses, stratigraphic ages and fault maps support long-term basin-subsidence analysis. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

Apparent acceleration in accumulation can reflect a changing sediment supply or preservation bias instead of tectonic subsidence. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Apply a simple porosity correction to a layer, then compare alternative initial porosities and compaction lengths. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Comparative synthesis and communication.** Move from a single log to a lateral comparison. Propose which properties ought to remain similar between neighbouring sections and which may change as environments migrate. A marker bed is only a reliable time line when evidence establishes its correlation; similar sandstone colour is not such evidence. Choose a visual representation that distinguishes observed boundaries from extrapolated ones, perhaps using solid versus dashed lines and a brief uncertainty legend. Present at least one alternative explanation in the figure caption rather than burying disagreement in the final paragraph. A coherent story should preserve contradictions instead of quietly removing inconvenient beds. In the conclusion identify the smallest claim that would remain valid if your preferred process model were overturned.

Evaluating this expression produces **16.7143 m**. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Present subsidence curves as model-dependent reconstructions, not direct measurements.

## Integrated research studio: Two possible shoreline histories

Given the same coarsening-upward packages at three synthetic wells, draw both a sediment-supply-driven and an accommodation-driven history. Choose a reasonable time marker and explain how uncertainty in its correlation changes the regional interpretation. Decompact the central mud-rich interval with two porosity assumptions. Explain why matching stacking patterns does not prove a unique sea-level curve.

### Practical workflow

A teaching dataset is a deliberately simplified set of observations with enough metadata to support a reproducible calculation. It is not evidence from a named real basin. Before graphing the numbers, decide which independent variable you think orders the processes and which observations might instead reflect changes in sampling position. Draw uncertainty markers or ranges where possible, even when the figures are hypothetical. A single good-fitting curve should be treated as a question: Which physical assumptions created it, and what independent data would disprove it? The submission must contain both a physical interpretation and a statement of one competing explanation. If a student uses a generative model or plotting program, they should preserve the original measurements and equations so the result can be independently reproduced.

The first deliverable is a clean measured-section or experimental-data table with a metadata line defining measurement units. The second is a graph or annotated cross-section that does not confuse elevation and stratigraphic thickness. The third is a two-model comparison in which each model must make at least one concrete additional prediction. The fourth is a brief calculation checked by an independent method and accompanied by a description of uncertainty. The fifth is a research note that states what cannot be resolved without additional evidence. Assign confidence to individual inferences, not to the diagram as a whole.

### Data critique and transfer

A reproducible student submission must preserve a copy of the raw values, retain any derived normalized quantities separately and state which values were inferred rather than measured. Consider the possibility that the most accessible outcrop or easiest-to-analyse core was systematically unrepresentative. Plan an independent observation that would most sharply reduce uncertainty. If the best test is not feasible, present the honest limit of the inference rather than substituting a plausible-looking numerical value. This is especially important when educational methods are adapted to real land-use or subsurface questions.

## Unit 6 synthesis and assessment

Across these five topics, the central scientific skill is to distinguish a physically possible depositional interpretation from one that is specifically supported by evidence. Submit all five numerical exercises with units, the integrated research studio, and a correlation or process diagram that uses line styles to separate observed boundaries from inferred ones. Cite the source of external classification terminology when appropriate. A brief oral defence should explain which sedimentary observation would best falsify the preferred interpretation, how a missing interval affects the reconstruction, and whether the strongest evidence is geometric, compositional, chronological or process-based.

### Self-assessment questions

- Which observations in this unit are primary depositional properties, and which could be modified by burial or structural deformation?
- Which quantities are measured directly and which are model-dependent estimates?
- Where would another measured section most effectively distinguish competing hypotheses?
- Does the proposed correlation connect similar rocks, synchronous time markers or a hypothesized environmental boundary?
- What remains valid if the preferred interpretation is wrong?
