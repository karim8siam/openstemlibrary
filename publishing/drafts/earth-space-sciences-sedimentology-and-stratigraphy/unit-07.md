# Unit 7 — Diagenesis, sedimentary geochemistry and archives

## Central question

How burial changes original sediments.

## Learning outcomes

Disentangle depositional signals from compaction, cementation, dissolution and chemical exchange.

## Extended unit perspective

Sedimentary rocks commonly acquire a second and third history while buried. Uncemented sand compacts, develops contacts, dissolves unstable grains and receives pore-filling minerals. Shallow depositional porosity is therefore different from present pore geometry. Chemical measurements also require caution: an isotope ratio recorded in a limestone can be influenced by the water and reaction conditions of later diagenesis. Biological mixing may destroy lamination while preserving evidence of benthic life. Students should prepare an event sequence using cross-cutting relations among grains, cements, pores and fractures. They should contrast a measured value with the proxy interpretation derived from it. A synthetic geochemical trend is useful for practising uncertainty propagation but is not a calibrated palaeoclimate reconstruction. Lab and field safety rules remain applicable for acid testing, mineral powders and core-handling equipment.

## Five lessons and worked calculations

## Lesson 7.1 — Compaction and porosity loss

### What is being explained?

Mechanical grain rearrangement and chemical pressure solution decrease pore volume as overburden and burial temperature increase. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Grain contacts, stylolites, deformed ductile grains and porosity gradients show multiple compaction pathways. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

Low porosity might reflect early cementation rather than deep burial and intense compaction. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Calculate bulk porosity from grain and bulk densities; compare thin-section point counts with plug-scale measurements. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Field notebook and facies recognition.** Write a two-column field record headed *observation* and *inference*. Enter grains, laminae, colour, contact shape and any measurable geometry in the observation column; reserve terms describing ancient rivers, shorelines or deep water for the inference column. Choose an outcrop reference scale suitable for the problem: centimetres for a ripple foreset, metres for a channel body, and kilometres for a basin map. Mark beds that cannot be observed because of cover. The same feature may be meaningful at one scale and misleading at another. Draw the simplest facies association consistent with the visible relationships and list one expected lateral equivalent. In your report do not call an exposure representative merely because it is accessible; accessibility itself may produce sampling bias.

### Worked quantitative exercise 31: Bulk porosity from density

**Hypothetical inputs:** dry bulk density 2150 kg/m3 and grain density 2650 kg/m3, no cracks or fluid mass. The transparent classroom expression is `100*(1-2150/2650)`. Evaluating this expression produces **18.8679 %**. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Microporosity and fracture pores vary with scale; one core plug may not represent an entire interval. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Identify which part of the porosity history was depositional and which was diagenetic.

3. Recalculate Exercise 31 after modifying one stated parameter, document units and explain sensitivity.
4. Design an independent test using the protocol above; state a clear predicted outcome for the leading model and for its rival.

## Lesson 7.2 — Cementation and dissolution histories

### What is being explained?

Carbonate, silica and clay cements alter sediment rigidity and permeability; dissolution may create secondary pores. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Cathodoluminescence zoning, cement overgrowths, cross-cutting pores and grain-coating minerals can distinguish episodes. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

High quartz-cement volume can reflect long burial, silica supply or favourable nucleation rather than only maximum temperature. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Build a paragenetic sequence from pore-filling and cross-cutting relations and check regional fluid-flow history. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Measurement and dimensional reasoning.** For the numerical component, write the governing relation and then translate every quantity into a unit consistent with that relation. State whether thickness is true stratigraphic thickness or apparent width, whether grain size is a mass-weighted or number-weighted distribution, and whether a flux represents sediment delivered or rock preserved. A point estimate may hide a large physical uncertainty. Recalculate after changing one assumed input by ten percent, and distinguish the mathematical sensitivity from the real variability of that input. Present units in the plotted axis titles rather than only in figure captions. A sensible arithmetic result is not sufficient evidence that the equation is valid at a particular outcrop. Discuss whether equilibrium, uniform flow, conserved grain mass or constant deposition has been assumed.

## Lesson 7.3 — Carbon isotope excursions and geochemical proxies

### What is being explained?

Elemental and isotopic measurements can record environmental conditions, but diagenetic alteration can overprint original marine chemistry. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Stratigraphic replicate samples, paired oxygen/carbon isotopes and petrographic screening help evaluate a geochemical excursion. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

A local carbon-isotope shift may be driven by changing organic-matter sources or recrystallization rather than global carbon cycling. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Cross-correlate geochemistry with physical stratigraphy and compare least-altered microfacies in multiple sections. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Hypothesis competition and counterexample.** Begin with a pair of incompatible explanations that both seem plausible from the first observation. Instead of collecting more features that merely illustrate the preferred story, identify the measurement on which the predictions disagree. Consider that one hypothesis may involve sediment transport while the other involves later erosion or cementation; these are not mutually exclusive unless the timing is explicitly constrained. Record the expected observation under each explanation before seeing any additional dataset. Evidence that fails to discriminate is still descriptive evidence, but it does not rank the competing models. Where a third process could imitate both, give that possibility a separate row in the reasoning table. The final verdict may be that present evidence supports only a family of environments or ages.

**Hypothetical inputs:** delta13C samples -0.7‰ and -2.1‰ vs same standard; difference not environmental temperature. The transparent classroom expression is `(-0.7)-(-2.1)`. Evaluating this expression produces **1.4 per mil**. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

### Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Proxy calibration and reservoir effects complicate climate and oceanographic interpretations. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Distinguish a measured isotope ratio from the environmental variable inferred from it.

## Lesson 7.4 — Organic-rich sediments and redox interpretations

### What is being explained?

Organic production, preservation, oxygen exposure and mineral reactions control whether organic-rich mud accumulates. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Total organic carbon, lamination, pyrite textures, trace-metal patterns and fauna provide complementary but imperfect redox indicators. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

Black shale is not direct proof of permanently anoxic bottom water; productivity and sedimentation rate matter. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Compare organic carbon and trace elements with sedimentological constraints on water depth, mixing and bottom-water exchange. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Sampling design and data integrity.** Prepare an explicit sampling protocol: what interval is the population of interest, how will representative sites be selected, which fraction of the sediment is included, and what precision is possible? Replicate critical counts because sedimentary deposits are patchy in both space and time. A sieve sample, thin section and borehole log observe different volumes and may not sample equivalent properties. Record locations and orientation without inventing precision that was not measured. For destructive sampling, respect collection permits and institution rules. Store the original readings separately from any normalized percentages so that the transformation can be inspected later. Treat missing data as missing, not as zeros, and write down every exclusion criterion before producing an attractive final plot.

## Lesson 7.5 — Ichnology and bioturbation

### What is being explained?

Burrowing organisms modify sediments and preserve information about oxygenation, substrate, energy and ecology. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

### The observational record

Trace-fossil assemblages, burrow cross-cutting and the degree of sediment mixing provide contextual environmental evidence. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

### Competing interpretations

Low trace-fossil diversity could reflect salinity stress, rapid deposition or poor preservation, not only oxygen depletion. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

### A discriminating investigation

Record ichnofabric intensity across facies and compare independent oxygen or salinity indicators. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

**Comparative synthesis and communication.** Move from a single log to a lateral comparison. Propose which properties ought to remain similar between neighbouring sections and which may change as environments migrate. A marker bed is only a reliable time line when evidence establishes its correlation; similar sandstone colour is not such evidence. Choose a visual representation that distinguishes observed boundaries from extrapolated ones, perhaps using solid versus dashed lines and a brief uncertainty legend. Present at least one alternative explanation in the figure caption rather than burying disagreement in the final paragraph. A coherent story should preserve contradictions instead of quietly removing inconvenient beds. In the conclusion identify the smallest claim that would remain valid if your preferred process model were overturned.

## Integrated research studio: A carbonate that changed after burial

A hypothetical carbonate sample shows two cement generations, reduced porosity and a carbon isotope excursion. Prepare a paragenetic sequence and distinguish early marine cement from later burial filling using cross-cutting evidence. Propose a test that would show whether the isotope change was synchronous across sections or controlled by local fluid flow. Quantify how excluding altered samples changes the apparent trend.

### Practical workflow

A teaching dataset is a deliberately simplified set of observations with enough metadata to support a reproducible calculation. It is not evidence from a named real basin. Before graphing the numbers, decide which independent variable you think orders the processes and which observations might instead reflect changes in sampling position. Draw uncertainty markers or ranges where possible, even when the figures are hypothetical. A single good-fitting curve should be treated as a question: Which physical assumptions created it, and what independent data would disprove it? The submission must contain both a physical interpretation and a statement of one competing explanation. If a student uses a generative model or plotting program, they should preserve the original measurements and equations so the result can be independently reproduced.

The first deliverable is a clean measured-section or experimental-data table with a metadata line defining measurement units. The second is a graph or annotated cross-section that does not confuse elevation and stratigraphic thickness. The third is a two-model comparison in which each model must make at least one concrete additional prediction. The fourth is a brief calculation checked by an independent method and accompanied by a description of uncertainty. The fifth is a research note that states what cannot be resolved without additional evidence. Assign confidence to individual inferences, not to the diagram as a whole.

### Data critique and transfer

A reproducible student submission must preserve a copy of the raw values, retain any derived normalized quantities separately and state which values were inferred rather than measured. Consider the possibility that the most accessible outcrop or easiest-to-analyse core was systematically unrepresentative. Plan an independent observation that would most sharply reduce uncertainty. If the best test is not feasible, present the honest limit of the inference rather than substituting a plausible-looking numerical value. This is especially important when educational methods are adapted to real land-use or subsurface questions.

## Unit 7 synthesis and assessment

Across these five topics, the central scientific skill is to distinguish a physically possible depositional interpretation from one that is specifically supported by evidence. Submit all five numerical exercises with units, the integrated research studio, and a correlation or process diagram that uses line styles to separate observed boundaries from inferred ones. Cite the source of external classification terminology when appropriate. A brief oral defence should explain which sedimentary observation would best falsify the preferred interpretation, how a missing interval affects the reconstruction, and whether the strongest evidence is geometric, compositional, chronological or process-based.

### Self-assessment questions

- Which observations in this unit are primary depositional properties, and which could be modified by burial or structural deformation?
- Which quantities are measured directly and which are model-dependent estimates?
- Where would another measured section most effectively distinguish competing hypotheses?
- Does the proposed correlation connect similar rocks, synchronous time markers or a hypothesized environmental boundary?
- What remains valid if the preferred interpretation is wrong?
