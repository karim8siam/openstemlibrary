# Unit 7 — Tectonic reconstruction and field synthesis

## Central question

Combine structural observations into falsifiable regional histories. Use structural observations and reproducible geometric tests to evaluate competing interpretations.

## Extended unit perspective

Regional structural reconstruction links geometrical measurements into a chronological sequence. This often means making two cross-sections that honour the same sparse observations but differ in their interpretation of the hidden subsurface. An apparently balanced section can still be wrong if its pin line is incorrect, if stratigraphic thickness varies laterally or if a detachment is placed for convenience rather than evidence. Similarly, extension across a rift cannot automatically be computed from the sum of apparent throws on one section. The geometry must be compatible with the strike of faults, plunge of markers, rotation of blocks and the selected restoration method. In contractional settings, fault-bend and fault-propagation folds predict different hinge histories; growth strata may discriminate them if deposition is dated. In strike-slip settings, transpression and transtension can produce complicated mixtures of folds and faults even where the overall displacement is lateral. The scientific objective is not to select the most visually persuasive tectonic picture, but to state predictions before consulting independent observations. An effective regional study contains a data inventory, scale, explicit uncertainty range, alternative geometry and criteria by which a new measurement would overturn the model.

### Field and scientific objectives

Describe relevant structural objects with location and orientation, translate them between map and section, construct a physically and chronologically consistent interpretation, and test at least one alternative. The aim is to communicate exactly which information is observed, which result follows by geometric calculation and which regional mechanism is inferred. Avoid interpreting one structural indicator as proof of a single tectonic event where earlier deformation, reactivation or anisotropic rock properties remain plausible.

## Lesson 7.1 — Cross-section construction and balancing

### Mechanism and observational scale

An admissible cross-section respects contact geometry and stratigraphic order; length and area balancing depend on assumptions about deformation and compaction. Draw a basic geometric sketch and define the coordinate system, sample orientation and reference state if one is needed. 

### Evidence and documentation

Surface attitudes, well control, seismic picks and repeatable stratigraphic thickness estimates anchor a section. Document the exposure, instrument or measurement technique, scale and uncertainty before discussing tectonic implications. Maintain an observation/inference table with **pin-line assumptions, section azimuth and independent subsurface constraints** and illustrate **unrestored section, restored section and held-out prediction**. If your evidence is a two-dimensional trace of a three-dimensional surface, state exactly what its projection does and does not resolve. 

### Competing interpretation

A plausible balanced geometry may still be wrong if unseen faults or layer-thickness changes exist. Record expected results for each explanation before carrying out the test. A model requiring hidden geometry must mark that geometry as inferred.

### Test and scientific protocol

Restore beds using stated pin lines and detachment assumptions, then compare predicted subsurface intersections against held-out constraints. State the predicted direction, location, quantity or material signature under both models. Use at least one measurement not previously employed to construct the model. For **cross-section construction and balancing**, define sample selection criteria, coordinate conventions and an acceptable range of observation error. 

### Worked numerical case 31: Restoration length residual

**Invented classroom inputs:** restored bed length 109 m compared with independent initial estimate 104 m. Evaluate `100*abs(109-104)/104` to obtain **4.8077 %**. Write the governing equation separately, substitute the stated values, and check the dimensions and sign convention. For a trigonometric expression identify whether angles are entered in radians or degrees; for percentages identify the reference length or population. 

Repeat the calculation after varying the least certain input. Explain whether the change is important relative to plausible field error. If the formula neglects a deformation component, assumes a planar fault or uses a restricted stress model, identify that assumption rather than interpreting the number as exact. Distinguish geometric separation from true displacement, finite strain from its time rate and stress from material strength whenever those concepts arise in the calculation. 

### Limitation, cross-check and synthesis

Cross-section balancing does not uniquely determine fault geometry or slip history. Consider the alternative that changes the interpretation most while retaining the measured observations. Make a two-column field record. Put verifiable geometric and material facts in the left column and causal or historical interpretation in the right. For each interpreted event, cite the specific measured feature and its location. An interpretation without such a link is a hypothesis to test rather than a supported result. 

Present restored and unrestored sections together with explicit limitations. A defensible conclusion therefore has five components: observed feature, ideal relation used, leading interpretation, decisive contrary test and residual uncertainty. Indicate whether each is local to the measured exposure or can legitimately be extended across the mapped domain.

### Evidence and documentation

### Analysis protocol and deliverables

The following integrated investigation uses fictional measurements expressly manufactured to demonstrate methods. It is not a published field survey or a substitute for collecting data. Begin with original measurements and metadata; present interpreted structures in separate layers. Compute at least one simple quantity with its units, make two competing predictions, and decide which additional piece of evidence would be most informative. Report any failure of the data to distinguish models. Submit a map or diagram, annotated calculations, a brief uncertainty table and a paragraph explaining whether the result has any practical limitations. The first panel of the submitted figure should contain observations only. The second may show an inferred geometry, but include a legend differentiating solid observation lines from dashed extrapolations. The third should compare an alternative model and identify the measurement that would select between them. A model should be judged against the data it did **not** use for fitting whenever possible.

### Independent review
