# U02-L01 — Rigid-body pose in two dimensions

**Unit 2: Planar Motion and Velocity Analysis · University-level learning draft · All examples synthetic**

## Engineering question and geometry

**Question:** How do local body coordinates become global point positions?

**Scenario:** An indexer plate turns around a shaft while a sensor observes a marked corner. Begin by drawing the machine in one reference configuration, identifying the ground, the input actuator and the intended output variable. Treat the device as a rigid planar assembly only if its geometry and operating conditions justify that approximation. Mark fixed points, moving joints, relevant dimensions, motion signs and any physical stop or contact condition. A computer animation is useful for communicating the proposed motion, but it cannot demonstrate that the physical parts have sufficient clearance or that the arrangement is safe.

## Principle, constraints and derivation

**Governing relation:** `p_B=p_A+R(θ)r_AB`.

The rotation matrix is orthogonal; its transpose is its inverse, preserving link lengths and angles. A velocity analysis is meaningful only after position closure has been established. Assign a fixed reference frame and choose positive counterclockwise rotation. For a rigid link, the velocity difference between endpoints lies perpendicular to the link; radial separation cannot change instantaneously in an ideal rigid-body model. Acceleration has both the tangential term proportional to angular acceleration and the normal term proportional to squared speed. The instantaneous center solves a velocity problem, not the full acceleration problem. When camera trajectories are sampled, reduce noise carefully and report the frame interval before differentiating. A numerical derivative can be stable and still be biased by perspective or marker motion.

Define each independent quantity and units before substitution. If a formula contains an angle derivative, convert degrees to radians and apply the chain rule to move from angle to time. For a constrained mechanism, first solve position closure and then differentiate to obtain velocity and acceleration. For a loaded mechanism, separate the purely kinematic result from required contact reactions and input torque. Identify when the formula assumes a fixed pivot, a constant input speed, a continuously engaged contact, or one assembly branch. A physically meaningful calculation states its domain, not just its output.

## Worked numerical case

**Illustrative inputs:** Body-fixed point radius 0.2 m, current body angle 60 degrees, origin at pivot.

**Calculation — Rotated endpoint horizontal coordinate:** **0.1 m**. These values were chosen for teaching, not measured from machinery. Put each input and its unit on a separate line in a reproducible calculation sheet, then verify the arithmetic with an independent computation. The sign of a rotational quantity indicates orientation; it does not imply an error. When the output is a force, check the implied work and reaction path. When it is a geometric dimension, check the assembly envelope and the full intended motion cycle, not only the illustrated pose.

**Sanity test:** Vary one relevant parameter by 10% and classify the resulting trend as linear, inverse, quadratic or configuration-dependent. Consider a near-limit case and ask which assumption would fail first. The calculation cannot independently prove its own assumptions: a derived value must eventually be compared with a separate observation, compatible published reference or physically grounded second model.

## Engineering interpretation and counterexample

**Frequent mistake:** Adding a local offset without rotating it to the current body orientation.

**Specific sensitivity investigation:** Rotate the entire coordinate system without altering the mechanism. Show that physical distances remain unchanged while component coordinates transform. State what is held fixed, what changes and whether the output should be continuous. For closed-loop linkages, a second numerical solution may correspond to a different assembly branch; continuous angle tracking and a small closure residual are essential. For cams, belts, transmissions and vibration models, identify contact state, speed range and sources of energy dissipation. If a model produces exceptionally high speed or force near an ideal singularity, report the sensitivity and stop short of treating an unbounded prediction as achievable hardware performance.

A result matters when it can influence an engineering decision: mechanism selection, operating speed, joint configuration, allowable tolerance or actuator requirement. Always distinguish theoretical feasibility from reliability over many cycles. Geometry can be correct while friction, wear, fatigue, excessive contact stress or dynamic instability invalidates the design. A response that is numerically precise but sensitive to unmeasured clearances should be given as a qualified estimate rather than a certified value.

## Test and reproducibility plan

**Investigation:** Mark two points on a disc, photograph several poses and compare transformed coordinates. Record the drawing and initial settings before running the test. An appropriate student exercise can use slow, guarded motion, video tracking, or a separate numerical implementation; an energized high-speed experiment needs professional safety controls. Document reference length, sampling interval, calibration method and expected uncertainty. Compare at least three configurations, including a near-boundary case if it can be studied safely. Keep synthetic values separated from measured observations, and do not count the same input data as independent validation.

The report should contain a labeled schematic, equations, raw input table, one calculated output and its units, a sensitivity result and a discrepancy explanation. If an observed path differs from the model, consider perspective error, misidentified joints, slippage, structural flexibility, bearing clearance and unintended motion. Decide which extra measurement would distinguish the most plausible causes. A useful verification plan explains not only what evidence would support a model but what evidence would require it to be revised.

## Exercises and assessment

1. **Numerical variation:** Recalculate the worked example after changing one driving parameter by 15%. State the new answer, its units and the percentage change. Identify any hidden domain restriction, such as a minimum rod length or positive stiffness.
2. **Mechanism reasoning:** How do local body coordinates become global point positions? Contrast the original situation with a changed link, joint, contact condition or speed regime. Draw the new physical picture and explain which equation remains valid, requires modification or fails.
3. **Model critique:** Design an independent test for the proposed investigation, including measured variables, instrument calibration, repeatability, one confounding factor and a rejection criterion.
