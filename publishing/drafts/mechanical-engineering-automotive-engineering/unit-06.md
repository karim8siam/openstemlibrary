# Unit 06, Lesson 01 — Springs, damping and ride response

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **second-order ride dynamic response**, then evaluate whether find a compromise ride calibration. The illustrative field case is **vehicle body moving over a shallow road bump**. The primary relation is `m*x_ddot + c*x_dot + k*x = F_road(t)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

## 1. Physical model: meaning before arithmetic

A vertical suspension must support static load and filter disturbances while maintaining tire contact. The one-degree-of-freedom model gives intuition: spring stiffness controls static displacement and natural frequency, while damping dissipates oscillation. Damping that suppresses repeated bounce can transmit a sharp impact more strongly. Road profiles and seat sensitivity span frequencies, so comfort cannot be represented by one stiffness number.

A vehicle's response is generated by several coupled subsystems. To study second-order ride dynamic response, isolate the relevant physical interaction without forgetting the boundary terms. Treat the vehicle as a system with an initial state: speed, loading, gear or drive mode, road slope and environmental conditions can all alter the answer. Otherwise the numerical output may appear precise while remaining only a scenario.

Put the chosen positive direction and sign convention on that diagram. The model is useful when its simplifications are consistent with the physical case; it is misleading when those assumptions are concealed. The critical mistake to prevent here is **treating greater damping as universally more comfortable**. Describe which term in the governing relation this mistake corrupts and how it would appear in a field measurement.

## 2. Derivation, variables and dimensional validation

Start with `m*x_ddot + c*x_dot + k*x = F_road(t)`. Identify every physical quantity, its SI unit and whether the relation is a force balance, kinematic identity, empirical approximation or definition.
