# Unit 3: Circular metallurgy and alloy quality

**Guiding question:** How do alloy chemistry, furnace operation and scrap composition govern secondary-metal value?

## The system before the material label

A foundry plans to raise post-consumer scrap fraction while continuing to promise high-ductility automotive components. Scrap contamination and energy demand interact with melt yield and quality losses. A product comparison only becomes meaningful after its required service, physical boundaries, time and mandatory safety specifications are written down. The system may contain long-lived inventories, poorly measured return streams, process losses, upstream impacts and financial constraints. The engineering objective is to find the particular intervention that actually improves useful output per resource and reduces meaningful harms. The unit combines an explicit material mechanism with a data and decision workflow, rather than presenting recycling, repair, substitution or efficiency as universally superior.

An evidence-ready project in this unit starts with **chemistry-controlled feed and qualified remelt output**. Its indispensable records include **record elemental assays, dross/slags, energy meter, batch yield and downstream acceptance**, and important counterevidence comes from **paired feedstock lots and reference-grade manufacturing controls**. Before reading any calculated result, consider **how much primary feed or sorting is required to meet every specification**. If the answer can be changed by a reasonable assumption about material quality or service life, the proper result is a bounded comparison and a plan to collect missing evidence, not a confident environmental winner.

## Lesson 3.1 — Primary and secondary steel production

### 1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Primary ironmaking commonly reduces iron oxides using carbon-bearing reductants, while scrap-based electric furnaces remelt metallic feed. The emissions and resource profiles depend on actual energy supply, reductants, scrap preparation, alloy grades and downstream operations. Recycled content does not erase furnaces, oxygen production, electrode consumption, slag handling or yield loss. Grade-matched clean scrap can support high-quality steelmaking; mixed scrap may require dilution with lower-contaminant iron.

**Working relationship:** Saleable steel = charged metal × melt-to-product yield, under a defined chemistry and quality specification. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

### 2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

**Problem statement:** An automotive sheet supplier proposes 100% mixed scrap. Evaluate tin, copper and residual limits before promising deep-drawing performance. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

**Constraint that can reverse the answer:** A carbon comparison based only on furnace labels hides electricity mix, upstream materials and downstream finishing. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

### 3. Transparent synthetic calculation and sensitivity

The paired interactive card for this lesson is **Ideal dilution of scrap impurity**. Its computational expression is `(0.5*(100-p)+0.02*p)/100`, with adjustable **Clean-metal blend share** in %. At 20 % the deterministic output is **0.404 wt% Cu**; at 62 % it is **0.2024 wt% Cu**. The two-point sensitivity is -0.0048 wt% Cu per %. These are synthetic teaching calculations, not measurements. ## Lesson 3.2 — Aluminum remelting and dross losses

### 1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Aluminum recycling avoids the chemical reduction step needed for primary metal production, but real remelting consumes energy and creates dross, oxidation losses and flux residues. Surface area matters: turnings and thin foil oxidize more readily than clean thick section scrap. Different alloy families impose strict impurity constraints; magnesium, iron and silicon control can limit routes. Melt recovery must be defined as qualified casting or ingot mass, not gross furnace tapping mass.

**Working relationship:** Qualified recovery fraction = accepted alloy mass meeting specification / incoming dry metallic mass. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

### 2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

## Lesson 3.3 — Copper purity, conductance and sorting

### 1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Copper is valuable for electrical conductivity and chemical applications, but contaminated recovered copper may not meet oxygen, sulfur or trace-element specifications. Purification technology, sorting precision and energy cost should be matched to target use. Sorting can preserve value by keeping high-purity conductors apart from mixed plumbing alloys, while indiscriminate melting destroys information about feedstock. Conductivity, ductility and surface integrity are acceptance tests, not mere cosmetic attributes.

**Working relationship:** Conductivity ratio = measured conductivity / declared reference conductivity under matched temperature and testing conditions. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

## Lesson 3.4 — Scrap residuals and dilution mathematics

### 1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

An impurity that is difficult to oxidize or remove may remain through re-melting. Dilution with low-impurity primary input can restore a composition target, but this consumes cost and can weaken the presumed environmental gain from recycling. A reliable mix calculation uses elemental mass balances and constraints, not a generic recycled-content percentage. Other alloying elements and yield losses must be accounted for separately.

**Working relationship:** Final impurity mass fraction = (m_s c_s + m_p c_p)/(m_s + m_p) for ideal mixing and no preferential loss. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

## Lesson 3.5 — Metallurgical furnace efficiency and losses

### 1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Thermal input, electricity, oxygen supply, dust filtration, slag, refractory replacement and yield affect actual resource intensity. Furnace energy quoted per tonne of liquid metal differs from energy per tonne of saleable rolled product. Temperature excursions and repeated processing of off-spec batches multiply impacts. Energy recovery and process improvements require real baselines; claimed efficiency cannot be assumed constant across grades, charge sizes or operating patterns.

**Working relationship:** Specific delivered energy = total measured energy input / qualified product output. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

## Unit 3 capstone — challenge, calculation, evidence and choice

**Integrated case:** A foundry plans to raise post-consumer scrap fraction while continuing to promise high-ductility automotive components. Scrap contamination and energy demand interact with melt yield and quality losses.

1. **Service definition and counterfactual.** Define the actual useful function and service horizon, identify the current material or process, and explain what product/service alternative is realistically available in the decision location. List safety and legal criteria that must be met before comparing optional environmental benefits.
2. **Mass, energy and quality ledger.** Construct a table of incoming material, recovered output, contaminants, rejects and inventory changes. Add the largest energy and water flows. Any unaccounted balance residual must be visibly labeled instead of silently treated as saleable recovered material.
3. **Two quantitative sensitivities.** Recalculate the unit's two interactive models. Change the default assumptions to two defensible adverse scenarios. Explain what is synthetic and what measurements would be needed to convert the question into a supplier-specific evaluation.
4. **Independent audit.** Design a sampling plan using **chemistry-controlled feed and qualified remelt output**, recording **record elemental assays, dross/slags, energy meter, batch yield and downstream acceptance** and challenging the decision with **paired feedstock lots and reference-grade manufacturing controls**. Explain how many independent production batches, assets or user cycles are needed before generalization and how the result could be biased by rejected output.
5. **Decision and falsification.** Present a one-page recommendation with a go/no-go pilot, responsible decision-maker, estimated economic and environmental trade-offs, and a measurement that would overturn your recommendation. Specifically challenge **how much primary feed or sorting is required to meet every specification**. No safety-critical release or environmental certification is authorized by the classroom exercises.

### After the capstone

Discuss whether a reduction in impact *per service* is likely to translate into lower *total* resource use. A more efficient or more recyclable product can still be produced in expanding quantities, and a durable product can still create local harms if its manufacturing is poorly controlled. Document the supply chain and user context in which the conclusion holds. A coherent sustainability argument preserves engineering functionality, mass conservation, traceability and the limits of the available evidence at the same time.
