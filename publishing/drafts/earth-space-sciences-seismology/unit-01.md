# Unit 1: Earthquake Sources, Faults, and Energy Release

## Guiding question

How does deformation accumulated in rock become an earthquake signal?

**Fictional unit investigation.** A synthetic mapped fault transect includes offset marker beds, borehole stress measurements, dated rupture traces and incomplete earthquake reports. Students separate what the rocks record from what a modern sensor records.

## Physical context

In the first unit, a central distinction is between directly measured ground motion and inferred rupture dynamics. Slip, stress and strength are spatially heterogeneous; simple source relations provide order-of-magnitude estimates, not fault-failure forecasts.

## Learning workflow

Before beginning, distinguish the scientific question, the measured quantity and the latent parameter that the student hopes to infer. Assign physical units, name a plausible alternate hypothesis, and state what data are fictional. For rupture studies, align field mapping with kinematic reconstruction. The product M0 = rigidity × area × slip represents an average, not the spatially variable rupture history; distinguish a source parameter from shaking intensity at a site. The experiments are deliberately simplified so their calculations can be independently checked. They are not validated field instruments, real-time forecasts, or site-specific hazard assessments.
