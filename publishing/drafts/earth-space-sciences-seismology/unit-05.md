# Unit 5: Ground Motion, Site Effects, and Engineering Interfaces

## Guiding question

Why do different sites shake differently during the same earthquake?

**Fictional unit investigation.** A synthetic urban transect crosses bedrock, alluvium and a sedimentary basin. Stations recorded the same distant event but display different frequency content, durations and amplitudes.

## Physical context

Source, path, site and engineered system each contribute to observed impact. A magnitude is not a direct measure of local shaking or expected losses. Teaching oscillators are valuable for reasoning about resonance but cannot certify real structures or replace seismic design standards.

## Learning workflow

Before beginning, distinguish the scientific question, the measured quantity and the latent parameter that the student hopes to infer. Assign physical units, name a plausible alternate hypothesis, and state what data are fictional. For ground motion, source, travel path, near-surface stratigraphy and structural response all matter. An average amplification ratio has frequency and motion-level dependence; do not apply simplified models as design coefficients. The experiments are deliberately simplified so their calculations can be independently checked. They are not validated field instruments, real-time forecasts, or site-specific hazard assessments.
