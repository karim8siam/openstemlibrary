# Unit 4: Earthquake Location, Depth, and Magnitude

## Guiding question

How can station recordings constrain an event in space, time and source size?

**Fictional unit investigation.** A fictional network surrounds a small event cluster, but two stations have uncertain clocks and one station lies far outside the network. Students compare a best-fit solution with uncertainty and network geometry.

## Physical context

Earthquake locations are inverse estimates obtained through physical models, arrival times and network geometry. The best-fitting coordinates may not be unique, while reported magnitude scales address different observables. A complete solution states phase picks, velocity assumptions, error model and station coverage.

## Learning workflow

Before beginning, distinguish the scientific question, the measured quantity and the latent parameter that the student hopes to infer. Assign physical units, name a plausible alternate hypothesis, and state what data are fictional. For locating an earthquake, a small residual is not proof of correct origin time or depth. P and S phase association, azimuthal coverage and velocity-model uncertainty can dominate. The experiments are deliberately simplified so their calculations can be independently checked. They are not validated field instruments, real-time forecasts, or site-specific hazard assessments.
