# Unit 07: Reproduction, Seeds and Diversity

**Level:** Undergraduate plant biology · **Scope:** theory, evidence, data and experimental interpretation.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, learners should be able to explain the four core topics, defend a prediction with a mechanistic model, identify measurement units, and evaluate at least one confounding variable.

### 7.1 Flower organization

**Core framework.** This section develops sporophyte, gametophytes, anthers, ovules and floral whorls. In plant biology, structure and process cannot be separated: the same tissue changes its behavior with development, water status and environmental history. Start with observable features, specify what is measured, and only then choose a mechanism that could explain those observations. For flower organization, distinguish a descriptive pattern from a causal explanation and state which comparison could falsify the proposed mechanism.

**Mechanistic reasoning.** Double fertilization creates embryo and endosperm with distinct developmental roles. Connect this claim to three levels: molecules and membranes, cells and tissues, and the whole organism within its habitat. Identify the driving gradient or regulatory signal, where the resistance lies, and the time-scale of the response. This prevents a common mistake: interpreting a correlation as evidence that a single molecular route is sufficient to produce an organism-level result.

**Worked scientific workflow.** Diagram diploid and haploid generations in a flowering-plant life cycle. Record the experimental unit, the treatment, a matched control, a measured response and potential confounders. Collect baseline information first. Repeated measurements from one individual are not independent biological replicates. Present uncertainty and an alternative explanation even when the predicted pattern occurs.

**Interpretation and limits.** A measured effect may be altered by species identity, genotype, developmental phase, growth conditions or instrument calibration. Report the measurement's physical units and whether the result is a rate, a concentration, a flux or a probability. Do not extrapolate the qualitative teaching model into an agronomic forecast. A scientifically persuasive account connects the observation to a testable mechanism while keeping alternative explanations visible.

**Practice prompts.** (1) Draw a causal diagram for flower organization. (2) Name two controls that would distinguish an environmental response from an ontogenetic effect. (3) Explain why biological replication and instrument calibration answer different questions. (4) Specify an outcome that would make you revise the initial hypothesis.

## Worked quantitative problems

### Problem 7.1 — controlled proportional model

A teaching experiment has a measured baseline of **14.4 units** and a fractional change of **0.4**. The task is to find the final value using the explicit assumption of proportional response. This is an arithmetic exercise, not a biological law.

1. **Define:** baseline = 14.4 units; fractional increase = 0.4; both must refer to the same observable and experimental time interval.
2. **Compute:** final value = baseline × (1 + fractional increase) = 14.4 × 1.40 = **20.160 units**.
3. **Evaluate:** the calculation assumes a linear proportional intervention without saturation. If biological feedback or resource limits operate, the measured final value can differ substantially. A valid test needs an independent control and biological replicates.

## Advanced investigation: plant reproduction, dispersal, and evolutionary interpretation

### Alternation of generations clarifies the meaning of gametes and spores

Land-plant life cycles alternate multicellular diploid sporophyte and multicellular haploid gametophyte phases. Meiosis in the sporophyte produces haploid spores; mitosis in the gametophyte produces gametes. Fertilization restores diploidy. In flowering plants, the gametophytes are highly reduced and depend on sporophytic tissues for much of their development. This is why calling pollen a 'male gamete' is inaccurate: the pollen grain is a male gametophyte, which generates or carries sperm cells depending on developmental stage.

Double fertilization in angiosperms usually yields a diploid embryo and a typically triploid endosperm, although endosperm ploidy varies among some lineages. Pollination moves pollen to a receptive structure; fertilization occurs later and may fail even after apparently successful pollination. Fruit formation, seed viability, and germination are sequential outcomes with different failure points. Seed dormancy is not synonymous with seed death, and a viable seed can remain ungerminated under unsuitable conditions.

### Breeding systems alter genetic interpretation

Self-compatibility, self-incompatibility, floral timing, pollinator access, and clonal reproduction influence realized mating patterns. A plant that possesses both male and female floral structures is not automatically self-fertilizing. In population studies, low seed set can reflect pollen limitation, resource limitation, herbivory, incompatibility, or abortion of developing fruits. An experiment adding compatible pollen should include sham-handled controls and must record whether pollination manipulations themselves damage stigmas or flowers.

### Quantitative case: germination and experimental units

Four independently prepared seed trays each receive 50 viable-looking seeds. Germination counts are 41, 44, 39, and 46 after a prespecified 10-day assessment, giving rates of 82%, 88%, 78%, and 92%. Their pooled proportion is 170/200 = 85%. Reporting only 200 seeds as 200 independent experimental units overstates independent replication if a common tray environment is influential. The tray-level mean is also 85%, but between-tray variability matters. Report the precise germination criterion (for example radicle emergence) and verify viability separately for ungerminated seeds where appropriate.

**Experimental design.** Compare treatments across randomly positioned replicate trays with equal seed source, storage history, substrate, moisture, and temperature. Record daily cumulative germination curves rather than only the final fraction, because equal final germination can mask different rates. To study pollen limitation, assign flowers across independent plants to supplemental cross-pollen, sham contact, and unmanipulated conditions and measure fruit as well as seed set.

**Diagnostic questions.** What does meiosis produce in a flowering plant life cycle? Why does pollen transfer not guarantee seed development? How could a seed be alive without germinating? Explain why flowering time can affect gene flow even when two species have overlapping geographic ranges.

## Practicum 7: Plant responses to environmental stress

### The question and why it matters

**System:** Salinity acclimation in two genotypes. The central aim of this investigation is to move from a descriptive observation to a reproducible biological measurement. Experimental interpretations are strongest when the instrument output, the biological quantity of interest, and the causal claim are kept distinct. A quantitative value is useful only with its definition, units, assumptions and uncertainty. Many textbook diagrams intentionally omit these constraints; laboratory investigation is where those omissions become visible.

### Experimental design

Compare a salt-sensitive and a salt-tolerant genotype in a randomized factorial experiment. Increase salt gradually to avoid confusing osmotic shock with longer-term ionic toxicity. Match pots for substrate and irrigation regime. Record electrical conductivity of drainage solution rather than assuming target salinity equals root-zone exposure. Measure shoot and root dry mass, leaf water status and visible damage at several time points. Control for differential growth rate before treatment and beware leaf-area normalization that can hide actual plant productivity losses.

Before collecting data, write a hypothesis with a directional prediction, define an observation window, and select a primary outcome. Predefine exclusion criteria for damaged material and log departures from the protocol. Plan at least three truly independent biological replicates for a small teaching pilot, while recognizing that a formal sample-size calculation depends on expected effect and variability. Random allocation mitigates positional bias but cannot fix systematic instrument calibration error. Mask sample identifiers during image scoring if practical; if blinding is impossible, use automated measurements and a predeclared scoring rubric to reduce subjective decisions.

### Worked quantitative interpretation

In an illustrative dataset, genotype A declines from 5.0 g to 3.5 g shoot dry mass under salinity, while genotype B declines from 4.0 g to 3.4 g. Relative retention is 70% and 85% respectively. Despite better retention, B has lower absolute stressed mass than A (3.4 versus 3.5 g). “Tolerance” must therefore be operationally defined before declaring a winner.

A reproducible report should retain raw readings alongside any normalized value. If repeated values appear, show their distribution and the source of variability before constructing confidence intervals. Converting a measured number into a larger biological conclusion requires an explicit assumption chain. For example, a bulk gravimetric measurement does not resolve cell-level transport; a count of responsive cells does not measure pathway activation; an averaged population response does not characterize every individual. These distinctions prevent overstating the meaning of the calculated estimate.

### Mistake to identify and repair

Compatible solutes necessarily cause salt tolerance: correlation is not proof. Stress can induce metabolites as damage markers as well as protective responses. Manipulation of synthesis or transport is needed to test causal contributions.

The repair is to formulate two competing explanations and state a discriminating measurement. Draw a causal graph with the manipulated exposure, the proposed mediator, the measured endpoint, and at least one confounder. Separating a mechanistic hypothesis from its observable consequences is essential because several mechanisms can predict the same direction of change. When no available measurement separates the explanations, report the result as descriptive rather than mechanistically decisive.

### Data record and quality controls

Record a unique replicate identifier; species, genotype and source where applicable; pretreatment growth conditions; measurement date; treatment assignment; instrument resolution; sample handling; and the raw endpoint. Use SI-compatible units and explicitly document any conversion. If a balance reads to 0.01 g, reporting a derived mass to six decimal places falsely suggests precision. Verify blank controls, out-of-range samples, and response saturation before averaging. If an instrument drifts over a session, record standards at both the beginning and end instead of simply assuming constancy. Archive sample exclusions with reasons, including exclusions made after seeing a result.

Distinguish **technical replication**, where one biological sample is measured repeatedly, from **biological replication**, where independently sampled individuals, populations, or experimental units are compared. Use the structure of the intervention to identify the correct unit for statistical analysis: if an entire container receives the treatment, the container is not interchangeable with each plant inside it. A design with many observations but few independent treated units can produce precise-looking yet misleading uncertainty estimates.

### Extension challenge

Choose a primary outcome for a breeding program (absolute stressed biomass or proportional retention), justify it, and discuss how that choice changes genotype ranking.

Write a short report containing (1) a falsifiable hypothesis; (2) a labelled experimental diagram; (3) a raw-data table; (4) the result with correct units and one numerical uncertainty statement where replicate data allow it; (5) an alternative mechanism and a proposed follow-up test; and (6) a clear boundary between classroom illustration and generalizable biological inference. ## Quantitative research clinic: Pollination, seed viability and population inference

Seed set is a composite endpoint. A flower may receive pollen but fail to set seed because of incompatibility, pollen viability, resource limitation or post-fertilization losses. Likewise, germination percentage depends on dormancy and incubation conditions, not only intrinsic embryo viability. Disentangling these processes requires treatment controls that target different stages of reproduction.

**Worked germination example.** Two seed lots are placed in controlled chambers. Lot A yields 74 germinants from 100 seeds and lot B yields 82 from 100. The observed difference is 8 percentage points. A simple approximation for the standard error of the difference, assuming independent seeds, is sqrt[0.74×0.26/100+0.82×0.18/100] ≈ 0.058. That uncertainty is substantial relative to the observed difference. If each lot came from just one maternal plant, treating the 100 seeds as 100 independent source plants would be pseudoreplication; maternal background might dominate.

**Pollination controls.** Include bagged flowers, sham-manipulated flowers and carefully controlled hand-pollination where ethically and legally permitted. Compare selfed, outcrossed and open-pollinated treatments only when species biology makes these contrasts meaningful. Species with self-incompatibility systems may show low selfed seed set even with viable pollen; insufficient stigmatic receptivity can produce a superficially similar result. A microscopy check of pollen tubes or an appropriately designed pollen-viability test may separate alternatives.

**Population scope.** Data from one greenhouse cultivar should not be generalized to the diversity of angiosperm reproductive systems. Field pollination is influenced by animal communities, wind, weather and landscape history. Distinguish pollinator visitation from successful pollination and distinguish fruit number from viable seed number. Report the number of flowers, individuals and field sites, rather than silently switching denominators between treatment summaries.
