# Unit 1: What Counts as Evidence for Life?

**Guiding research question:** How can a claim about unfamiliar life be tested without defining the answer in advance?

Astrobiology is an inference-driven science. Researchers combine geochemistry, astronomy, biology, geology, statistics and engineering to ask what sorts of environments could support life, what chemical and physical processes could produce complex systems, and which observations can distinguish living processes from abiotic chemistry. The strength of an explanation depends on compatibility with measurements and on explicit testing of alternatives. This unit builds a sequence of five complementary questions rather than presenting an inventory of established answers about extraterrestrial life. There is no generally accepted confirmed detection of life beyond Earth within the scope of the cited background resources, so laboratory analogs and fictional mission examples must never be portrayed as discovered organisms.

The distinction between **necessary conditions**, **sufficient conditions** and **observed evidence** controls the interpretation throughout the unit. A resource such as water may be necessary for terrestrial organisms in a particular scenario while still being insufficient to show that any organism is present. A biosignature candidate can be ambiguous even when multiple measurements are precise. Conversely, the failure to observe an indicator does not establish that a site is uninhabited, especially if the instrument cannot access shielded or chemically altered material. Wherever numerical examples appear, the reader should identify the physical equation, the assumed boundary conditions and the unknown quantities deliberately simplified away. The goal is defensible reasoning, not a probability-of-life calculator.

## Learning objectives

After this unit, students should be able to state a relevant mechanistic hypothesis, define measurable observables and units, organize competing nonbiological explanations, calculate an explicitly idealized numerical example, design a contamination-control or instrument-calibration strategy, explain where a synthetic dataset stops being informative, and write a conclusion bounded by the actual evidence. Students should be able to identify links between the molecular scale and the whole-planet scale without confusing a laboratory success with a proven historical sequence.

## Lessons

### 1.1 Astrobiology as a comparative science

**Core explanation.** Origins, evolution, distribution and future of life are connected questions, not four separate inventories. Earth supplies a known example, but its evolutionary history is not necessarily a universal recipe. A productive comparison uses organisms, minerals, fluids and radiation environments as interacting systems. A concrete measurement plan is: Construct a matrix linking an observation to a mechanism, a necessary environmental condition and a competing explanation. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

**Worked thought experiment.** A rover identifies a fine-grained sediment with carbonaceous matter but cannot initially establish the carbon source. The explanation compatible with biology must identify a causal process, a suitable environment and a route to the observable. A serious alternative is: Organic carbon may be delivered by meteorites or formed through hydrothermal and photochemical reactions without biology. A useful follow-up should compare a paired geological control and a negative instrument blank, while preserving the original location and sample treatment. If both hypotheses predict the same result at current precision, the outcome remains underdetermined.

**Quantitative example — Illustrative evidence update.** Take the fictional inputs **prior probability = 0.05; likelihood ratio = 1.8**. Under the explicitly stated classroom relation **P(H|E) = LR·P(H)/(LR·P(H) + 1−P(H))**, the result is **0.086538 probability**. The prior and likelihood ratio are made up for a method demonstration, not empirically estimated probabilities of life. Vary one measured input, inspect the sensitivity, and distinguish mathematical precision from the error in the physical assumptions. The result is an instructional calculation, not a mission observation or an estimate of the probability that extraterrestrial life exists.

**Exercises and discussion.** (1) State exactly what the hypothetical case measures. (2) Explain how organic carbon may be delivered by meteorites or formed through hydrothermal and photochemical reactions without biology. (3) Write a control that distinguishes source chemistry from analytical contamination. (4) Recalculate the example after increasing one input by ten percent and justify units. (5) Identify an independent measurement that would discriminate mechanisms. (6) Write a two-sentence conclusion containing both the result and its major uncertainty.

### 1.3 Habitability versus inhabitation

An environment can be habitable without containing organisms; conversely life may occupy transient microscopic niches missed by bulk environmental measurements. ### 1.4 Biosignatures and their ambiguities

**Core explanation.** A biosignature is a measurable property that could provide evidence of life in its geological, planetary and instrumental context. Candidate indicators include isotope patterns, spatial textures, selected organic molecules or atmospheric gas combinations. Their diagnostic value depends on known abiotic formation and destruction routes. A concrete measurement plan is: Draw a causal graph relating proposed production, transport, alteration and detection for a measured signal. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

**Worked thought experiment.** A laboratory instrument detects an organic distribution that resembles biological lipids. The explanation compatible with biology must identify a causal process, a suitable environment and a route to the observable. ### 1.5 Evidence standards and scientific integrity

**Core explanation.** Stronger life-detection claims typically combine independent measurements, controls, temporal or spatial context and explicit assessment of false positives. Many observations can be exciting without becoming a discovery. Good communication separates what a sensor detected from what a model inferred. A concrete measurement plan is: Write an evidence ledger with columns for raw measurement, calibration, inference, alternative and what new test would change confidence. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

**Worked thought experiment.** Two sensor channels report a feature in one experimental blank and in several planetary samples. ## Integrated investigation 1: A synthetic evidence review

**Case definition.** A fictional interdisciplinary team is reviewing a proposed astrobiological target related to **what counts as evidence for life?**. Its science lead receives eight columns of instrument-like readings, contextual notes and uncertainty ranges. The numbers are simulated and have deliberately incomplete information. The task is to decide whether the available resources should fund another measurement, whether a proposed life-related explanation is currently distinguishable, and what sample-handling records would be required to rule out a contamination event. The team must not silently upgrade a promising habitability assessment into a claim that organisms have been detected.

**Research workflow, phase one: reconstruct the observation.** Read the unit's synthetic CSV in `datasets/unit-01-synthetic.csv`. Every record should be interpreted as a fictitious spatial or temporal sampling event with a proxy signal and an uncertainty. Plot the raw proxy against sampling index, retain the baseline and calculate a median and interquartile range. Inspect whether signal varies smoothly with the contextual index or whether a small number of points dominate the result. Check for impossible physical values and missing units before attempting any fitting. If you choose to normalize, retain the original so another reader can reproduce the transformation. A nonzero proxy reading is not itself a biosignature because the proxy is intentionally not tied to a unique biological mechanism.

**Research workflow, phase two: compare mechanisms.** Construct three possible origins for the pattern: one biology-compatible pathway, a plausible inorganic reaction or transport process, and an instrumental artifact. The biological interpretation must identify how energy, matter and relevant environmental stability could support it. The abiotic candidate should identify specific minerals, radiation conditions or chemical pathways rather than the vague claim that 'nature could do it.' The instrumental candidate should identify how data handling could introduce a trend. For each explanation state one observation it uniquely predicts and one that it shares with the alternatives. Investigate whether a proposed test is truly independent. Repeating the same assay on samples prepared in the same batch might increase precision but not reveal preparation contamination.

**Research workflow, phase three: evaluate uncertainty.** Distinguish counting noise, calibration drift, incomplete spatial sampling, alteration after deposition and structural uncertainty in the chosen mechanism. The first two may be amenable to repeated measurements; the latter three require better design or a different observable. Report an interval or a plausible range when numerical assumptions permit, but do not assign empirical probabilities to the existence of life from invented classroom inputs. A negative result should explain the measurement detection threshold and the range of biological scenarios that the assay might miss. A positive result should describe alternate mechanisms, including routes that make organics, gases, textures or isotope patterns without living processes.

**Research workflow, phase four: propose the next instrument.** The team can choose one enhanced measurement, but must justify its effect on discrimination rather than on aesthetic resolution. For example, a spatially resolved mineral–organic association might distinguish transport histories, whereas a high-precision repeated spectrum could still be ambiguous if multiple species overlap. Design calibration standards, positive controls, negative blanks and records of transfer between instruments. Specify which data and code versions will be released so that independent investigators can attempt to reproduce the result. If destructive sample preparation is required, explain what sample fraction is retained and how archived material is linked to the original geological context.

**Deliverable.** Submit an 800–1,200 word fictional study report consisting of an abstract, instrument and data description, three mechanistic hypotheses, one worked idealized calculation, uncertainty table, proposed discriminating follow-up and public-facing conclusion. The public version must not use 'life discovered' language unless the defined evidence threshold has been independently satisfied; nothing in this synthetic exercise satisfies that standard. A strong report may conclude that the proposed biological interpretation is unresolved. Such a conclusion is valuable if it identifies a feasible measurement that could resolve uncertainty.

## Knowledge check

1. Explain a distinction between a necessary environmental condition and a life-specific indicator.
2. Describe why an organic molecule or water observation cannot independently demonstrate extraterrestrial life.
3. Identify two ways different sensors can share one source of systematic error.
4. Explain how an abiotic production pathway can change the meaning of a candidate biosignature.
5. Distinguish a detection limit from evidence of absence.
6. Provide a physically defined next test and the result that would falsify your preferred interpretation.

## Teaching-model interpretation

The two sliders in this unit operate on simple equations with explicitly specified parameters. Use them to discover the mathematical behavior of the equations, **not** to predict whether or where life exists. Change the input through the full displayed range, inspect whether the output responds monotonically and identify where the simplifications become unrealistic. A model that reproduces its own intended equation has passed only a software correctness check. Physical validation against independent observations and literature is a separate scientific activity. Take particular care when the displayed result is labeled probability: its prior odds and conditional assumptions are invented, not estimated from alien biospheres.

## Unit-specific advanced analysis: What Counts as Evidence for Life?

Compare the following two invented observations: a carbon-rich vein adjacent to sedimentary laminations, and a spatially separate carbonate patch with no organic signal. The first case can be explained through organic delivery along fractures; the second could reflect aqueous precipitation without organic incorporation. Construct a three-node causal chain for each explanation and decide whether observation of the vein's carbon isotope ratio would be decisive. Show how a terrestrial background control and an instrument blank test different sources of uncertainty. Your laboratory note must avoid the claim that both measurements are independent if they share collection tools and extraction solvents. Identify a variable that could be observed directly, such as mineral phase mapping, rather than one inferred only from the preferred mechanism. Finally, write two possible follow-up outcomes that lower biological confidence and one outcome that supports further investigation without automatically confirming it.
