# Unit 2: Cosmic Chemistry and Habitable Worlds

**Guiding research question:** How do matter, energy and planetary history establish the circumstances that life might exploit?

Astrobiology is an inference-driven science. Researchers combine geochemistry, astronomy, biology, geology, statistics and engineering to ask what sorts of environments could support life, what chemical and physical processes could produce complex systems, and which observations can distinguish living processes from abiotic chemistry. The strength of an explanation depends on compatibility with measurements and on explicit testing of alternatives. This unit builds a sequence of five complementary questions rather than presenting an inventory of established answers about extraterrestrial life. There is no generally accepted confirmed detection of life beyond Earth within the scope of the cited background resources, so laboratory analogs and fictional mission examples must never be portrayed as discovered organisms.

The distinction between **necessary conditions**, **sufficient conditions** and **observed evidence** controls the interpretation throughout the unit. A resource such as water may be necessary for terrestrial organisms in a particular scenario while still being insufficient to show that any organism is present. A biosignature candidate can be ambiguous even when multiple measurements are precise. Conversely, the failure to observe an indicator does not establish that a site is uninhabited, especially if the instrument cannot access shielded or chemically altered material. Wherever numerical examples appear, the reader should identify the physical equation, the assumed boundary conditions and the unknown quantities deliberately simplified away. The goal is defensible reasoning, not a probability-of-life calculator.

## Learning objectives

After this unit, students should be able to state a relevant mechanistic hypothesis, define measurable observables and units, organize competing nonbiological explanations, calculate an explicitly idealized numerical example, design a contamination-control or instrument-calibration strategy, explain where a synthetic dataset stops being informative, and write a conclusion bounded by the actual evidence. Students should be able to identify links between the molecular scale and the whole-planet scale without confusing a laboratory success with a proven historical sequence.

## Lessons

### 2.1 Interstellar and circumstellar organic chemistry

**Core explanation.** Astrochemistry studies molecules in gas, ice and dust, including organics that form without biology. Spectroscopy identifies species through characteristic absorption or emission patterns, whereas laboratory analogs help explain grain-surface reaction pathways. Detection of an organic compound in space establishes chemistry, not extraterrestrial life. A concrete measurement plan is: Relate a spectral absorption feature to transition assignments, background subtraction and detection limits. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

**Worked thought experiment.** A cold molecular cloud presents overlapping spectral lines assigned to two candidate species. The explanation compatible with biology must identify a causal process, a suitable environment and a route to the observable. A serious alternative is: Temperature, excitation conditions, line blends and instrumental response can mimic a simple abundance change. A useful follow-up should compare a paired geological control and a negative instrument blank, while preserving the original location and sample treatment. If both hypotheses predict the same result at current precision, the outcome remains underdetermined.

**Quantitative example — Incident flux from a Sun-like star.** Take the fictional inputs **a = 0.7 AU; L = 1 solar luminosity**. Under the explicitly stated classroom relation **S/S⊕ = (L/L⊙)/(a/AU)^2**, the result is **2.0408 Earth flux**. Bolometric flux is not a prediction of surface habitability. Vary one measured input, inspect the sensitivity, and distinguish mathematical precision from the error in the physical assumptions. The result is an instructional calculation, not a mission observation or an estimate of the probability that extraterrestrial life exists.

**Exercises and discussion.** (1) State exactly what the hypothetical case measures. (2) Explain how temperature, excitation conditions, line blends and instrumental response can mimic a simple abundance change. (3) Write a control that distinguishes source chemistry from analytical contamination. (4) Recalculate the example after increasing one input by ten percent and justify units. (5) Identify an independent measurement that would discriminate mechanisms. (6) Write a two-sentence conclusion containing both the result and its major uncertainty.

### 2.2 Planet formation and volatile inventories

**Core explanation.** Accretion, collisions, differentiation and subsequent delivery can determine whether rock, carbon, nitrogen and water are available. Volatile inventories evolve under escape, impacts, degassing and sequestration. Planets with similar current masses need not have followed similar trajectories. A concrete measurement plan is: Make a conservation diagram of initial reservoirs and transfers during accretion and late evolution. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. ### 2.3 Stellar radiation and atmospheric erosion

**Core explanation.** Host-star luminosity, ultraviolet radiation, flares and stellar wind change atmospheric chemistry and can alter long-term water retention. Habitable-zone distance is a first radiative screen, not a life probability. Spectral energy distributions influence photochemical pathways even at the same total incident flux. A concrete measurement plan is: Compute incident bolometric flux and identify why equal integrated flux can conceal ultraviolet differences. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

### 2.4 Planetary energy budgets and climate states

**Core explanation.** Absorbed stellar energy, thermal emission, albedo and infrared opacity interact with heat transport and planetary rotation. The blackbody equilibrium-temperature estimate illustrates a balance but does not simulate clouds, greenhouse gases, oceans or circulation. Positive and negative feedbacks can permit different climate states at similar forcing. A concrete measurement plan is: Calculate blackbody temperature under stated albedo, emissivity and redistribution assumptions. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

**Worked thought experiment.** A model exoplanet has a moderate effective temperature but a dense carbon dioxide atmosphere. The explanation compatible with biology must identify a causal process, a suitable environment and a route to the observable. A serious alternative is: Greenhouse heating or reflective clouds can make a surface temperature very different from the radiative estimate. ### 2.5 Geological cycling and surface stability

**Core explanation.** Weathering, volcanism, plate tectonics or other forms of crustal recycling may regulate reservoirs of reactive gases and nutrients. No single geodynamic pathway is demonstrated to be mandatory for life. Geological processes may also preserve or erase evidence that investigators later seek. A concrete measurement plan is: Construct a qualitative carbon-reservoir mass balance with flux uncertainties and unobserved compartments. The interpretation should specify instrument limitations, physical context and which rival models make distinct predictions. The purpose is to test a mechanism, not simply to accumulate correlated observations.

**Worked thought experiment.** Ancient lake sediments alternate between oxidized and reduced minerals. The explanation compatible with biology must identify a causal process, a suitable environment and a route to the observable. ## Integrated investigation 2: A synthetic evidence review

**Case definition.** A fictional interdisciplinary team is reviewing a proposed astrobiological target related to **cosmic chemistry and habitable worlds**. Its science lead receives eight columns of instrument-like readings, contextual notes and uncertainty ranges. The numbers are simulated and have deliberately incomplete information. The task is to decide whether the available resources should fund another measurement, whether a proposed life-related explanation is currently distinguishable, and what sample-handling records would be required to rule out a contamination event. The team must not silently upgrade a promising habitability assessment into a claim that organisms have been detected.

**Research workflow, phase one: reconstruct the observation.** Read the unit's synthetic CSV in `datasets/unit-02-synthetic.csv`. Every record should be interpreted as a fictitious spatial or temporal sampling event with a proxy signal and an uncertainty. Plot the raw proxy against sampling index, retain the baseline and calculate a median and interquartile range. Inspect whether signal varies smoothly with the contextual index or whether a small number of points dominate the result. Check for impossible physical values and missing units before attempting any fitting. If you choose to normalize, retain the original so another reader can reproduce the transformation. A nonzero proxy reading is not itself a biosignature because the proxy is intentionally not tied to a unique biological mechanism.

**Research workflow, phase two: compare mechanisms.** Construct three possible origins for the pattern: one biology-compatible pathway, a plausible inorganic reaction or transport process, and an instrumental artifact. The biological interpretation must identify how energy, matter and relevant environmental stability could support it. The abiotic candidate should identify specific minerals, radiation conditions or chemical pathways rather than the vague claim that 'nature could do it.' The instrumental candidate should identify how data handling could introduce a trend. For each explanation state one observation it uniquely predicts and one that it shares with the alternatives. Investigate whether a proposed test is truly independent. Repeating the same assay on samples prepared in the same batch might increase precision but not reveal preparation contamination.

**Research workflow, phase three: evaluate uncertainty.** Distinguish counting noise, calibration drift, incomplete spatial sampling, alteration after deposition and structural uncertainty in the chosen mechanism. The first two may be amenable to repeated measurements; the latter three require better design or a different observable. Report an interval or a plausible range when numerical assumptions permit, but do not assign empirical probabilities to the existence of life from invented classroom inputs. A negative result should explain the measurement detection threshold and the range of biological scenarios that the assay might miss. A positive result should describe alternate mechanisms, including routes that make organics, gases, textures or isotope patterns without living processes.

**Research workflow, phase four: propose the next instrument.** The team can choose one enhanced measurement, but must justify its effect on discrimination rather than on aesthetic resolution. For example, a spatially resolved mineral–organic association might distinguish transport histories, whereas a high-precision repeated spectrum could still be ambiguous if multiple species overlap. Design calibration standards, positive controls, negative blanks and records of transfer between instruments. Specify which data and code versions will be released so that independent investigators can attempt to reproduce the result. If destructive sample preparation is required, explain what sample fraction is retained and how archived material is linked to the original geological context.

**Deliverable.** Submit an 800–1,200 word fictional study report consisting of an abstract, instrument and data description, three mechanistic hypotheses, one worked idealized calculation, uncertainty table, proposed discriminating follow-up and public-facing conclusion. The public version must not use 'life discovered' language unless the defined evidence threshold has been independently satisfied; nothing in this synthetic exercise satisfies that standard. A strong report may conclude that the proposed biological interpretation is unresolved. Such a conclusion is valuable if it identifies a feasible measurement that could resolve uncertainty.

## Knowledge check

1. Explain a distinction between a necessary environmental condition and a life-specific indicator.
2. Describe why an organic molecule or water observation cannot independently demonstrate extraterrestrial life.
3. Identify two ways different sensors can share one source of systematic error.
4. Explain how an abiotic production pathway can change the meaning of a candidate biosignature.
5. Distinguish a detection limit from evidence of absence.
6. Provide a physically defined next test and the result that would falsify your preferred interpretation.

## Teaching-model interpretation

The two sliders in this unit operate on simple equations with explicitly specified parameters. Use them to discover the mathematical behavior of the equations, **not** to predict whether or where life exists. Change the input through the full displayed range, inspect whether the output responds monotonically and identify where the simplifications become unrealistic. A model that reproduces its own intended equation has passed only a software correctness check. Physical validation against independent observations and literature is a separate scientific activity. Take particular care when the displayed result is labeled probability: its prior odds and conditional assumptions are invented, not estimated from alien biospheres.

## Unit-specific advanced analysis: Cosmic Chemistry and Habitable Worlds

Consider two fictitious rocky worlds orbiting stars that provide identical total radiative flux at the planets. One star has a different ultraviolet spectral distribution, while the worlds also have distinct volatile inventories and possible atmospheric escape histories. Build a comparison table covering photochemistry, high-energy irradiation, cloud reflectance, surface pressure and surface water stability. Calculate blackbody temperature only after making albedo and redistribution assumptions explicit. Design a spectral test that could distinguish hydrogen-rich and carbon-dioxide-rich atmospheric scenarios. A temperate effective temperature should be interpreted as one property of the model, not proof of oceans. Explain how a geological record, unavailable for distant exoplanets, would constrain the temporal history of volatiles. State which uncertainties enter from the star and which depend on the planet. Your conclusion should distinguish what current light measurements could reveal from what would remain unconstrained about biology.
