§6.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Capital, Operating Costs and Project Economics

Design objective: Use synthetic transparent costs and profitability sensitivity without presenting them as market prices.

Common teaching scenario: A candidate modular process plant with equipment costs, annual product contribution and a finite economic horizon.

Unit-level engineering decision

The essential problem in this unit is not memorizing equations. It is forming a defensible decision from incomplete evidence. Use synthetic transparent costs and profitability sensitivity without presenting them as market prices. The case studies are synthetic; they provide a transparent set of inputs that can be checked without access to an operating facility. Before a numerical conclusion is adopted, identify the required physical evidence, constraints, and operating modes. Make clear whether the outcome is a conceptual screening result, a preliminary design basis, a vendor-specified rating, or a compliance decision. The four lessons examine distinct aspects of this decision.

Structured lesson sequence

1. [Cost-estimate classes and scaling limits](../lessons/unit-06-lesson-01.html)

Estimate maturity determines precision; a scalar cost law is at best early screening. Governing equation: C_2 = C_1 (S_2/S_1)^n. Numerical benchmark: With n=0.65, doubling size multiplies fitted base equipment cost by approximately 1.569. Decision question: Attach an uncertainty range and scope exclusions to every screening estimate.

2. [Cash flows, NPV and opportunity cost](../lessons/unit-06-lesson-02.html)

Discounting compares when investments and returns occur under a declared financial basis. Governing equation: NPV = −I_0 + Σ CF_t/(1+r)^t. Numerical benchmark: An illustrative investment 4 million and level annual net cash inflow 1 million for six years at 10% yields NPV about 0.355 million. Decision question: A positive illustrative NPV is not evidence that market demand or cost assumptions are true.

3. [Break-even production and operating leverage](../lessons/unit-06-lesson-03.html)

Calculate how fixed costs, margin and achievable saleable output interact. Governing equation: break-even units = annual fixed cost/(price per unit − variable cost per unit). Numerical benchmark: A $600000 fixed burden and $150/t contribution require 4000 saleable tonnes per year. Decision question: Test demand downside and yield loss separately from mechanical availability.

4. [Sensitivity and stage-gate investment](../lessons/unit-06-lesson-04.html)

Capital should advance when uncertainty has been reduced, not just when the base-case spreadsheet is positive. Governing equation: sensitivity ≈ ΔNPV/Δinput for finite, clearly stated perturbations. Numerical benchmark: Compare the NPV with and without an extra 20% investment; report the change, not just one favorable baseline. Decision question: A good investment gate includes disqualifying evidence and an accountable next measurement.

Integrated design activity

Construct a compact design dossier for the following system: A candidate modular process plant with equipment costs, annual product contribution and a finite economic horizon. The dossier must contain a flowsheet or decision diagram, a variable and unit register, an assumption register, at least two checks based on first principles, a sensitivity chart from one of the interactive models, a documented failure case, and an explicit decision with a reversal condition. Begin with the minimum information needed to formulate the problem; do not assign invented precise values to unknown vendor or market inputs. When data is unavailable, use a marked synthetic scenario and report the evidence gap.

Evidence and model boundaries

Topic 1 — Cost-estimate classes and scaling limits. Never represent fabricated illustrative costs as supplier quotations. The next evidence sources are vendor budget quotations, location factors, installation scope and estimate basis. Any screening estimate using C_2 = C_1 (S_2/S_1)^n remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Topic 2 — Cash flows, NPV and opportunity cost. Do not conflate accounting profit, EBITDA and cash available to investors. The next evidence sources are tax assumptions, working-capital model, prices, vendor estimate and implementation schedule. Any screening estimate using NPV = −I_0 + Σ CF_t/(1+r)^t remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Topic 3 — Break-even production and operating leverage. The break-even equation is invalid when contribution per unit is nonpositive. The next evidence sources are customer prices, contracts, variable utilities and fixed staffing expenses. Any screening estimate using break-even units = annual fixed cost/(price per unit − variable cost per unit) remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Topic 4 — Sensitivity and stage-gate investment. Differentiate uncertainty reduction, contingency and financial risk transfer. The next evidence sources are cost confidence class, commercial tests, utilities offers and permitting status. Any screening estimate using sensitivity ≈ ΔNPV/Δinput for finite, clearly stated perturbations remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Unit laboratories

  • [Lab 16: Illustrative cost–capacity scaling](../labs/lab-16.html) — Test dependence of a screening equipment-cost estimate on capacity.. Verification: At candidate equal reference size, estimate must equal reference cost.
  • [Lab 17: Discounted cash flow and NPV](../labs/lab-17.html) — Evaluate synthetic level annual cash flows under a declared real or nominal discount basis.. Verification: At discount rate zero, NPV must be −I+CF×N; NPV falls with higher rate for positive inflows.
  • [Lab 18: Break-even annual output](../labs/lab-18.html) — Connect contribution margin with required saleable mass.. Verification: When unit contribution is nonpositive, show no finite break-even quantity.

Unit review protocol

For each model, run default and altered cases. Check the governing relation algebraically for one point; verify dimensional consistency and domain validity; identify at least one limiting case; export a CSV and preserve the selected parameter settings in the report. A smooth graph can still be wrong, so treat independent calculations and physical evidence as separate gates. Before using the result outside a classroom, request suitable measured inputs, professional design reviews and applicable approvals.

§6.2 Engineering Topic 6.2: Unit 6 · Lesson 1: Cost-estimate classes and scaling limits

Unit 6 · Lesson 1: Cost-estimate classes and scaling limits

Learning pathway: [Capital, Operating Costs and Project Economics](../units/unit-06.html) · [Simulation 16: Illustrative cost–capacity scaling](../labs/lab-16.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-06.html).

Engineering problem and conceptual boundary

Estimate maturity determines precision; a scalar cost law is at best early screening. The central design case for this part of the course is: A candidate modular process plant with equipment costs, annual product contribution and a finite economic horizon. Design work should begin by deciding what the process is required to accomplish, what physical behavior is known, what remains an assumption, and which constraints make a candidate concept unacceptable. A calculated number that is not connected to a defined engineering decision has little project value. For this lesson the decision is: Attach an uncertainty range and scope exclusions to every screening estimate.

The exponent is a scenario assumption and may not apply across technology change, pressure class or material changes. Interpret that physical observation as a condition on the model rather than as a measured result. A screening model can provide a reliable direction of sensitivity without providing a trustworthy absolute plant rating. Before applying a particular numerical result, identify its process boundary, time basis, component definition and operating mode. A rate evaluated under steady conditions cannot represent a transient upset unless accumulation and duration are modeled explicitly.

Governing relationship and dimensional basis

The principal starting relationship is C_2 = C_1 (S_2/S_1)^n. Write out the quantities in this equation and ask which are independently specified and which are inferred from other assumptions. Check that both sides have compatible units. When an output is expressed as a fraction it must not be treated as a mass or molar rate without multiplying by an appropriate total flow. When a cost or duty is given per year, make the operating-hour and utilization basis visible; a nameplate rate alone does not produce an annual total.

For cost-estimate classes and scaling limits, the model is deliberately formulated as a minimal relation. A relation can be algebraically correct but physically incomplete, because it omits phenomena that do not appear in its chosen boundary. The specific concern here is: Never represent fabricated illustrative costs as supplier quotations. In a design review, show the equation as an auditable expression and keep the evidence for its coefficients separate from the mathematical logic. If an assumed parameter later changes, the calculation may need to be revisited across the entire flowsheet rather than edited only in one table.

Worked engineering check

Illustrative case, not plant measurement: With n=0.65, doubling size multiplies fitted base equipment cost by approximately 1.569. Copy the stated inputs into a notebook, calculate the result independently using the governing expression and retain enough significant digits to detect a balance error. When you write the result, distinguish an exact identity within the model from a rounded numerical example. For example, an exact component balance can hold to floating-point tolerance, while an empirical estimate of reaction behavior may be uncertain by orders of magnitude if fitted kinetics are unavailable.

Explain why the chosen arithmetic is useful for cost-estimate classes and scaling limits but not sufficient for a detailed design decision. The calculation provides a specific benchmark for checking the interactive lab, not a guarantee that the input values are representative. If an initial calculation produces a result that contradicts expected physical bounds—such as composition outside the feed range, negative absolute inventory, positive losses with a perfect collection assumption, or implausible unit conversions—do not tune inputs simply to improve the display. Revisit the boundary and the assumption that creates the inconsistency.

Laboratory investigation with a falsifiable prediction

Open [Simulation 16: Illustrative cost–capacity scaling](../labs/lab-16.html). The implemented relation is C2 = Cref × (Q/Qref)^n. Before moving a control, predict what will happen to the output when the dominant parameter increases. Move that parameter to a value roughly halfway between the default and one end of its permitted interval. Record the previous and revised numerical outputs, the units, and a one-sentence explanation based on the equation. Then test a second parameter that changes the trend or limits the conclusion.

Specific falsification task: At candidate equal reference size, estimate must equal reference cost. If the code violates this check, the appropriate result is a failing test and a documented defect, not an attractive screenshot. Investigation prompt: At what scale ratio does the cost-saving from sublinear scaling meaningfully affect investment? The numerical lab is intentionally small enough to trace by hand; it should stimulate a quantitative question about process behavior rather than replace a commercial flowsheet simulator.

A defensible design record

For this lesson, create a one-page calculation note with: a clearly stated objective, a simple process or equipment boundary, all specified inputs including source and units, the model equation, the worked numeric output, one independent check, at least two changes of a parameter, and a decision with a stated uncertainty. The immediate evidence package to acquire is: vendor budget quotations, location factors, installation scope and estimate basis. Label synthetic data plainly. Do not call an illustrative scenario a site survey, a vendor quotation, a laboratory result or a market forecast.

The most important decision consequence is: Attach an uncertainty range and scope exclusions to every screening estimate. A useful design note therefore contains a condition under which the recommendation would reverse. For example, the preferred concept may change if an availability assumption falls, an energy price rises, a flow composition moves beyond a separation range, or the estimated installed cost is outside the available budget. A reviewer needs the reversal threshold or at least a targeted experiment, not an unconditional claim that the concept is optimal.

Failure modes, omissions and escalation

Failure mode 1 — invalid physical simplification: Never represent fabricated illustrative costs as supplier quotations. If a physical mechanism outside the model dominates the real process, numerical refinement cannot rescue the conclusion. Failure mode 2 — missing input provenance: vendor budget quotations, location factors, installation scope and estimate basis Without that evidence, parameter values remain unverified. Failure mode 3 — misuse of precision: a software result displayed to four decimals may still originate in an order-of-magnitude engineering assumption. Accuracy is controlled by evidence, not the number of digits shown.

Applicability boundary: Synthetic currency values; no vendor quote, installation, pressure rating, escalation or index correction. This educational module does not size pressure vessels, specify safety instrumented functions, certify emissions compliance, supply construction documents or establish investment attractiveness. For an actual facility, relevant professional review, regulatory analysis, vendor thermal and mechanical design, and empirical validation are separate requirements that cannot be delegated to this one equation.

Independent study and examiner prompts

Conceptual: In what circumstances does the assumed relationship for cost-estimate classes and scaling limits lose predictive value? Name one physical effect and one organizational or data-management effect. Quantitative: Reproduce the worked case with units and change one input by ten percent. State the sensitivity of the output, including whether a finite-difference result agrees with the local algebraic trend. Application: Relate the result to the unit case—A candidate modular process plant with equipment costs, annual product contribution and a finite economic horizon.—and identify the critical tradeoff between an apparently favorable result and its implementation.

Critical appraisal: Draft the most persuasive argument against acting on this lesson’s estimate. Your argument must refer to evidence rather than taste. It should include at least one unavailable measurement from this list: vendor budget quotations, location factors, installation scope and estimate basis. Then give the smallest practical study—vendor request, sample test, pilot run, site visit or model comparison—that could narrow the uncertainty. Communication: Write a two-sentence summary that separates a verified equation from a synthetic scenario assumption.

Short calculation audit for reviewers

Check that the supplied equation C_2 = C_1 (S_2/S_1)^n is the same one used in the laboratory, or state why the lab computes a related screening quantity rather than the entire model. Confirm that the active scenario does not violate stated slider ranges. Confirm that no unit conversion is silently required between mass flow, molar flow, volume, energy or currency. Separate conservation-law residuals from empirical-correlation fit errors. Keep the response to a failed check in the delivered report; an invalid baseline should not be silently replaced with a different input set.

Checklist for mastery

  • I can define the process boundary and the measured or assumed quantities behind Cost-estimate classes and scaling limits.
  • I can reproduce the stated working example with units and without copying a displayed answer.
  • I can name the main omitted mechanism: Never represent fabricated illustrative costs as supplier quotations.
  • I can run and explain the numerical limit test: At candidate equal reference size, estimate must equal reference cost.
  • I can distinguish a planning estimate from a mechanical, regulatory or safety approval.
  • I can name the next evidence source: vendor budget quotations, location factors, installation scope and estimate basis

§6.5 Engineering Topic 6.5: Unit 6 · Lesson 4: Sensitivity and stage-gate investment

Unit 6 · Lesson 4: Sensitivity and stage-gate investment

Learning pathway: [Capital, Operating Costs and Project Economics](../units/unit-06.html) · [Simulation 17: Discounted cash flow and NPV](../labs/lab-17.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-06.html).

Engineering problem and conceptual boundary

Capital should advance when uncertainty has been reduced, not just when the base-case spreadsheet is positive. The central design case for this part of the course is: A candidate modular process plant with equipment costs, annual product contribution and a finite economic horizon. Design work should begin by deciding what the process is required to accomplish, what physical behavior is known, what remains an assumption, and which constraints make a candidate concept unacceptable. A calculated number that is not connected to a defined engineering decision has little project value. For this lesson the decision is: A good investment gate includes disqualifying evidence and an accountable next measurement.

Single-variable sweeps do not identify correlations or linked constraints. Interpret that physical observation as a condition on the model rather than as a measured result. A screening model can provide a reliable direction of sensitivity without providing a trustworthy absolute plant rating. Before applying a particular numerical result, identify its process boundary, time basis, component definition and operating mode. A rate evaluated under steady conditions cannot represent a transient upset unless accumulation and duration are modeled explicitly.

Governing relationship and dimensional basis

The principal starting relationship is sensitivity ≈ ΔNPV/Δinput for finite, clearly stated perturbations. Write out the quantities in this equation and ask which are independently specified and which are inferred from other assumptions. Check that both sides have compatible units. When an output is expressed as a fraction it must not be treated as a mass or molar rate without multiplying by an appropriate total flow. When a cost or duty is given per year, make the operating-hour and utilization basis visible; a nameplate rate alone does not produce an annual total.

For sensitivity and stage-gate investment, the model is deliberately formulated as a minimal relation. A relation can be algebraically correct but physically incomplete, because it omits phenomena that do not appear in its chosen boundary. The specific concern here is: Differentiate uncertainty reduction, contingency and financial risk transfer. In a design review, show the equation as an auditable expression and keep the evidence for its coefficients separate from the mathematical logic. If an assumed parameter later changes, the calculation may need to be revisited across the entire flowsheet rather than edited only in one table.

Worked engineering check

Illustrative case, not plant measurement: Compare the NPV with and without an extra 20% investment; report the change, not just one favorable baseline. Copy the stated inputs into a notebook, calculate the result independently using the governing expression and retain enough significant digits to detect a balance error. When you write the result, distinguish an exact identity within the model from a rounded numerical example. For example, an exact component balance can hold to floating-point tolerance, while an empirical estimate of reaction behavior may be uncertain by orders of magnitude if fitted kinetics are unavailable.

Explain why the chosen arithmetic is useful for sensitivity and stage-gate investment but not sufficient for a detailed design decision. The calculation provides a specific benchmark for checking the interactive lab, not a guarantee that the input values are representative. If an initial calculation produces a result that contradicts expected physical bounds—such as composition outside the feed range, negative absolute inventory, positive losses with a perfect collection assumption, or implausible unit conversions—do not tune inputs simply to improve the display. Revisit the boundary and the assumption that creates the inconsistency.

Laboratory investigation with a falsifiable prediction

Open [Simulation 17: Discounted cash flow and NPV](../labs/lab-17.html). The implemented relation is NPV = −I + CF×[1−(1+r)^−N]/r. Before moving a control, predict what will happen to the output when the dominant parameter increases. Move that parameter to a value roughly halfway between the default and one end of its permitted interval. Record the previous and revised numerical outputs, the units, and a one-sentence explanation based on the equation. Then test a second parameter that changes the trend or limits the conclusion.

Specific falsification task: At discount rate zero, NPV must be −I+CF×N; NPV falls with higher rate for positive inflows. If the code violates this check, the appropriate result is a failing test and a documented defect, not an attractive screenshot. Investigation prompt: Identify the annual cash-flow threshold giving NPV zero for the selected horizon. The numerical lab is intentionally small enough to trace by hand; it should stimulate a quantitative question about process behavior rather than replace a commercial flowsheet simulator.

A defensible design record

For this lesson, create a one-page calculation note with: a clearly stated objective, a simple process or equipment boundary, all specified inputs including source and units, the model equation, the worked numeric output, one independent check, at least two changes of a parameter, and a decision with a stated uncertainty. The immediate evidence package to acquire is: cost confidence class, commercial tests, utilities offers and permitting status. Label synthetic data plainly. Do not call an illustrative scenario a site survey, a vendor quotation, a laboratory result or a market forecast.

The most important decision consequence is: A good investment gate includes disqualifying evidence and an accountable next measurement. A useful design note therefore contains a condition under which the recommendation would reverse. For example, the preferred concept may change if an availability assumption falls, an energy price rises, a flow composition moves beyond a separation range, or the estimated installed cost is outside the available budget. A reviewer needs the reversal threshold or at least a targeted experiment, not an unconditional claim that the concept is optimal.

Failure modes, omissions and escalation

Failure mode 1 — invalid physical simplification: Differentiate uncertainty reduction, contingency and financial risk transfer. If a physical mechanism outside the model dominates the real process, numerical refinement cannot rescue the conclusion. Failure mode 2 — missing input provenance: cost confidence class, commercial tests, utilities offers and permitting status Without that evidence, parameter values remain unverified. Failure mode 3 — misuse of precision: a software result displayed to four decimals may still originate in an order-of-magnitude engineering assumption. Accuracy is controlled by evidence, not the number of digits shown.

Applicability boundary: Cash flow is a fixed synthetic amount; no financing, construction timing, taxes or disposal value. This educational module does not size pressure vessels, specify safety instrumented functions, certify emissions compliance, supply construction documents or establish investment attractiveness. For an actual facility, relevant professional review, regulatory analysis, vendor thermal and mechanical design, and empirical validation are separate requirements that cannot be delegated to this one equation.
