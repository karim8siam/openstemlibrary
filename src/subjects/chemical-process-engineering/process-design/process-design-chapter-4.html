Equipment Specifications and Preliminary Sizing

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and process engineering validation of Equipment Specifications and Preliminary Sizing. Developed for undergraduate chemical engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Process Design study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§4.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Equipment Specifications and Preliminary Sizing

Design objective: Use transparent first-cut calculations, keep cost scaling distinct from ratings and identify vendor-required information.

Common teaching scenario: A tank, transfer pump, process heater and heat-exchanger candidate are provisionally sized for the conceptual flowsheet.

Unit-level engineering decision

The essential problem in this unit is not memorizing equations. It is forming a defensible decision from incomplete evidence. Use transparent first-cut calculations, keep cost scaling distinct from ratings and identify vendor-required information. The case studies are synthetic; they provide a transparent set of inputs that can be checked without access to an operating facility. Before a numerical conclusion is adopted, identify the required physical evidence, constraints, and operating modes. Make clear whether the outcome is a conceptual screening result, a preliminary design basis, a vendor-specified rating, or a compliance decision. The four lessons examine distinct aspects of this decision.

Structured lesson sequence

1. [Tanks and buffer holdup](../lessons/unit-04-lesson-01.html)

Residence time and surge allowances determine initial working volume, not vessel mechanical design. Governing equation: V_work = Q_volumetric × residence time. Numerical benchmark: A 20 m³/h feed and a 30-minute hold-up target require 10 m³ working liquid capacity. Decision question: Separate normal working holdup from upset inventory; show which scenario drives capacity.

2. [Heat exchanger preliminary area](../lessons/unit-04-lesson-02.html)

Estimate area from a defensible duty, overall U and endpoint temperature differences. Governing equation: A = Q/(U × LMTD) for a feasible countercurrent terminal temperature profile. Numerical benchmark: At duty 250 kW, U=0.5 kW/(m² K), and LMTD=25 K, area is 20 m². Decision question: Record both energy balance residual and temperature driving-force validity.

3. [Pipes, pressure loss and pump energy](../lessons/unit-04-lesson-03.html)

Hydraulics connect diameter, velocity, friction and pump power to layout choices. Governing equation: ΔP = f(L/D)(ρ v²/2); P = ΔP Q / η. Numerical benchmark: For a fixed roughness-independent f, halving diameter raises velocity fourfold at equal volumetric flow. Decision question: Test a diameter range and calculate both pressure loss and mechanical power before selecting a preliminary line size.

4. [Design margins and vendor datasheets](../lessons/unit-04-lesson-04.html)

An engineering specification should distinguish estimated process duty from specified design rating. Governing equation: specified heat load = verified heat load × (1+ documented allowance). Numerical benchmark: A 300 kW calculated thermal load with 15% allowance yields a provisional 345 kW datasheet duty. Decision question: A datasheet is useful only if assumptions, limits and interfaces are traceable.

Integrated design activity

Construct a compact design dossier for the following system: A tank, transfer pump, process heater and heat-exchanger candidate are provisionally sized for the conceptual flowsheet. The dossier must contain a flowsheet or decision diagram, a variable and unit register, an assumption register, at least two checks based on first principles, a sensitivity chart from one of the interactive models, a documented failure case, and an explicit decision with a reversal condition. Begin with the minimum information needed to formulate the problem; do not assign invented precise values to unknown vendor or market inputs. When data is unavailable, use a marked synthetic scenario and report the evidence gap.

Evidence and model boundaries

Topic 1 — Tanks and buffer holdup. Do not use a residence-time result as a pressure-vessel or foundation design. The next evidence sources are inlet variability, overflow protection, fluid properties and maintenance constraints. Any screening estimate using V_work = Q_volumetric × residence time remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Topic 2 — Heat exchanger preliminary area. Zero or negative endpoint temperature differences indicate infeasible specified profiles. The next evidence sources are service-side fouling resistances, phase behavior and vendor thermal-rating check. Any screening estimate using A = Q/(U × LMTD) for a feasible countercurrent terminal temperature profile remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Topic 3 — Pipes, pressure loss and pump energy. Pressure equipment, NPSH and cavitation are separate engineering checks. The next evidence sources are fluid density, viscosity, operating envelope, line routing and pump curves. Any screening estimate using ΔP = f(L/D)(ρ v²/2); P = ΔP Q / η remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Topic 4 — Design margins and vendor datasheets. Vendor design and code calculations, relief sizing and materials compatibility are outside this workbook. The next evidence sources are applicable equipment code, corrosion data, design pressure and temperature, vendor quotes. Any screening estimate using specified heat load = verified heat load × (1+ documented allowance) remains conditional until those inputs are independently confirmed. A graph can make sensitivity visible; it cannot supply absent physical evidence.

Unit laboratories

  • [Lab 10: Tank working volume from holdup](../labs/lab-10.html) — Estimate steady liquid inventory for an ideal tank.. Verification: Working volume must scale linearly with flow at constant residence time.
  • [Lab 11: Countercurrent LMTD and area](../labs/lab-11.html) — Screen an exchanger only when both terminal temperature differences are positive.. Verification: If either endpoint approach is nonpositive, declare profile infeasible instead of drawing false area.
  • [Lab 12: Darcy pressure loss and pump power](../labs/lab-12.html) — Test the effect of pipe diameter on pressure loss at fixed throughput.. Verification: Pressure loss is nonnegative and scales with pipe length for fixed f and flow.

Unit review protocol

For each model, run default and altered cases. Check the governing relation algebraically for one point; verify dimensional consistency and domain validity; identify at least one limiting case; export a CSV and preserve the selected parameter settings in the report. A smooth graph can still be wrong, so treat independent calculations and physical evidence as separate gates. Before using the result outside a classroom, request suitable measured inputs, professional design reviews and applicable approvals.

§4.2 Engineering Topic 4.2: Unit 4 · Lesson 1: Tanks and buffer holdup

Unit 4 · Lesson 1: Tanks and buffer holdup

Learning pathway: [Equipment Specifications and Preliminary Sizing](../units/unit-04.html) · [Simulation 10: Tank working volume from holdup](../labs/lab-10.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-04.html).

Engineering problem and conceptual boundary

Residence time and surge allowances determine initial working volume, not vessel mechanical design. The central design case for this part of the course is: A tank, transfer pump, process heater and heat-exchanger candidate are provisionally sized for the conceptual flowsheet. Design work should begin by deciding what the process is required to accomplish, what physical behavior is known, what remains an assumption, and which constraints make a candidate concept unacceptable. A calculated number that is not connected to a defined engineering decision has little project value. For this lesson the decision is: Separate normal working holdup from upset inventory; show which scenario drives capacity.

Total shell volume also needs freeboard, minimum operating volume and any mandated hazardous-service requirements. Interpret that physical observation as a condition on the model rather than as a measured result. A screening model can provide a reliable direction of sensitivity without providing a trustworthy absolute plant rating. Before applying a particular numerical result, identify its process boundary, time basis, component definition and operating mode. A rate evaluated under steady conditions cannot represent a transient upset unless accumulation and duration are modeled explicitly.

Governing relationship and dimensional basis

The principal starting relationship is V_work = Q_volumetric × residence time. Write out the quantities in this equation and ask which are independently specified and which are inferred from other assumptions. Check that both sides have compatible units. When an output is expressed as a fraction it must not be treated as a mass or molar rate without multiplying by an appropriate total flow. When a cost or duty is given per year, make the operating-hour and utilization basis visible; a nameplate rate alone does not produce an annual total.

For tanks and buffer holdup, the model is deliberately formulated as a minimal relation. A relation can be algebraically correct but physically incomplete, because it omits phenomena that do not appear in its chosen boundary. The specific concern here is: Do not use a residence-time result as a pressure-vessel or foundation design. In a design review, show the equation as an auditable expression and keep the evidence for its coefficients separate from the mathematical logic. If an assumed parameter later changes, the calculation may need to be revisited across the entire flowsheet rather than edited only in one table.

Worked engineering check

Illustrative case, not plant measurement: A 20 m³/h feed and a 30-minute hold-up target require 10 m³ working liquid capacity. Copy the stated inputs into a notebook, calculate the result independently using the governing expression and retain enough significant digits to detect a balance error. When you write the result, distinguish an exact identity within the model from a rounded numerical example. For example, an exact component balance can hold to floating-point tolerance, while an empirical estimate of reaction behavior may be uncertain by orders of magnitude if fitted kinetics are unavailable.

Explain why the chosen arithmetic is useful for tanks and buffer holdup but not sufficient for a detailed design decision. The calculation provides a specific benchmark for checking the interactive lab, not a guarantee that the input values are representative. If an initial calculation produces a result that contradicts expected physical bounds—such as composition outside the feed range, negative absolute inventory, positive losses with a perfect collection assumption, or implausible unit conversions—do not tune inputs simply to improve the display. Revisit the boundary and the assumption that creates the inconsistency.

Laboratory investigation with a falsifiable prediction

Open [Simulation 10: Tank working volume from holdup](../labs/lab-10.html). The implemented relation is Working volume = volumetric rate × residence time. Before moving a control, predict what will happen to the output when the dominant parameter increases. Move that parameter to a value roughly halfway between the default and one end of its permitted interval. Record the previous and revised numerical outputs, the units, and a one-sentence explanation based on the equation. Then test a second parameter that changes the trend or limits the conclusion.

Specific falsification task: Working volume must scale linearly with flow at constant residence time. If the code violates this check, the appropriate result is a failing test and a documented defect, not an attractive screenshot. Investigation prompt: Find the difference between working liquid volume and total conceptual shell capacity. The numerical lab is intentionally small enough to trace by hand; it should stimulate a quantitative question about process behavior rather than replace a commercial flowsheet simulator.

A defensible design record

For this lesson, create a one-page calculation note with: a clearly stated objective, a simple process or equipment boundary, all specified inputs including source and units, the model equation, the worked numeric output, one independent check, at least two changes of a parameter, and a decision with a stated uncertainty. The immediate evidence package to acquire is: inlet variability, overflow protection, fluid properties and maintenance constraints. Label synthetic data plainly. Do not call an illustrative scenario a site survey, a vendor quotation, a laboratory result or a market forecast.

The most important decision consequence is: Separate normal working holdup from upset inventory; show which scenario drives capacity. A useful design note therefore contains a condition under which the recommendation would reverse. For example, the preferred concept may change if an availability assumption falls, an energy price rises, a flow composition moves beyond a separation range, or the estimated installed cost is outside the available budget. A reviewer needs the reversal threshold or at least a targeted experiment, not an unconditional claim that the concept is optimal.

Failure modes, omissions and escalation

Failure mode 1 — invalid physical simplification: Do not use a residence-time result as a pressure-vessel or foundation design. If a physical mechanism outside the model dominates the real process, numerical refinement cannot rescue the conclusion. Failure mode 2 — missing input provenance: inlet variability, overflow protection, fluid properties and maintenance constraints Without that evidence, parameter values remain unverified. Failure mode 3 — misuse of precision: a software result displayed to four decimals may still originate in an order-of-magnitude engineering assumption. Accuracy is controlled by evidence, not the number of digits shown.

Applicability boundary: Total shell volume includes only an illustrative freeboard percentage; pressure rating and inventory for upsets absent. This educational module does not size pressure vessels, specify safety instrumented functions, certify emissions compliance, supply construction documents or establish investment attractiveness. For an actual facility, relevant professional review, regulatory analysis, vendor thermal and mechanical design, and empirical validation are separate requirements that cannot be delegated to this one equation.

Independent study and examiner prompts

Conceptual: In what circumstances does the assumed relationship for tanks and buffer holdup lose predictive value? Name one physical effect and one organizational or data-management effect. Quantitative: Reproduce the worked case with units and change one input by ten percent. State the sensitivity of the output, including whether a finite-difference result agrees with the local algebraic trend. Application: Relate the result to the unit case—A tank, transfer pump, process heater and heat-exchanger candidate are provisionally sized for the conceptual flowsheet.—and identify the critical tradeoff between an apparently favorable result and its implementation.

Critical appraisal: Draft the most persuasive argument against acting on this lesson’s estimate. Your argument must refer to evidence rather than taste. It should include at least one unavailable measurement from this list: inlet variability, overflow protection, fluid properties and maintenance constraints. Then give the smallest practical study—vendor request, sample test, pilot run, site visit or model comparison—that could narrow the uncertainty. Communication: Write a two-sentence summary that separates a verified equation from a synthetic scenario assumption.

Short calculation audit for reviewers

Check that the supplied equation V_work = Q_volumetric × residence time is the same one used in the laboratory, or state why the lab computes a related screening quantity rather than the entire model. Confirm that the active scenario does not violate stated slider ranges. Confirm that no unit conversion is silently required between mass flow, molar flow, volume, energy or currency. Separate conservation-law residuals from empirical-correlation fit errors. Keep the response to a failed check in the delivered report; an invalid baseline should not be silently replaced with a different input set.

Checklist for mastery

  • I can define the process boundary and the measured or assumed quantities behind Tanks and buffer holdup.
  • I can reproduce the stated working example with units and without copying a displayed answer.
  • I can name the main omitted mechanism: Do not use a residence-time result as a pressure-vessel or foundation design.
  • I can run and explain the numerical limit test: Working volume must scale linearly with flow at constant residence time.
  • I can distinguish a planning estimate from a mechanical, regulatory or safety approval.
  • I can name the next evidence source: inlet variability, overflow protection, fluid properties and maintenance constraints

§4.5 Engineering Topic 4.5: Unit 4 · Lesson 4: Design margins and vendor datasheets

Unit 4 · Lesson 4: Design margins and vendor datasheets

Learning pathway: [Equipment Specifications and Preliminary Sizing](../units/unit-04.html) · [Simulation 11: Countercurrent LMTD and area](../labs/lab-11.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-04.html).

Engineering problem and conceptual boundary

An engineering specification should distinguish estimated process duty from specified design rating. The central design case for this part of the course is: A tank, transfer pump, process heater and heat-exchanger candidate are provisionally sized for the conceptual flowsheet. Design work should begin by deciding what the process is required to accomplish, what physical behavior is known, what remains an assumption, and which constraints make a candidate concept unacceptable. A calculated number that is not connected to a defined engineering decision has little project value. For this lesson the decision is: A datasheet is useful only if assumptions, limits and interfaces are traceable.

The margin must be justified and reconciled with possible simultaneous maximum cases. Interpret that physical observation as a condition on the model rather than as a measured result. A screening model can provide a reliable direction of sensitivity without providing a trustworthy absolute plant rating. Before applying a particular numerical result, identify its process boundary, time basis, component definition and operating mode. A rate evaluated under steady conditions cannot represent a transient upset unless accumulation and duration are modeled explicitly.

Governing relationship and dimensional basis

The principal starting relationship is specified heat load = verified heat load × (1+ documented allowance). Write out the quantities in this equation and ask which are independently specified and which are inferred from other assumptions. Check that both sides have compatible units. When an output is expressed as a fraction it must not be treated as a mass or molar rate without multiplying by an appropriate total flow. When a cost or duty is given per year, make the operating-hour and utilization basis visible; a nameplate rate alone does not produce an annual total.

For design margins and vendor datasheets, the model is deliberately formulated as a minimal relation. A relation can be algebraically correct but physically incomplete, because it omits phenomena that do not appear in its chosen boundary. The specific concern here is: Vendor design and code calculations, relief sizing and materials compatibility are outside this workbook. In a design review, show the equation as an auditable expression and keep the evidence for its coefficients separate from the mathematical logic. If an assumed parameter later changes, the calculation may need to be revisited across the entire flowsheet rather than edited only in one table.

Worked engineering check

Illustrative case, not plant measurement: A 300 kW calculated thermal load with 15% allowance yields a provisional 345 kW datasheet duty. Copy the stated inputs into a notebook, calculate the result independently using the governing expression and retain enough significant digits to detect a balance error. When you write the result, distinguish an exact identity within the model from a rounded numerical example. For example, an exact component balance can hold to floating-point tolerance, while an empirical estimate of reaction behavior may be uncertain by orders of magnitude if fitted kinetics are unavailable.

Explain why the chosen arithmetic is useful for design margins and vendor datasheets but not sufficient for a detailed design decision. The calculation provides a specific benchmark for checking the interactive lab, not a guarantee that the input values are representative. If an initial calculation produces a result that contradicts expected physical bounds—such as composition outside the feed range, negative absolute inventory, positive losses with a perfect collection assumption, or implausible unit conversions—do not tune inputs simply to improve the display. Revisit the boundary and the assumption that creates the inconsistency.

Laboratory investigation with a falsifiable prediction

Open [Simulation 11: Countercurrent LMTD and area](../labs/lab-11.html). The implemented relation is LMTD = (ΔT1−ΔT2)/ln(ΔT1/ΔT2); A=Q/(U LMTD). Before moving a control, predict what will happen to the output when the dominant parameter increases. Move that parameter to a value roughly halfway between the default and one end of its permitted interval. Record the previous and revised numerical outputs, the units, and a one-sentence explanation based on the equation. Then test a second parameter that changes the trend or limits the conclusion.

Specific falsification task: If either endpoint approach is nonpositive, declare profile infeasible instead of drawing false area. If the code violates this check, the appropriate result is a failing test and a documented defect, not an attractive screenshot. Investigation prompt: Explore the sudden area growth when a specified endpoint temperature approach becomes very small. The numerical lab is intentionally small enough to trace by hand; it should stimulate a quantitative question about process behavior rather than replace a commercial flowsheet simulator.

A defensible design record

For this lesson, create a one-page calculation note with: a clearly stated objective, a simple process or equipment boundary, all specified inputs including source and units, the model equation, the worked numeric output, one independent check, at least two changes of a parameter, and a decision with a stated uncertainty. The immediate evidence package to acquire is: applicable equipment code, corrosion data, design pressure and temperature, vendor quotes. Label synthetic data plainly. Do not call an illustrative scenario a site survey, a vendor quotation, a laboratory result or a market forecast.

The most important decision consequence is: A datasheet is useful only if assumptions, limits and interfaces are traceable. A useful design note therefore contains a condition under which the recommendation would reverse. For example, the preferred concept may change if an availability assumption falls, an energy price rises, a flow composition moves beyond a separation range, or the estimated installed cost is outside the available budget. A reviewer needs the reversal threshold or at least a targeted experiment, not an unconditional claim that the concept is optimal.

Failure modes, omissions and escalation

Failure mode 1 — invalid physical simplification: Vendor design and code calculations, relief sizing and materials compatibility are outside this workbook. If a physical mechanism outside the model dominates the real process, numerical refinement cannot rescue the conclusion. Failure mode 2 — missing input provenance: applicable equipment code, corrosion data, design pressure and temperature, vendor quotes Without that evidence, parameter values remain unverified. Failure mode 3 — misuse of precision: a software result displayed to four decimals may still originate in an order-of-magnitude engineering assumption. Accuracy is controlled by evidence, not the number of digits shown.

Applicability boundary: Fixed four terminal temperatures and constant U; no energy-balance reconciliation, pressure losses or phase change. This educational module does not size pressure vessels, specify safety instrumented functions, certify emissions compliance, supply construction documents or establish investment attractiveness. For an actual facility, relevant professional review, regulatory analysis, vendor thermal and mechanical design, and empirical validation are separate requirements that cannot be delegated to this one equation.
