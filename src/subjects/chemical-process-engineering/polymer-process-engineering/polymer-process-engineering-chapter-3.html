§3.2 Engineering Topic 3.2: Unit 03 · Lesson 01 — Shear rate, stress and generalized Newtonian flow

Unit 03 · Lesson 01 — Shear rate, stress and generalized Newtonian flow

[Unit 03 course map](../units/unit-03.html) · [Interactive simulation 07](../labs/lab-07.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-03.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-03.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

Many polymer melts are strongly shear-thinning: apparent viscosity decreases as imposed shear rate increases. A power-law representation τ=Kγdot^n is useful over a finite regime; n<1 indicates shear thinning. The parameter K changes units with n and has no material-independent meaning. Zero-shear and high-shear Newtonian plateaus require richer models. A rheometer reports a torque and geometry-dependent inference, not an unconditional viscosity for a molding screw. Melt temperature and pressure must be specified because both influence measured flow.

This lesson belongs to Polymer melt rheology, whose goal is to interpret time-dependent and non-Newtonian response through suitable, testable constitutive models and rheometric evidence. The challenge is to connect chemical structure or thermomechanical history to an output that can be measured, rather than reporting attractive numerical results without a clear object of study. A number obtained on dry pellets can be inappropriate for a moist, filled, highly oriented or degraded finished article.

2. Mathematical description and physical quantities

Starting relationship: Power law: ηapp=K γdot^(n−1); τ=K γdot^n.

A formula is meaningful only with its variable definitions. Identify the dependent variable, its SI unit, the independent variables and the dimensional basis of every fitted coefficient. For a ratio, inspect positive denominators and the zero-input limit; for a rate law, distinguish a rate per mass from a rate per volume; for a thermal transition, use kelvin if a reciprocal temperature is used. Reconstruct the equation's origin from counting, conservation, constitutive behavior or an explicitly stated empirical fit. Those origins cannot be interchanged. In particular, a pure mass-balance identity may survive changes in polymer chemistry while a viscosity correlation usually does not.

Distinguish viscous dissipation from stored elastic energy — A steady viscosity curve reports one relation between shear stress and rate. An elastic melt also remembers deformation history, giving time-dependent stress and extensional effects. Select the rheometer geometry and time scale that probe the proposed shaping mechanism; do not fit a smooth generalized Newtonian curve and then claim it predicts melt strength.

Unit 03 · Lesson 02 — Carreau models and processing shear windows

1. The polymer-processing question

The Carreau family bridges zero-shear behavior and decreasing viscosity over an intermediate shear range. Its characteristic time is related to the onset of non-Newtonian response but is not automatically equal to a unique molecular relaxation time. Fit parameters can be correlated; a smooth curve through a few points does not imply a unique mechanistic explanation. When the high-shear limit is much smaller than the low-shear viscosity, shear thinning changes die pressure and viscous dissipation. Temperature variation often needs separate shift factors.

Unit 03 · Lesson 03 — Viscoelastic relaxation and normal stresses

1. The polymer-processing question

Polymer melts store and dissipate mechanical energy over different time scales. In a single-mode Maxwell model the relaxation modulus decays exponentially; the integral of stress relaxation connects to zero-shear viscosity, and Deborah or Weissenberg numbers compare material response with process time scales. A one-mode model is an instructional proxy: real polymers exhibit broad spectra from chains of different sizes and architectures. Extensional deformation can behave very differently from simple shear, which helps explain die swell, melt fracture and bubble drawability.

Unit 03 · Lesson 04 — Capillary flow, slip and entrance effects

1. The polymer-processing question

Capillary rheometry offers high shear rates relevant to dies, but apparent wall stress inferred from pressure drop is confounded by entrance losses and pressure sensitivity. A Newtonian Hagen–Poiseuille estimate provides a unit-checking baseline; real polymer melts often require Rabinowitsch correction for non-Newtonian shear and Bagley correction for entrance pressure. Wall slip may lead to misleading rates, and melt fracture can make steady, smooth-flow assumptions invalid. Separate a measured total machine pressure from the pressure drop in a single known die section.

§3.6 Process Simulation & Industrial Design Case: Polymer Melt Rheology and Viscoelasticity

Unit 03 applied engineering design study — Polymer melt rheology

Case status: fictional scenario. All measurements, market costs, process coefficients and material specifications are synthetic unless properly sourced by the learner. No calculated output authorizes a machine setting, product certification or environmental claim.

Case brief

Three rheometry datasets from synthetic polymer grades fit different power laws over a narrow high-shear range. The team wants to extrapolate one grade toward zero shear to estimate storage-tank pumpability and die swell.

The essential discipline is to prevent a model from silently becoming evidence. A design team should draw a boundary, define a function, preserve material and energy units, and identify a measurement capable of changing its conclusion. In polymer systems the same nominal resin can behave differently after drying, heating, oxidation, stretching and reprocessing. Product acceptance must be assessed on the relevant final article and sampling plan.

Technical analyses

Calculate power-law and Carreau curves, test the low-shear plateau and Maxwell relaxation. Distinguish steady shear viscosity, time-dependent stress relaxation and extensional melt strength.

Describe where the physical state changes, where material is lost, where energy crosses the boundary and where quality is gained or degraded. If two alternatives differ by material type or final function, normalize them before comparing. State which inputs are experimentally measured, independently sourced, nominal manufacturer data or assumed illustrative coefficients. Use an uncertainty interval for any decision-critical property and do not invent a measured value.

Three interactive investigations

  1. [Simulation 07](../labs/lab-07.html): sweep the first parameter and reconstruct the default numerical output from its equation; record an analytical limit.
  2. [Simulation 08](../labs/lab-08.html): assess which second physical mechanism the model adds; state whether both calculations use the same material state.

Evidence protocol

Design a measurement schedule with sensor or analytical method, instrument range, calibration date, specimen preparation, sampling interval and acceptable data completeness. Define a control sample and a replicate strategy. For process comparisons, separate startup and steady production and keep separate numerator/denominator definitions for material yield, rejected-part rate and energy use. State which claim you would withdraw if an independent material or product test contradicts the predicted model curve. When the claim concerns safety, food contact, biodegradation or a regulated end use, require the relevant complete, current standard and qualified expert review.

Deliverables

In addition, submit a one-page management summary: intended function and specification, explicit assumptions, normalized numerical comparison, evidence gaps, the chosen next experiment and the decision that cannot yet be made. Append all formulas, spreadsheet inputs and raw exports so another person can reconstruct the case. A recommendation is not complete until it specifies what new evidence would change it.
