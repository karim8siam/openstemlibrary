Learning objective: Investigate fibers, films, coatings and cellular plastics using draw, orientation, continuity and expansion models.

What this unit requires

Product-formation models constrain the amount of polymer per length, area or volume. They do not independently establish microstructure, anisotropy or fracture resistance. A reliable design compares conservation predictions with distributed geometry measurements.

An engineering statement is useful only after its units, system boundary, assumptions and evidentiary status are defined. This unit deliberately distinguishes mathematical identity, conditional model prediction, laboratory observation and verified process claim. §5.2 Engineering Topic 5.2: Unit 05 · Lesson 01 — Melt spinning and fiber draw ratio

Unit 05 · Lesson 01 — Melt spinning and fiber draw ratio

[Unit 05 course map](../units/unit-05.html) · [Interactive simulation 13](../labs/lab-13.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-05.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-05.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

Melt spinning forms filaments by conveying molten polymer through spinnerets and drawing them as they cool and orient. Conservation relates mass throughput, density, speed and cross-sectional area; molecular orientation and tensile properties require additional constitutive and thermal history data. High draw can raise axial stiffness in some systems while causing breakage if extensional stress or solidification is poorly managed. Denier and tex are mass-per-length measures, not directly diameter unless density and filament count are known.

Starting relationship: A=ṁ/(ρv); round filament diameter d=√[4ṁ/(πρv)].

§5.3 Engineering Topic 5.3: Unit 05 · Lesson 02 — Film casting, biaxial stretching and orientation

Unit 05 · Lesson 02 — Film casting, biaxial stretching and orientation

[Unit 05 course map](../units/unit-05.html) · [Interactive simulation 14](../labs/lab-14.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-05.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-05.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

Cast and blown films undergo cooling and stretching in different directions. Machine-direction and transverse-direction orientation influence modulus, tear resistance, haze and shrinkage. Under incompressible homogeneous stretching, thickness scales inversely with the product of directional stretch ratios. Real film production introduces edge effects, neck-in, nonuniform cooling and temperature-dependent crystallization. Distinguish bubble expansion ratio, take-up ratio and actual local draw measured on a marked film; they are related but not interchangeable.

§5.4 Engineering Topic 5.4: Unit 05 · Lesson 03 — Coating thickness and drying mass balance

Unit 05 · Lesson 03 — Coating thickness and drying mass balance

[Unit 05 course map](../units/unit-05.html) · [Interactive simulation 15](../labs/lab-15.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-05.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-05.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

Polymer solutions and dispersions are applied to substrates by slot, knife, roll or spray methods. Wet pickup, solids fraction and dried coating density determine an initial thickness estimate, whereas solvent evaporation, leveling, substrate absorption and shrinkage change the actual coating. Coating quality includes adhesion, pinholes, residual solvent, optical uniformity and surface defects; thickness alone is insufficient. Energy and vapor removal may constrain line speed, and solvent systems introduce serious hazard and regulatory requirements beyond a classroom mass balance.

§5.5 Engineering Topic 5.5: Unit 05 · Lesson 04 — Foaming, expansion and cellular structure

Unit 05 · Lesson 04 — Foaming, expansion and cellular structure

[Unit 05 course map](../units/unit-05.html) · [Interactive simulation 15](../labs/lab-15.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-05.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-05.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

Polymer foams obtain low apparent density from gas cells formed and stabilized during processing. The expansion ratio compares solid-polymer density with bulk foam density; cell-size distribution, open-cell fraction and skin layers are additional important descriptors. Gas evolution, nucleation and growth couple pressure, temperature, diffusivity and melt strength. A large calculated expansion ratio does not imply a mechanically stable product; thin struts and coalescence can reduce stiffness or thermal durability. Chemical and physical blowing agents require different safety assessment.

Values here are synthetic to explain algebra and must not be presented as published resin property tables.

4. Model verification is not material validation

The density relation assumes mass conservation and no fillers or mass losses; it does not predict mechanical or insulation properties.

Rearrange the equation to infer one input from the target output. If the rearrangement is ill-conditioned, explain how small measurement error may produce a large inferred-parameter error. Identify a pair of conceptually different mechanisms capable of moving the observed output in the same direction. A sound diagnosis cannot select one mechanism solely from that output; it needs an orthogonal measurement. This is the central protection against overinterpreting a polymer melt curve, a DSC peak, a strength measurement or a recycling-yield statistic.

Compare Foaming, expansion and cellular structure with Melt spinning and fiber draw ratio as a neighboring subject in this unit. Do the two relationships describe the same physical quantity or two independent descriptors? Would their characteristic times, spatial scales, material states or experimental procedures be comparable? Propose one experiment that measures both and write what a disagreement would imply. A second model is useful only if it provides independent information rather than repeating the same assumption in a new notation.

5. Evidence and experimental design

Measure apparent density, mass, sectioned-cell images and compression response; check whether void distribution is homogeneous.

Measurement planning: Define specimens, sampling locations, conditioning, calibration method, measurement resolution and number of independent replicates. Specify one reference material or control and the expected direction of a genuine effect. Organize a data ledger with columns for parameter, unit, instrument, method, timestamp, operator, uncertainty and calibration status. Do not fit and validate a model on the same observations without acknowledging that the validation is not independent. Where the property depends on processing history, preserve the exact prior temperature, deformation and storage conditions.

A robust plan explicitly separates structural uncertainty, parameter uncertainty and observation uncertainty. Structural uncertainty asks whether important physics are absent; parameter uncertainty asks whether fitted numbers are poorly identified; observation uncertainty asks whether instrumentation or sampling is noisy or biased. Repeated sampling can reduce some random errors but cannot repair an incorrect equation or uncalibrated specimen history. §5.6 Process Simulation & Industrial Design Case: Injection Molding and Die Forming Processes

Unit 05 applied engineering design study — Product formation

Case status: fictional scenario. All measurements, market costs, process coefficients and material specifications are synthetic unless properly sourced by the learner. No calculated output authorizes a machine setting, product certification or environmental claim.

Case brief

A packaging producer chooses between a biaxially oriented film, coated film and cellular foam for different barrier and stiffness objectives. Each sales claim is supported by one average thickness or density only.

The essential discipline is to prevent a model from silently becoming evidence. A design team should draw a boundary, define a function, preserve material and energy units, and identify a measurement capable of changing its conclusion. In polymer systems the same nominal resin can behave differently after drying, heating, oxidation, stretching and reprocessing. Product acceptance must be assessed on the relevant final article and sampling plan.

Technical analyses

Reconstruct geometric stretch, coating dry-mass thickness and expansion ratio, then identify orientation, residual solvent, cell structure and anisotropic performance as missing evidence.

Describe where the physical state changes, where material is lost, where energy crosses the boundary and where quality is gained or degraded. If two alternatives differ by material type or final function, normalize them before comparing. State which inputs are experimentally measured, independently sourced, nominal manufacturer data or assumed illustrative coefficients. Use an uncertainty interval for any decision-critical property and do not invent a measured value.

Three interactive investigations

  1. [Simulation 13](../labs/lab-13.html): sweep the first parameter and reconstruct the default numerical output from its equation; record an analytical limit.
  2. [Simulation 14](../labs/lab-14.html): assess which second physical mechanism the model adds; state whether both calculations use the same material state.

Evidence protocol

Design a measurement schedule with sensor or analytical method, instrument range, calibration date, specimen preparation, sampling interval and acceptable data completeness. Define a control sample and a replicate strategy. For process comparisons, separate startup and steady production and keep separate numerator/denominator definitions for material yield, rejected-part rate and energy use. A small systematic bias can outweigh an impressive-looking simulated benefit.

Propose at least two counterhypotheses that could explain the same visible process defect or laboratory trend. Specify the smallest experiment that can discriminate them. State which claim you would withdraw if an independent material or product test contradicts the predicted model curve. When the claim concerns safety, food contact, biodegradation or a regulated end use, require the relevant complete, current standard and qualified expert review.

Deliverables

Deliver unit-aware geometry calculations, three offline lab sweeps, targeted experimental test methods and a rejection criterion when surface or internal defects become unacceptable.

In addition, submit a one-page management summary: intended function and specification, explicit assumptions, normalized numerical comparison, evidence gaps, the chosen next experiment and the decision that cannot yet be made. Append all formulas, spreadsheet inputs and raw exports so another person can reconstruct the case. A recommendation is not complete until it specifies what new evidence would change it.

Assessment rubric

  • 25% correctness of mass, dimensional and mathematical reasoning.
  • 20% physically appropriate model scope and identification of omissions.
  • 20% reproducible model sweeps and independent numerical checks.
  • 20% design of experiments and measurement-system integrity.
  • 15% appropriate conditional decision and transparent communication.
