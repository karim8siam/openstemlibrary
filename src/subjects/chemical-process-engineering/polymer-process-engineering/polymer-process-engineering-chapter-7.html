§7.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Mechanical, Thermal and Rheological Characterization

Learning objective: Interpret thermal analysis, spectroscopy, mechanical testing and microscopy using measurement limitations and uncertainty.

What this unit requires

Materials characterization is a chain of evidence with instrument resolution and protocol dependence. A precise numerical result with poor sample preparation can be less useful than a broader but validated observation; support causality with orthogonal measurement methods.

An engineering statement is useful only after its units, system boundary, assumptions and evidentiary status are defined. This unit deliberately distinguishes mathematical identity, conditional model prediction, laboratory observation and verified process claim. Calculated values in these pages are synthetic worked examples, not measured commercial-resin data. Seek grade- and application-specific property evidence before any equipment or product decision.

Four-lesson pathway

  1. [Differential scanning calorimetry and thermal transitions](../lessons/unit-07-lesson-01.html) — [numerical model 19](../labs/lab-19.html)

Demonstrate learning

  • [Sixteen multi-part assessment questions](../assessments/unit-07.html): numerical reconstruction, equation audit, model challenge and design trade-off.
  • [Unit-level engineering project](../projects/unit-07.html): a practical but fictional application with traceable mass, quality and evidence requirements.
  • [Interactive offline laboratory manual](../LABORATORY-MANUAL.html): adjustable, source-visible calculations with CSV export.

Completion standard

Explain a material mechanism, reconstruct the featured calculation with consistent dimensions, test one limiting case and specify an experiment that could invalidate the conclusion. An equation that reproduces its own arithmetic has been software-verified; it has not thereby been validated against polymer specimens or factory operation.

§7.2 Engineering Topic 7.2: Unit 07 · Lesson 01 — Differential scanning calorimetry and thermal transitions

Unit 07 · Lesson 01 — Differential scanning calorimetry and thermal transitions

[Unit 07 course map](../units/unit-07.html) · [Interactive simulation 19](../labs/lab-19.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-07.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

DSC estimates energy flow during heating or cooling; melting, crystallization and glass-transition signatures depend on baseline selection, heating rate, purge gas and prior thermal history. Percent crystallinity calculations often require correction for cold crystallization and inert fillers. A reference enthalpy for a nominally fully crystalline resin must be appropriate for the polymer and crystalline form; different literature values give different results. Report the integration and subtraction explicitly and do not treat an isolated peak as conclusive identification.

This lesson belongs to Polymer characterization, whose goal is to interpret thermal analysis, spectroscopy, mechanical testing and microscopy using measurement limitations and uncertainty. The challenge is to connect chemical structure or thermomechanical history to an output that can be measured, rather than reporting attractive numerical results without a clear object of study. Name the material family and the actual specimen state before using a correlation. A number obtained on dry pellets can be inappropriate for a moist, filled, highly oriented or degraded finished article.

Materials characterization is a chain of evidence with instrument resolution and protocol dependence. A precise numerical result with poor sample preparation can be less useful than a broader but validated observation; support causality with orthogonal measurement methods.

2. Mathematical description and physical quantities

Starting relationship: Xc=100(ΔHm−ΔHcc)/(wpoly ΔHm°).

A formula is meaningful only with its variable definitions. Identify the dependent variable, its SI unit, the independent variables and the dimensional basis of every fitted coefficient. For a ratio, inspect positive denominators and the zero-input limit; for a rate law, distinguish a rate per mass from a rate per volume; for a thermal transition, use kelvin if a reciprocal temperature is used. Reconstruct the equation's origin from counting, conservation, constitutive behavior or an explicitly stated empirical fit. Those origins cannot be interchanged. In particular, a pure mass-balance identity may survive changes in polymer chemistry while a viscosity correlation usually does not.

The test method is part of the reported property — Thermal, chemical, mechanical and microscopic measurements involve protocols and specimen history. Two different methods may answer different questions without either instrument being faulty. Before combining test results, record sample preparation, material orientation, instrument calibration, temperature, atmosphere and response time.

3. Worked numerical investigation

With ΔHm=85 J/g sample, ΔHcc=15 J/g sample, wpoly=0.80 and reference ΔHm°=200 J/g polymer, Xc=43.75%. Negative corrected enthalpy would require review, not automatic negative crystallinity.

Constraint check: Identify any physically impossible input: a negative molecule count, mass recovery above the feed, mass fraction exceeding unity, a temperature on an incorrect absolute scale, a stress beyond a known material domain, or a nonfinite denominator. 4. Model verification is not material validation

DSC crystallinity is model-dependent; overlapping transitions, curing, filler reactions and uncertain reference enthalpy limit interpretation.

Rearrange the equation to infer one input from the target output. If the rearrangement is ill-conditioned, explain how small measurement error may produce a large inferred-parameter error. Identify a pair of conceptually different mechanisms capable of moving the observed output in the same direction. A sound diagnosis cannot select one mechanism solely from that output; it needs an orthogonal measurement. This is the central protection against overinterpreting a polymer melt curve, a DSC peak, a strength measurement or a recycling-yield statistic.

Compare Differential scanning calorimetry and thermal transitions with Spectroscopy, composition and chemical aging as a neighboring subject in this unit. Do the two relationships describe the same physical quantity or two independent descriptors? Would their characteristic times, spatial scales, material states or experimental procedures be comparable? Propose one experiment that measures both and write what a disagreement would imply. A second model is useful only if it provides independent information rather than repeating the same assumption in a new notation.

5. Evidence and experimental design

Calibrate heat-flow and temperature response, record atmosphere and rate, and cross-check against X-ray scattering or density where useful.

Measurement planning: Define specimens, sampling locations, conditioning, calibration method, measurement resolution and number of independent replicates. Specify one reference material or control and the expected direction of a genuine effect. Organize a data ledger with columns for parameter, unit, instrument, method, timestamp, operator, uncertainty and calibration status. Do not fit and validate a model on the same observations without acknowledging that the validation is not independent. Where the property depends on processing history, preserve the exact prior temperature, deformation and storage conditions.

A robust plan explicitly separates structural uncertainty, parameter uncertainty and observation uncertainty. Structural uncertainty asks whether important physics are absent; parameter uncertainty asks whether fitted numbers are poorly identified; observation uncertainty asks whether instrumentation or sampling is noisy or biased. Repeated sampling can reduce some random errors but cannot repair an incorrect equation or uncalibrated specimen history. For Differential scanning calorimetry and thermal transitions, name one of each, and predict which category will dominate extrapolation into an unmeasured processing regime.

Infrared, Raman, NMR and other methods identify functional groups or chemical environments, with different spatial resolution and quantitative sensitivity. A characteristic band can support the presence of a group without uniquely proving a polymer grade or extent of degradation. Carbonyl indices in oxidative-aging studies depend on baseline choice, specimen thickness and absorbance normalization. Copolymer composition needs calibrated peak assignments, often including standards and molecular structure. Spectroscopy complements—not replaces—molar-mass analysis, thermal tests and mechanical performance.

§7.4 Engineering Topic 7.4: Unit 07 · Lesson 03 — Mechanical testing, creep and failure

Unit 07 · Lesson 03 — Mechanical testing, creep and failure

[Unit 07 course map](../units/unit-07.html) · [Interactive simulation 21](../labs/lab-21.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied case](../projects/unit-07.html)

1. The polymer-processing question

Tensile modulus, yield strength, elongation, fracture toughness, creep compliance and impact resistance describe different responses. Polymers are sensitive to temperature, strain rate, moisture, orientation and notches; a single modulus cannot summarize product performance. Engineering stress uses original area; true stress uses instantaneous area, and divergence grows at high deformation. Viscoelastic creep can cause long-term failure below short-term yield stress. A Maxwell or Kelvin–Voigt relation can teach the time response but does not replace validated service-life experiments.

Optical microscopy, SEM, AFM, polarized-light microscopy and small- or wide-angle scattering probe different features and length scales. A large, visually appealing micrograph is not a representative population unless sampling is controlled. Spherulite size, fiber diameter, void fraction and filler dispersion may vary across a manufactured part. Image thresholding converts grayscale into objects under subjective choices and measurement resolution. Morphology-property explanations should test alternate causes, such as crystallinity, molecular orientation and surface treatment.

Values here are synthetic to explain algebra and must not be presented as published resin property tables.

4. Model verification is not material validation

2D sections can underrepresent hidden pores, overrepresent elongated objects and confound surface with volumetric fraction.

Rearrange the equation to infer one input from the target output. If the rearrangement is ill-conditioned, explain how small measurement error may produce a large inferred-parameter error. Identify a pair of conceptually different mechanisms capable of moving the observed output in the same direction. A sound diagnosis cannot select one mechanism solely from that output; it needs an orthogonal measurement. This is the central protection against overinterpreting a polymer melt curve, a DSC peak, a strength measurement or a recycling-yield statistic.

Compare Microscopy, scattering and morphology evidence with Differential scanning calorimetry and thermal transitions as a neighboring subject in this unit. Do the two relationships describe the same physical quantity or two independent descriptors? Would their characteristic times, spatial scales, material states or experimental procedures be comparable? Propose one experiment that measures both and write what a disagreement would imply. A second model is useful only if it provides independent information rather than repeating the same assumption in a new notation.

5. Evidence and experimental design

Collect fields from defined locations with scale bars, calibration and alternative threshold sensitivity; compare to bulk measurements.

Measurement planning: Define specimens, sampling locations, conditioning, calibration method, measurement resolution and number of independent replicates. Specify one reference material or control and the expected direction of a genuine effect. Organize a data ledger with columns for parameter, unit, instrument, method, timestamp, operator, uncertainty and calibration status. Do not fit and validate a model on the same observations without acknowledging that the validation is not independent. Where the property depends on processing history, preserve the exact prior temperature, deformation and storage conditions.

A robust plan explicitly separates structural uncertainty, parameter uncertainty and observation uncertainty. Structural uncertainty asks whether important physics are absent; parameter uncertainty asks whether fitted numbers are poorly identified; observation uncertainty asks whether instrumentation or sampling is noisy or biased. Repeated sampling can reduce some random errors but cannot repair an incorrect equation or uncalibrated specimen history. For Microscopy, scattering and morphology evidence, name one of each, and predict which category will dominate extrapolation into an unmeasured processing regime.

§7.6 Process Simulation & Industrial Design Case: Mechanical, Thermal and Rheological Characterization

Unit 07 applied engineering design study — Polymer characterization

Case status: fictional scenario. All measurements, market costs, process coefficients and material specifications are synthetic unless properly sourced by the learner. No calculated output authorizes a machine setting, product certification or environmental claim.

Case brief

An R&D report claims a filled semicrystalline polymer is superior because its DSC peak is large, tensile modulus is high, and a microscopy image shows good dispersion. The specimen processing histories are undocumented.

The essential discipline is to prevent a model from silently becoming evidence. A design team should draw a boundary, define a function, preserve material and energy units, and identify a measurement capable of changing its conclusion. In polymer systems the same nominal resin can behave differently after drying, heating, oxidation, stretching and reprocessing. Product acceptance must be assessed on the relevant final article and sampling plan.

Technical analyses

Recompute polymer-mass-corrected crystallinity; compare creep and short-term stiffness; inspect image-sampling bias and spectroscopy evidence. Build a consistent specimen conditioning and method register.

Describe where the physical state changes, where material is lost, where energy crosses the boundary and where quality is gained or degraded. If two alternatives differ by material type or final function, normalize them before comparing. State which inputs are experimentally measured, independently sourced, nominal manufacturer data or assumed illustrative coefficients. Use an uncertainty interval for any decision-critical property and do not invent a measured value.

Three interactive investigations

  1. [Simulation 19](../labs/lab-19.html): sweep the first parameter and reconstruct the default numerical output from its equation; record an analytical limit.
  2. [Simulation 20](../labs/lab-20.html): assess which second physical mechanism the model adds; state whether both calculations use the same material state.
  3. [Simulation 21](../labs/lab-21.html): deliberately test parameter sensitivity or an invalid-domain warning and explain why treating a fictitious curve as a specification would be unsafe.

Calculate at least one output independently in a spreadsheet or short code segment, save the CSV curves, and annotate differences between numerical rounding and structural model assumptions. The three simulations may complement one another without forming a complete virtual factory.

Evidence protocol

Design a measurement schedule with sensor or analytical method, instrument range, calibration date, specimen preparation, sampling interval and acceptable data completeness. Define a control sample and a replicate strategy. For process comparisons, separate startup and steady production and keep separate numerator/denominator definitions for material yield, rejected-part rate and energy use. A small systematic bias can outweigh an impressive-looking simulated benefit.

Propose at least two counterhypotheses that could explain the same visible process defect or laboratory trend. Specify the smallest experiment that can discriminate them. State which claim you would withdraw if an independent material or product test contradicts the predicted model curve. When the claim concerns safety, food contact, biodegradation or a regulated end use, require the relevant complete, current standard and qualified expert review.

Deliverables

Deliver measurement traceability, corrected DSC arithmetic, mechanical time-scale limitations, representative image sampling plan and a conclusion that names unresolved grade performance.

In addition, submit a one-page management summary: intended function and specification, explicit assumptions, normalized numerical comparison, evidence gaps, the chosen next experiment and the decision that cannot yet be made. Append all formulas, spreadsheet inputs and raw exports so another person can reconstruct the case. A recommendation is not complete until it specifies what new evidence would change it.

Assessment rubric

  • 25% correctness of mass, dimensional and mathematical reasoning.
  • 20% physically appropriate model scope and identification of omissions.
  • 20% reproducible model sweeps and independent numerical checks.
  • 20% design of experiments and measurement-system integrity.
  • 15% appropriate conditional decision and transparent communication.
