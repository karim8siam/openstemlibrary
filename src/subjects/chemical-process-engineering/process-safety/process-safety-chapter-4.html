Structured Process Hazard Analysis

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and process engineering validation of Structured Process Hazard Analysis. Developed for undergraduate chemical engineering study.

§4.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Structured Process Hazard Analysis

Learning objective. Use HAZID, HAZOP, checklists, fault trees and event trees to ask systematically how safeguards fail, including human and common-cause contributions.

Linked synthetic case. A line-up error during tank transfer can be initiated by an incorrect valve state, ambiguous labeling, instrument failure or poor shift turnover.

What an adequate output contains. A worksheet is a prompt for multidisciplinary reasoning, not proof that the identified scenario list is exhaustive.

Why this unit is needed

Process safety concerns preventing and limiting catastrophic releases of hazardous substances or energy, rather than merely maintaining a low count of routine injuries. A real project cannot infer its safety from a single score, accident-free period or calculation. For this unit, use hazid, hazop, checklists, fault trees and event trees to ask systematically how safeguards fail, including human and common-cause contributions. The basis of this reasoning is the physical configuration of equipment and operations, not the appearance of a matrix or dashboard. Assign each critical assertion an owner and identify how field observations could contradict it.

The case used across the lessons is deliberately hypothetical: A line-up error during tank transfer can be initiated by an incorrect valve state, ambiguous labeling, instrument failure or poor shift turnover. It contains no named real chemicals, equipment certificates, actual incident frequencies or approved operating limits. That distinction is central to interpreting models. For a plant, process safety information must describe what can be hazardous, how the process can deviate, and which barriers genuinely remain functional in abnormal conditions. A model may reveal sensitivity while omitting the mechanism that actually dominates a serious accident.

The five-layer engineering argument

1. Hazard and inventory: Identify substances, energy and populations or environmental receptors that could be exposed. Document what remains unknown before assigning a numerical risk category.

2. Initiating mechanism: State a credible cause, including external utilities, human-machine interfaces, maintenance, abnormal startup and non-routine conditions; separate initiating cause from the loss-of-control top event.

3. Prevention: Identify physical design limits, independently verified interlocks, procedures and maintenance controls. Do not assume independence without checking power, instruments and common personnel.

4. Mitigation: Assess containment, shutdown, relief and emergency arrangements in the context of the actual consequence mechanism. A mitigation may reduce severity without reducing the initiating frequency.

5. Assurance: Specify evidence, proof tests, field observations and responsible action owners. Controls that are unavailable, bypassed or degraded must not be credited as if fully available.

How to use the three numerical laboratories

These interactive models intentionally isolate individual relationships rather than claiming to predict a hazardous plant. Before moving a slider, write the governing equation and the physical units. Decide which outcomes should rise or fall when one parameter changes. After moving it, check whether the response and the plotted curve agree with that expectation. Export numerical data and calculate one point independently. If a curve seems favorable, identify a missing mechanism that could reverse the real decision.

This unit's laboratories are:

  • [Lab 10: Independent OR fault-tree gate](../labs/lab-10.html) — Compute union of two independent basic events. Boundary: Independence assumed and same observation basis; shared causes require separate model.
  • [Lab 11: Independent AND fault-tree gate](../labs/lab-11.html) — Compute intersection of independent basic events. Boundary: Does not model dependencies, systematic failures, repair or demand frequency.
  • [Lab 12: Event-tree failed-branch frequency](../labs/lab-12.html) — Follow a hypothetical initiating frequency through two conditional failures. Boundary: Frequencies and probabilities are invented; assumes conditional independence and stated branch timing.

How to review evidence

For each lesson prepare a three-column evidence ledger: declared fact, supporting observation/source, and status (measured, specified, assumed, inferred, or unresolved). Do not promote an assumption to a measurement when an equation happens to give a precise answer. In a team review, challenge the source of every probability and time constant. If an engineering standard is cited, verify the correct edition and legal applicability; standards differ by jurisdiction and project date. For serious events, methods used by qualified specialists should replace these elementary illustrations.

The course encourages deliberate disagreement: two reviewers should independently identify the hazardous deviation and potentially defeated safeguards before reconciling their lists. Record both the agreement and remaining uncertainty. The presence of a highly consequential unresolved scenario should not be hidden inside a low composite average. Closure means that a response has been implemented and verified to work, not that a spreadsheet cell has been colored green.

Progression and assessment

Complete the four lessons in order. Each assessment demands a short derivation, a sensitivity or limitation check, a scenario analysis and a documented decision. A numerical result without a stated boundary, units and credible evidence should receive no engineering credit. The final unit project asks for an auditable decision memo that separates a screening observation from an approval to build or operate. Peer review should specifically challenge common-cause failures and untested assumptions.

Lesson 1: HAZOP guidewords and nodes

A structured HAZOP compares design intent with deviations such as no flow, more pressure and reverse flow. The team examines credible causes and consequences, safeguards and recommendations for each important deviation. Key analytical anchor: Deviation = guideword applied to a defined process parameter. Investigate the following case: For a transfer line, investigate NO FLOW with a running pump, then compare with REVERSE FLOW when downstream pressure is higher. The most likely conceptual error is using a guideword list as a substitute for a facilitated multi-disciplinary meeting. In the lesson, assemble updated p&ids, operating procedures, instrument and equipment descriptions, experienced team members before drawing the operational conclusion: record decisions and evidence for actions, including reasons for no recommendation. Further discussion: Write two complete worksheet rows; distinguish cause from safeguard and consequence.

Lesson 2: Fault trees and dependent events

A fault tree starts from a defined top event and uses logic gates for combinations of basic events. OR probability differs from AND probability; independence is rarely automatic and shared utilities can defeat multiple barriers. Key analytical anchor: P(A∨B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B). Investigate the following case: If independent valve failure and sensor failure probabilities are 0.03 and 0.02, their OR event is 0.0494. The most likely conceptual error is multiplying dependent failure probabilities or mixing demand probabilities with annual frequencies. In the lesson, assemble equipment failure modes, proof test histories and common-cause mechanisms before drawing the operational conclusion: use trees to expose logical structure, then obtain justified failure data. Further discussion: Draw one fault tree in which a common electrical supply invalidates the independent-branch estimate.

Lesson 3: Event trees and conditional branch outcomes

An event tree begins at an initiating event and follows successful or failed protective functions. Branch frequencies are products of initiating frequency and conditional probabilities only when branch assumptions are justified. Key analytical anchor: f_branch = f_initiator × ∏P(branch state | prior states). Investigate the following case: An illustrative initiating frequency of 0.1/y and a 0.1 failure probability of a safeguard yield a 0.01/y branch under the model assumptions. The most likely conceptual error is reporting illustrative conditional branch values as measured event frequencies. In the lesson, assemble initiating data, barrier design, maintenance, testing, bypass procedures and correlation analysis before drawing the operational conclusion: make assumptions and units explicit in every event-tree calculation. Further discussion: Compare a branch with and without a shared-cause failure and state which data are missing.

Lesson 4: FMEA, prioritization and uncertainty

Failure modes and effects analysis is useful for asset-level failure identification and action ownership. A severity-occurrence-detection product is ordinal and not a substitute for quantitative risk or consequence analysis. Key analytical anchor: Illustrative priority index = S × O × D (ordinal ranking only). Investigate the following case: A failure mode with scores 8, 3 and 4 has an index 96; scores are internal meeting scales, not hazard probabilities. The most likely conceptual error is ranking a high-consequence mode low because a detector seems easy to use. In the lesson, assemble asset function, failure history, inspection records, assessor definitions and consequences before drawing the operational conclusion: keep severe single-point failures visible regardless of composite score. Further discussion: Construct two score sets with equal product but different consequence severity and discuss priority.

Unit completion checkpoint

The minimum acceptable submission contains an annotated process boundary for structured process hazard analysis, a cause-to-consequence pathway, a register of assumptions, an independent numerical reproduction of each model, and an unresolved-information list. In the synthetic case, a line-up error during tank transfer can be initiated by an incorrect valve state, ambiguous labeling, instrument failure or poor shift turnover. Identify which missing single item is most likely to change your recommendation and why. Then state who would have authority to accept or reject the real project after a specialist review.

The engineering question

A structured HAZOP compares design intent with deviations such as no flow, more pressure and reverse flow. The team examines credible causes and consequences, safeguards and recommendations for each important deviation. This lesson belongs to Structured Process Hazard Analysis, where the course objective is to use HAZID, HAZOP, checklists, fault trees and event trees to ask systematically how safeguards fail, including human and common-cause contributions. The governing issue is not whether one can calculate a number; it is whether that number describes the failure mechanism under study. A competent analysis separates a claim about what happens, an argument about why it would happen, and evidence that the safeguards could withstand real operation.

Specific thought experiment. For a transfer line, investigate NO FLOW with a running pump, then compare with REVERSE FLOW when downstream pressure is higher. Work from the equipment boundary outward. Name the initiating event, the operating mode, the top event, the potentially exposed people or environment, and the time over which the consequences could develop. If any one of these is unspecified, maintain the uncertainty rather than inventing a value.

Principles and analytical relationship

The working relation or structured method is Deviation = guideword applied to a defined process parameter. It is useful because it forces an analyst to state inputs and ask whether the units, time basis and conditioning assumptions match. A rate per year cannot be multiplied by an arbitrary probability without asking what event that probability conditions upon. Similarly, an ordinary fraction is not a mass fraction until the relevant mass balance has been declared. In a causal diagram, an arrow represents a proposed physical or organizational dependency; it is not, by itself, proof of causality.

The physical interpretation for hazop guidewords and nodes is the following: A structured HAZOP compares design intent with deviations such as no flow, more pressure and reverse flow. The team examines credible causes and consequences, safeguards and recommendations for each important deviation. Thus the result needs an audit trail back to observations, design documents or a controlled estimate. There is no universal conversion from a neat calculation to safe operation. Where the relationship is qualitative, a complete answer explains the conditions for each step and which alternative cause could produce the same observed symptom. When the relation is quantitative, a complete answer supplies units, ranges and a check against a limiting case.

For the course case, a line-up error during tank transfer can be initiated by an incorrect valve state, ambiguous labeling, instrument failure or poor shift turnover. Consider three boundaries before deciding whether the model is applicable: the system under study, the observation period, and the intervention boundary. System boundaries determine which inventories and energy flows are included. Observation periods distinguish transient loss of control from a steady process average. Intervention boundaries identify where automatic protection, human response or passive containment can plausibly act. A safeguard that acts after the harm does not prevent the initiating event, although it may limit the consequence.

Worked reasoning example

Unverified numerical or qualitative illustration: For a transfer line, investigate NO FLOW with a running pump, then compare with REVERSE FLOW when downstream pressure is higher. Begin with the stated conditions only. Write the relationships that must hold, then identify at least one variable not specified in the example. If an exact arithmetic result is given, reproduce it independently and add a clear qualifier about the simplified model. If the example is qualitative, draw a two-branch argument: one where the existing barrier is available and one where it is defeated by a plausible condition. Do not fill missing branches with assumed success simply to obtain a final score.

A useful way to test the claim is to ask what change would produce the opposite conclusion. For this topic, the specific analytical trap is using a guideword list as a substitute for a facilitated multi-disciplinary meeting. Try to construct a counterexample with the same nominal process equipment but an altered startup, maintenance, utility, human or instrument condition. A single plausible counterexample indicates that the original claim was conditional. It does not automatically establish that the process is unsafe; it establishes which fact must be investigated before any stronger judgment.

Evidence and traceability

The minimum relevant evidence set is updated p&ids, operating procedures, instrument and equipment descriptions, experienced team members. For each item record source, revision, date, scope, owner and confidence. Separate field-measured values from vendor guarantees, industry generic estimates and deliberately fictional learning inputs. For example, two pumps sold at the same nominal capacity do not necessarily share seal arrangements, failure modes or instrument interfaces. Even an authoritative standard does not substitute for actual testing or determine whether a particular facility falls within its legal scope.

An effective review checks whether evidence is current for the operating mode. Startup differs from steady production; temporary operation after a maintenance outage may differ from both. Procedures, alarms and emergency equipment must be evaluated for those modes explicitly. A reliability assertion based on one short interval must not silently be extended to years of service. Missing evidence should generate a specific work request instead of a generic recommendation to “improve safety.”

Laboratory investigation and sensitivity

Use [Lab 10: Independent OR fault-tree gate](../labs/lab-10.html). The printed equation is P(A ∪ B) = 1−(1−p_A)(1−p_B). Compute union of two independent basic events. Start from each slider's default, record the displayed metric and vary the first parameter over five or more settings. The graph and CSV should agree with your hand calculations. Next, change one other parameter while keeping the first fixed; this distinguishes a mathematical sensitivity from a trend caused by simultaneous input changes.

Investigation target: Check result is at least the larger component probability. The major simplification is Independence assumed and same observation basis; shared causes require separate model.. A good lab report predicts the sign of the derivative or change before observing the plot, then explains what physical process that derivative represents. If an output is nonphysical or a limiting denominator becomes zero, it should be reported as an invalid domain rather than manipulated into a finite reassuring result. Place the lesson's analytical relationship alongside the lab relation; explain whether they describe the same physical quantity or different levels of abstraction.

As a challenge, compare this model with [Lab 11: Independent AND fault-tree gate](../labs/lab-11.html). The second model's relation is P(A ∩ B) = p_A p_B, and its stated limitation is Does not model dependencies, systematic failures, repair or demand frequency.. The comparison is not an invitation to combine numbers blindly. Explain what would need to be measured so the two simplified relationships could appear in one defensible engineering argument.

Failure-first decision review

The unacceptable shortcut is using a guideword list as a substitute for a facilitated multi-disciplinary meeting. Make a table with columns assumption, possible failure, observation that would expose it, cheapest credible check, owner, and decision consequence. For this particular topic the review should prioritize the evidence sources above rather than produce a broad list of every conceivable failure. Then ask what is the most important remaining uncertainty if all routine checks pass. Low-frequency high-consequence scenarios often cannot be validated simply by waiting for an incident.

The recommended analytical action is: Record decisions and evidence for actions, including reasons for no recommendation. This recommendation is a learning exercise, not a control-system change or site-specific directive. Describe its boundaries. What would have to be established by independent qualified review? How would an operator learn that the intended protective measure had become unavailable? Which record would demonstrate closure after a modification? A paper action with no verification is not the same as an effective protective function.

Interpreting uncertainty and competing explanations

Uncertainty enters from input data, model structure, operating variability and human or organizational factors. Input uncertainty might be reduced by a calibrated measurement, whereas model-structure uncertainty demands a different physical model or field trial. Increasing the number of decimal places addresses neither. Separate uncertainty that could reverse the conclusion from uncertainty that changes only the reported numerical precision. The severity of a credible major-accident scenario should influence how urgently an evidence gap is reviewed, even when its probability is difficult to quantify.

For hazop guidewords and nodes, an apparently successful barrier test may demonstrate only the modes exercised by the test. It might not probe lost utilities, common-cause failure, maintenance bypass or the specific initiating condition. A useful counterfactual question is: If the expected result were wrong tomorrow, which observation would reveal it first? Identify an observable precursor and a clear escalation action. This turns an educational calculation into a disciplined evidence requirement without representing it as a plant certification.

Independent exercises

1. Boundary and mechanism: Write two complete worksheet rows; distinguish cause from safeguard and consequence. Label at least one fact, one assumption and one unknown.

2. Quantitative check: Reproduce one result from Lab 10. Show units, the governing relationship and a simple limiting-case or conservation check.

3. Scenario variation: Replace one favorable assumption with an unfavorable but plausible operating condition. Explain which safeguarding claim is weakened and why.

4. Decision note: Summarize in 200–300 words whether record decisions and evidence for actions, including reasons for no recommendation. Specify the additional observation that could change your conclusion.

5. Critical review: Explain why using a guideword list as a substitute for a facilitated multi-disciplinary meeting would be misleading when a high-consequence scenario is involved.

Lesson outcome checklist

  • A credible scenario chain is drawn, without conflating initiating cause, top event and outcome.
  • Model assumptions and units are explicit and the numerical output is checked independently.
  • Any barrier credit is tied to function, independence, availability, evidence and an owner.
  • The decision specifies what remains unverified; it does not present an educational model as a safe operating limit.
