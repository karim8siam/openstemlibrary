Reaction Hazards and Thermal Stability

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and process engineering validation of Reaction Hazards and Thermal Stability. Developed for undergraduate chemical engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Process Safety study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§2.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Reaction Hazards and Thermal Stability

Learning objective. Interpret heat of reaction, heat generation, cooling duty and thermal inertia without confusing toy energy balances with reactive-chemistry hazard testing.

Linked synthetic case. An exothermic batch vessel experiences a changing feed schedule while its utility supply is vulnerable to interruption.

What an adequate output contains. Laboratory reaction calorimetry, thermal screening, relief studies, qualified review and defensible kinetics are essential before any scale-up.

Why this unit is needed

Process safety concerns preventing and limiting catastrophic releases of hazardous substances or energy, rather than merely maintaining a low count of routine injuries. A real project cannot infer its safety from a single score, accident-free period or calculation. For this unit, interpret heat of reaction, heat generation, cooling duty and thermal inertia without confusing toy energy balances with reactive-chemistry hazard testing. The basis of this reasoning is the physical configuration of equipment and operations, not the appearance of a matrix or dashboard. Assign each critical assertion an owner and identify how field observations could contradict it.

The case used across the lessons is deliberately hypothetical: An exothermic batch vessel experiences a changing feed schedule while its utility supply is vulnerable to interruption. It contains no named real chemicals, equipment certificates, actual incident frequencies or approved operating limits. That distinction is central to interpreting models. For a plant, process safety information must describe what can be hazardous, how the process can deviate, and which barriers genuinely remain functional in abnormal conditions. A model may reveal sensitivity while omitting the mechanism that actually dominates a serious accident.

The five-layer engineering argument

1. Hazard and inventory: Identify substances, energy and populations or environmental receptors that could be exposed. Document what remains unknown before assigning a numerical risk category.

2. Initiating mechanism: State a credible cause, including external utilities, human-machine interfaces, maintenance, abnormal startup and non-routine conditions; separate initiating cause from the loss-of-control top event.

3. Prevention: Identify physical design limits, independently verified interlocks, procedures and maintenance controls. Do not assume independence without checking power, instruments and common personnel.

4. Mitigation: Assess containment, shutdown, relief and emergency arrangements in the context of the actual consequence mechanism. A mitigation may reduce severity without reducing the initiating frequency.

5. Assurance: Specify evidence, proof tests, field observations and responsible action owners. Controls that are unavailable, bypassed or degraded must not be credited as if fully available.

How to use the three numerical laboratories

These interactive models intentionally isolate individual relationships rather than claiming to predict a hazardous plant. Before moving a slider, write the governing equation and the physical units. Decide which outcomes should rise or fall when one parameter changes. After moving it, check whether the response and the plotted curve agree with that expectation. Export numerical data and calculate one point independently. If a curve seems favorable, identify a missing mechanism that could reverse the real decision.

This unit's laboratories are:

  • [Lab 04: Adiabatic heat rise estimate](../labs/lab-04.html) — Estimate thermal energy divided by effective sensible heat capacity. Boundary: No kinetics, boiling, heat loss, vapor generation or secondary reaction; not a runaway analysis.
  • [Lab 05: Net thermal duty](../labs/lab-05.html) — Track a lumped heat balance around one temperature state. Boundary: UA and Q generation constant; no kinetics, local hot spots or coolant outages.
  • [Lab 06: Constant-power heating interval](../labs/lab-06.html) — Calculate only a hypothetical heat-capacity time to a chosen temperature limit. Boundary: Constant heat input and sensible heat capacity; never use for runaway response or emergency timing.

How to review evidence

For each lesson prepare a three-column evidence ledger: declared fact, supporting observation/source, and status (measured, specified, assumed, inferred, or unresolved). Do not promote an assumption to a measurement when an equation happens to give a precise answer. In a team review, challenge the source of every probability and time constant. If an engineering standard is cited, verify the correct edition and legal applicability; standards differ by jurisdiction and project date. For serious events, methods used by qualified specialists should replace these elementary illustrations.

The course encourages deliberate disagreement: two reviewers should independently identify the hazardous deviation and potentially defeated safeguards before reconciling their lists. Record both the agreement and remaining uncertainty. The presence of a highly consequential unresolved scenario should not be hidden inside a low composite average. Closure means that a response has been implemented and verified to work, not that a spreadsheet cell has been colored green.

Progression and assessment

Complete the four lessons in order. Each assessment demands a short derivation, a sensitivity or limitation check, a scenario analysis and a documented decision. A numerical result without a stated boundary, units and credible evidence should receive no engineering credit. The final unit project asks for an auditable decision memo that separates a screening observation from an approval to build or operate. Peer review should specifically challenge common-cause failures and untested assumptions.

Lesson 1: Reaction enthalpy and energy inventory

An exothermic system can release sensible and chemical energy. The adiabatic temperature rise is a conservation-law estimate that depends on conversion, participating mass and effective heat capacity. Key analytical anchor: ΔT_ad = n_reacted |ΔH_r| /(m Cp). Investigate the following case: A hypothetical conversion of 100 mol with 50 kJ/mol released into 500 kg at 4 kJ/(kg K) adds 2.5 K adiabatically. The most likely conceptual error is applying a constant-cp single-phase estimate during boiling, decomposition or semibatch addition. In the lesson, assemble reaction calorimetry, mass balance, credible conversion range and heat capacities before drawing the operational conclusion: screen energy scales, then commission qualified reactive-hazard assessment. Further discussion: Calculate how dilution changes ΔT_ad while holding reaction extent fixed, then discuss the missing dynamics.

Lesson 2: Heat generation and heat removal

The direction of temperature change is set by net generation minus removal. UA(T−Tc) is only a lumped heat-transfer approximation, and both heat generation and heat-transfer coefficients can be temperature-dependent. Key analytical anchor: m Cp dT/dt = Q_gen − UA(T−T_c). Investigate the following case: A 50 kW synthetic heat source and 30 kW cooling duty produce positive initial heat accumulation. The most likely conceptual error is equating zero net heat at one temperature to global thermal stability. In the lesson, assemble heat-transfer coefficients, reaction rates, agitation state, fouling and utility availability before drawing the operational conclusion: treat model intersections as candidate states requiring stability and upset analysis. Further discussion: Explain why lower coolant temperature may help duty while creating viscosity or freezing problems.

Lesson 3: Cooling failure and thermal inertia

During a loss of cooling, remaining time to a selected threshold depends on current energy, net heat generation and the feasible intervention response. Constant heat production makes a rough screening bound, not a runaway prediction. Key analytical anchor: t_screen = m Cp (T_limit−T_0)/Q_net. Investigate the following case: A liquid has 2000 kJ/K thermal capacity and 40 kW net input; a 10 K rise takes 500 s only if assumptions hold. The most likely conceptual error is using an estimated threshold time as a certified safe response time. In the lesson, assemble worst-case reaction kinetics, runaway calorimetry, interlock function and emergency cooling evidence before drawing the operational conclusion: use conservative scenario envelopes; never design response timing from this toy model. Further discussion: Recalculate for 1.5× heat generation and critique why it still cannot predict self-accelerating chemistry.

Lesson 4: Incompatible mixing, decomposition and escalation

Chemical interaction hazards include contamination, wrong material addition, concentration effects, catalysts, oxygen ingress and abnormal residence time. Safety assessment should include combinations of credible deviations, not just the main reaction. Key analytical anchor: Review matrix: material A × material B × condition × plausible pathway. Investigate the following case: During maintenance a cleaning chemical could enter a batch vessel; map credible mixing routes without prescribing a hazardous test. The most likely conceptual error is assuming compatibility based only on individually benign handling descriptions. In the lesson, assemble compatibility studies, historical incidents, impurity specifications and qualified chemistry review before drawing the operational conclusion: require positive isolation and independent material verification when uncertainty persists. Further discussion: Design a nonexperimental verification checklist that detects mistaken line connection before charging.

Unit completion checkpoint

The minimum acceptable submission contains an annotated process boundary for reaction hazards and thermal stability, a cause-to-consequence pathway, a register of assumptions, an independent numerical reproduction of each model, and an unresolved-information list. In the synthetic case, an exothermic batch vessel experiences a changing feed schedule while its utility supply is vulnerable to interruption. Identify which missing single item is most likely to change your recommendation and why. Then state who would have authority to accept or reject the real project after a specialist review.

The engineering question

An exothermic system can release sensible and chemical energy. The adiabatic temperature rise is a conservation-law estimate that depends on conversion, participating mass and effective heat capacity. This lesson belongs to Reaction Hazards and Thermal Stability, where the course objective is to interpret heat of reaction, heat generation, cooling duty and thermal inertia without confusing toy energy balances with reactive-chemistry hazard testing. The governing issue is not whether one can calculate a number; it is whether that number describes the failure mechanism under study. A competent analysis separates a claim about what happens, an argument about why it would happen, and evidence that the safeguards could withstand real operation.

Specific thought experiment. A hypothetical conversion of 100 mol with 50 kJ/mol released into 500 kg at 4 kJ/(kg K) adds 2.5 K adiabatically. Work from the equipment boundary outward. Name the initiating event, the operating mode, the top event, the potentially exposed people or environment, and the time over which the consequences could develop. If any one of these is unspecified, maintain the uncertainty rather than inventing a value.

Principles and analytical relationship

The working relation or structured method is ΔT_ad = n_reacted |ΔH_r| /(m Cp). It is useful because it forces an analyst to state inputs and ask whether the units, time basis and conditioning assumptions match. A rate per year cannot be multiplied by an arbitrary probability without asking what event that probability conditions upon. Similarly, an ordinary fraction is not a mass fraction until the relevant mass balance has been declared. In a causal diagram, an arrow represents a proposed physical or organizational dependency; it is not, by itself, proof of causality.

The physical interpretation for reaction enthalpy and energy inventory is the following: An exothermic system can release sensible and chemical energy. The adiabatic temperature rise is a conservation-law estimate that depends on conversion, participating mass and effective heat capacity. Thus the result needs an audit trail back to observations, design documents or a controlled estimate. There is no universal conversion from a neat calculation to safe operation. Where the relationship is qualitative, a complete answer explains the conditions for each step and which alternative cause could produce the same observed symptom. When the relation is quantitative, a complete answer supplies units, ranges and a check against a limiting case.

For the course case, an exothermic batch vessel experiences a changing feed schedule while its utility supply is vulnerable to interruption. Consider three boundaries before deciding whether the model is applicable: the system under study, the observation period, and the intervention boundary. System boundaries determine which inventories and energy flows are included. Observation periods distinguish transient loss of control from a steady process average. Intervention boundaries identify where automatic protection, human response or passive containment can plausibly act. A safeguard that acts after the harm does not prevent the initiating event, although it may limit the consequence.

Worked reasoning example

Unverified numerical or qualitative illustration: A hypothetical conversion of 100 mol with 50 kJ/mol released into 500 kg at 4 kJ/(kg K) adds 2.5 K adiabatically. Begin with the stated conditions only. Write the relationships that must hold, then identify at least one variable not specified in the example. If an exact arithmetic result is given, reproduce it independently and add a clear qualifier about the simplified model. If the example is qualitative, draw a two-branch argument: one where the existing barrier is available and one where it is defeated by a plausible condition. Do not fill missing branches with assumed success simply to obtain a final score.

A useful way to test the claim is to ask what change would produce the opposite conclusion. For this topic, the specific analytical trap is applying a constant-cp single-phase estimate during boiling, decomposition or semibatch addition. Try to construct a counterexample with the same nominal process equipment but an altered startup, maintenance, utility, human or instrument condition. A single plausible counterexample indicates that the original claim was conditional. It does not automatically establish that the process is unsafe; it establishes which fact must be investigated before any stronger judgment.

Evidence and traceability

The minimum relevant evidence set is reaction calorimetry, mass balance, credible conversion range and heat capacities. For each item record source, revision, date, scope, owner and confidence. Separate field-measured values from vendor guarantees, industry generic estimates and deliberately fictional learning inputs. For example, two pumps sold at the same nominal capacity do not necessarily share seal arrangements, failure modes or instrument interfaces. Even an authoritative standard does not substitute for actual testing or determine whether a particular facility falls within its legal scope.

An effective review checks whether evidence is current for the operating mode. Startup differs from steady production; temporary operation after a maintenance outage may differ from both. Procedures, alarms and emergency equipment must be evaluated for those modes explicitly. A reliability assertion based on one short interval must not silently be extended to years of service. Missing evidence should generate a specific work request instead of a generic recommendation to “improve safety.”

Laboratory investigation and sensitivity

Use [Lab 04: Adiabatic heat rise estimate](../labs/lab-04.html). The printed equation is ΔT = n|ΔH|/(m Cp). Estimate thermal energy divided by effective sensible heat capacity. Start from each slider's default, record the displayed metric and vary the first parameter over five or more settings. The graph and CSV should agree with your hand calculations. Next, change one other parameter while keeping the first fixed; this distinguishes a mathematical sensitivity from a trend caused by simultaneous input changes.

Investigation target: What happens if the mass doubles while reacted moles remain fixed? The major simplification is No kinetics, boiling, heat loss, vapor generation or secondary reaction; not a runaway analysis.. A good lab report predicts the sign of the derivative or change before observing the plot, then explains what physical process that derivative represents. If an output is nonphysical or a limiting denominator becomes zero, it should be reported as an invalid domain rather than manipulated into a finite reassuring result. Place the lesson's analytical relationship alongside the lab relation; explain whether they describe the same physical quantity or different levels of abstraction.

As a challenge, compare this model with [Lab 05: Net thermal duty](../labs/lab-05.html). The second model's relation is Q_net = Q_gen − UA(T−T_c), and its stated limitation is UA and Q generation constant; no kinetics, local hot spots or coolant outages.. The comparison is not an invitation to combine numbers blindly. Explain what would need to be measured so the two simplified relationships could appear in one defensible engineering argument.

Failure-first decision review

The unacceptable shortcut is applying a constant-cp single-phase estimate during boiling, decomposition or semibatch addition. Make a table with columns assumption, possible failure, observation that would expose it, cheapest credible check, owner, and decision consequence. For this particular topic the review should prioritize the evidence sources above rather than produce a broad list of every conceivable failure. Then ask what is the most important remaining uncertainty if all routine checks pass. Low-frequency high-consequence scenarios often cannot be validated simply by waiting for an incident.

The recommended analytical action is: Screen energy scales, then commission qualified reactive-hazard assessment. This recommendation is a learning exercise, not a control-system change or site-specific directive. Describe its boundaries. What would have to be established by independent qualified review? How would an operator learn that the intended protective measure had become unavailable? Which record would demonstrate closure after a modification? A paper action with no verification is not the same as an effective protective function.

Interpreting uncertainty and competing explanations

Uncertainty enters from input data, model structure, operating variability and human or organizational factors. Input uncertainty might be reduced by a calibrated measurement, whereas model-structure uncertainty demands a different physical model or field trial. Increasing the number of decimal places addresses neither. Separate uncertainty that could reverse the conclusion from uncertainty that changes only the reported numerical precision. The severity of a credible major-accident scenario should influence how urgently an evidence gap is reviewed, even when its probability is difficult to quantify.

For reaction enthalpy and energy inventory, an apparently successful barrier test may demonstrate only the modes exercised by the test. It might not probe lost utilities, common-cause failure, maintenance bypass or the specific initiating condition. A useful counterfactual question is: If the expected result were wrong tomorrow, which observation would reveal it first? Identify an observable precursor and a clear escalation action. This turns an educational calculation into a disciplined evidence requirement without representing it as a plant certification.

Independent exercises

1. Boundary and mechanism: Calculate how dilution changes ΔT_ad while holding reaction extent fixed, then discuss the missing dynamics. Label at least one fact, one assumption and one unknown.

2. Quantitative check: Reproduce one result from Lab 04. Show units, the governing relationship and a simple limiting-case or conservation check.

3. Scenario variation: Replace one favorable assumption with an unfavorable but plausible operating condition. Explain which safeguarding claim is weakened and why.

4. Decision note: Summarize in 200–300 words whether screen energy scales, then commission qualified reactive-hazard assessment. Specify the additional observation that could change your conclusion.

5. Critical review: Explain why applying a constant-cp single-phase estimate during boiling, decomposition or semibatch addition would be misleading when a high-consequence scenario is involved.

Lesson outcome checklist

  • A credible scenario chain is drawn, without conflating initiating cause, top event and outcome.
  • Model assumptions and units are explicit and the numerical output is checked independently.
  • Any barrier credit is tied to function, independence, availability, evidence and an owner.
  • The decision specifies what remains unverified; it does not present an educational model as a safe operating limit.
