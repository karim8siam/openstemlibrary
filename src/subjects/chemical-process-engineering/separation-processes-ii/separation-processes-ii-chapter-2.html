§2.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Gas Permeation, Electrodialysis and Advanced Membranes

Engineering context

A plant uses a membrane cascade to recover valuable solvent vapor while discharging a depleted stream. The first pass appears favorable because the permeate is rich in the desired component. A system audit reveals that compression power and purge losses grow sharply with the recycle ratio. Design comparisons should use total product recovery and energy per unit saleable product, not only membrane selectivity. For ionic brines a separate electrodialysis step may be possible, but charge-equivalent balances and membrane chemical stability must be considered.

Unit learning outcomes

  1. Develop and check nanofiltration, selectivity and solute passage using R_obs=1-C_p/C_f; identify the principal assumptions and explain an experimental validation path.
  2. Develop and check gas permeation modules and stage cut using theta=F_p/F_f; identify the principal assumptions and explain an experimental validation path.
  3. Develop and check multistage membranes and recycle balances using F z_F=P y_P+R x_R; identify the principal assumptions and explain an experimental validation path.
  4. Develop and check electrodialysis and electrical work using n=eta I t/(z F); identify the principal assumptions and explain an experimental validation path.

Concept map

1. Nanofiltration, selectivity and solute passage

Observed rejection compares mixed samples at the module outlets; intrinsic membrane rejection would require near-wall concentration and transport models. Charged membranes can repel multivalent ions strongly while giving more modest monovalent rejection. Ionic strength screens membrane charge. A negative apparent rejection may occur for one ion due to coupling and electroneutrality; it does not automatically indicate measurement error. Report the test solution, pH, pressure, recovery and flux with rejection data.

Central relation: R_obs=1-C_p/C_f. Operating boundary: Ion-specific transport is complex; a constant-rejection balance is a bookkeeping exercise, not a predictive electrodiffusion model.

2. Gas permeation modules and stage cut

Stage cut sets the fraction of feed withdrawn as permeate. In a highly selective membrane, increasing stage cut often increases recovery but can lower permeate purity because the upstream composition and driving forces change. The high-pressure retentate composition also changes, influencing flux and downstream duty. A single ideal selectivity parameter cannot predict an industrial membrane module: concentration polarization, nonideal gas transport and axial pressure profiles matter.

Central relation: theta=F_p/F_f. Operating boundary: The permeate purity is an assumed module outcome, not a prediction of selectivity alone.

3. Multistage membranes and recycle balances

A cascade can polish the permeate or recover valuable solute from the retentate. Recompressing a recycle may recover product but incur compression energy and raise sensitivity to fouling. Always label streams and select a consistent component basis before solving multi-unit flows. When a splitter sends part of the retentate back, the inlet composition is not equal to fresh-feed composition. Calculate steady-state recycle using simultaneous balances rather than a one-pass approximation.

Central relation: F z_F=P y_P+R x_R. Operating boundary: A balance determines feasible streams only after sufficient concentrations or stage performances are specified.

4. Electrodialysis and electrical work

Electrodialysis transports ions through ion-exchange membranes under an electric field. A successful design must respect electroneutrality, current efficiency, membrane resistance and limiting-current constraints. Electricity consumed by a stack includes electrode and solution voltage losses, which are not captured by Faraday’s law alone. Report concentration change on a known volumetric basis and state whether the current-efficiency estimate refers to salt-equivalent transfer or a particular ion.

Central relation: n=eta I t/(z F). Operating boundary: Energy per removed mole requires the time-varying stack voltage; charge-only models cannot estimate operating cost.

What to test experimentally

Establish the basis. A useful calculation begins with a consistent basis: mass rate, molar rate, solution volume, dry adsorbent mass or solvent mass. For equipment sizing a rate must not be confused with a cumulative inventory. Record every conversion as a separate line. Does the result represent an instantaneous rate, a time-integrated amount, a concentration, or a dimensionless ratio?

Identify equilibrium and rate assumptions. Equilibrium constrains an attainable composition or loading; kinetics determines how quickly a process approaches that constraint. Where an empirical coefficient is used, report its calibration range, units and experimental provenance. Which measured observation would show that an assumed equilibrium relation is not applicable?

Connect the model to a unit operation. A local model should be embedded within balances, operating constraints and product targets. Report useful recovery, purity and waste flows on comparable bases rather than optimizing a single local response. Which outlet becomes the limiting waste stream when product recovery increases?

Test uncertainty and reversal. If two alternatives are close, measurement error may reverse their apparent ranking. Estimate the sensitivity of performance to the largest uncertain inputs, and check that the conclusion survives a credible parameter range. Could an operating choice preferred at the reference feed become inferior when feed quality changes?

Deliverables and evidence

  2. A labeled process diagram naming the physical location of the driving force and the location of the main measurement.
  3. A one-page model statement with every coefficient, unit, validity range and inferred rather than measured property.

  • Gas permeation modules and stage cut — employ theta=F_p/F_f only within its assumptions. The permeate purity is an assumed module outcome, not a prediction of selectivity alone.
  • Multistage membranes and recycle balances — employ F z_F=P y_P+R x_R only within its assumptions. A balance determines feasible streams only after sufficient concentrations or stage performances are specified.
