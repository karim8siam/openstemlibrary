§2.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Convective Mass Transfer and Boundary Layers

Scope: Boundary layers; coefficients; correlations; analogies. Four lessons connect microscopic transport or process operating principles to practical calculations. Relevant laboratory models: 5, 6.

Central engineering ideas

Convective transfer coefficients connect a driving concentration difference to a net flux through a near-wall region. Their numerical value depends on length scale and boundary conditions; a coefficient measured around a sphere cannot be carried unchanged to a plate or pipe. The Schmidt number compares momentum and molecular species diffusivity, so using the same liquid viscosity at all temperatures can misstate transfer trends.

The most valuable experimental observable is often the slope of mass released or absorbed versus time under carefully controlled bulk flow. Measure velocity, density and viscosity to identify Reynolds number. A proposed Sherwood correlation should be tested only across its reported Reynolds, Schmidt and geometry range. If the surface reaction is fast, its apparent concentration may approximate zero; if the surface reaction is slow, fluid-side transfer may not control.

Analysis, synthesis and laboratory practice

First create a one-page property table: list fluid phase, bulk composition, surface or interface state, temperature, pressure, and the reference from which viscosity or diffusivity was obtained. Next sketch the principal control volume and write a component balance even when a transfer correlation is already known. Conservation closure should be computed using independent inventory data, not simply by substituting the computed flux back into the same equation.

Then use the simulation models to build a two-dimensional experiment: perform one sweep in the primary adjustable parameter and repeat at two distinct fixed values of a secondary parameter. Compare the resulting curves and explain whether the ranking changes. When model rankings do change, identify the competing effects. Where the equation predicts no change with a parameter that matters experimentally, the discrepancy is useful evidence of a missing mechanism rather than a reason to manipulate the fit.

For technical reporting, preserve the input CSV, baseline calculation, units and full list of assumptions. Include a short paragraph that distinguishes model sensitivity from actual process uncertainty, and identify one input that should be measured more precisely before design decisions. Link the interpretation to a measurable concentration, flow, pressure or inventory instead of treating the plotted output as a stand-alone achievement.

Suggested learning evidence: a control-volume sketch, two parameter sweeps, a worked balance, one falsification experiment and a paragraph about uncertainty.

Start with a physical sketch showing control volumes, phase boundaries, sensor positions and the relevant flow rates. Identify the driving potential, compute a result on a consistent mole, mass, time and area basis, and translate the result into a quantity that a laboratory could measure. Confirm conservation independently. A predicted high flux is not automatically useful: it may require an unacceptable pressure, cause overheating, or imply an unrealistic solvent inventory. Make a small mass-balance closure table with inflow, outflow, transfer rate and accumulation. Flux integrated over area must agree with the change in inventory after any time integration. When a calculation satisfies a local transfer law but violates total inventory, the likely issue is a basis conversion, interface area or hidden phase holdup.

Practical measurements have time constants. A concentration electrode or humidity sensor may respond more slowly than the process, causing a fitted diffusion time or mass-transfer coefficient to absorb instrument delay. Record probe location and sampling interval, and compare apparent rates in the first, middle and final periods of the experiment. If the response changes with stirring intensity, external transfer may be important.

§2.7 Experimental Laboratory Investigation: Laboratory 03: Transient diffusion concentration

Laboratory 03: Transient diffusion concentration

Relevant unit: Diffusion. Governing relationship: (c-c₀)/(cₛ-c₀) = erfc(z / (2√Dt)).

Physical question

What happens to the predicted transient diffusion concentration when its principal adjustable parameter changes, and how does that sensitivity relate to the mechanism described in this unit? The baseline parameter values are synthetic teaching inputs, not measurements from a named plant. This experiment is designed to expose a mathematical trend and make its units visible before real property data are substituted.

Experimental setup and protocol

Semi-infinite medium; constant D and maintained surface condition. List the assumptions on paper before changing inputs. Treat the first control as the independent variable; keep the remaining parameters fixed for the first sweep. Read the numerical output at the minimum, baseline and maximum setting, then export the 121-point curve. Repeat at a second value of the parameter most strongly coupled to the driving force. Use the on-screen schematic to identify the direction of transport and the conceptual location of the limiting resistance. It is not a scale drawing.

Parameters

  • Depth: default 2 mm, adjustable range 0.05–8 mm. Check the measurement source and correlation domain before substitution.
  • Diffusivity: default 1e-09 m²/s, adjustable range 1e-10–4e-09 m²/s. Check the measurement source and correlation domain before substitution.
  • Elapsed time: default 4000 s, adjustable range 100–25000 s. Check the measurement source and correlation domain before substitution.

Interpretation and model boundaries

Diffusion is caused by microscopic motion even when the bulk fluid is nominally stationary. The engineer must identify whether flux refers to a fixed plane, a moving mixture, or an individual component relative to the molar-average velocity. These distinctions are not cosmetic: in an evaporating gas film a nonzero total molar flow adds a convective term to the component flux. In porous materials the accessible cross-sectional area is smaller than the specimen envelope and tortuous paths make the effective diffusion coefficient less than the free-fluid coefficient.

The response curve is a local sensitivity plot in which other controls remain fixed. It does not automatically represent a physical trajectory through a reactor or a separation column. A reported ''outside model validity'' result indicates a mathematical or physical constraint such as an inverted concentration difference, invalid pinch, an incompatible moisture ordering, or a correlation beyond its specified regime. The laboratory deliberately leaves these cases visible so they can be diagnosed instead of silently clipped.

Investigation questions

  1. Calculate the baseline result independently using (c-c₀)/(cₛ-c₀) = erfc(z / (2√Dt)). Convert all parameter units first. What is the largest source of numerical uncertainty?
  2. Explain the curvature or straightness of the sweep. Does doubling the first parameter double, halve, or otherwise change the response?
  3. Which plausible operating effect is excluded by the model: property variation, pressure drop, fouling, chemical reaction, unsteady accumulation, or imperfect contact? Describe a test for that effect.
  4. If a customer specification required the output to improve by 15%, identify two possible changes and discuss an operational cost or safety implication of each.

Use restriction: Educational sensitivity model; it is not a substitute for verified correlations, material data, equipment design standards or engineering sign-off.

Interfacial area, energy dissipation, liquid circulation and residence-time distribution often change even when fluid composition and bulk temperature are preserved. A prototype can fit mass-transfer coefficients over a narrow regime yet fail at scale because gas bubbles coalesce or flow bypasses wetted surfaces.

The response curve is a local sensitivity plot in which other controls remain fixed. It does not automatically represent a physical trajectory through a reactor or a separation column. A reported ''outside model validity'' result indicates a mathematical or physical constraint such as an inverted concentration difference, invalid pinch, an incompatible moisture ordering, or a correlation beyond its specified regime. The laboratory deliberately leaves these cases visible so they can be diagnosed instead of silently clipped.
