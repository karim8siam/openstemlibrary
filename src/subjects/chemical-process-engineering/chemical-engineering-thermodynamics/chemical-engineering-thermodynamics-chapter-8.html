§8.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Chemical Reaction Equilibria and Multi-Phase Reaction Systems

Why this unit matters

Thermodynamic workflows, cycles, separations and uncertainty management. The preceding prerequisite is Reaction equilibrium; the destination is capstone process decisions. Engineering students should be able to move from a physical scenario to a transparent mathematical model and back to an interpretation of what would be observed in equipment. Thermodynamic design becomes unreliable when an input is precise to many decimal places while the phase model, standard states or material identities remain unspecified.

Four connected lessons

1. Compression and expansion efficiency

Adiabatic machines are benchmarked against reversible isentropic endpoints; real performance is defined by enthalpy changes.

Useful calculation: For an ideal gas with constant k, T2s/T1=(P2/P1)^((k−1)/k); compressor η_is=(h2s−h1)/(h2−h1).

Physical interpretation: A compressor with reduced efficiency delivers hotter discharge and consumes more shaft power for the same inlet and outlet pressures.

Failure to watch: Do not use ideal-gas k at supercritical conditions or near condensation; multi-stage compression may require intercooling and a real-fluid EOS.

Decision: Calculate heat rejection needs from efficiency trends.

2. Refrigeration and heat pumps

A vapor-compression cycle transfers heat from low to high temperature by consuming work and exploiting phase changes.

Useful calculation: COP_refrigerator=Q_c/W; COP_heatpump=Q_h/W; energy conservation gives Q_h=Q_c+W.

Physical interpretation: Refrigerant approach temperatures and compressor inefficiency strongly influence practical electricity consumption.

Failure to watch: An ideal Carnot COP is an upper bound and not a forecast of real equipment performance; refrigerant environmental and safety constraints also matter.

Decision: Analyze seasonal sensitivity without inventing vendor performance data.

3. Thermodynamics of separation work

Mixing creates entropy, and unmixing requires energy or available work even before real equipment losses are included.

Useful calculation: For ideal mixing ΔG_mix=nRT Σ x_i ln x_i; minimum reversible separation work is −ΔG_mix for an appropriate specified process.

Physical interpretation: Very dilute solutes may demand substantial work per mole recovered because an extreme purity specification is thermodynamically demanding.

Failure to watch: The minimum reversible work is not the steam or electric use of a real column, which includes finite heat transfer and imperfect separation.

Decision: Explain why recovery and purity targets increase cost.

4. Validation, uncertainty and process decisions

A defensible thermodynamic prediction has explicit references, assumptions, validity limits, sensitivity tests and property-data provenance.

Useful calculation: Propagated first-order variance: σ_y²≈Σ(∂f/∂x_i)²σ_i² when inputs are independent.

Physical interpretation: If vapor-pressure data and temperature measurements each have uncertainty, report a range of flash fractions rather than a false exact operating point.

Failure to watch: Numerically smooth graphs do not certify a physical model; uncertainty in phase behavior often exceeds floating-point solver error.

Decision: Build a review checklist and reject unqualified designs.

Unit 8 · Lesson 3: Thermodynamics of separation work

Learning objectives. Explain thermodynamics of separation work in physical terms; identify the relevant state or balance variables; apply a quantitative relation only within its validity range; and critically review a proposed process-engineering decision.

The physical question

Mixing creates entropy, and unmixing requires energy or available work even before real equipment losses are included.

Governing relation and interpretation

Key relation: For ideal mixing ΔG_mix=nRT Σ x_i ln x_i; minimum reversible separation work is −ΔG_mix for an appropriate specified process.

Engineering example and model boundary

Very dilute solutes may demand substantial work per mole recovered because an extreme purity specification is thermodynamically demanding.

Worked investigation

At 300 K, separate an equimolar ideal liquid mixture containing 100 mol total into pure components. The ideal mixing Gibbs energy has magnitude nRT ln2.

The minimum reversible separation work is positive because spontaneous mixing lowers Gibbs energy; real separation heat demand is larger.

Where the relation breaks down

The minimum reversible work is not the steam or electric use of a real column, which includes finite heat transfer and imperfect separation.

§8.6 Experimental Laboratory Investigation: Laboratory 15: Henry-law dissolved-gas screening

Laboratory 15: Henry-law dissolved-gas screening

Model: c=kH p

Controllable variables: Gas partial pressure (kPa) and Concentration-form kH (mol m⁻³ kPa⁻¹).

Governing relation

c=kH p. This relation is used for an educational screening calculation, not certified physical-property or process-safety design. Every output must be checked against dimensional consistency, equilibrium assumptions and an independent reference when applied to real chemicals.

Stated limitations

Dilute dissolved gas, fixed temperature and chosen coefficient convention.

Step-by-step investigation

  1. Record the default inputs and the readout with its units. Sketch the qualitative expected trend before inspecting the chart.
  2. Decrease Gas partial pressure to its minimum while keeping Concentration-form kH fixed. Record the change and explain which term in the governing equation is responsible.
  3. Increase Gas partial pressure to its maximum and compare the response with the first observation. Determine whether the dependence is linear, logarithmic, exponential or nonlinear for a reason you can explain.
  4. Reset, then alter Concentration-form kH and repeat. Estimate a finite-difference sensitivity between two nearby parameter values, including its units.
  5. Export the response curve to CSV. Explain how a plotted sensitivity helps select a range for a laboratory measurement or a process-control test.
  6. Identify the first physical mechanism omitted by the model that would become important if the system were pressurized, heated, mixed or scaled up substantially.

Questions

Reasoning: What error arises if a reference defines the reciprocal form of Henry law?

Extension: Double gas partial pressure and compare predicted equilibrium concentration.

Uncertainty: How would uncertainty in concentration-form kh influence your interpretation of the response curve? Which single additional measurement would best distinguish model error from input uncertainty?
