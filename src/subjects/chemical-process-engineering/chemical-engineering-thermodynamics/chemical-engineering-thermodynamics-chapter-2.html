§2.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Volumetric Properties of Pure Fluids and Cubic Equations of State

Why this unit matters

Conservation, irreversibility and available work in process engineering. The preceding prerequisite is Property relations; the destination is Real fluids. Engineering students should be able to move from a physical scenario to a transparent mathematical model and back to an interpretation of what would be observed in equipment. Thermodynamic design becomes unreliable when an input is precise to many decimal places while the phase model, standard states or material identities remain unspecified.

Four connected lessons

1. First law for flowing equipment

Steady-flow energy balances include enthalpy, kinetic, potential, shaft work and heat transfer with a declared sign convention.

Useful calculation: For a one-inlet one-outlet steady device: q−w_s = Δh+Δ(v²/2)+gΔz.

Physical interpretation: An insulated turbine can convert a decrease in steam enthalpy into useful shaft work; a valve with negligible kinetic change has approximately equal inlet and outlet enthalpy.

Failure to watch: Neglecting velocity and elevation must be justified; a nozzle is precisely the equipment where kinetic energy dominates.

Decision: Build balances for turbines, throttles, pumps and heat exchangers.

2. Entropy balance and second-law limits

Entropy transport by heat and mass is supplemented by nonnegative entropy generation in real processes.

Useful calculation: At steady state: 0=Σ m_in s_in−Σ m_out s_out+Σ Q_j/T_j+S_gen, with S_gen≥0.

Physical interpretation: Heat leaking through a finite temperature difference creates entropy even if energy is fully conserved.

Failure to watch: A negative computed S_gen in a passive device signals inconsistent signs, inaccurate state data or an invalid process assumption.

Decision: Estimate irreversibility in mixing and heat exchange.

3. Exergy and lost-work accounting

Available useful work depends on both the system and its chosen environmental reference state.

Useful calculation: Specific flow exergy ψ=(h−h0)−T0(s−s0)+V²/2+gz; destroyed exergy=T0 S_gen.

Physical interpretation: Two streams having equal energy can possess very different work potential depending on their temperature and pressure relative to the environment.

Failure to watch: Exergy is not a conserved property: it is destroyed by irreversibility, and its value depends on ambient temperature and composition assumptions.

Decision: Locate avoidable losses in plant utility systems.

4. Cycles and thermodynamic efficiency

A cycle returns its working fluid to its initial state while transferring energy and entropy to external reservoirs.

Useful calculation: Carnot engine η_max=1−T_c/T_h; ideal refrigerator COP_max=T_c/(T_h−T_c), using kelvin.

Physical interpretation: A heat pump should be compared using COP, not a thermal engine efficiency, because the useful output is heat delivered to a space.

Failure to watch: Carnot limits require heat exchange with thermal reservoirs and exclude real pressure drops, mixing and finite heat-transfer rates.

Decision: Compare ideal cycles with feasible process utilities.
