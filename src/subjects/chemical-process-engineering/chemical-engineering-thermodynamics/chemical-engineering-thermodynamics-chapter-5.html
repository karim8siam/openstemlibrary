§5.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Thermodynamic Properties of Real Fluids, Departures and Fugacity

Why this unit matters

Bubble, dew, activity coefficients and flash separation. The preceding prerequisite is Phase equilibrium; the destination is Other equilibria. Engineering students should be able to move from a physical scenario to a transparent mathematical model and back to an interpretation of what would be observed in equipment. Thermodynamic design becomes unreliable when an input is precise to many decimal places while the phase model, standard states or material identities remain unspecified.

Four connected lessons

1. Raoult-law bubble pressure

For an ideal liquid solution at modest pressure, vapor partial pressures scale with liquid mole fractions and pure-component saturation pressures.

Useful calculation: P_bubble=Σ x_i P_i^sat; y_i=x_i P_i^sat/P.

Physical interpretation: A richer light-component liquid typically has a higher bubble pressure at fixed temperature when its pure saturation pressure is greater.

Failure to watch: An ideal mixture approximation may fail for hydrogen-bonding or strongly dissimilar liquids; vapor nonideality matters at high pressure.

Decision: Estimate vaporization onset for binary solvent handling.

2. Dew-pressure and dew-temperature calculations

The first droplet formed from vapor must satisfy vapor and incipient-liquid compositions simultaneously.

Useful calculation: At ideal low pressure Σ y_i P/P_i^sat(T)=1 at dew temperature.

Physical interpretation: A condenser can be screened for its first-liquid appearance by solving a nonlinear scalar temperature equation.

Failure to watch: A dew calculation performed with feed liquid fractions instead of vapor fractions solves a different problem and produces false results.

Decision: Set condenser approach temperatures with uncertainty margins.

3. Nonideal liquid activities

Activity coefficients correct chemical potential in a liquid mixture when interactions depart from ideal mixing.

Useful calculation: Modified Raoult relation y_i P≈x_i γ_i P_i^sat under low-pressure vapor conditions.

Physical interpretation: A nonideal mixture may exhibit an azeotrope where vapor and liquid compositions become equal at a particular equilibrium state.

Failure to watch: Using γ_i=1 without evidence can conceal azeotropes and cause impossible separation specifications.

Decision: Use experimental VLE data to decide whether simple correlations are appropriate.

4. Flash vaporization and Rachford–Rice

A constant-T,P flash solves component mass balances and equilibrium partitioning together with unknown vapor fraction.

Useful calculation: F(β)=Σ z_i(K_i−1)/(1+β(K_i−1))=0, with 0≤β≤1 and denominator positive.

Physical interpretation: For known equilibrium ratios a feasible two-phase solution produces consistent liquid and vapor compositions and an overall component balance.

Failure to watch: Blindly solving the scalar equation without a phase-feasibility check may return a nonphysical vapor fraction.

Decision: Perform process-level flash checks and sensitivity analysis.

§5.6 Experimental Laboratory Investigation: Laboratory 9: Two-phase steam quality

Laboratory 9: Two-phase steam quality

Model: h=hf+x hfg

Controllable variables: Mass vapor quality (fraction) and Latent enthalpy (kJ kg⁻¹).

Governing relation

h=hf+x hfg. This relation is used for an educational screening calculation, not certified physical-property or process-safety design. Every output must be checked against dimensional consistency, equilibrium assumptions and an independent reference when applied to real chemicals.

Stated limitations

Reference saturated-liquid h_f=600 kJ/kg; both enthalpies at one saturation pressure.

Step-by-step investigation

  1. Record the default inputs and the readout with its units. Sketch the qualitative expected trend before inspecting the chart.
  2. Decrease Mass vapor quality to its minimum while keeping Latent enthalpy fixed. Record the change and explain which term in the governing equation is responsible.
  3. Increase Mass vapor quality to its maximum and compare the response with the first observation. Determine whether the dependence is linear, logarithmic, exponential or nonlinear for a reason you can explain.
  4. Reset, then alter Latent enthalpy and repeat. Estimate a finite-difference sensitivity between two nearby parameter values, including its units.
  5. Export the response curve to CSV. Explain how a plotted sensitivity helps select a range for a laboratory measurement or a process-control test.
  6. Identify the first physical mechanism omitted by the model that would become important if the system were pressurized, heated, mixed or scaled up substantially.

Questions

Reasoning: Why is quality not a volume fraction?

Extension: Compare 50% quality with the midpoint of vapor and liquid specific volumes.
