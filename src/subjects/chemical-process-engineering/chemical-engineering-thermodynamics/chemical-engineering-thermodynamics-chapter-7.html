§7.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Solution Thermodynamics, Excess Properties and Activity Models

Why this unit matters

Reaction Gibbs energy, equilibrium constants, temperature and extent. The preceding prerequisite is Other equilibria; the destination is Engineering applications. Engineering students should be able to move from a physical scenario to a transparent mathematical model and back to an interpretation of what would be observed in equipment. Thermodynamic design becomes unreliable when an input is precise to many decimal places while the phase model, standard states or material identities remain unspecified.

Four connected lessons

1. Standard reaction Gibbs energy

For a defined stoichiometric reaction, equilibrium is governed by the Gibbs energy change and dimensionless reaction quotient.

Useful calculation: Δ_rG=Δ_rG°+RT ln Q; K=exp(−Δ_rG°/(RT)).

Physical interpretation: When Q

Failure to watch: Q must be dimensionless using standard-state activities; substituting raw dimensional partial pressures is a convention error.

Decision: Predict reaction direction from measured compositions.

2. Temperature effects and van’t Hoff relation

Equilibrium constants shift with temperature according to the standard reaction enthalpy, under the usual assumptions for its integration.

Useful calculation: d(ln K)/dT=Δ_rH°/(RT²); for constant Δ_rH°, ln(K2/K1)=−Δ_rH°/R(1/T2−1/T1).

Physical interpretation: An exothermic reaction generally has smaller K at higher temperature; kinetics may accelerate simultaneously, creating a tradeoff.

Failure to watch: Do not infer economic reactor performance from K alone: rate, pressure drop, catalyst deactivation and heat removal matter.

Decision: Compare operating-temperature targets with conversion limits.

3. Reaction extent and equilibrium conversion

Stoichiometric extent tracks every species consistently while a nonlinear equilibrium constraint determines feasible composition.

Useful calculation: n_i=n_i0+ν_i ξ, together with Q(ξ,T,P)=K(T).

Physical interpretation: A feed containing excess reactant may shift an equilibrium reaction but changes separation and recycle requirements downstream.

Failure to watch: The allowable extent interval comes from nonnegative mole numbers; solving outside this interval is not a valid chemical state.

Decision: Build a safeguarded scalar equilibrium solver.

4. Coupled phase and reaction equilibria

Reactive absorption and synthesis processes can involve gas–liquid distribution, reactions and ionic speciation simultaneously.

Useful calculation: At equilibrium satisfy component balances, elemental balances, μ equality and reaction Δ_rG=0 for each independent reaction.

Physical interpretation: CO₂ absorption in alkaline water is not captured by a single nonreactive gas-solubility coefficient because dissolved speciation affects the equilibrium.

Failure to watch: A unique set of independently specified reactions is required; redundant equilibrium constraints may create singular numerical systems.

Decision: Plan a staged model hierarchy from ideal screening to validated software.

Laboratory 13: Rachford–Rice binary flash

Model: Σ zi(Ki−1)/(1+β(Ki−1))=0

Controllable variables: Feed mole fraction A (fraction) and Equilibrium ratio K_A (dimensionless).

Governing relation

Σ zi(Ki−1)/(1+β(Ki−1))=0. This relation is used for an educational screening calculation, not certified physical-property or process-safety design. Every output must be checked against dimensional consistency, equilibrium assumptions and an independent reference when applied to real chemicals.

Stated limitations

Fixed K_B=0.4 and fixed T,P; endpoint flags identify single-phase states.
