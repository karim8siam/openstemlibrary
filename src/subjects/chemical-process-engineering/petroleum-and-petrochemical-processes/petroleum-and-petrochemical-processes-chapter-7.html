§7.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Refinery Energy, Hydrogen Integration and Flare Networks

[Book navigation](../index.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-07.html)

Unit target

Close resource balances across processing units, energy networks, utilities and emissions boundaries.

Engineering context

Refinery optimization crosses unit limits. A proposed yield increase changes the hydrogen, steam, cooling, wastewater, fuel-gas and carbon balances. The right system boundary makes waste heat and off-gas visible; the wrong one can move impacts from one accounting line to another. The modeler should decide what constitutes a material stream, what was measured directly, and which quantities follow from an equation. Whenever possible, start with an inlet-and-outlet balance in physical units rather than with an index or a normalized graph. An index can be useful for a classroom sensitivity exercise, but it can also conceal the absolute process magnitude needed to estimate utilities, storage or emissions.

Decision boundary

Distinguish gross from net energy, mass from purity of hydrogen, and direct process emissions from downstream life-cycle emissions. A good recommendation distinguishes what is established by conservation from what depends on kinetic, thermodynamic or operating assumptions. Record your data source and test method. Identify unknown parameters before asserting any performance. If two alternatives seem very close numerically, compute the range of results that plausible uncertainty could produce rather than picking the one with more decimals.

Technical study sequence

  1. [Hydrogen generation, purification and allocation](../lessons/unit-07-lesson-01.html) — [interactive lab 19](../labs/lab-19.html)
  2. [Heat recovery and exchanger networks](../lessons/unit-07-lesson-02.html) — [interactive lab 20](../labs/lab-20.html)
  3. [Steam, water and power utility balances](../lessons/unit-07-lesson-03.html) — [interactive lab 21](../labs/lab-21.html)
  4. [Direct emissions and carbon inventories](../lessons/unit-07-lesson-04.html) — [interactive lab 21](../labs/lab-21.html)

Laboratory strategy

These three distinct models illuminate different physics or accounting relationships in the unit: Hydrogen availability network, Heat exchanger effectiveness, Fuel-carbon direct emissions. Take each model as a conditional hypothesis. For one model, reconstruct the default answer from the equation; for a second, find a limiting case; for the third, propose a measurement that could disprove it. A smooth trend is not an indicator of industrial accuracy, and sensitivity does not establish causality when the underlying variables are correlated.

Deliverable and independent scrutiny

Prepare a two-page engineering memo with a material and energy boundary, one calculation table, at least two uncertainty or failure scenarios, and a decision that names the data still needed. Use the [16 question assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) to identify technical gaps. The [applied project](../projects/unit-07.html) then adds constraints from adjacent units so that a local process improvement can be evaluated for its plant-wide consequence.

§7.2 Engineering Topic 7.2: Unit 07 · Lesson 01 — Hydrogen generation, purification and allocation

Unit 07 · Lesson 01 — Hydrogen generation, purification and allocation

[Unit map](../units/unit-07.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-19.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-07.html)

The engineering problem

Hydrogen is produced by reforming and purpose-built plants, then purified, compressed and distributed to consuming reactors. Different streams carry different purity, pressure and contaminant loads. The network optimization problem must account for both mass and quality, not only gross supply.

The practical starting point is not the equation by itself; it is a description of the process boundary. Write down the feed, product, recovered co-product, waste and possible accumulation terms before choosing an indicator. In this lesson, the title hydrogen generation, purification and allocation indicates a specific decision lens within plant integration. Its purpose is to connect a measured or assumed characteristic to an operational consequence with a statement of what would prove the link. Separate primary evidence, indirect proxy and calculated prediction; those three categories are not interchangeable.

Quantity definition and model reasoning

Available hydrogen mass = Σ(Fi yi), where Fi is a total stream mass flow and yi is its hydrogen mass fraction. A simple demand deficit is max(0, D−available). Pressure compatibility and impurity requirements introduce additional constraints beyond this scalar arithmetic.

To audit a model, begin with units. Write every mass flow on a common basis such as kg/h, every species flow on a consistent molar basis, and every energy rate as kW or MJ/h. Converting a component fraction from molar to mass basis requires component molar masses. A dimensionless index can be compared across scenarios only if the reference conditions and denominator have not changed. When the result is a percentage, specify percentage of what: total feed mass, converted feed, recovered product, sulfur atoms, or an observed time interval. The wrong denominator can change the interpretation even when the arithmetic is correct.

One useful way to check the numerical relationship is to inspect its limiting behavior. At zero conversion or removal, no product can appear or disappear through that mechanism; at complete removal, the tracked contaminant should be accounted for in another stream; at a cutpoint moved above or below the stated assay range, extrapolation should be rejected. A monotonic curve may be mathematically well behaved yet physically incomplete if secondary reactions, equilibrium or equipment capacity create a turning point outside the model.

Worked investigation: hypothetical data

A 1000 kg/h stream is 80 mass% H₂ and a 500 kg/h stream is 50 mass% H₂. Compare the total contained H₂ with demand of 1200 kg/h, then state why direct connection may still be infeasible.

Worked answer and interpretation

Contained H₂ in stream 1 is 1000×0.80=800 kg/h, and in stream 2 it is 500×0.50=250 kg/h. Total contained H₂ is 1050 kg/h. Against demand 1200 kg/h, the scalar deficit is 150 kg/h. The purity and pressure requirements at individual sinks can prevent the apparent 1050 kg/h from being used directly.

Make a compact worksheet with four rows: starting assumptions, symbolic equation, numerical substitution and mass/energy/quality consistency check. Always place the units in the substitution row. An answer with four significant figures may be mathematically reproducible but should not be presented as a plant forecast when the assumed feed is only known roughly. Repeat the worksheet with one changed input, quantify the absolute and percentage change in the output, and ask which upstream measurement would be needed to defend that perturbation.

For numerical work, explicitly mark the inputs in this lesson as synthetic instructional values, including any yield factor, catalyst rate, representative boiling temperature or solvent efficiency. They must not be used as operating limits. If you wish to compare with published performance, define an independent, dated and source-specific dataset and evaluate whether its units and stream definitions match those of the exercise.

Plant integration and operating evidence

Build a plant-wide table of sources, sinks, purges and compressor requirements; use conservative purity envelopes. Compare recovery upgrades with make-up production using utility and equipment constraints.

Place the result in a real flowsheet context. A network model needs flow, quality, pressure, temperature and availability. A nominal surplus of material or energy can be unusable because of incompatible state conditions. Audit gross and net flows and avoid double counting of an exchanged stream. Ask where the upstream stream comes from, which downstream unit receives it, and how a change would shift at least one other required resource. A product-quality improvement might raise utility demand; more extensive contaminant removal might increase solvent regeneration; higher conversion may move load to gas compression or wastewater treatment. Draw the additional arrows on the block flow diagram, and do not claim that the local calculation has optimized the full plant.

For an evidence-based evaluation, design a measurement table with columns for variable, unit, sample or instrument location, time basis, quality method, expected repeatability and action on discrepancy. Include one direct process variable, one product property and one integration variable. A useful model-validation report should show residuals or errors for several cases, not only agreement at the nominal case used to choose parameters. Record potential confounding changes in feed, temperature, operator procedure and catalyst condition.

Assumption challenge and failure diagnosis

Treating all hydrogen in low-purity gas as immediately usable can be wrong. Compression and purification can reduce net benefit or make a nominal surplus unusable.

A critical reader should ask which implicit assumptions were required to write the equation: steady state or dynamic accumulation, ideal mixing or plug flow, fixed or changing properties, homogeneous or multiphase composition, constant versus variable selectivity, and independence versus coupling of disturbances. Pick the assumptions that matter here. If a model fails, identify whether the defect originates in incorrect units, an invalid physical assumption, unmeasured variability, or numerical implementation. Merely adding complexity does not fix poor calibration data.

In practical terms, define a stop condition for inference: a measurement outside the sampled composition range, evidence of a new phase, an unexpected hazard, a negative concentration, or an implausible material imbalance invalidates the shortcut. Escalate to a validated model or an expert review before making a design or safety claim. The appropriate corrective action can be to collect data, not to alter equipment.

§7.3 Engineering Topic 7.3: Unit 07 · Lesson 02 — Heat recovery and exchanger networks

Unit 07 · Lesson 02 — Heat recovery and exchanger networks

[Unit map](../units/unit-07.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-20.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-07.html)

The engineering problem

Heat integration reduces utility consumption by transferring heat between compatible process streams. Maximum heat recovery depends on supply and target temperatures, stream heat-capacity flow rates, minimum approach temperature, fouling and heat exchanger area. A larger exchanger does not remove all temperature-approach constraints.

The practical starting point is not the equation by itself; it is a description of the process boundary. Write down the feed, product, recovered co-product, waste and possible accumulation terms before choosing an indicator. In this lesson, the title heat recovery and exchanger networks indicates a specific decision lens within plant integration. Its purpose is to connect a measured or assumed characteristic to an operational consequence with a statement of what would prove the link. Separate primary evidence, indirect proxy and calculated prediction; those three categories are not interchangeable.

Quantity definition and model reasoning

For constant capacity rates and a countercurrent exchanger, Q = ε Cmin(Th,in−Tc,in), where ε is computed by NTU-effectiveness formulas; NTU = UA/Cmin. In a simplified classroom case Cmin≈Cmax, ε=NTU/(1+NTU).

To audit a model, begin with units. Write every mass flow on a common basis such as kg/h, every species flow on a consistent molar basis, and every energy rate as kW or MJ/h. Converting a component fraction from molar to mass basis requires component molar masses. Worked investigation: hypothetical data

With Cmin=Cmax=2 kW/K, inlet difference 100 K and UA=4 kW/K, compute NTU, ε and Q. Consider why increasing UA has diminishing marginal benefit.

Worked answer and interpretation

With equal heat-capacity rates C=2 kW/K and UA=4 kW/K, NTU=2 and ε=2/(1+2)=2/3. Predicted transfer is Q=(2/3)(2)(100)=133.33 kW, less than the maximum 200 kW. Extra area increases duty with diminishing marginal gain; this does not account for a complete pinch network.

Unit 07 · Lesson 03 — Steam, water and power utility balances

[Unit map](../units/unit-07.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-21.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-07.html)

The engineering problem

Utility systems connect furnaces, boilers, cooling, water treatment, compressors and turbines. Fuel-gas balances depend on heating value, boiler efficiency and export or import. Steam network pressure levels matter because energy cannot be transferred without considering the quality or availability of that energy.

§7.5 Engineering Topic 7.5: Unit 07 · Lesson 04 — Direct emissions and carbon inventories

Unit 07 · Lesson 04 — Direct emissions and carbon inventories

[Unit map](../units/unit-07.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-21.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-07.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-07.html)

The engineering problem

Fuels and process chemistry generate direct emissions; refinery inventories also include flares, vents, fugitive sources and imported energy depending on scope. A boundary declaration is essential before comparing plants or interventions. Carbon dioxide from full oxidation of fuel carbon is a stoichiometric calculation, not a total life-cycle analysis.

For fuel mass m, carbon mass fraction wC and oxidized fraction f, emitted CO₂ mass mCO2 = m wC f(44/12). The ratio converts carbon mass to CO₂ mass under the specified chemical assumption. Other greenhouse gases require separate species accounting.

For 10 t/h model fuel at 85% carbon with 99% complete carbon oxidation, calculate CO₂ t/h. Explain which additional sources a plant inventory would include.

Unit 07 applied project — Plant integration

Commissioning question

A fictional integrated refinery-and-petrochemicals teaching facility is reviewing its plant integration decisions after receiving evidence that the assumed nominal feed or operating condition is no longer reliable. Your task is to produce an engineering screening memo, not an equipment datasheet or operating instruction. The central problem is: Close resource balances across processing units, energy networks, utilities and emissions boundaries.

Refinery optimization crosses unit limits. A proposed yield increase changes the hydrogen, steam, cooling, wastewater, fuel-gas and carbon balances. The right system boundary makes waste heat and off-gas visible; the wrong one can move impacts from one accounting line to another.

Synthetic case boundary

Start from a nominal notional feed of 1,000 mass units per day for a single material balance; choose its actual chemical meaning and reporting basis before calculations. Allocate an assumed 10% uncertainty to the most sensitive unmeasured characteristic. Do not silently convert the same number into tonnes, cubic metres, kmol and standard gas volume. If a composition is needed, invent and label a test vector whose fractions sum exactly to unity. Keep the upstream and downstream units visible in a block flow diagram.

For this unit, the four required technical workstreams are Hydrogen generation, purification and allocation, Heat recovery and exchanger networks, Steam, water and power utility balances, Direct emissions and carbon inventories. Use the lesson descriptions to select what evidence exists and what has to be assumed. If your assumed inputs are internally inconsistent—for example, a product yield above converted material, more recovered hydrogen than contained hydrogen, or a heat exchanger duty exceeding its thermodynamic ceiling—correct the conceptual error rather than tuning an output.

Computational experiment

Run three independent unit laboratories: [lab 19: Hydrogen availability network](../labs/lab-19.html), [lab 20: Heat exchanger effectiveness](../labs/lab-20.html), [lab 21: Fuel-carbon direct emissions](../labs/lab-21.html). For each lab, define a base case, one credible perturbation and a stress case near the end of the control range. Record the JSON model identifier, all parameter values, model output, units and qualitative interpretation. Export CSV. Reconstruct at least two displayed values with an independent worksheet or script, and plot both the baseline and a sensitivity trajectory.

Then write an uncertainty section: distinguish numerical precision, parameter uncertainty, structural model uncertainty and sampling uncertainty. An exact identity can be extremely precise but may be irrelevant to a poorly sampled feed. Conversely, a fitted kinetic coefficient can capture a short operating period and fail when catalyst activity changes. Explain which uncertainties could reverse the ranking of proposed alternatives.

Whole-plant coordination

Distinguish gross from net energy, mass from purity of hydrogen, and direct process emissions from downstream life-cycle emissions. Name at least one impact on a different department: sulfur or wastewater handling, hydrogen consumption, off-gas, steam and power, product specifications, instrumentation, emergency response or carbon accounting. A nominally favorable local change is not a positive overall result until its resource and product interactions are reconciled. Identify an affected stakeholder who owns the required measurement or approval.

Deliverables and scoring

1. Boundary diagram and data dictionary (20%): readable stream labels, units, basis, data provenance and calculation assumptions.

2. Calculation appendix (25%): auditable mass or energy balance, three lab experiments and independent numerical recomputation.

3. Validation design (20%): at least two measurements, instrument/assay location, measurement frequency and acceptance criteria.

4. Trade-off memorandum (20%): alternative scenarios, constraints, uncertainty range and a reasoned but conditional comparison.

5. Responsible practice (15%): safety and environmental exclusions, uncertainty about regulations, and an explicit statement that the work is not plant-commissioning advice.

Challenge extension

Suppose the feed characteristic that controls the main model suddenly moves beyond the laboratory range. Write a plan for collecting the minimum data that would permit responsible recalibration without risking an unsupported process change. Your memo should explain why extrapolation is not a substitute for evidence and should name a decision point where engineering review is mandatory. The strongest result may be a refusal to infer a safe or profitable configuration from insufficient information.
