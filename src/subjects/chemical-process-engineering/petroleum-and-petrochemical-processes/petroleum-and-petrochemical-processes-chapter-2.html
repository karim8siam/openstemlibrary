§2.1 Foundational Principles & Theory: Atmospheric and Vacuum Crude Distillation Units

[Book navigation](../index.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-02.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-02.html)

Unit target

Connect physical pretreatment and boiling-range separation to real material and energy boundaries.

Engineering context

Atmospheric and vacuum distillation split a feed according to vapor–liquid behavior and side-draw operations. A crude tower cannot create molecules; the yields of distillate and residue must reconcile with feed, slop, losses, and internal reflux. Utility estimates need heat-capacity and heat-integration assumptions. The modeler should decide what constitutes a material stream, what was measured directly, and which quantities follow from an equation. Whenever possible, start with an inlet-and-outlet balance in physical units rather than with an index or a normalized graph. An index can be useful for a classroom sensitivity exercise, but it can also conceal the absolute process magnitude needed to estimate utilities, storage or emissions.

Decision boundary

Identify what can be calculated from a TBP surrogate and what would require tray hydraulics, assays, vapor–liquid data and licensed column models. A good recommendation distinguishes what is established by conservation from what depends on kinetic, thermodynamic or operating assumptions. Record your data source and test method. Identify unknown parameters before asserting any performance. If two alternatives seem very close numerically, compute the range of results that plausible uncertainty could produce rather than picking the one with more decimals.

Technical study sequence

  1. [Crude desalting mass balances](../lessons/unit-02-lesson-01.html) — [interactive lab 04](../labs/lab-04.html)
  2. [Atmospheric crude distillation](../lessons/unit-02-lesson-02.html) — [interactive lab 05](../labs/lab-05.html)
  3. [Vacuum distillation and thermal constraints](../lessons/unit-02-lesson-03.html) — [interactive lab 06](../labs/lab-06.html)
  4. [Stabilization and light-end handling](../lessons/unit-02-lesson-04.html) — [interactive lab 06](../labs/lab-06.html)

Laboratory strategy

These three distinct models illuminate different physics or accounting relationships in the unit: Two-stage desalter, Atmospheric cut sensitivity, Vacuum boiling temperature. Take each model as a conditional hypothesis. For one model, reconstruct the default answer from the equation; for a second, find a limiting case; for the third, propose a measurement that could disprove it. A smooth trend is not an indicator of industrial accuracy, and sensitivity does not establish causality when the underlying variables are correlated.

Deliverable and independent scrutiny

Prepare a two-page engineering memo with a material and energy boundary, one calculation table, at least two uncertainty or failure scenarios, and a decision that names the data still needed. Use the [16 question assessment](../assessments/unit-02.html) to identify technical gaps. The [applied project](../projects/unit-02.html) then adds constraints from adjacent units so that a local process improvement can be evaluated for its plant-wide consequence.

§2.2 Engineering Topic 2.2: Unit 02 · Lesson 01 — Crude desalting mass balances

Unit 02 · Lesson 01 — Crude desalting mass balances

[Unit map](../units/unit-02.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-04.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-02.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-02.html)

The engineering problem

Desalting mixes water with crude to transfer water-soluble salts into the aqueous phase, then separates brine. Actual performance depends on mixing, wash-water quality, electrostatic coalescence, residence time, emulsion stability and water carryover. Oil contamination of the brine and residual water in the desalted crude are separate outcomes from salt removal.

The practical starting point is not the equation by itself; it is a description of the process boundary. Write down the feed, product, recovered co-product, waste and possible accumulation terms before choosing an indicator. In this lesson, the title crude desalting mass balances indicates a specific decision lens within primary refining. Its purpose is to connect a measured or assumed characteristic to an operational consequence with a statement of what would prove the link. Separate primary evidence, indirect proxy and calculated prediction; those three categories are not interchangeable.

Quantity definition and model reasoning

For a simplified stage, C_out = C_in(1 − η) on a consistent ppmw basis when removal fraction η is specified. Two independent stages give C2 = C0(1 − η1)(1 − η2). Salt leaving with brine equals salt entering minus salt leaving with crude, neglecting additional salt in wash water.

To audit a model, begin with units. Write every mass flow on a common basis such as kg/h, every species flow on a consistent molar basis, and every energy rate as kW or MJ/h. Converting a component fraction from molar to mass basis requires component molar masses. A dimensionless index can be compared across scenarios only if the reference conditions and denominator have not changed. When the result is a percentage, specify percentage of what: total feed mass, converted feed, recovered product, sulfur atoms, or an observed time interval. The wrong denominator can change the interpretation even when the arithmetic is correct.

One useful way to check the numerical relationship is to inspect its limiting behavior. At zero conversion or removal, no product can appear or disappear through that mechanism; at complete removal, the tracked contaminant should be accounted for in another stream; at a cutpoint moved above or below the stated assay range, extrapolation should be rejected. A monotonic curve may be mathematically well behaved yet physically incomplete if secondary reactions, equilibrium or equipment capacity create a turning point outside the model.

Worked investigation: hypothetical data

For 50 ppmw salt entering two model stages with 60% and 65% nominal removal, estimate the outlet concentration and the removal per stage. Explain what wash-water salt would do to the answer.

Worked answer and interpretation

Stage 1 leaves 50(1−0.60)=20 ppmw. Stage 2 leaves 20(1−0.65)=7 ppmw, an overall 86% removal under the independent ideal-stage assumption. A paired inlet and outlet salt analysis can test the apparent effectiveness, but the result alone does not bound residual water or chloride hydrolysis risk.

Make a compact worksheet with four rows: starting assumptions, symbolic equation, numerical substitution and mass/energy/quality consistency check. Always place the units in the substitution row. An answer with four significant figures may be mathematically reproducible but should not be presented as a plant forecast when the assumed feed is only known roughly. Repeat the worksheet with one changed input, quantify the absolute and percentage change in the output, and ask which upstream measurement would be needed to defend that perturbation.

For numerical work, explicitly mark the inputs in this lesson as synthetic instructional values, including any yield factor, catalyst rate, representative boiling temperature or solvent efficiency. They must not be used as operating limits. If you wish to compare with published performance, define an independent, dated and source-specific dataset and evaluate whether its units and stream definitions match those of the exercise.

Plant integration and operating evidence

Account for incoming salt, injected wash-water salt, brine disposal, chemical use and oily wastewater. Validate stage efficiencies with paired inlet/outlet samples; vary mixing and emulsion treatment cautiously in accordance with site procedures.

Place the result in a real flowsheet context. Physical separation concentrates rather than destroys impurities. The simplifying assumption of sharp cuts can help a student audit material balances, but commercial columns show overlaps and side-stream contamination. Include inventories, side draws, overhead systems and energy integration when interpreting performance. Ask where the upstream stream comes from, which downstream unit receives it, and how a change would shift at least one other required resource. A product-quality improvement might raise utility demand; more extensive contaminant removal might increase solvent regeneration; higher conversion may move load to gas compression or wastewater treatment. Draw the additional arrows on the block flow diagram, and do not claim that the local calculation has optimized the full plant.

For an evidence-based evaluation, design a measurement table with columns for variable, unit, sample or instrument location, time basis, quality method, expected repeatability and action on discrepancy. Include one direct process variable, one product property and one integration variable. A useful model-validation report should show residuals or errors for several cases, not only agreement at the nominal case used to choose parameters. Record potential confounding changes in feed, temperature, operator procedure and catalyst condition.

Assumption challenge and failure diagnosis

Perfect salt-transfer or separation assumptions can hide difficult emulsions. A successful salt mass balance does not prove that hydrochloric acid will not form later in the process.

A critical reader should ask which implicit assumptions were required to write the equation: steady state or dynamic accumulation, ideal mixing or plug flow, fixed or changing properties, homogeneous or multiphase composition, constant versus variable selectivity, and independence versus coupling of disturbances. Pick the assumptions that matter here. If a model fails, identify whether the defect originates in incorrect units, an invalid physical assumption, unmeasured variability, or numerical implementation. Merely adding complexity does not fix poor calibration data.

In practical terms, define a stop condition for inference: a measurement outside the sampled composition range, evidence of a new phase, an unexpected hazard, a negative concentration, or an implausible material imbalance invalidates the shortcut. Escalate to a validated model or an expert review before making a design or safety claim. The appropriate corrective action can be to collect data, not to alter equipment.

§2.3 Engineering Topic 2.3: Unit 02 · Lesson 02 — Atmospheric crude distillation

Unit 02 · Lesson 02 — Atmospheric crude distillation

[Unit map](../units/unit-02.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-05.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-02.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-02.html)

The engineering problem

The atmospheric crude unit takes heated, desalted feed into a flash region and fractionation system. Internal reflux and steam stripping influence cut separation, while side strippers help remove lighter constituents from distillates. Pumparounds recover heat and shape tower internal flows. Each product draw has a rate, composition and specification constraint that cannot be deduced from a single set of theoretical cutpoints.

The practical starting point is not the equation by itself; it is a description of the process boundary. Write down the feed, product, recovered co-product, waste and possible accumulation terms before choosing an indicator. In this lesson, the title atmospheric crude distillation indicates a specific decision lens within primary refining. Its purpose is to connect a measured or assumed characteristic to an operational consequence with a statement of what would prove the link. Separate primary evidence, indirect proxy and calculated prediction; those three categories are not interchangeable.

Quantity definition and model reasoning

A material balance is F = ΣPi + losses + measured inventory change on a common time basis. With cumulative assay F(T), a first cut fraction may be estimated by differences at cut temperatures. The real enthalpy balance also requires feed and product enthalpies, furnace duty, pumparound removal and reflux.

Worked answer and interpretation

§2.4 Engineering Topic 2.4: Unit 02 · Lesson 03 — Vacuum distillation and thermal constraints

Unit 02 · Lesson 03 — Vacuum distillation and thermal constraints

[Unit map](../units/unit-02.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-06.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-02.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-02.html)

The engineering problem

Vacuum operation lowers the required boiling temperature for a given heavy pseudo-component. This permits recovery of vacuum gas oil while limiting severe thermal exposure of heavier material. Vacuum systems involve ejectors or pumps, steam, condensers and leakage control; lower pressure does not eliminate residence-time and wall-temperature concerns.

The practical starting point is not the equation by itself; it is a description of the process boundary. Write down the feed, product, recovered co-product, waste and possible accumulation terms before choosing an indicator. In this lesson, the title vacuum distillation and thermal constraints indicates a specific decision lens within primary refining. Its purpose is to connect a measured or assumed characteristic to an operational consequence with a statement of what would prove the link. Separate primary evidence, indirect proxy and calculated prediction; those three categories are not interchangeable.

Quantity definition and model reasoning

For a simplified constant enthalpy vaporization approximation, ln(P/P0) = −ΔHvap/R (1/T − 1/T0). This relation describes one representative pseudo-component over a restricted range; a whole crude fraction needs a distribution of components and credible thermodynamic methods.

Worked investigation: hypothetical data

For a pseudo-component boiling at 640 K near 101 kPa with ΔHvap 40 kJ/mol, estimate the boiling temperature near 15 kPa. Compare with the atmospheric reference temperature.

Worked answer and interpretation

For pressure 15 kPa, ln(15/101)=−1.907; with ΔHvap=40,000 J/mol, 1/T=1/640−(8.314/40000)(−1.907)=0.001959 K⁻¹, so T≈510.5 K. The lower boiling temperature illustrates the vacuum principle but is not a furnace or column thermal-design prediction.

§2.5 Engineering Topic 2.5: Unit 02 · Lesson 04 — Stabilization and light-end handling

Unit 02 · Lesson 04 — Stabilization and light-end handling

[Unit map](../units/unit-02.html) · [Open numerical laboratory](../labs/lab-06.html) · [Assessment](../assessments/unit-02.html) · [Applied project](../projects/unit-02.html)

The engineering problem

Stabilization removes light volatile compounds from a hydrocarbon liquid to improve downstream handling and product requirements. Naphtha stabilization, LPG fractionation and gas recovery rely on multicomponent phase equilibrium and pressure-dependent separation. The overhead stream must be accounted for as a product or fuel component, never as a disappearance.

The practical starting point is not the equation by itself; it is a description of the process boundary. Write down the feed, product, recovered co-product, waste and possible accumulation terms before choosing an indicator. In this lesson, the title stabilization and light-end handling indicates a specific decision lens within primary refining. Its purpose is to connect a measured or assumed characteristic to an operational consequence with a statement of what would prove the link. Separate primary evidence, indirect proxy and calculated prediction; those three categories are not interchangeable.

Quantity definition and model reasoning

For a simplified binary flash, a component split depends on K values, feed composition and vapor fraction. Rachford–Rice expresses Σzi(Ki−1)/(1+β(Ki−1))=0, with admissible vapor fraction β between zero and one when a two-phase region exists.

Worked investigation: hypothetical data

A 100 kmol/h synthetic liquid contains 5 mol% lumped light ends. If a model removes 80% of those light ends to overhead, compute the overhead light-end molar rate and the residual in liquid, before considering entrained heavy material.

Worked answer and interpretation

The feed has 100×0.05=5 kmol/h notional light ends. At 80% transfer, overhead receives 4 kmol/h light ends and the liquid retains 1 kmol/h. Total overhead flow cannot be deduced without knowing how much heavier material also enters the vapor; claiming the overhead total is exactly 4 kmol/h would be an unjustified assumption.

In practical terms, define a stop condition for inference: a measurement outside the sampled composition range, evidence of a new phase, an unexpected hazard, a negative concentration, or an implausible material imbalance invalidates the shortcut. Escalate to a validated model or an expert review before making a design or safety claim. The appropriate corrective action can be to collect data, not to alter equipment.
