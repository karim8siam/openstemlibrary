§2.3 Physical and chemical indicators

2.1 Physical and chemical indicators

Turbidity is an optical scattering measurement; it is not a direct mass concentration of microorganisms or sediment. Conductivity is an indicator of dissolved ions, whereas total dissolved solids depend on ion composition and the conversion method used. Dissolved oxygen responds to temperature, salinity, photosynthesis, respiration and reaeration. A parameter can be a useful alarm without being a direct measure of health risk.

Field readings require instrument verification, temperature compensation and sample context. A grab sample cannot represent a highly variable storm hydrograph unless a justification is provided. If two laboratories disagree, evaluate filtration, preservation, holding time, blank contamination and analytical method before averaging their values.

Governing relationship and units

Mass load (kg/day)=Q(m³/day)×C(mg/L)×0.001.

Worked context

A stream flow of 2,500 m³/day at 18 mg/L suspended solids carries 45 kg/day. If the discharge doubles while concentration remains constant, the total delivered load doubles even though the laboratory result looks unchanged.

§2.4 Oxygen demand and nutrient cycling

2.2 Oxygen demand and nutrient cycling

Biochemical oxygen demand estimates oxygen consumed by microbial oxidation under specified test conditions. Chemical oxygen demand captures a different set of oxidizable substances and generally cannot be converted to BOD using a universal ratio. Nitrification consumes oxygen; phosphorus and nitrogen can accelerate downstream growth when light and ecological conditions permit. Nutrient status is governed by limitation and biological uptake, not concentration alone.

Interpret BOD, COD, ammonia, nitrate and orthophosphate as related but distinct state variables. A measured five-day BOD is not necessarily ultimate demand. In a river reach, deoxygenation competes with reaeration; nighttime respiration and daylight photosynthesis can produce strong daily cycles that a noon grab sample misses.

Governing relationship and units

dD/dt = k_d L(t) − k_r D(t), where D is oxygen deficit.

Worked context

At 20°C, a river with DO saturation near 9 mg/L and measured DO 5 mg/L has a deficit of about 4 mg/L. The saturation value changes with local conditions; a simplified demonstration must not be used as an ecological compliance assessment.

§2.5 Microbial quality and sampling designs

2.3 Microbial quality and sampling designs

Indicator organisms such as E. coli can signal fecal contamination but do not measure every pathogen or infective dose. A sample with no detected organisms is not evidence that the supply is sterile. The probability of missing a rare contamination event depends on sample size, timing and detection limits. Chlorine residual alone is also insufficient evidence of microbiological safety.

Separate the sampling objective into surveillance, process control, incident response and source characterization. For a distribution network, choose points representing storage, hydraulic age, pressure disturbances and vulnerable endpoints. Include field blanks, duplicate samples, chain-of-custody records and immediate response criteria agreed with the responsible authority.

Governing relationship and units

Percent positive = positive samples / total valid samples ×100%.

Worked context

A surveillance plan with 2 positive results among 40 valid tests reports 5% positive, but the uncertainty is large. If the two positives followed a pipe repair, investigating pressure loss is more useful than treating all samples as independent random observations.

§2.6 Hydrochemistry and transport in receiving waters

2.4 Hydrochemistry and transport in receiving waters

pH controls carbonate equilibria, metal solubility and the proportion of ammonia present as un-ionized NH₃. Alkalinity describes acid-neutralizing capacity, not simply the pH value. Mixing of two different-quality streams requires flow-weighted concentrations when the constituent behaves conservatively, while reactions and settling demand additional terms.

Record whether concentrations are expressed as an element, ion or compound and track equivalents when working with acid–base balance. For a mixing zone, calculate both flows on a consistent time basis and evaluate whether complete mixing is physically plausible at the location where a compliance sample is collected.

Governing relationship and units

C_mix=(Q₁C₁+Q₂C₂)/(Q₁+Q₂).

Worked context

A river at 3 m³/s and 2 mg/L conservatively mixes with an effluent at 0.5 m³/s and 14 mg/L to give about 3.71 mg/L after complete mixing. The concentration close to the outfall may be higher because lateral dispersion takes time.
