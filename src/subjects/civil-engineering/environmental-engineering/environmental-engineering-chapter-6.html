§6.3 Waste characterization and collection systems

6.1 Waste characterization and collection systems

Waste composition varies with location, season, income, packaging and collection system. A municipal mass estimate that excludes informal recovery or uncollected material is not a complete generation inventory. Bulk density affects truck capacity, storage needs and landfill airspace, while wet organic fraction strongly influences odour and leachate potential.

Carry out a stratified composition study rather than extrapolating one market-day sample to a whole city. Measure wet and dry masses as appropriate, account for sorting losses and contamination, and distinguish generation, collection and disposal. Route design should consider vehicle accessibility, transfer points, worker safety, fuel use and the reliability of collection schedules.

Governing relationship and units

Annual generated mass = population × per-capita generation ×365, with consistent kg/person/day units.

Worked context

A population of 180,000 at 0.55 kg/person/day generates about 99 tonnes/day. A collection rate of 75% leaves roughly 24.75 tonnes/day outside formal collection, not necessarily inside a controlled disposal site.

§6.4 Recycling quality and materials recovery

6.2 Recycling quality and materials recovery

The presence of recyclable material in mixed waste does not establish an economically viable recovered product. Capture efficiency, contamination, sorting purity, demand, transport distance and occupational safety determine outcomes. Source separation can improve quality but depends on participation, reliable separate collection and actual processing capacity.

Track input mass, rejected contamination, recovered saleable mass and final destination for each material stream. A high facility diversion claim may be inflated if rejected bales later go to landfill. Compare avoided virgin-material production with sorting, washing and transport impacts using an explicit functional unit and locality-specific emissions data.

Governing relationship and units

Recovered mass = incoming mass × target fraction × capture rate × processing yield.

Worked context

A 100 t/day stream containing 18% recoverable plastic at 70% capture and 85% processing yield produces about 10.71 t/day of recovered plastic, before any later product losses.

§6.5 Organics treatment and landfill dynamics

6.3 Organics treatment and landfill dynamics

Composting uses managed aerobic decomposition to stabilize organics when moisture, oxygen, porosity and carbon-to-nitrogen balance are suitable. Anaerobic digestion produces biogas in the absence of oxygen but requires feedstock control and digestate management. Landfills produce leachate and gas whose composition and timing depend on waste age, moisture, composition and containment.

A first-order landfill methane model is a screening inventory model; landfill-cover oxidation, collection efficiency, site climate and historical disposal alter actual releases. Decisions about composting and digestion should consider contamination by glass, plastics or metals and whether the end product has a viable, safe use.

Governing relationship and units

Illustrative gas generation G(t)=k L₀ M exp(−kt), with unit-consistent mass and gas potential.

Worked context

A legacy deposit can continue producing gas decades after placement. Doubling the assumed first-order decay coefficient increases initial predicted generation but does not double integrated lifetime potential.

§6.6 Landfill engineering and governance

6.4 Landfill engineering and governance

An engineered landfill needs a defensible site, drainage control, containment, leachate collection, gas management, stable slopes, operational cover and monitoring. A liner does not eliminate long-term risk; leak detection, maintenance, groundwater surveillance and financial provisions matter. Unregulated dumping often transfers environmental cost to neighbours and water resources rather than eliminating it.

Compare disposal sites by geological and hydrogeological context, flood exposure, access, leachate treatment, settlement, gas generation and land-use conflict. Build a closure and post-closure plan early. Avoid design-level conclusions from simple landfill volume or gas estimates; actual capacity and stability require geotechnical studies and local regulations.

Governing relationship and units

Nominal airspace volume = waste mass / achieved in-place density; add cover and operational allowances separately.

Worked context

If 50,000 tonnes of compacted waste average 0.8 t/m³, nominal waste airspace is 62,500 m³ before cover, settlement and buffer volumes. A design that ignores cover could exhaust usable space early.
