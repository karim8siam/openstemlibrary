Structural Dynamics and Vibration

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and design validation of Structural Dynamics and Vibration. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Structural Analysis II study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§7.1 Conceptual overview

Conceptual overview

Dynamic analysis accounts for mass, damping and time-dependent forcing, rather than assuming that every load acts infinitely slowly. Two frames with similar static stiffness can have different natural periods if their mass distributions differ. A two-degree-of-freedom shear building is a compact way to see modal shapes, modal participation and frequency separation. The governing equation in linear structural dynamics is M ü + C u̇ + K u = p(t). Units of acceleration and force must be consistent. Modal analysis solves a generalized eigenproblem using both M and K. Time integration introduces numerical approximations, so the time step and integration method must be recorded alongside the physical assumptions.

§7.2 Learning outcomes

Learning outcomes

After working through this unit you should be able to: (1) construct a physically consistent idealization, (2) express the relevant force, stiffness and displacement variables using compatible units, (3) solve a demonstrative problem by hand or a transparent numerical method, (4) interpret response patterns and their sensitivity to input parameters, and (5) identify what the simplified model cannot prove. In this unit the most important observation is not merely a numerical answer but the connection between structural behavior and the assumed support or constitutive conditions.

§7.3 Unit engineering case — Office-floor vibration

Unit engineering case — Office-floor vibration

A two-floor idealized shear structure has different floor masses and lateral story stiffnesses. Compute its two modal frequencies, explain mode shape normalization and compare a time-history forced vibration of a single ideal mode. Differentiate displacement amplitudes from acceleration amplitudes when discussing human comfort. A clean classroom oscillation does not establish performance under real wind, occupant walking or earthquakes without realistic forcing spectra and structural damping.

Investigation sequence

Begin by preparing a diagram that names every member, support, load, imposed movement and unknown. Choose an independent coordinate system and assign a sign to every generalized force. Derive a minimal reference calculation before using a computational model. Then run a sensitivity study in which only one physical assumption changes at a time. Record both the predicted movement and the reaction or internal-force counterpart. Check conservation of force and moment where the physical model supports those checks. Finally compare the calculation with a plausible measurement or with a more complete independent model. Label the evidence as a verification benchmark, an assumption or a genuinely observed fact.

Suggested written report

A useful report has a one-paragraph engineering question, a table of parameter values with units, an explicit statement of boundary conditions, the governing equations, a readable figure of the system, a table of numerical results, at least one independent verification, and a conclusion that identifies unverified assumptions. State what happens when the governing input doubles, when stiffness increases, and when a support is relaxed or stiffened. Explain whether the observed changes follow from algebraic scaling or from a change in load path. Keep the original units in the file even if a graph displays a converted unit.

§7.4 Mass and stiffness idealization

7.1 Mass and stiffness idealization

Learning target and physical interpretation

A structural lumped-mass representation allocates inertia to chosen degrees of freedom. Story stiffness models summarize frame resistance but do not automatically capture rotational inertia, shear lag or flexible diaphragms.

Governing relation

For two lateral floor DOFs, M=diag(m1,m2), K=[[k1+k2,−k2],[−k2,k2]].

Worked engineering interpretation

A 50 tonne first floor and 35 tonne second floor generate very different inertia reactions under the same acceleration even if their story stiffnesses are equal.

What the idealization omits

Replacing tonnes with kilograms without converting stiffness units can shift predicted frequencies by a factor of √1000.

Verification and interpretation of results

Check units for ω²: stiffness (N/m) divided by mass (kg) must yield s⁻².

§7.5 Natural modes and periods

7.2 Natural modes and periods

Learning target and physical interpretation

Set applied force and damping to zero and look for harmonic solutions. The characteristic condition det(K−ω²M)=0 gives squared frequencies; corresponding eigenvectors describe relative movement.

Governing relation

(K−ω_i² M)φ_i=0; natural period T_i=2π/ω_i.

Worked engineering interpretation

If a natural angular frequency is 10 rad/s, the corresponding cyclic frequency is approximately 1.592 Hz and the period 0.628 s.

What the idealization omits

Multiplying an eigenvector by −1 does not create a different physical mode; sign and normalization are arbitrary.

§7.6 Modal superposition and participation

7.3 Modal superposition and participation

Learning target and physical interpretation

Under suitable linear assumptions, a response may be represented as a sum of modal coordinates. The excitation direction determines which modes are strongly excited.

Governing relation

u(t)=Σ φ_i q_i(t); Γ_i=(φ_iᵀ M r)/(φ_iᵀ M φ_i) for an influence vector r.

Worked engineering interpretation

An in-phase first mode often dominates roof displacement under uniform base excitation, but a higher mode can matter for story shear.

What the idealization omits

Summing unsigned modal maxima directly generally overestimates correlated response; spectral combination rules need defined assumptions.

§7.7 Damping and harmonic response

7.4 Damping and harmonic response

Learning target and physical interpretation

Viscous damping is a linear idealization that dissipates energy in proportion to velocity. A sinusoidal load can create resonance-like amplification as excitation approaches natural frequency.

Governing relation

For SDOF: m ü + 2ζmωn u̇ + k u = F sin(ωt), with ωn=√(k/m).

Worked engineering interpretation

With ζ=0.05 and ω≈ωn, the steady-state displacement amplification is approximately 1/(2ζ)=10 relative to F/k.

What the idealization omits

A transient numerical integration starting from rest does not instantly show the asymptotic steady-state amplitude; it contains a free-vibration component.

§7.8 Time integration and numerical stability

7.5 Time integration and numerical stability

Learning target and physical interpretation

Newmark average acceleration is widely used for linear structural dynamics because it is unconditionally stable for ordinary positive-definite linear systems; accuracy still depends on time step.

Governing relation

With β=1/4 and γ=1/2, predictor and corrector equations solve effective stiffness m+γΔt c+βΔt²k for each scalar acceleration update.

Worked engineering interpretation

Reducing the time step from T/30 to T/90 should reduce phase error when resolving a smooth harmonic response.

Unconditional linear stability is not a promise of physical accuracy, nor does it establish stability for arbitrary nonlinear or irregular implementations.

Solved Problem Example 7.1: Worked Problem 7.1: Load Path Resolution & Free-Body Equilibrium

A civil engineering structure is analyzed under Structural Dynamics and Vibration criteria. Calculate support reactions, joint forces, and verify global force-moment equilibrium. Formulate the governing equations, carry out dimensional verification, and verify limiting behavior.

Step 1: Problem Idealization and Known Parameters Identify structural geometry, support fixities, material constitutive laws (modulus $E$, shear modulus $G$), and applied load combinations for Structural Dynamics and Vibration.
