Displacement Methods and Moment Distribution

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and design validation of Displacement Methods and Moment Distribution. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Structural Analysis II study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§2.1 Conceptual overview

Conceptual overview

A displacement method chooses rotations and translations as unknowns and computes restoring member forces from these movements. Instead of releasing redundants, one imagines restraints added to prevent joint movement and then releases them by satisfying force and moment equilibrium. Slope–deflection, moment distribution and matrix stiffness all express the same linear mechanics using different bookkeeping. In a non-sway continuous beam, support vertical displacement may be prescribed, so joint rotations become the principal unknowns. In a sway frame, lateral translations must also be retained. The stiffness coefficient for an Euler–Bernoulli prismatic member is dependent on its far-end condition; a far-end fixed member exhibits rotational stiffness 4EI/L, while a far-end hinged member exhibits 3EI/L. These coefficients do not apply to a short deep beam when shear flexibility is significant.

§2.2 Learning outcomes

Learning outcomes

After working through this unit you should be able to: (1) construct a physically consistent idealization, (2) express the relevant force, stiffness and displacement variables using compatible units, (3) solve a demonstrative problem by hand or a transparent numerical method, (4) interpret response patterns and their sensitivity to input parameters, and (5) identify what the simplified model cannot prove. In this unit the most important observation is not merely a numerical answer but the connection between structural behavior and the assumed support or constitutive conditions.

§2.3 Unit engineering case — Two-span classroom girder

Unit engineering case — Two-span classroom girder

A classroom floor girder crosses one interior column and carries different distributed live loads on each span. Estimate support moments by slope–deflection and by the direct stiffness method. Explain any sign differences from end-action conventions. Test three load patterns: full-span dead load, live load on the left span only and live load on the right span only. For each pattern, record joint equilibrium, total support reaction and a deformation sketch. Use at least two stiffness values to illustrate that under linear, uniformly scaled EI, moment distribution depends more on relative EI than absolute EI.

Investigation sequence

Begin by preparing a diagram that names every member, support, load, imposed movement and unknown. Choose an independent coordinate system and assign a sign to every generalized force. Derive a minimal reference calculation before using a computational model. Then run a sensitivity study in which only one physical assumption changes at a time. Record both the predicted movement and the reaction or internal-force counterpart. Check conservation of force and moment where the physical model supports those checks. Finally compare the calculation with a plausible measurement or with a more complete independent model. Label the evidence as a verification benchmark, an assumption or a genuinely observed fact.

Suggested written report

A useful report has a one-paragraph engineering question, a table of parameter values with units, an explicit statement of boundary conditions, the governing equations, a readable figure of the system, a table of numerical results, at least one independent verification, and a conclusion that identifies unverified assumptions. State what happens when the governing input doubles, when stiffness increases, and when a support is relaxed or stiffened. Explain whether the observed changes follow from algebraic scaling or from a change in load path. Keep the original units in the file even if a graph displays a converted unit.

§2.4 Slope–deflection equations

2.1 Slope–deflection equations

Learning target and physical interpretation

Relate a beam end moment to both end rotations, chord rotation from differential support displacement, and moments fixed by member loading. Use a clearly stated sign convention throughout the calculation.

Governing relation

For a prismatic beam without chord rotation: M_ab= (2EI/L)(2θa+θb)+FEM_ab.

Worked engineering interpretation

A joint between two fixed-ended spans is allowed to rotate. Its unknown θ enters both adjacent member end moments, and ΣMjoint=0 completes the analysis.

What the idealization omits

Copying fixed-end moment signs from a different convention without transforming them can make a physically symmetric loading condition produce asymmetric moments.

Verification and interpretation of results

Solve the joint balance, substitute θ into both member formulas and check equal-and-opposite member action at the joint.

Applied case and decision boundary

§2.5 Moment distribution factors

2.2 Moment distribution factors

Learning target and physical interpretation

Distribute an unbalanced joint moment in proportion to the rotational stiffnesses of connected members. Carry-over transmits part of the correction to a restrained far end.

Governing relation

Distribution factor DF_i=k_i/Σ_j k_j, with ΣDF=1; fixed-far-end k=4EI/L.

Worked engineering interpretation

If members entering a joint have stiffnesses 4 and 6 MN·m/rad, a 50 kN·m balancing action distributes 20 and 30 kN·m with the corrective sign.

What the idealization omits

Using a fixed-end carry-over factor of one-half for a member whose far end is released can corrupt the balancing sequence.

Verification and interpretation of results

At the end of each sweep, record joint residual moments; repeated sweeps should reduce the residual for a stable linear structure.

§2.6 Continuous beam stiffness assembly

2.3 Continuous beam stiffness assembly

Learning target and physical interpretation

A two-node Euler–Bernoulli beam carries transverse translation and rotation at each end. Assemble shared DOFs by summing local matrices, then restrain the supported vertical DOFs while retaining joint rotation.

Governing relation

k_e=(EI/L³)[[12,6L,−12,6L],[6L,4L²,−6L,2L²],[−12,−6L,12,−6L],[6L,2L²,−6L,4L²]].

Worked engineering interpretation

For two spans of 5 m and 7 m, shared vertical and rotation DOFs at the interior support make a six-DOF system before boundary conditions.

What the idealization omits

Treating the interior support as a hinge by accidentally duplicating its rotation eliminates continuity and produces incorrect negative moments.

Verification and interpretation of results

Check matrix symmetry, reaction sum against total UDL, and that interior end moments satisfy joint equilibrium.

§2.7 Fixed-end loads and consistent nodal forces

2.4 Fixed-end loads and consistent nodal forces

Learning target and physical interpretation

A distributed member load cannot be replaced by arbitrary nodal forces without changing equilibrium or deformation response. Consistent nodal loads are obtained by the same interpolation functions used for the displacement field.

Governing relation

For a downward constant UDL w on an upward-positive beam DOF system, f_e=[−wL/2,−wL²/12,−wL/2,+wL²/12].

Worked engineering interpretation

A 10 kN/m load over 6 m contributes −30 kN at each end and fixed-end generalized nodal moments of magnitude 30 kN·m.

What the idealization omits

Forgetting the equivalent nodal moments causes the rotations to be wrong, even if the total applied vertical force remains correct.

Verification and interpretation of results

Sum all equivalent nodal forces and moments about an arbitrary datum and confirm that they reproduce the distributed load.

§2.8 Comparison of hand and matrix methods

2.5 Comparison of hand and matrix methods

Learning target and physical interpretation

Hand calculations expose mechanics while matrix assembly scales to numerous joints. Compare moment distribution with direct stiffness on the same ideal model, not merely similarly named structures.

Governing relation

Kθ θ=−M_fixed for a rotationally restrained two-span beam with one free interior rotation.

Worked engineering interpretation

For left and right spans fixed at outer ends, the unknown central rotation is θB=−(FEM_B,left+FEM_B,right)/(4EI/L1+4EI/L2).

What the idealization omits

Different end-moment sign conventions can produce numbers with opposite signs that are physically equivalent; draw the action pairs before concluding a disagreement.

Verification and interpretation of results

Check that the moment distribution correction and the matrix θB give the same final joint end moments to the selected numerical tolerance.
