Equilibrium, Reactions, Supports and Static Determinacy

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and design validation of Equilibrium, Reactions, Supports and Static Determinacy. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Civil Engineering Statics study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§2.1 Theoretical Framework: Equilibrium, Reactions, Supports and Static Determinacy

Global equilibrium is necessary for a structure to remain at rest, but its equations do not prove that a foundation has adequate capacity or a beam has sufficient strength. Pinned supports, rollers, fixed connections and contact surfaces restrain different degrees of freedom. The first audit is a boundary-condition audit: identify which movement is resisted and what reaction the idealized support can supply. A stable determinate beam usually has sufficient independent reactions to solve force and moment balance. Real construction tolerances, restraint stiffness and settlement complicate the idealized picture; they are introduced later in structural analysis.

Engineering boundary: These exercises analyze idealized statics models for education. They do not certify structural strength, earthquake/wind resistance, geotechnical bearing, connection design, construction safety, or compliance with any jurisdiction’s building code. Real works require local standards, authoritative loading data and qualified professional checking.

§2.2 Learning outcomes

Learning outcomes

  • Formulate an explicit free-body model for equilibrium, reactions, supports and static determinacy.
  • Use signed forces, consistent units and geometric measurements to construct equilibrium relations.
  • Check results with an independently chosen equilibrium statement or limiting case.
  • Distinguish what idealized statics establishes from what structural design and field verification must establish.

§2.3 Free-body diagrams and support reactions

Lesson 2.1: Free-body diagrams and support reactions

Isolating the member eliminates hidden internal interactions but requires every actual external force and couple to remain. Support symbols are hypotheses about constraints, not decorative drawings.

Governing relationships

In two-dimensional equilibrium, ΣFx=0, ΣFy=0 and ΣMz=0. A simple pin transmits two force components; a frictionless roller normal to a horizontal surface transmits one vertical component.

Worked engineering example

A 6 m span with a 12 kN downward point load 2 m from A has RB=12×2/6=4 kN and RA=8 kN. Recompute moments about B to independently check.

Model audit and common error

Never assign a horizontal reaction to a frictionless horizontal roller.

Interpretation task

Prepare a free-body sketch of a civil infrastructure element involving free-body diagrams and support reactions. Mark coordinate axes, every restraint action, all known magnitudes and signed reference distances. Explain which connections or soil assumptions would invalidate the model. Report results in both a compact table and an independent equilibrium check.

Engineering discussion

This topic should be evaluated with the full load path rather than by its equation alone. For equilibrium, reactions, supports and static determinacy, the quantities that matter are the action itself, where it enters the idealized body, and which physical connection or foundation provides the balancing action. A model that omits one of these produces a formally neat answer without identifying the actual mechanism. Separating gross structural equilibrium from local member strength prevents that confusion. The analyst should first decide what the isolated body is, then measure or state geometry, then apply equilibrium. If an intermediate step changes the sign convention or datum, it should be flagged in the calculation record. Numerical solutions should preserve enough precision for checks but not imply more site accuracy than the input geometry supports.

§2.4 Uniform and variable loads on simply supported beams

Lesson 2.2: Uniform and variable loads on simply supported beams

A loading diagram may contain permanent dead weight, temporary construction loading and localized point forces. Reactions can be found by combining equivalents, then internal sections must use the original load distribution.

Governing relationships

For constant w on a simply supported beam of length L, RA=RB=wL/2. If a point force P is at a from A, RB=wL/2+Pa/L and RA=wL/2+P(L−a)/L.

Worked engineering example

For L=8 m, w=2 kN/m and P=10 kN at a=6 m, RB=8+7.5=15.5 kN and RA=8+2.5=10.5 kN. The reactions add to 26 kN.

Model audit and common error

Carry all loads at the correct geometric positions, not at the nearest support.

§2.5 Determinacy versus stability

Lesson 2.3: Determinacy versus stability

The count of unknown reaction components is a useful screening tool, but geometry determines whether their directions are independent. Three nominal support reactions may still form a mechanism when their lines of action are concurrent or parallel in an unsuitable manner.

Governing relationships

Compare independent equilibrium equations with independent reaction unknowns, then examine whether a rigid-body displacement is geometrically possible. The determinant of the reaction-equilibrium matrix offers an algebraic check.

Worked engineering example

A beam with a vertical roller at each end has only two vertical reactions and no horizontal restraint. It cannot resist arbitrary horizontal loading under the ideal support model, although its vertical reactions may be calculated.

Model audit and common error

Do not confuse the ability to solve one selected loading case with stable restraint under all allowed actions.

§2.6 Internal cuts and action–reaction consistency

Lesson 2.4: Internal cuts and action–reaction consistency

The free-body diagram of part of a beam makes internal axial force, shear and bending moment appear on the cut. Opposite cut faces carry equal and opposite internal actions. A globally balanced structure can still experience high internal demand.

Governing relationships

For the left segment x of a simply supported uniform beam, V(x)=RA−wx and M(x)=RA x−wx²/2 when 0≤x≤L, with the chosen convention.

Worked engineering example

For L=6 m and w=4 kN/m, RA=12 kN. At x=2 m, V=12−8=4 kN and M=24−8=16 kN·m. At midspan Mmax=wL²/8=18 kN·m.

Model audit and common error

Support reactions describe external equilibrium, while section forces describe how load is transferred internally.

Worked problem 2.1

Given: A simply supported 4.0 m beam carries 1.50 kN/m across the span and a 5.0 kN point load at x=0.80 m. Determine end reactions.

Solution: Uniform action = wL=6.000 kN at L/2. Taking moments about A yields RB=wL/2+Pa/L=4.000 kN. Vertical balance gives RA=wL/2+P(L−a)/L=7.000 kN. Check RA+RB=11.000 kN matches wL+P.
