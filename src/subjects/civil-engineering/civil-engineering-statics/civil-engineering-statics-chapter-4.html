Frames, Cables, Arches and Structural Load Paths

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and design validation of Frames, Cables, Arches and Structural Load Paths. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

§4.1 Theoretical Framework: Frames, Cables, Arches and Structural Load Paths

Civil structural systems are not all trusses. Building frames develop shear and bending, flexible cables carry tension, arches can carry dominant compression, and pinned connections may turn a frame into a mechanism. A load path identifies how each applied action travels through slabs, beams, columns, braces and foundations. Drawing that path is as important as solving a small equation set. If a load path is interrupted—for example by an omitted connection, a discontinuous support or a temporary removal—the simplest equilibrium model may produce impossible reactions.

Engineering boundary: These exercises analyze idealized statics models for education. They do not certify structural strength, earthquake/wind resistance, geotechnical bearing, connection design, construction safety, or compliance with any jurisdiction’s building code. Real works require local standards, authoritative loading data and qualified professional checking.

§4.2 Learning outcomes

Learning outcomes

  • Formulate an explicit free-body model for frames, cables, arches and structural load paths.
  • Use signed forces, consistent units and geometric measurements to construct equilibrium relations.
  • Check results with an independently chosen equilibrium statement or limiting case.
  • Distinguish what idealized statics establishes from what structural design and field verification must establish.

§4.3 Frames and multi-force rigid bodies

Lesson 4.1: Frames and multi-force rigid bodies

A frame member with three or more applied forces usually develops shear and bending, not merely axial force. Cutting at joints requires correct moments and interaction forces. Rigid frame joints transmit moments; pin idealizations do not.

Governing relationships

A fixed base in planar idealization can transmit Fx, Fy and a reaction moment. A member isolated between two pin joints without distributed action can be treated as a two-force member, but only under that strict condition.

Worked engineering example

A 3 m cantilever column subjected to 8 kN horizontal force at its top needs a base shear of 8 kN and a base moment of 24 kN·m, neglecting its self-weight.

Model audit and common error

Do not model a welded moment connection as frictionless pin without a justified reason.

Interpretation task

Prepare a free-body sketch of a civil infrastructure element involving frames and multi-force rigid bodies. Mark coordinate axes, every restraint action, all known magnitudes and signed reference distances. Explain which connections or soil assumptions would invalidate the model. Report results in both a compact table and an independent equilibrium check.

Engineering discussion

This topic should be evaluated with the full load path rather than by its equation alone. For frames, cables, arches and structural load paths, the quantities that matter are the action itself, where it enters the idealized body, and which physical connection or foundation provides the balancing action. A model that omits one of these produces a formally neat answer without identifying the actual mechanism. Separating gross structural equilibrium from local member strength prevents that confusion. The analyst should first decide what the isolated body is, then measure or state geometry, then apply equilibrium. If an intermediate step changes the sign convention or datum, it should be flagged in the calculation record. Numerical solutions should preserve enough precision for checks but not imply more site accuracy than the input geometry supports.

§4.4 Flexible cables and sag sensitivity

Lesson 4.2: Flexible cables and sag sensitivity

An ideal cable carries axial tension only, and the cable shape depends on load distribution. For uniform load per horizontal projection, an ideal parabolic shape is appropriate; a cable under its own constant weight per actual cable length has a catenary shape.

Governing relationships

For horizontal span L, midspan sag f and uniform vertical load w per unit horizontal span, the parabolic model gives H=wL²/(8f). Approximate end tension is sqrt(H²+(wL/2)²).

Worked engineering example

For L=20 m, sag f=2 m, and w=1 kN/m horizontal, H=25 kN and support vertical reaction is 10 kN; end tension is sqrt(25²+10²)=26.93 kN.

Model audit and common error

Small sag increases horizontal force: a visually shallow cable is not necessarily a lightly loaded cable.

§4.5 Arches, horizontal thrust and load line

Lesson 4.3: Arches, horizontal thrust and load line

Arches redirect loading partly through compression and may generate significant horizontal support thrust. A funicular arch under its design loading may carry little bending, but other loading patterns and support movements introduce bending.

Governing relationships

For an ideal three-hinged parabolic arch with span L, central rise f and uniform vertical load w, horizontal thrust H=wL²/(8f), obtained from zero bending moment at the crown hinge.

Worked engineering example

A symmetric 30 m arch with rise 5 m and w=3 kN/m has H=3×900/(40)=67.5 kN. Global vertical support reaction is wL/2=45 kN at each end.

Model audit and common error

An arch solution is valid only with its specified hinge, geometry and loading assumptions.

§4.6 Load-path continuity and construction detailing

Lesson 4.4: Load-path continuity and construction detailing

A system can satisfy an attractive free-body calculation and still be unbuildable because forces cannot cross an absent or weak connection. A useful load-path diagram traces each force to ground and identifies the member or interface responsible for every transfer.

Governing relationships

At a floor-to-beam-to-column interface, the tributary floor load enters the beam, beam end reactions load columns and columns send resultant forces into the foundation. Compatibility and deformation redistribute load when the system is indeterminate.

Worked engineering example

A precast plank bearing on a girder transfers vertical reaction through contact. If the bearing seat is removed during construction, the intended gravity load path does not exist even though a drawing still shows nominal support reactions.

Model audit and common error

Equilibrium of individual elements must agree with the complete structural assembly.

Given: A parabolic cable spans 12 m with sag 1.20 m and vertical load 1.00 kN per horizontal metre. Compute horizontal thrust and end tension.
