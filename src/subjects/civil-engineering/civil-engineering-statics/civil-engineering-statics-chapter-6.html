Friction, Sliding, Overturning and Stability

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and design validation of Friction, Sliding, Overturning and Stability. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Civil Engineering Statics study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§6.1 Theoretical Framework: Friction, Sliding, Overturning and Stability

Foundation contact and retaining systems can fail by sliding, overturning, loss of bearing contact or internal structural distress. These mechanisms are different; passing one check does not prove the system safe. A rigid-body sliding calculation is governed by the maximum friction or other available resistance, whereas overturning depends on the relative location of stabilizing and destabilizing resultants. Soil–structure interaction, groundwater, passive pressure, anchorage and applicable factors require specialist treatment beyond introductory statics.

Engineering boundary: These exercises analyze idealized statics models for education. They do not certify structural strength, earthquake/wind resistance, geotechnical bearing, connection design, construction safety, or compliance with any jurisdiction’s building code. Real works require local standards, authoritative loading data and qualified professional checking.

§6.2 Learning outcomes

Learning outcomes

  • Formulate an explicit free-body model for friction, sliding, overturning and stability.
  • Use signed forces, consistent units and geometric measurements to construct equilibrium relations.
  • Check results with an independently chosen equilibrium statement or limiting case.
  • Distinguish what idealized statics establishes from what structural design and field verification must establish.

§6.3 Coulomb friction with unilateral contact

Lesson 6.1: Coulomb friction with unilateral contact

A dry contact model bounds tangential friction by μN. In static equilibrium the friction magnitude adjusts to demand up to its upper limit. A compressive normal reaction N cannot become negative when contact cannot carry tension.

Governing relationships

For a simple horizontal base without other vertical loads, N=W and static friction may satisfy |F|≤μsN. At impending slip, |F|=μsN under the idealized model.

Worked engineering example

A 60 kN gravity load on a level base with coefficient μ=0.4 offers at most 24 kN nominal friction resistance. A 10 kN horizontal demand requires 10 kN static friction, not automatically 24 kN.

Model audit and common error

Friction coefficient values depend on interface condition, moisture, contamination and testing; do not treat classroom numbers as design guarantees.

Interpretation task

Prepare a free-body sketch of a civil infrastructure element involving coulomb friction with unilateral contact. Mark coordinate axes, every restraint action, all known magnitudes and signed reference distances. Explain which connections or soil assumptions would invalidate the model. Report results in both a compact table and an independent equilibrium check.

Engineering discussion

This topic should be evaluated with the full load path rather than by its equation alone. For friction, sliding, overturning and stability, the quantities that matter are the action itself, where it enters the idealized body, and which physical connection or foundation provides the balancing action. A model that omits one of these produces a formally neat answer without identifying the actual mechanism. Separating gross structural equilibrium from local member strength prevents that confusion. The analyst should first decide what the isolated body is, then measure or state geometry, then apply equilibrium. If an intermediate step changes the sign convention or datum, it should be flagged in the calculation record. Numerical solutions should preserve enough precision for checks but not imply more site accuracy than the input geometry supports.

§6.4 Sliding checks and load combinations

Lesson 6.2: Sliding checks and load combinations

Sliding equilibrium compares driving and resisting horizontal actions. If external anchorage, passive pressure or geometry contributes resistance, distinguish which mechanisms are reliably mobilized and compatible.

Governing relationships

For an isolated rigid block with H acting horizontally and μW available, the nominal ratio against sliding is μW/H for H>0. A code-based check may use different resistance and load factors.

Worked engineering example

With W=80 kN, μ=0.35 and H=20 kN, nominal available friction is 28 kN and ratio is 1.40. That simple ratio does not certify geotechnical safety.

Model audit and common error

Do not add incompatible peak resistances from different movement states without engineering justification.

§6.5 Overturning moments and contact resultant

Lesson 6.3: Overturning moments and contact resultant

Overturning resistance is a moment balance around a specified toe. A nominal positive stabilizing moment does not alone ensure compression across the entire base; resultant eccentricity can create partial contact.

Governing relationships

For base width B, downward load W centered at B/2 and lateral force H at height h, stabilizing moment about the toe is WB/2, overturning moment is Hh. Resultant eccentricity e=M/W in a simple vertical-resultant model.

Worked engineering example

For B=2 m, W=100 kN, H=20 kN at h=3 m, resisting moment WB/2=100 kN·m and overturning moment Hh=60 kN·m. Nominal toe ratio is 1.67.

Model audit and common error

Local bearing pressures, soil capacity and load factors are beyond the friction-and-moment calculation.

§6.6 Retaining-wall pressures and conceptual stability

Lesson 6.4: Retaining-wall pressures and conceptual stability

A retained-soil wall experiences lateral stress that often increases with depth, producing a triangular resultant for simple idealizations. Real earth-pressure distribution depends on soil strength, groundwater, wall movement, surcharge and drainage.

Governing relationships

Under a uniform effective earth-pressure gradient increasing from 0 at ground to pmax at depth H, total lateral resultant per metre of wall is P=pmax H/2, acting H/3 above the base.

Worked engineering example

With H=4 m and base lateral pressure pmax=24 kPa, the ideal triangular resultant is 48 kN per metre wall, at 1.333 m above the base. Units: kPa×m=kN/m.

Model audit and common error

This pressure shape is a teaching assumption; real wall design requires geotechnical investigation and appropriate earth-pressure theory.
