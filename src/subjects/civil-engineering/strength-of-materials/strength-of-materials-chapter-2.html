§2.1 Theoretical Framework: Axially Loaded Bars and Thermal Effects

Prerequisite knowledge: force equilibrium, SI dimensional analysis, algebra and first-year calculus.

Learning objectives: state a model and its validity range, calculate physically meaningful engineering quantities, reconcile equations with a free-body diagram or deformation compatibility, and identify at least two failure modes excluded by the idealization.

§2.2 Unit context and engineering interpretation

Unit context and engineering interpretation

Axial behavior provides a clean setting in which equilibrium, compatibility and constitutive response must work together. If two members lie end to end, the same axial force passes through each segment, assuming no additional load enters between them; their extensions add. If two rods share rigid end plates, their total extensions are equal but the forces differ according to EA/L. A rigid support can attract an internal reaction in the absence of any external mechanical load when temperature or fabrication prestrain is imposed. These observations are not separate tricks: each is a consequence of the same force-displacement law combined with mechanical constraints.

Suppose an anchored steel element experiences a prescribed temperature rise. The unconstrained thermal strain is alpha ΔT, whereas actual mechanical strain is the difference between total strain and thermal strain. Under perfect restraint, total axial strain vanishes, so a compressive stress develops. If one end is attached to a flexible spring, part of the free expansion occurs as actual movement and the remaining part develops stress. This intermediate case is common in pipe supports and clamped equipment. A fully fixed model is sometimes an upper bound on force, but not always on local stress once geometry, contact and eccentricity enter.

Stored energy offers an independent perspective. A uniform elastic bar has a triangular force-displacement diagram, so its strain energy equals one half of final force times extension. The relation is not generally correct for a nonlinear material unless the actual area under the curve is computed. If the stiffness doubles while force remains fixed, deflection and strain energy halve. If instead a prescribed displacement is applied, force and strain energy both increase with stiffness. The chosen control variable matters.

§2.3 Axial stiffness and variable-section members

Lesson 2.1 — Axial stiffness and variable-section members

Physical mechanism and governing idea

An axial bar behaves like an elastic spring under small deformation when its ends are appropriately constrained. Uniform axial stiffness k=EA/L rises with modulus and area and decreases with length. For a variable axial force, nonuniform area or multiple materials, integrate compliance along the load path. The elongation is the sum of segment deformations in series.

Governing relationship

delta = integral(N(x)/(E(x)A(x)) dx); k = EA/L

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

A 1.5 m steel bar with E=200 GPa and A=400 mm² has k about 53.3 MN/m.

What the simplified formula misses

The formula presumes linear elastic material behavior, axial loading and a member without important bending or connection slip.

§2.4 Statically indeterminate axial systems

Lesson 2.2 — Statically indeterminate axial systems

Physical mechanism and governing idea

Some parallel rods and fixed-ended members have more unknown support forces than independent equilibrium equations. Instead of making an arbitrary equal-force assumption, impose shared end displacement or another geometric compatibility condition. The resulting distribution depends on EA/L. A stiffer rod attracts more force when parallel members experience the same extension.

For parallel rods: F_i = k_i delta and sum(F_i)=P

§2.5 Thermal expansion and restraint

Lesson 2.3 — Thermal expansion and restraint

Physical mechanism and governing idea

A temperature rise causes a free isotropic bar to expand by alpha L deltaT. If the bar is restrained, equilibrium and compatibility convert the prevented expansion into stress. For an ideal fully constrained uniform bar, sigma=-E alpha deltaT, with compression for positive temperature rise under the adopted sign convention.

Governing relationship

delta_free = alpha L deltaT; sigma_restrained = -E alpha deltaT

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

Steel with E=200 GPa and alpha=12e-6/K under a 30 K rise develops roughly 72 MPa compressive stress if expansion is fully prevented.

What the simplified formula misses

A real restraint may move. Thermal gradients, buckling and connection flexibility invalidate the fully restrained uniform-bar shortcut.

§2.6 Axial energy and superposition

Lesson 2.4 — Axial energy and superposition

Physical mechanism and governing idea

Linear elastic axial deformation stores recoverable strain energy. For a uniform bar in constant axial tension, U=N²L/(2EA)=N delta/2. Energy is useful for checking a calculation or obtaining deflection with Castigliano-type methods. Superposition can combine independent loads only while geometry and material response remain linear.

Governing relationship

U = integral(N(x)^2/(2E(x)A(x)) dx)

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

Doubling the force in a linear bar doubles extension but quadruples stored energy.

What the simplified formula misses

A nonlinear stress-strain curve, changing contact state or large deflection makes linear superposition unreliable.

§2.8 Engineering case study: Thermally restrained pipeline support

Engineering case study: Thermally restrained pipeline support

A heated water pipe runs between anchors but incorporates expansion loops and flexible supports. Under a purely fixed bar approximation, the thermal stress is proportional to E alpha deltaT and does not depend on pipe length. Under a free expansion approximation, the displacement is proportional to length. Neither extreme alone describes a pipe with spring supports and sliding friction. The engineer must characterize anchor stiffness and temperature distribution along the line.

Deliverable: Construct a three-case comparison: free expansion, full restraint and a finite axial restraint spring. Identify where compatibility enters the model and which instrumentation could measure anchor movement and actual axial strain.

Case-study discussion

Start by differentiating data from assumptions: measured loads and geometry should be separated from guessed restraints and material properties. Build one simple calculation whose result can be checked independently. List at least three mechanisms by which the idealization could fail, and prioritize them by whether they would change the engineering conclusion. Translate the numerical demand into a practical question about safety, serviceability, testing or maintenance. Keep a clear audit trail so a reviewer can follow the unit conversions, load path and model limitations.

§2.9 Calculation verification

Calculation verification

  • Compare two extreme restraints: unconstrained free expansion and perfectly restrained zero displacement.
  • For several segments in series, sum segment deformations and check that the transmitted force is consistent.
  • Check positivity, zero-load behavior, symmetry, and asymptotic scaling whenever applicable.
  • Do not use a graph or slider response as the only evidence that an equation is correct.
