§5.1 Theoretical Framework: Shear Forces and Bending Moments in Beams

Prerequisite knowledge: force equilibrium, SI dimensional analysis, algebra and first-year calculus.

Learning objectives: state a model and its validity range, calculate physically meaningful engineering quantities, reconcile equations with a free-body diagram or deformation compatibility, and identify at least two failure modes excluded by the idealization.

§5.2 Unit context and engineering interpretation

Unit context and engineering interpretation

Beam diagrams describe internal force resultants, not displacements. A beam can have a large bending moment yet small deflection if its flexural rigidity is high. Conversely, a low moment in a flexible long span can produce appreciable serviceability deformation. The first task in any beam problem is identifying correct supports and externally applied loads, including their units. A roller supplies a reaction force normal to its travel surface; an ideal pin supplies forces but not a moment; a built-in end may supply a moment as well. Real supports lie between these extremes.

A localized downward force produces a step in shear equal to its magnitude. A concentrated couple produces a jump in moment. Distributed forces determine diagram slopes, and the areas under w(x) and V(x) determine changes in shear and moment. These integral relationships create a powerful consistency check: the change in V over an interval must equal the negative total applied distributed load in that interval, and the change in moment must equal the integral of shear over that interval. A computational diagram should be subjected to these checks, not merely admired.

Live-load envelopes add a second layer of reasoning. A roadway beam might experience a vehicle in many possible locations. The largest midspan moment and the largest support shear may occur under different vehicle placements. Optimizing one load case does not automatically envelope all demands. For students, moving a point load in the simulator reveals this distinction. For professionals, code-prescribed load patterns, construction staging and system continuity must be incorporated in the structural model.

§5.3 Beam loads, supports and sign conventions

Lesson 5.1 — Beam loads, supports and sign conventions

Physical mechanism and governing idea

A beam supports transverse loading through shears and bending moments. External loads and reactions must balance before internal functions are calculated. A distributed load has units of force per length, so its resultant force is the integral of intensity along the beam. The diagrams depend on location and support restraint, not just the total applied force.

Governing relationship

dV/dx=-w(x); dM/dx=V(x) under a consistent sign convention

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

A simply supported six-meter beam with uniform load 3 kN/m carries 18 kN total, producing 9 kN reactions at each end.

What the simplified formula misses

The same total force applied as a point load at an end gives very different internal diagrams. A reaction is not automatically half the total for unsymmetric loading.

§5.4 Concentrated loads and discontinuities

Lesson 5.2 — Concentrated loads and discontinuities

Physical mechanism and governing idea

A concentrated vertical force introduces a step in the shear-force diagram. The bending moment remains continuous across a simple point force in Euler-Bernoulli beam theory. A concentrated couple instead introduces a moment jump. These graphical features are strong debugging checks for a computer-generated diagram.

Governing relationship

V(x+) - V(x-) = -P for downward point load

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

A 12 kN downward point load causes a 12 kN downward jump in V at its application point.

What the simplified formula misses

Drawing a jump in bending moment at an ordinary point force is a sign-convention or free-body mistake.

§5.5 Distributed loading and diagram shape

Lesson 5.3 — Distributed loading and diagram shape

Physical mechanism and governing idea

The slope of shear equals negative load intensity, while the slope of bending moment equals shear. Uniform loading therefore creates a straight shear segment and a quadratic moment segment. A maximum or minimum smooth moment occurs where shear passes through zero, as long as no applied couple creates a jump.

Governing relationship

V(x)=R_A - integral_0^x w(s) ds; M(x)=integral_0^x V(s) ds

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

For uniform load w on a simply supported beam, Mmax=wL²/8 at midspan.

What the simplified formula misses

The differential identities have convention-dependent signs. A diagram should be checked by returning to the original free-body equilibrium.

§5.6 Load envelopes and structural reasoning

Lesson 5.4 — Load envelopes and structural reasoning

Physical mechanism and governing idea

Real beams experience multiple load combinations and moving load positions. A single nominal diagram is only one scenario. Load envelopes capture maximum and minimum internal demands across specified combinations, while code factors and pattern loading remain separate decisions. A safe design begins with correct demand definitions and relevant limit states.

Governing relationship

M_envelope(x)=max over admissible load cases of |M_case(x)|

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

Moving a concentrated load toward a support reduces its maximum simple-span moment even when the load magnitude is unchanged.

What the simplified formula misses

An envelope over only the cases you chose says nothing about omitted traffic positions, construction stages or redistribution due to cracking.

§5.8 Engineering case study: Temporary construction loading on a floor beam

Engineering case study: Temporary construction loading on a floor beam

During renovation, material pallets are placed close to one end of a simply supported beam. A uniform load approximation may miss concentrated reaction and moment peaks. The engineer should represent the actual pallet footprint or applied point-force idealization, solve support reactions and plot piecewise shear and bending moments. Repositioning the load changes support demands and bending behavior even when total weight remains unchanged.

Deliverable: Develop two loading cases: one centered and one near a support. Compare reactions, maximum moment location and the shear jump. State why this demonstration cannot replace a building-code load combination and restraint investigation.

Case-study discussion

Start by differentiating data from assumptions: measured loads and geometry should be separated from guessed restraints and material properties. Build one simple calculation whose result can be checked independently. List at least three mechanisms by which the idealization could fail, and prioritize them by whether they would change the engineering conclusion. Translate the numerical demand into a practical question about safety, serviceability, testing or maintenance. Keep a clear audit trail so a reviewer can follow the unit conversions, load path and model limitations.

§5.9 Calculation verification

Calculation verification

  • Differentiate your bending function to recover the shear function away from concentrated loads.
  • Verify RA + RB equals total applied downward load and that the end moments vanish for simple supports.
  • Check positivity, zero-load behavior, symmetry, and asymptotic scaling whenever applicable.
  • Do not use a graph or slider response as the only evidence that an equation is correct.
