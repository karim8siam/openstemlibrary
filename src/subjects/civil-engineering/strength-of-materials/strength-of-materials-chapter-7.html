§7.1 Theoretical Framework: Stress Transformation and Combined Loads

Prerequisite knowledge: force equilibrium, SI dimensional analysis, algebra and first-year calculus.

Learning objectives: state a model and its validity range, calculate physically meaningful engineering quantities, reconcile equations with a free-body diagram or deformation compatibility, and identify at least two failure modes excluded by the idealization.

§7.2 Unit context and engineering interpretation

Unit context and engineering interpretation

Stress is a tensor, which means the traction on a plane depends on its orientation. Mohr circle is a visual tool for this transformation, not a separate physical phenomenon. A principal plane has zero in-plane shear, while maximum in-plane shear occurs on another plane. The circle radius captures the combined effect of normal-stress difference and shear. In a uniaxial tensile coupon, a plane inclined at 45 degrees carries shear even though the original axial cross-section does not. That does not imply the coupon is externally twisted.

Combined loading illustrates how several simple models interact. A column subjected to eccentric compression experiences both axial force and bending, so one edge may become more compressed than the average and the other may be less compressed or even tensile. This may trigger cracking, instability or contact separation before a gross-section yield check is exceeded. Superposition assumes linear elastic response and no important change in geometry or boundary conditions. Once the section cracks or contact opens, redistribution is nonlinear.

Failure theories compress multiaxial demand into a scalar for a specified material failure mechanism. Von Mises equivalent stress is relevant to yielding of many isotropic ductile metals, but brittle fracture, reinforced concrete, fatigue and local instability have their own criteria. Even for ductile steel, design verification also involves buckling, weld detailing and adequate deformation capacity. Interpretation matters more than collecting formulas.

§7.3 Stress at an inclined plane

Lesson 7.1 — Stress at an inclined plane

Physical mechanism and governing idea

Normal and shear components depend on plane orientation. A plane-stress state defined by sigma_x, sigma_y and tau_xy can be rotated to find stresses on another surface. The transformation has twice-angle trigonometric terms because an oriented plane returns to the same physical plane after a half turn.

Governing relationship

sigma_theta=(sigma_x+sigma_y)/2+(sigma_x-sigma_y)/2 cos(2theta)+tau_xy sin(2theta)

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

Equal biaxial normal stresses with zero shear produce the same normal stress on every rotated plane.

What the simplified formula misses

Rotating the body sketch and rotating the coordinate axes use opposite angular conventions. State explicitly which one your equations implement.

§7.4 Mohr circle and principal stresses

Lesson 7.2 — Mohr circle and principal stresses

Physical mechanism and governing idea

Mohr circle provides a geometric map of normal and shear components on planes through a material point. The center is mean normal stress; the radius combines half-difference of normal stresses and in-plane shear. Principal planes have zero in-plane shear, and maximum in-plane shear equals the circle radius.

Governing relationship

C=(sigma_x+sigma_y)/2; R=sqrt(((sigma_x-sigma_y)/2)^2+tau_xy²)

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

For sigma_x=80 MPa, sigma_y=20 MPa and tau_xy=40 MPa, principal stresses are approximately 100 and 0 MPa.

What the simplified formula misses

The in-plane maximum shear is not necessarily the absolute three-dimensional maximum unless the third principal stress is considered.

§7.5 Combined axial load and bending

Lesson 7.3 — Combined axial load and bending

Physical mechanism and governing idea

When linear elasticity and small deformations hold, a concentric axial force and bending moment produce stresses that may be superposed. At a section, sigma=N/A ± M c/I. One face becomes more tensile while the opposite becomes more compressive. The location of maximum tension changes with the sign of the moment.

Governing relationship

sigma(y)=N/A - M y/I

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

An axial stress of 20 MPa plus or minus 35 MPa bending gives extreme fiber stresses of 55 MPa tension and 15 MPa compression under one sign convention.

What the simplified formula misses

The method assumes the load acts through a known section, plane sections remain plane and stress concentration is not controlling.

§7.6 Equivalent stress and failure theories

Lesson 7.4 — Equivalent stress and failure theories

Physical mechanism and governing idea

A ductile-metal yield screen often uses the von Mises equivalent stress computed from stress components. For plane stress, sigma_VM=sqrt(sigma_x²-sigma_x sigma_y+sigma_y²+3tau_xy²). The expression is not a universal fracture or concrete design criterion, and its interpretation depends on the material and loading state.

Governing relationship

sigma_VM = sqrt(sigma_x²-sigma_x sigma_y+sigma_y²+3 tau_xy²)

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

Pure shear of 50 MPa produces a von Mises equivalent stress near 86.6 MPa.

What the simplified formula misses

Brittle fracture, fatigue, buckling, weld details and nonmetallic failure mechanisms require other checks even if equivalent stress is below yield.

§7.8 Engineering case study: Offset load on a column base plate

Engineering case study: Offset load on a column base plate

A nominal axial force arrives at a base plate with a measurable eccentricity. The force can be represented as an equivalent centric force and an applied moment about the section centroid. A linear stress distribution may show tension at one edge, but a concrete-bearing base with no tensile contact responds nonlinearly; the initial superposition no longer predicts the actual pressure distribution.

Deliverable: Calculate the elastic linear stress estimate, then state how a compression-only support changes equilibrium and contact area. Record the distinctions among material stress, plate bending and anchor forces.

Case-study discussion

Start by differentiating data from assumptions: measured loads and geometry should be separated from guessed restraints and material properties. Build one simple calculation whose result can be checked independently. List at least three mechanisms by which the idealization could fail, and prioritize them by whether they would change the engineering conclusion. Translate the numerical demand into a practical question about safety, serviceability, testing or maintenance. Keep a clear audit trail so a reviewer can follow the unit conversions, load path and model limitations.

§7.9 Calculation verification

Calculation verification

  • Check that the Mohr-circle radius squared equals ((σx−σy)/2)²+τxy².
  • At a principal orientation, the transformed in-plane shear must vanish numerically.
  • Check positivity, zero-load behavior, symmetry, and asymptotic scaling whenever applicable.
  • Do not use a graph or slider response as the only evidence that an equation is correct.

A ductile-metal yield screen often uses the von Mises equivalent stress computed from stress components. For plane stress, sigma_VM=sqrt(sigma_x²-sigma_x sigma_y+sigma_y²+3tau_xy²). The expression is not a universal fracture or concrete design criterion, and its interpretation depends on the material and loading state.

Governing relationship

sigma_VM = sqrt(sigma_x²-sigma_x sigma_y+sigma_y²+3 tau_xy²)

Reading the equation: Start from a properly oriented free body or a measured constitutive response, identify the force or displacement controlled externally, and decide which geometric quantity belongs in the denominator. Check units before substituting numbers, and compare the sign of the result with the physical action.

Worked interpretive example

Pure shear of 50 MPa produces a von Mises equivalent stress near 86.6 MPa.

What the simplified formula misses

Brittle fracture, fatigue, buckling, weld details and nonmetallic failure mechanisms require other checks even if equivalent stress is below yield.

Engineering case study: Offset load on a column base plate

A nominal axial force arrives at a base plate with a measurable eccentricity. The force can be represented as an equivalent centric force and an applied moment about the section centroid. A linear stress distribution may show tension at one edge, but a concrete-bearing base with no tensile contact responds nonlinearly; the initial superposition no longer predicts the actual pressure distribution.

Deliverable: Calculate the elastic linear stress estimate, then state how a compression-only support changes equilibrium and contact area. Record the distinctions among material stress, plate bending and anchor forces.

Keep a clear audit trail so a reviewer can follow the unit conversions, load path and model limitations.

Calculation verification

  • Check that the Mohr-circle radius squared equals ((σx−σy)/2)²+τxy².
  • At a principal orientation, the transformed in-plane shear must vanish numerically.
  • Check positivity, zero-load behavior, symmetry, and asymptotic scaling whenever applicable.
  • Do not use a graph or slider response as the only evidence that an equation is correct.
