§6.1 Theoretical Framework: Durability and Environmental Transport

Core investigation: Coastal reinforced structure.

Unit framework. Exposure is a boundary condition, not a material property. Chemical action may be controlled by moisture, temperature and cracking rather than bulk paste permeability alone.

Capstone evidence task. For a chloride-profile simulation, document which parameters are measured, estimated or held constant, then write a short paragraph explaining why an apparent time to threshold is not a guarantee.

§6.4 Permeability and sorptivity

6.1 Permeability and sorptivity

Physical explanation

Liquid transport depends on pore connectivity, degree of saturation and pressure or capillary gradients. Permeability, sorptivity, electrical resistivity and diffusion are not interchangeable measurements. A high-quality concrete can still crack, and a crack may govern ingress even when bulk paste transport is slow.

Worked engineering interpretation

Compare two mixes with equal laboratory strength but different curing: one has lower measured surface sorptivity after seven days of moist curing. This supports a conclusion about that test, not a universal proof that all ions and gases will penetrate more slowly under every exposure.

Application A: precast yard

Consider precast yard, where the central practical constraints include demoulding schedule, moisture retention, batch consistency and accelerated curing. The lesson on permeability and sorptivity becomes a decision only after a responsible engineer states the exposure, intended function, material evidence and tolerable uncertainty. For this case start by identifying which quantities come from a mixture record and which depend on field construction. Make a table with the current state, intended change and predicted consequence. Do not equate a clean theoretical trend with successful placement: operation-specific limits can reverse the preferred option.

A useful thought experiment is to compare an apparently favorable laboratory value against an observed field defect. If the laboratory result supports the material but the member shows insufficient consolidation or cracking, the next investigation is not to discard the observation. Look for restrained slab formwork, finishing timing, transport history and the position of reinforcement; these can make the same nominal composition behave differently. State a falsifiable prediction for each alternative. For example, if the issue arises from a changed moisture condition, repeated batch-level corrections should reduce the discrepancy while other variables remain controlled.

Application B: mid-rise residential slab

Now transfer the principle to mid-rise residential slab. The governing constraint changes to restrained slab formwork, finishing timing, transport history and the position of reinforcement. §6.5 Carbonation and corrosion risk

6.2 Carbonation and corrosion risk

Physical explanation

Atmospheric carbon dioxide reacts with alkaline constituents and can reduce pore-solution alkalinity near the surface. A square-root-of-time depth approximation is common for illustrating diffusion-influenced fronts, but the coefficient depends on exposure, moisture, binder and curing. Reaching reinforcement depth indicates a change in corrosion conditions, not automatic proof that corrosion has occurred.

Worked engineering interpretation

A conceptual front model x=k√t with k=3 mm/√year gives x=12 mm after 16 years. If nominal cover is 30 mm, the simple front has not reached steel; this result must not be used to assign a design service life without exposure-dependent calibration and cover variation.

§6.6 Chloride ingress and reinforcement

6.3 Chloride ingress and reinforcement

Physical explanation

A diffusion-type model can describe chloride profiles in a saturated idealization, but binding, wetting cycles, cracks and changing surface concentrations complicate reality. Threshold chloride contents vary and are not a single universal number. Corrosion risk should be evaluated with cover, exposure, mix history and actual chloride measurements.

Worked engineering interpretation

For a semi-infinite Fickian illustration, C(x,t)=Cs·erfc[x/(2√(Dt))]. Doubling cover does not simply double the time to the same concentration; at fixed Cs and D, corresponding penetration times scale approximately with cover squared. The model assumes constant D and surface concentration.

§6.7 Chemical attack, freezing and sulfate action

6.4 Chemical attack, freezing and sulfate action

Physical explanation

Different exposures damage concrete through different paths. Freezing damage depends on saturation and the presence of an appropriate entrained-air void system. Sulfate exposure involves reactions and transport, with binder composition and permeability both relevant. Acidic environments can dissolve hydration products even when compressive strength was initially high.

Worked engineering interpretation

An unair-entrained specimen survives one low-temperature event while an adequately air-entrained specimen withstands many cycles. The comparison does not show that air entrainment makes concrete stronger in compression; entrained air often trades some strength for better freeze–thaw resistance when properly proportioned.

§6.9 Extended reading: transport, exposure and durability inference

Extended reading: transport, exposure and durability inference

A low-permeability paste is not automatically a durable member

Concrete durability depends on the interaction of its material and environment. A dense uncracked matrix may restrict fluid ingress, but a through-crack can provide a much faster local pathway. Surface curing, consolidation and finish quality can create a near-surface zone different from the bulk tested in a laboratory. Transport is further affected by whether the pores are saturated, partially wet or repeatedly cycled. An engineer must therefore specify the expected exposure and the mechanism likely to govern deterioration before selecting laboratory indicators.

Sorptivity describes capillary uptake in suitable early-time conditions. Permeability reflects flow through a porous medium under pressure, while diffusion addresses movement driven by concentration gradients. Electrical resistivity can provide indirect information related to pore solution and connectivity, but it is not an interchangeable measurement of every transport process. Two specimens may rank differently across tests because their moisture states, ionic concentrations and pore geometries differ. Where a result is used as a proxy for long-term behavior, the correlation should be demonstrated for the relevant materials and exposure.

Carbonation is a moving chemical boundary

Carbon dioxide reacts with alkaline concrete constituents, shifting pore-solution chemistry near the exposed surface. A simple model often illustrates carbonation depth as a coefficient times the square root of exposure time. That relationship captures a possible trend in diffusion-influenced conditions but compresses many mechanisms into one coefficient. Humidity affects both gas movement and chemical reactions; very dry or constantly saturated surfaces can behave differently from moderately moist ones. Binder composition, curing and cracks further change the result.

If a nominal front reaches reinforcement depth, the concrete near steel may no longer provide the same chemical environment. Corrosion still depends on electrochemical conditions such as oxygen, moisture and local chemistry. Cover is itself variable, and one reported average cover should not be treated as a uniform value. For a sound interpretation, measure or estimate a cover distribution, investigate exposure history, and compare an appropriate profile with multiple cores. The model's calculated “intersection time” is not a warranty of service life.

Chloride diffusion is an idealization with important assumptions

A common classroom solution uses the complementary error function for a semi-infinite domain with a constant surface concentration and diffusivity. It is valuable for exploring sensitivity to depth, time and diffusion coefficient. It is less reliable when chloride binding, wetting cycles, aging diffusivity or cracks dominate. A nominal threshold for corrosion initiation is not universal. The actual state depends on steel, pore solution and the exposure history, and the threshold itself may need to be treated as uncertain rather than exact.

A useful laboratory assignment compares the idealized curve with measurements from a series of depth-resolved samples. Students should avoid fitting a high-precision diffusivity to only two noisy points. Instead, define surface concentration assumptions, use multiple depths, report the fit quality, and consider whether the parameter varies with age. When a surface concentration changes seasonally, the simple solution's boundary condition is wrong; adding more decimal places to the fit will not correct the physics.

Distinguish sulfate, acid and freezing mechanisms

Sulfate-related deterioration involves transport and potentially expansive reactions whose details depend on binder chemistry and environment. Acid attack can dissolve calcium-bearing reaction products. Freeze–thaw damage involves the behavior of pore water during repeated temperature changes, saturation and an appropriate air-void structure. A concrete optimized only for strength may lack the air void system needed for severe freeze–thaw exposure, while increased entrained air may reduce nominal compressive strength. Those tradeoffs demand mechanism-specific tests rather than one generic “durability index.”

For an exposed reinforced member, draw a degradation map locating cracks, joints, drainage paths, corners and areas with different cover. Then rank failure mechanisms by evidence and uncertainty. Specify which tests would distinguish carbonation, chloride, freeze–thaw, sulfate or workmanship defects. A repair strategy should address the actual ingress route and exposure, not just the most visible surface symptom.

§6.10 Integrated case 6 — Chloride exposure in a car park edge beam

Integrated case 6 — Chloride exposure in a car park edge beam

Project setting. A reinforced edge beam is repeatedly exposed to wind-driven rain and salt-bearing splashes. Engineers have a concrete strength certificate but limited information about cover distribution, surface cracks and chloride transport. One consultant proposes estimating the year of corrosion initiation from a single laboratory diffusion coefficient. Another argues that no useful calculation is possible without decades of measurements.

Reasonable middle approach. An idealized diffusion model can identify sensitivity and help plan measurements, but its assumptions must be explicit. Suppose the surface chloride concentration is fixed at 0.8% by binder mass, effective D is 22 mm²/year and exposure duration is twenty years. With depth 35 mm, the error-function solution gives a concentration of roughly 0.19% binder mass at that location. This result is conditional on a constant boundary concentration, constant diffusivity, a semi-infinite uniform body and no cracks or binding change. It is not a universal steel-corrosion threshold calculation.

Field evidence. Measure cover distribution rather than a single nominal cover. Locate visible cracks, joints, drainage paths and repairs. If possible, obtain several depth-resolved chloride profiles in representative exposure zones; record whether surfaces were sampled near a crack or sound concrete. Investigate moisture and exposure history, because repeated wetting changes boundary conditions. If measured profiles depart from the idealized curve, do not silently force the equation to match through an unrealistic fitted D. Evaluate whether the model class is appropriate at all.

Parallel carbonation concern. The structure may also experience carbonation, which depends on humidity and the alkalinity of the concrete. A square-root-of-time front model can compare an approximate penetration depth with cover, but that front is not the same as electrochemical corrosion. A dry cover layer could carbonate while corrosion remains moisture-limited; a wet chloride-exposed area can behave differently. Analyze the mechanisms separately and then consider their interaction, rather than using whichever simple equation predicts the shortest remaining life.

Decision. Develop a risk-based inspection and testing plan, classify evidence quality and define what observation would trigger repair assessment. Include service consequences and access requirements. The educational simulations offer transparent sensitivity analysis but cannot set a design life or authorize repairs without engineering judgment, verified tests and the applicable standards. The practical distinction is between refusing any model and using a limited model responsibly: the latter is often appropriate when uncertainty is disclosed and the next measurement is chosen to reduce it.
