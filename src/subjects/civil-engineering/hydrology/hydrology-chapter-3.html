Evapotranspiration, Soil Moisture and Green-Ampt Infiltration

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, computational hydraulics, and engineering design validation of Evapotranspiration, Soil Moisture and Green-Ampt Infiltration. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

§3.1 Case study: Irrigated floodplain soil profile

Case study: Irrigated floodplain soil profile

A field experiment measures water entry into loamy soil under ponding. Conductivity and wetting-front suction produce a plausible Green–Ampt response, but the experimental surface develops a sediment seal midway through the test. A single constant-parameter fit may then reproduce total infiltration while misrepresenting the rate history.

Investigation task. Estimate soil wetness and test the fit against early and late observations separately; state why ET0 does not measure infiltration.

The unit as part of a watershed investigation

This unit asks an engineer to defend infiltration and et as a quantitative argument rather than a number inserted into a generic formula. The natural control volume is the selected hydrologic subsystem and its actual inflow and outflow boundaries. Write the unit of every parameter and specify the temporal support of the observations; an hourly intensity, a daily depth and an annual volume cannot be substituted for each other without integration. A concept sketch should show the site boundaries, measurement stations, surface and subsurface pathways, relevant human infrastructure and the time interval over which the process operates. Interpret the final result relative to the real decision at irrigated floodplain soil profile, not merely against an arbitrary calculator threshold.

Field evidence, scale and uncertainty

The most important practical complication in evapotranspiration and infiltration is that model inputs are estimates of spatially and temporally varying states. Two instruments reporting the same variable may not have comparable averaging periods or calibration. The impact of an uncertain coefficient depends on the derivative of the output with respect to that coefficient and on where the operating point falls relative to a threshold. If a predicted response appears robust, deliberately test a neighboring process that the simple model omits: channel blockage, antecedent saturation, high tailwater, sensor drift, groundwater exchange or a different storm shape. Record failures of the model openly, rather than editing the inputs until a visually pleasing curve appears.

Professional inference standard

An educational simulation is useful when it generates a falsifiable prediction. Before using any of the four lessons to make a design claim, identify an independently measurable output, a second approximation with different assumptions and a failure consequence. For irrigated floodplain soil profile, a field campaign might require time-synchronized precipitation and flow observations, a georeferenced inventory of hydraulic structures, separate dry- and wet-weather site visits and a provenance ledger for every parameter. The exercises below are intentionally synthetic and no parameter set is asserted to describe a real Bangladesh, US or other watershed. Local codes and agency-approved hydrologic methods supersede these teaching screens.

§3.2 Radiation and evaporation processes

Lesson 3.1 — Radiation and evaporation processes

Hydrological mechanism

Evaporation transfers liquid water to vapor, consuming energy; transpiration moves water through plants. The combination depends on net radiation, humidity, wind, canopy conductance, root access and water availability. Potential, reference and actual evapotranspiration describe different constructs and should not be interchanged. A high potential demand can coexist with low actual evapotranspiration during soil-water stress.

Governing expression and dimensions

Teaching relation: Water loss E=latent-energy/(rho_w L_v) when energy balance is appropriate. Every term must be traced to its physical definition before numerical evaluation. A hydrologic depth expressed in millimeters corresponds to one thousand cubic meters for every square kilometer of area. A discharge expressed in cubic meters per second becomes volume only after integrating over seconds. A rate reported over a nominal day or hour is not a spatial average by itself. State whether the equation is a conservation balance, idealization, calibrated empirical law or fitted statistical relation; that classification determines what evidence is needed before transfer to a new catchment.

Units and source audit. For radiation and evaporation processes, list the observed quantities, estimated coefficients and chosen reference conditions separately. Include the dates and methods used to obtain each. Calculate one reference state using all quantities in compatible units and round only after a dimensional check. Then reverse a sign convention or convert to a different set of units and verify that the physical meaning survives. A unit-consistent equation may still be wrong because its assumptions do not match the setting.

Applied decision: Assess dry-season water loss from an exposed irrigation reservoir

The central decision is not whether a formula can be evaluated; it is whether the value carries information sufficient for an engineering action. In this situation, first identify the outlet, observation period and decision variable. Establish a low, central and high forcing scenario using values that can be defended by measurements or explicitly labeled hypotheses. Predict how the response changes before running the model. A prediction that contradicts conservation of water, plausible timing or an independently measured constraint requires investigation even when the graph looks smooth.

For this particular case the dominant caution is that floating covers, vegetation and advective heat can invalidate a single coefficient. Assess which boundary condition is most likely to be violated. For an event analysis, compare the timing of inputs and outputs; for a seasonal balance, separate fluxes from storage; for statistical estimates, distinguish frequency assumptions from observed counts. Do not claim a coefficient calibrated in one environment is universal. State one missing measurement and the practical difficulty of obtaining it.

Mechanism checks and falsification

Counterfactual A — scale. Suppose the area or response time is changed while keeping some input depth or intensity fixed. Decide whether the output should scale linearly, sublinearly or nonlinearly, and explain which physical assumption controls that conclusion. For radiation and evaporation processes, the relevant limiting cases include a nearly dry condition, a very large forcing and a coefficient approaching the edge of its documented range. A model that produces negative water depth, impossible storage or negative coefficients without a clear warning is not fit for student use.

Counterfactual B — observation. Ask how a stage recorder, pluviometer, soil-moisture probe or properly maintained flow measurement could disprove the chosen interpretation. Compare two alternatives: an error in the input and an error in the process structure. Error in a timestamp may alter the inferred lag without changing storm totals; an unmodeled flow diversion may alter volume without a rainfall error. Use these fingerprints to choose the next data collection action.

Cross-topic connection. This lesson connects to reference evapotranspiration and crop demand because a model transfers its assumptions through the wider analysis. It also affects soil moisture, groundwater recharge and baseflow, which may rely on the same boundary records but at a different temporal or spatial resolution. A strong report shows these dependencies explicitly, identifies the quantities shared by two calculations and avoids treating correlated errors as independent measurements.

§3.3 Reference evapotranspiration and crop demand

Lesson 3.2 — Reference evapotranspiration and crop demand

Hydrological mechanism

Reference ET provides a weather-based atmospheric demand proxy. The Hargreaves relation uses temperature range and extraterrestrial radiation to estimate daily reference ET where other weather measurements are missing; its empirical calibration is regional. Crop coefficients and soil moisture stress are then needed to infer actual crop use. Do not insert precipitation into an ET formula without a defensible water-balance framework.

§3.4 Infiltration capacity and wetting fronts

Lesson 3.3 — Infiltration capacity and wetting fronts

Hydrological mechanism

Infiltration capacity varies with soil conductivity, capillarity, initial water content and surface conditions. The Green–Ampt model idealizes a sharp wetting front and constant effective suction. For ponded infiltration, accumulated depth follows an implicit equation that must be solved numerically. Actual infiltration cannot exceed available water and is modified by layered soils, crusting and cracks.

Governing expression and dimensions

Teaching relation: F=K t+psi DeltaTheta ln(1+F/(psi DeltaTheta)) for ponded conditions. Every term must be traced to its physical definition before numerical evaluation. A hydrologic depth expressed in millimeters corresponds to one thousand cubic meters for every square kilometer of area. §3.5 Soil moisture, groundwater recharge and baseflow

Lesson 3.4 — Soil moisture, groundwater recharge and baseflow

Hydrological mechanism

Water that infiltrates can be held in soil, taken up by plants, move laterally through the shallow subsurface or recharge groundwater. Recharge is not equivalent to infiltration at the surface. River baseflow responds to geology, aquifer connectivity and human pumping; its time course may be approximated by a recession model after effective recharge stops.
