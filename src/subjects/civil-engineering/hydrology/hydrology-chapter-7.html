§7.1 Case study: Tidal city detention pond

Case study: Tidal city detention pond

An urban catchment covers 19 hectares with assumed C=0.7. During a sustained 50 mm/h storm the Rational Method gives screening inflow approximately 1.85 m³/s. The permitted outfall is 1.0 m³/s under favorable tailwater but may be lower at high tide. The rectangular inflow assumption is not enough to size emergency storage.

Investigation task. Compare required volume for two hours under two outlet scenarios, discuss hydraulic tailwater and propose sensor placement.

The unit as part of a watershed investigation

This unit asks an engineer to defend compound drainage as a quantitative argument rather than a number inserted into a generic formula. The natural control volume is the selected hydrologic subsystem and its actual inflow and outflow boundaries. Write the unit of every parameter and specify the temporal support of the observations; an hourly intensity, a daily depth and an annual volume cannot be substituted for each other without integration. A concept sketch should show the site boundaries, measurement stations, surface and subsurface pathways, relevant human infrastructure and the time interval over which the process operates. Interpret the final result relative to the real decision at tidal city detention pond, not merely against an arbitrary calculator threshold.

Field evidence, scale and uncertainty

The most important practical complication in drought, urban drainage and water resources is that model inputs are estimates of spatially and temporally varying states. Two instruments reporting the same variable may not have comparable averaging periods or calibration. The impact of an uncertain coefficient depends on the derivative of the output with respect to that coefficient and on where the operating point falls relative to a threshold. If a predicted response appears robust, deliberately test a neighboring process that the simple model omits: channel blockage, antecedent saturation, high tailwater, sensor drift, groundwater exchange or a different storm shape. Record failures of the model openly, rather than editing the inputs until a visually pleasing curve appears.

Professional inference standard

An educational simulation is useful when it generates a falsifiable prediction. Before using any of the four lessons to make a design claim, identify an independently measurable output, a second approximation with different assumptions and a failure consequence. For tidal city detention pond, a field campaign might require time-synchronized precipitation and flow observations, a georeferenced inventory of hydraulic structures, separate dry- and wet-weather site visits and a provenance ledger for every parameter. The exercises below are intentionally synthetic and no parameter set is asserted to describe a real Bangladesh, US or other watershed. Local codes and agency-approved hydrologic methods supersede these teaching screens.

§7.2 Seasonal drought and water supply reliability

Lesson 7.1 — Seasonal drought and water supply reliability

Hydrological mechanism

Meteorological drought, agricultural soil-water deficit and hydrological flow or reservoir drought are different phenomena connected by lags. A rainless month need not coincide with the lowest river flow, and a wet annual total can hide a severe seasonal supply shortage. Reliability analysis must account for demand, available storage, environmental flows and operating constraints.

Governing expression and dimensions

Teaching relation: Supply deficit=max(0,demand-available water). Every term must be traced to its physical definition before numerical evaluation. A hydrologic depth expressed in millimeters corresponds to one thousand cubic meters for every square kilometer of area. A discharge expressed in cubic meters per second becomes volume only after integrating over seconds. A rate reported over a nominal day or hour is not a spatial average by itself. State whether the equation is a conservation balance, idealization, calibrated empirical law or fitted statistical relation; that classification determines what evidence is needed before transfer to a new catchment.

Units and source audit. For seasonal drought and water supply reliability, list the observed quantities, estimated coefficients and chosen reference conditions separately. Include the dates and methods used to obtain each. Calculate one reference state using all quantities in compatible units and round only after a dimensional check. Then reverse a sign convention or convert to a different set of units and verify that the physical meaning survives. A unit-consistent equation may still be wrong because its assumptions do not match the setting.

Applied decision: Review water allocation during a two-month dry season

The central decision is not whether a formula can be evaluated; it is whether the value carries information sufficient for an engineering action. In this situation, first identify the outlet, observation period and decision variable. Establish a low, central and high forcing scenario using values that can be defended by measurements or explicitly labeled hypotheses. Predict how the response changes before running the model. A prediction that contradicts conservation of water, plausible timing or an independently measured constraint requires investigation even when the graph looks smooth.

For this particular case the dominant caution is that demand assumptions and illegal abstraction may dominate system uncertainty. Assess which boundary condition is most likely to be violated. For an event analysis, compare the timing of inputs and outputs; for a seasonal balance, separate fluxes from storage; for statistical estimates, distinguish frequency assumptions from observed counts. Do not claim a coefficient calibrated in one environment is universal. State one missing measurement and the practical difficulty of obtaining it.

Mechanism checks and falsification

Counterfactual A — scale. Suppose the area or response time is changed while keeping some input depth or intensity fixed. Decide whether the output should scale linearly, sublinearly or nonlinearly, and explain which physical assumption controls that conclusion. For seasonal drought and water supply reliability, the relevant limiting cases include a nearly dry condition, a very large forcing and a coefficient approaching the edge of its documented range. A model that produces negative water depth, impossible storage or negative coefficients without a clear warning is not fit for student use.

Counterfactual B — observation. Ask how a stage recorder, pluviometer, soil-moisture probe or properly maintained flow measurement could disprove the chosen interpretation. Compare two alternatives: an error in the input and an error in the process structure. Error in a timestamp may alter the inferred lag without changing storm totals; an unmodeled flow diversion may alter volume without a rainfall error. Use these fingerprints to choose the next data collection action.

Cross-topic connection. This lesson connects to urban runoff and drainage capacity because a model transfers its assumptions through the wider analysis. It also affects watershed management and nature-based measures, which may rely on the same boundary records but at a different temporal or spatial resolution. A strong report shows these dependencies explicitly, identifies the quantities shared by two calculations and avoids treating correlated errors as independent measurements.

§7.3 Urban runoff and drainage capacity

Lesson 7.2 — Urban runoff and drainage capacity

Hydrological mechanism

Urban runoff is shaped by impervious connectivity, local depression storage, drainage inlets, pipes, outfalls, storm tides and receiving river levels. A simple rational peak can help screen inflow but cannot prove that a drainage network will perform. During a tidal or river flood, outlet tailwater may prevent gravity discharge even when rainfall remains unchanged. Green infrastructure can help but requires maintenance and verified infiltration capacity.

§7.4 Stormwater detention and simple mass balance

Lesson 7.3 — Stormwater detention and simple mass balance

Hydrological mechanism

A detention facility temporarily stores excess inflow and releases it through an outlet subject to restrictions. For a flat inflow and constant outlet, required volume can be estimated from excess rate times storm duration, but real design uses a time-varying hydrograph and a nonlinear stage-discharge relation. Storage calculated without freeboard, emergency overflow and sediment allowance is not a safe engineering capacity.
