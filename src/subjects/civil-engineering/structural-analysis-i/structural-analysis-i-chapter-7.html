Redundancy, Compatibility and Force Method

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical derivations, numerical modeling, and design validation of Redundancy, Compatibility and Force Method. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Structural Analysis I study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§7.1 Unit purpose

Unit purpose

Redundancy changes the logic of analysis. Determinate structures allow reactions to be found from equilibrium regardless of stiffness; redundant structures require deformations and support movement to be included. A small settlement can create significant force in a stiff restrained structure.

§7.2 Field scenario

Field scenario

A roof beam fixed at one end and touching a prop at the other can lose support contact if the end settles too far or if the load combination changes. Evaluate the analytical reaction and then decide whether the assumed support can physically apply it.

§7.3 Topics and worked reasoning

Topics and worked reasoning

Topic 7.1: Static indeterminacy and redundant choices

An indeterminate structure has more unknown generalized forces than independent equilibrium conditions, so equilibrium alone cannot identify all internal actions. Compatibility and constitutive relations supply the missing relations. The first step in the force method is to choose redundants and release enough restraints to obtain a stable determinate primary system. A poor redundant selection can generate a mechanism or a difficult flexibility calculation. The choice does not alter the correct physical answer, but it can strongly affect computation and transparency.

Engineering practice: For a propped cantilever, release the vertical reaction at the free end and analyze the remaining cantilever as the primary system. Define the redundant R_B positive upward, then express tip displacement from actual loads and from an upward unit reaction. Reintroduce the support condition through a displacement compatibility equation rather than guessing how the fixed end and prop share vertical load.

Fully evaluated study example

Consider a propped cantilever of length 6.0 m, uniform load 3.1 kN/m and a downward force 28 kN applied at the right support location. With E=200 GPa and I=2.35×10^-4 m^4, zero-settlement upward reaction is P+3wL/8=34.975 kN. If that prop settles downward 4 mm, the elastic compatibility correction is -3EI s/L^3=-2.611 kN, so the predicted reaction becomes 32.364 kN. Contact loss or support uplift would invalidate a bilateral restraint model.

Reasoning and interpretation

Is the released primary system stable? Start by writing the physical reason, then use an equation or laboratory parameter sweep to support the claim. Compare the result against an extreme case: zero applied load, negligible rise, very long span, perfectly fixed restraint, or load at a support, whichever is physically appropriate. In a valid limiting case, the response should approach a predictable behavior. When a calculation fails an extreme-case check, investigate modeling assumptions before blaming numerical precision.

Topic 7.2: Flexibility coefficients and compatibility

A single redundant X may be found from the linear relation delta_load+f X=delta_prescribed, where signs must be consistent. A flexibility coefficient f is displacement per unit generalized force and can be evaluated from a unit-load field. In a propped cantilever of span L under full uniform downward line load w, the free-end downward deflection of the released cantilever is wL to the fourth/(8EI). The vertical flexibility under an upward tip force is L cubed/(3EI) in magnitude. With zero prop settlement the resulting upward prop reaction is 3wL/8.

Engineering practice: Use the primary cantilever free-body diagram to calculate fixed-end shear and moment after finding the prop reaction. Apply global equilibrium as an independent check. Distinguish a compatibility equation imposed on displacement from an equation that merely balances vertical forces. Consider whether the prop can transfer tension if the computed redundant reverses sign.

Topic 7.3: Support settlement and thermal strain

Prescribed support movement enters compatibility even when it produces no applied external force in the equilibrium free-body diagram. If the right support of a propped cantilever settles downward by s, the compatibility equation gives R_B=3wL/8+P-3EI s/L cubed when a downward tip force P is also applied at the prop location. The tip force and prop reaction act at the same coordinate and may cancel in the released system. A signed downward settlement reduces upward prop reaction, potentially opening contact if the real support cannot pull downward.

Engineering practice: Calculate a support reaction for a specified settlement in mm after converting it to meters. Compare the result with the zero-settlement reaction and explain why the change is proportional to EI. The response may include a nonlinearity when contact opens; the simple bilateral compatibility formula should then be replaced by a contact-aware model.

Topic 7.4: Limits of first-order elastic analysis

Force-method results assume an elastic constitutive law, small deformation and a stable fixed connectivity of supports and hinges. Cracking, yielded connections, uplift, large geometric change and construction staging can alter stiffness or introduce unilateral conditions. A numerical model that converges is not proof the input idealization is appropriate. An indeterminate support arrangement may be more sensitive to temperature and settlement than a determinate one, because restrained displacement generates internal forces.

Engineering practice: Perform a parameter sweep of EI, settlement and load intensity in the propped-cantilever laboratory. Explain which output is independent of EI under zero settlement and which term depends on EI once settlement is imposed. State an explicit condition under which the chosen closed-form model would be rejected and a more complete analysis requested.

§7.4 Deep dive and misconception repair

Deep dive and misconception repair

Compatibility clinic: why equilibrium is not enough

Consider a cantilever that is fixed at the left and supported vertically at the right. If the right prop is removed, the beam is a stable cantilever that can resist the original loads. This makes the prop reaction a convenient force-method redundant. Reintroducing the prop requires the right-end deflection to match the support elevation. When the prop does not settle, the total right-end deflection must vanish; when it settles downward by s, the beam's right end must move downward by s while contact remains active.

The unloaded free-end compliance of a cantilever under a downward tip force is L³/(3EI). For a full-span line load w, free-end downward displacement is wL⁴/(8EI). An upward prop reaction R_B produces upward movement R_B L³/(3EI). Balancing these terms against prescribed settlement yields R_B=3wL/8-3EI s/L³ for distributed load alone. If an additional downward point force P acts at the prop coordinate, include P in the right-end reaction expression. The point force and prop action are at the same location but represent different physical agents.

Once R_B is known, the remaining support reactions follow from global equilibrium. For vertical load wL+P, fixed-end vertical reaction is wL+P-R_B. The fixed-end moment can be found from moments about the fixed support: distributed resultant at L/2 and tip force at L are balanced against prop reaction at L. This is a critical independent check: a compatibility solution that produces a reaction inconsistent with global equilibrium is not acceptable.

Settlement, stiffness and contact

The settlement correction contains EI. A very stiff cantilever resists a prescribed support movement strongly, so the induced redundant force can be large. Under zero settlement, by contrast, the uniform-load reaction fraction 3wL/8 is independent of EI for a prismatic beam in this simple model. This apparent contradiction disappears when the two cases are separated: imposed displacement causes a force proportional to stiffness, while purely force-driven deformation and redistribution in a uniform structure may cause stiffness to cancel from a particular reaction ratio.

A real prop often supplies compression only. If the bilateral linear-elastic formula predicts negative upward reaction, it would require the prop to pull the beam downward. A contact support cannot do that and the idealized constraint would open. The correct revised model excludes that prop and analyzes the free cantilever under the applicable loads, checking whether the gap closes elsewhere. This is a qualitative model transition, not a small numerical adjustment to the same contact equation.

Other sources of compatibility demand include thermal expansion, fabrication error, shrinkage and movement at foundations. A determinately supported member can accommodate some imposed movements without generating the same redundant actions. This is one reason redundant systems may be strong and stiff yet also sensitive to construction tolerances and support behavior. Every support settlement analysis should identify whether movement is measured relative to a fixed datum or to another moving support.
