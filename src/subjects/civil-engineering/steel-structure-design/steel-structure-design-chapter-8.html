Steel Design Practice, Fabrication, Tolerances and Fatigue

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical formulations, numerical modeling, and code-based design validation of Steel Design Practice, Fabrication, Tolerances and Fatigue. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Steel Structure Design study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§8.1 Intended learning outcomes

Intended learning outcomes

After this unit, a student should be able to construct a relevant free-body diagram, state the controlling simplifying assumptions, calculate an appropriate order-of-magnitude response, identify a competing limit state, and explain why a real building requires detailed verification under the governing jurisdiction's adopted rules.

§8.2 Design context

Design context

Prepare a condition-assessment plan for an exposed steel canopy. Use the corrosion simulation as a sensitivity screen, define actual field thickness measurement locations, and sketch drainage and coating repairs. Include a fire-exposure review and a verification matrix with remaining uncertainties assigned to responsible specialists.

A productive way to study this unit is to connect its smallest material or member model to the whole building. Every force has an entry point and an exit point; every brace must connect to a supporting system; and every design resistance belongs to a particular structural limit state. The educational calculations in this book aim to train reliable modeling judgment, not to automate sign-off. When the calculation appears simple, spend additional time determining whether the assumed support, material response and loading state can actually occur.

§8.3 Fabrication tolerances and constructability

Lesson 8.1: Fabrication tolerances and constructability

Physical principle

Fabricated structures differ from ideal centerline models because parts contain tolerances, weld shrinkage, camber, out-of-straightness and hole clearance. These variations are not automatically defects; design specifications establish acceptable ranges and analysis may already rely on expected imperfections.

Engineering method and interpretation

Review fabrication drawings for workable splices, shipping lengths, access openings, weld sequences and erection equipment clearances. Coordinate structural, architectural and MEP penetrations: cutting an unreviewed hole in a beam web can disrupt shear flow and invalidate a connection design.

Worked engineering illustration

A 12 m fabricated beam with specified precamber might appear straight under dead load once placed. The predicted deflection depends on actual load, end restraint and composite action; geometric camber does not increase the elastic bending stiffness EI.

Model boundary and field check

A completed analysis is only one element of a delivered structure. Record tolerances, inspection hold points and approved substitutions so the model matches the steel built on site.

§8.4 Corrosion, coatings and inspection planning

Lesson 8.2: Corrosion, coatings and inspection planning

Physical principle

Atmospheric corrosion requires moisture, oxygen and suitable electrochemical conditions, and the local rate varies widely with exposure, drainage, coating condition and details that trap water. Uniform section-loss extrapolation is a useful sensitivity tool but rarely a reliable forecast without field measurements and a documented environment.

Engineering method and interpretation

Design details for drainage, access, maintenance and compatible coating systems. Choose corrosion protection for actual exposure rather than applying one universal paint schedule. Inspect inaccessible crevices, contact surfaces and dissimilar metals; localized pits can be more damaging than average thinning.

Worked engineering illustration

A plate initially 16 mm thick losing an assumed 0.035 mm per year on each exposed broad face would have modeled thickness 13.9 mm after 30 years. If width is unchanged, nominal gross area reduces by 13.125%, but real pits may cause local demand concentrations.

Model boundary and field check

Do not translate the illustration into remaining service life without corrosion data, coating history, actual stress demand, inspection findings and code-based evaluation.

§8.5 Fire behavior and protection strategy

Lesson 8.3: Fire behavior and protection strategy

Physical principle

Steel stiffness and strength change with increasing temperature, while heated members also expand and can experience restraint forces. Compartment fire exposure is nonuniform in space and time; heating rate depends on section factor, protection, ventilation and heat transfer. A simple room temperature design check cannot establish fire resistance.

Engineering method and interpretation

Identify the required fire rating and accepted verification route. Review passive protection continuity around connections, penetrations and exposed members. Coordinated fire scenarios may require thermal analysis, structural response and construction details that remain protective after maintenance.

Worked engineering illustration

Using α≈12×10⁻⁶ per °C only as an illustrative ambient thermal-expansion coefficient, a freely expanding 6 m steel member heated uniformly by 100°C changes length by ΔL≈7.2 mm. If expansion is restrained, additional force develops, depending on surrounding stiffness and thermal gradients.

Model boundary and field check

Real elevated-temperature material properties are nonlinear and grade-dependent. Use tested systems or approved fire design methods, never a simple ambient-temperature elasticity model.

§8.6 Integrated verification and engineering communication

Lesson 8.4: Integrated verification and engineering communication

Physical principle

A defensible design package tells a reviewer what was modeled, which codes and combinations governed, how capacities were checked, and what details realize the assumed load paths. Engineering communication requires reproducible units and assumptions rather than screenshots of untraceable software output.

Engineering method and interpretation

Create a verification matrix covering material grade and product, loads and combination, global stability, member strength, serviceability, connections, durability, fire and construction stages. Cross-reference drawings to calculations and inspection requirements. Where uncertainties remain, identify the person authorized to resolve them.

Worked engineering illustration

For an illustrative roof system, the verification chain might begin with 1.8 kN/m² surface load, convert it into 12 kN/m beam line load for a tributary width of 6.67 m, evaluate beam reaction and moments, and then check bolt forces, column stability and base bearing. Every handoff must retain the correct load level and units.

Model boundary and field check

A passing calculation that assumes uninstalled restraints or ignores fabricator feedback is incomplete. Independent checking and responsible engineering sign-off are fundamental requirements for any real structure.

§8.7 Numerical case studies

Numerical case studies

Case 8.1: Uniform corrosion sensitivity

Given: Plate width=250 mm, initial thickness=14 mm, loss=0.04 mm/year per broad face, duration=25 years.

Computation: Remaining thickness=14−2×0.04×25=12 mm; remaining area=3000 mm².

Interpretation: Localized pitting, environment and inspection data are excluded; assumed rate is not a service-life prediction.

Independent audit: Before accepting the numerical answer, identify the dimension of the resulting quantity and confirm it matches the left-hand side of the formula. Replace the nominal input with an independently selected second input and repeat the calculation by hand. A single correct number can conceal an incorrect sign convention, wrong load level, or missing connection effect. State the physical phenomenon left outside the formula.

Case 8.2: Free steel thermal expansion

Given: α=12×10⁻⁶/°C, L=6500 mm, temperature rise=85°C.

Computation: Free expansion αLΔT≈6.63 mm.

Interpretation: Restrained expansion may generate forces: free expansion alone cannot predict them.

Unit engineering studio

Deliverables: A one-page model statement; free-body diagrams with force directions; a tabulated input sheet with units and named sources; independent hand checks; one critical alternative model; a note on code and construction-stage exclusions; and a short professional-style design review. No project outcome should be labeled an approved structural capacity.
