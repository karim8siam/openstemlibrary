Compression Members, Column Buckling and Slenderness

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical formulations, numerical modeling, and code-based design validation of Compression Members, Column Buckling and Slenderness. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Steel Structure Design study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§3.1 Intended learning outcomes

Intended learning outcomes

After this unit, a student should be able to construct a relevant free-body diagram, state the controlling simplifying assumptions, calculate an appropriate order-of-magnitude response, identify a competing limit state, and explain why a real building requires detailed verification under the governing jurisdiction's adopted rules.

§3.2 Design context

Design context

Select a prismatic column and calculate Euler sensitivity to length and boundary conditions along both principal axes. Create a restraint plan for intermediate bracing and specify how the brace carries its reaction to the rest of the frame. Avoid claiming an effective length factor without a stiffness justification.

A productive way to study this unit is to connect its smallest material or member model to the whole building. Every force has an entry point and an exit point; every brace must connect to a supporting system; and every design resistance belongs to a particular structural limit state. The educational calculations in this book aim to train reliable modeling judgment, not to automate sign-off. When the calculation appears simple, spend additional time determining whether the assumed support, material response and loading state can actually occur.

§3.3 Euler instability and geometric sensitivity

Lesson 3.1: Euler instability and geometric sensitivity

Physical principle

Compression introduces an equilibrium instability: a perfectly straight column can admit an alternative laterally deflected configuration once an eigenvalue is reached. Euler’s ideal result π²EI/(KL)² explains why unsupported length and weak-axis stiffness dominate slender-column behavior; it does not define a reliable design strength for a real imperfect column.

Engineering method and interpretation

Inspect end restraints carefully. A member pinned within one plane may be restrained differently in the other plane. The effective-length coefficient is a representation of system stability and connection stiffness, not a universal property of a steel grade. Distinguish between member buckling and entire-frame sidesway.

Worked engineering illustration

For E=200,000 N/mm², I=20×10⁶ mm⁴, effective length KL=5000 mm, Euler load is π²×200,000×20×10⁶/(5000²)=1.579×10⁶ N, approximately 1579 kN. Doubling KL divides this value by four.

Model boundary and field check

Initial crookedness, residual stress and eccentric load introduce deflection before the perfect-column eigenvalue. Use the governing code’s compression curve or stability analysis for actual design.

§3.4 Column slenderness and axis identification

Lesson 3.2: Column slenderness and axis identification

Physical principle

The radius of gyration r=√(I/A) packages stiffness relative to area. The slenderness KL/r is dimensionless but must be computed about the relevant buckling axis. A column with strong major-axis bending capacity can still buckle first about its weak axis, depending on restraint and member geometry.

Engineering method and interpretation

Extract both Iy and Ix from the section and determine the effective lengths for each. Check intermediate lateral supports and whether a restraint can actually transfer force to a stable subsystem. A beam that touches a column is not automatically a qualified brace: connection and diaphragm stiffness matter.

Worked engineering illustration

For area 5200 mm² and weak-axis I=24×10⁶ mm⁴, radius r=√(24,000,000/5200)=67.94 mm. With K=1 and L=6000 mm, KL/r≈88.3. A numerical comparison with a different steel grade should hold geometry constant to isolate material effects.

Model boundary and field check

Increasing yield strength does not increase elastic Euler buckling load if elastic modulus and geometry remain unchanged. Shape and bracing can be more effective than material grade.

§3.5 Local buckling and built-up sections

Lesson 3.3: Local buckling and built-up sections

Physical principle

An I-section flange outstand, a web panel and a hollow-section wall have different edge restraints and stress patterns. Their width-to-thickness ratios therefore have different stability thresholds. Thin plate elements may buckle before the member can mobilize a simple gross-area yield force, even when the overall column is short.

Engineering method and interpretation

Classify each compression element under the chosen specification before using a compact-section formula. For built-up columns inspect lacing, batten plates, weld spacing, eccentricity between components and shear flexibility. Overall flexural buckling does not capture the local behavior of a slender connector panel.

Worked engineering illustration

A 180 mm wide flat element that is 9 mm thick has b/t=20. Doubling thickness reduces the ratio to 10, but whether either geometry is acceptable depends on element type, stress distribution, material strength, code thresholds and fabrication tolerances.

Model boundary and field check

Do not use the lab’s plotted √(E/fy) line as a code limit. It is merely a material-property reference scale intended to show trends.

§3.6 Compression system checks and column bases

Lesson 3.4: Compression system checks and column bases

Physical principle

The load on a column passes through a base plate, anchors, grout and supporting concrete or foundation. A compression member with adequate axial resistance can still cause a local bearing, base-plate bending or anchor failure. Moment and shear may also reach the base even when a drawing idealizes it as pinned.

Engineering method and interpretation

Prepare a stability map showing unbraced lengths, frame sway assumptions, weak-axis directions, moment transfer, base rotational stiffness and construction-stage bracing. Inspect potential torsional or torsional–flexural modes for open, singly symmetric and specially shaped columns.

Worked engineering illustration

If a 1500 kN compressive force is spread uniformly on an ideal 400×400 mm contact area, average pressure is 1500,000/160,000=9.375 MPa. The actual distribution beneath a base plate with moment is nonuniform, and concrete bearing resistance is not equal to steel yield stress.

Model boundary and field check

Temporary base plates, anchor setting tolerances and erection sequences can govern during construction; permanent frame analysis should not be assumed valid before all braces are connected.

§3.7 Numerical case studies

Numerical case studies

Case 3.1: Euler load for a weak-axis member

Given: E=200000 MPa, I=22×10⁶ mm⁴, K=1.0, L=6000 mm.

Computation: Pcr=π²EI/(KL)²≈1206 kN.

Interpretation: A different K or restraining geometry changes critical load. This is not an imperfection-adjusted design strength.

Independent audit: Before accepting the numerical answer, identify the dimension of the resulting quantity and confirm it matches the left-hand side of the formula. Replace the nominal input with an independently selected second input and repeat the calculation by hand. A single correct number can conceal an incorrect sign convention, wrong load level, or missing connection effect. State the physical phenomenon left outside the formula.

Case 3.2: Radius of gyration and effective slenderness

Given: A=5400 mm², I=22×10⁶ mm⁴, K=0.9, L=6100 mm.

Computation: r=√(I/A)=63.83 mm; KL/r≈86.0.

Interpretation: Evaluate both axes separately and ensure a brace really restrains the purported buckling mode.

Lesson 3.4: Compression system checks and column bases

Physical principle

The load on a column passes through a base plate, anchors, grout and supporting concrete or foundation. A compression member with adequate axial resistance can still cause a local bearing, base-plate bending or anchor failure. Moment and shear may also reach the base even when a drawing idealizes it as pinned.

Engineering method and interpretation

Prepare a stability map showing unbraced lengths, frame sway assumptions, weak-axis directions, moment transfer, base rotational stiffness and construction-stage bracing. Inspect potential torsional or torsional–flexural modes for open, singly symmetric and specially shaped columns.

Worked engineering illustration

If a 1500 kN compressive force is spread uniformly on an ideal 400×400 mm contact area, average pressure is 1500,000/160,000=9.375 MPa. The actual distribution beneath a base plate with moment is nonuniform, and concrete bearing resistance is not equal to steel yield stress.

Model boundary and field check

Temporary base plates, anchor setting tolerances and erection sequences can govern during construction; permanent frame analysis should not be assumed valid before all braces are connected.
