Connections: Bolted, Welded and Moment Resisting

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical formulations, numerical modeling, and code-based design validation of Connections: Bolted, Welded and Moment Resisting. Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Steel Structure Design study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§6.1 Intended learning outcomes

Intended learning outcomes

After this unit, a student should be able to construct a relevant free-body diagram, state the controlling simplifying assumptions, calculate an appropriate order-of-magnitude response, identify a competing limit state, and explain why a real building requires detailed verification under the governing jurisdiction's adopted rules.

§6.2 Design context

Design context

For a steel beam-to-column joint, sketch a force path through either a bolted end plate or a welded detail. Quantify an illustrative bolt-group elastic shear distribution and a direct shear weld throat stress. Evaluate fabrication access, quality assurance, maintenance and inspection; do not label either option code compliant without full checks.

A productive way to study this unit is to connect its smallest material or member model to the whole building. Every force has an entry point and an exit point; every brace must connect to a supporting system; and every design resistance belongs to a particular structural limit state. The educational calculations in this book aim to train reliable modeling judgment, not to automate sign-off. When the calculation appears simple, spend additional time determining whether the assumed support, material response and loading state can actually occur.

§6.3 Bolt-group mechanics and load sharing

Lesson 6.1: Bolt-group mechanics and load sharing

Physical principle

Bolted joints transfer load by mechanisms such as bolt shear, plate bearing or controlled slip resistance depending on construction and connection class. Bolt strength alone does not define joint strength: net fracture, block shear, plate tear-out, edge geometry and prying can govern. The force distribution may be eccentric and nonuniform.

Engineering method and interpretation

Identify the applied shear and moment at the actual joint reference point. For an elastic bolt-group calculation, position fasteners around their centroid and combine direct force per bolt with the secondary components created by eccentricity. Use a consistent sign convention and confirm vector equilibrium after computing bolt resultants.

Worked engineering illustration

For a four-bolt group resisting 120 kN direct vertical shear, a perfectly concentric load gives 30 kN per bolt in an equal-sharing idealization. A 100 mm eccentricity adds a 12 kN·m group moment, increasing some bolt forces and reducing others, depending on bolt coordinates.

Model boundary and field check

A joint may require slip control even when a bearing-type ultimate-strength check appears satisfactory. Service movement, pretension installation and inspection requirements must be selected from the governing code.

§6.4 Welded connections and load introduction

Lesson 6.2: Welded connections and load introduction

Physical principle

Fillet welds carry force through their effective throat rather than the nominal leg dimension. Continuous or intermittent weld layouts influence stress flow, heat input and distortion. Weld metal strength, base-metal strength and fatigue details are not interchangeable; joint access and welding procedure qualification affect whether the intended geometry is constructible.

Engineering method and interpretation

Start by resolving the actual direction and eccentricity of the transmitted force. A straight shear path can be estimated by force over effective throat area; a moment-carrying weld group needs spatial distribution of stress. Include fit-up, gap, minimum size, heat-affected regions and inspection access in the design review.

Worked engineering illustration

Two 180 mm-long fillet welds of 6 mm leg size have ideal effective throat area 2×180×0.707×6≈1527 mm². If they transmit 160 kN in pure direct shear with equal sharing, the mean throat stress is about 104.8 MPa, prior to any code resistance or fatigue check.

Model boundary and field check

Welds need fabrication controls and qualified procedures. A theoretical stress below a nominal number does not demonstrate a sound weld or a code-compliant detail.

§6.5 End plates, base plates and connection stiffness

Lesson 6.3: End plates, base plates and connection stiffness

Physical principle

End-plate connections, seated connections, web shear plates and moment joints differ by rotational stiffness, ductility and failure mechanism. A frame model using ideal pins may be inconsistent with a physically stiff welded end plate; a model using rigid joints may overestimate the moment transfer of a flexible connection.

Engineering method and interpretation

Sketch the internal load path through bolt tension, plate bending, column flange bending, web shear and any stiffeners. For column bases include anchor tension, compressive bearing, grout, plate flexure and foundation force transfer. Where prying action matters, bolt tension can exceed a naive applied-force distribution.

Worked engineering illustration

A connection resisting 200 kN·m using a lever arm of 0.4 m between resultant tension and compression forces requires roughly 500 kN in each resultant for an ideal couple. This is only a force-equilibrium starting point, not a calculation of individual bolt tension or end-plate bending.

Model boundary and field check

Connection stiffness and ductility can govern system robustness. Confirm that the connection can realize the behavior assumed in global analysis throughout construction and service.

§6.6 Connection detailing, fatigue and inspection

Lesson 6.4: Connection detailing, fatigue and inspection

Physical principle

Connections concentrate geometric discontinuities, residual stress and fabrication variation. Repeated load reversals can initiate fatigue cracks at weld toes, bolt holes or abrupt stiffness changes. Good detailing reduces secondary bending and improves access for corrosion protection and nondestructive inspection.

Engineering method and interpretation

A connection schedule should record bolt grades, hole types, edge/pitch requirements, welding specifications, surface treatments and inspection scope. Look for clashes among bolts, washers, flanges, decking and installation tools; a detail that cannot be assembled is not a usable design.

Worked engineering illustration

A bolted lap splice may transfer static force through bearing while experiencing initial slip under cyclic loads if bolts are not pretensioned as assumed. The consequent change in deformation can matter for fatigue-sensitive equipment supports or repeated bridge loading.

Model boundary and field check

The correct RCSC or local fastener specification, fabrication procedures and quality assurance requirements must be checked. A generic teaching diagram cannot certify a production joint.

§6.7 Numerical case studies

Numerical case studies

Case 6.1: Direct shear in a fastener group

Given: V=160 kN, four identical bolts, concentric loading.

Computation: Equal-sharing force=40 kN per bolt.

Interpretation: Eccentricity, stiffness variation, bolt-hole slip and prying can make the real distribution different.

Independent audit: Before accepting the numerical answer, identify the dimension of the resulting quantity and confirm it matches the left-hand side of the formula. Replace the nominal input with an independently selected second input and repeat the calculation by hand. A single correct number can conceal an incorrect sign convention, wrong load level, or missing connection effect. State the physical phenomenon left outside the formula.

Case 6.2: Ideal throat area of paired fillet welds

Given: Two welds each 160 mm long, leg size 8 mm, direct shear=180 kN.

Computation: Total throat≈2×160×0.707×8=1809.92 mm²; average shear≈99.45 MPa.

Interpretation: The result is stress demand, not an allowable or nominal weld resistance.
