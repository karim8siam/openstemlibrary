Combined Axial and Bending Actions (Beam-Columns)

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental theory, mathematical formulations, numerical modeling, and code-based design validation of Combined Axial and Bending Actions (Beam-Columns). Developed for undergraduate civil engineering study.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Steel Structure Design study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§5.1 Intended learning outcomes

Intended learning outcomes

After this unit, a student should be able to construct a relevant free-body diagram, state the controlling simplifying assumptions, calculate an appropriate order-of-magnitude response, identify a competing limit state, and explain why a real building requires detailed verification under the governing jurisdiction's adopted rules.

§5.2 Design context

Design context

Build a two-story portal idealization and track gravity loads, lateral drift and second-order demand. Compare the first-order result with the educational P–Δ screening multiplier. Document why global and member instability must not be conflated. Identify required code-specific stiffness reductions, imperfections and combinations before professional use.

A productive way to study this unit is to connect its smallest material or member model to the whole building. Every force has an entry point and an exit point; every brace must connect to a supporting system; and every design resistance belongs to a particular structural limit state. The educational calculations in this book aim to train reliable modeling judgment, not to automate sign-off. When the calculation appears simple, spend additional time determining whether the assumed support, material response and loading state can actually occur.

§5.3 Axial force with bending and torsion

Lesson 5.1: Axial force with bending and torsion

Physical principle

Real columns and beam-columns rarely carry perfectly concentric compression. Frame sway, eccentric gravity loads, connection geometry and lateral forces create moments together with axial load. The response combines material stress with stability effects, and the interaction cannot generally be verified by adding two unrelated allowable stress values.

Engineering method and interpretation

Separate first-order internal actions from second-order amplification. Determine whether the member experiences uniaxial or biaxial flexure, shear and torsion. A section interaction diagram can be useful for understanding competing resistances, but member instability requires separate consideration and code-specific equations.

Worked engineering illustration

Consider an illustrative reference P_ref=1500 kN and M_ref=410 kN·m. A demand of P=850 kN, M=180 kN·m gives a simple unity-sum screen 850/1500+180/410≈1.006. This index is not an AISC or Eurocode acceptance calculation.

Model boundary and field check

A load path that appears acceptable when moments are small may become sensitive to bending once the column is slender. The correct interaction check must account for the frame system and limit state.

§5.4 P–Delta and P–delta phenomena

Lesson 5.2: P–Delta and P–delta phenomena

Physical principle

Global P–Δ effects arise when compressive gravity loads act through a laterally displaced story. Member P–δ effects involve curvature along an individual compressed member. Both create additional moment and can increase deformation, but their magnitudes depend on elastic and geometric stiffness and boundary conditions.

Engineering method and interpretation

Examine story drift, gravity loads, notional imperfections and stability bracing. In a numerical frame model, confirm whether second-order analysis and imperfections are active, whether effective stiffness is appropriate, and which load combinations govern. A first-order moment diagram alone may hide a sensitive sidesway system.

Worked engineering illustration

The simplified single-mode multiplier 1/(1−P/Pcr) equals 1.667 at P/Pcr=0.4 and 5.0 at P/Pcr=0.8. It illustrates how rapidly the estimate grows near instability; it does not replace a second-order frame analysis or a specification-required stability method.

Model boundary and field check

Never extrapolate the simple multiplier above P/Pcr or conclude that a negative factor represents stable behavior. Real structures show imperfections, nonlinear response and potentially different failure modes.

§5.5 Frame stability and lateral systems

Lesson 5.3: Frame stability and lateral systems

Physical principle

Braced and moment-resisting frames transmit lateral loads in different ways. A diagonal brace develops axial force and can create concentrated collector forces; a moment frame uses joint rotations and member flexure. Both are three-dimensional systems even if an idealized plane model is used for a particular calculation.

Engineering method and interpretation

Model diaphragm force transfer, anchorage, brace stiffness, connection behavior and realistic support conditions. Check whether removing a single brace creates a soft story or an unintended mechanism. Identify which frame members act as gravity columns and which contribute directly to the lateral system.

Worked engineering illustration

For a 4 m story with horizontal displacement of 20 mm, the apparent drift ratio is 20/4000=0.005, or 0.5%. Whether this is acceptable depends on structural system, load case, nonstructural components and governing standard.

Model boundary and field check

Nonlinear connection behavior, bolt slip, residual stress and geometric imperfections can modify measured stiffness. Design assumptions must remain compatible with detailing and inspection.

§5.6 Practical combined-load verification

Lesson 5.4: Practical combined-load verification

Physical principle

A reliable design review follows demand from global analysis to individual sections, members, connections and foundations. Software outputs should have reproducible units, axes and sign conventions. The reviewer should know whether the analysis used cracked composite stiffness, effective steel stiffness, releases and consistent boundary conditions.

Engineering method and interpretation

Construct independent order-of-magnitude checks for gravity reactions, portal drift, strong and weak-axis bending and axial force. A complex model should be challenged when its results contradict load path intuition. Check member end moments together with connection capacities and potential unbalanced joint loads.

Worked engineering illustration

If a column supports 650 kN gravity force and 45 kN horizontal story shear through a 3.6 m story, an isolated cantilever analogy gives first-order base moment 45×3.6=162 kN·m. A real frame may share this shear and moment through several members; the analogy is useful only as a screening bound.

Model boundary and field check

Design review is incomplete if forces are checked but fabrication drawings, connection detailing, erection restraints and corrosion/fire conditions are not considered.

§5.7 Numerical case studies

Numerical case studies

Case 5.1: P–Delta amplification screening

Given: P=400 kN, ideal Pcr=1000 kN, δ0=12 mm.

Computation: P/Pcr=0.4; ideal multiplier=1.667; δ=20 mm.

Interpretation: As P approaches Pcr the linearized formula becomes extremely sensitive and is not a code-approved design method.

Independent audit: Before accepting the numerical answer, identify the dimension of the resulting quantity and confirm it matches the left-hand side of the formula. Replace the nominal input with an independently selected second input and repeat the calculation by hand. A single correct number can conceal an incorrect sign convention, wrong load level, or missing connection effect. State the physical phenomenon left outside the formula.
