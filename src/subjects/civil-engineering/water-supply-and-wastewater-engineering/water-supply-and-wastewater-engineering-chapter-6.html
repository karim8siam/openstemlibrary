§6.1 Biological treatment and process control

Unit 6: Biological treatment and process control

Engineering problem

Use mass balances and reaction-rate thinking to interpret primary, secondary and nutrient-removal treatment.

System context. A regional utility and its engineering consultant must keep household access, source protection, operating budget, plant controls, maintenance, public-health risk and the downstream environment in a single decision record. This unit focuses on materials characterization, oxygen kinetics, microbial inventory, nutrient transformations. A calculated capacity or ideal removal percentage only answers a narrow question; it never certifies operation.

What to carry into the next unit

Treat assumptions as named data rather than invisible constants. Where a model omits fluctuating inflow, temperature, maintenance, unserved customers or instrument uncertainty, state the omission before drawing conclusions. The case studies show how apparently plausible outputs can fail after a changed boundary condition.

Lesson sequence

  • Lesson 1: [Primary treatment and influent fractionation](lessons/unit-06-lesson-01.md)
  • Lesson 2: [BOD kinetics, oxygen demand and process time](lessons/unit-06-lesson-02.md)
  • Lesson 3: [Activated sludge loading and oxygen balance](lessons/unit-06-lesson-03.md)
  • Lesson 4: [Nitrification, denitrification and phosphorus removal](lessons/unit-06-lesson-04.md)

Operating envelope

The linked lessons address Primary treatment and influent fractionation; BOD kinetics, oxygen demand and process time; Activated sludge loading and oxygen balance; Nitrification, denitrification and phosphorus removal. Separate predictable day-to-day variation from rare hazardous events. A well-built model should expose its domain: the range of flow, concentration, temperature and operating condition over which its assumptions are defensible. Utility investigations often require repeat observations during dry weather and storms, and distribution or collection networks need spatially distributed data rather than a single inlet measurement.

Structured investigation

Choose one real or synthetic utility. Sketch a flow schematic with three explicitly labeled boundaries: incoming quantity and quality, transformed or stored inventory, and outgoing quantity and quality. Provide at least one mass or energy balance, justify the time horizon, and make a quantitative uncertainty or sensitivity comparison. The main practical limitation to discuss is a bod rate constant depends on the test definition and temperature.

Conditions to challenge

  • A BOD rate constant depends on the test definition and temperature.
  • MLSS is not equivalent to active biomass.
  • Nutrient removal depends on actual redox zones, carbon and alkalinity.

§6.2 Primary treatment and influent fractionation

Unit 6 · Lesson 1: Primary treatment and influent fractionation

Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, explain materials characterization using a defensible system boundary; identify the measurements needed to test a conceptual calculation; and distinguish an illustrative formula from an engineering acceptance decision.

Core reasoning

Screening, grit removal and primary clarification prepare influent for downstream biological processes while protecting equipment. Total suspended solids, soluble substrate and particulate substrate do not behave identically.

Unlike a textbook exercise that starts by offering all required parameters, a real engineering team normally starts with incomplete measurements. The first analytical task is to decide which boundary encloses the operation being investigated. For primary treatment and influent fractionation, confirm whether the accounting period is hourly, daily, seasonal, or lifetime; whether the reported figures refer to flow, mass or concentration; and where streams enter, leave or are temporarily stored. If a reservoir is refilling, the local flow balance must include a storage term. If a laboratory result is a concentration, multiplying by the simultaneous volumetric flow gives a load; the two quantities answer different planning questions.

Data quality determines what can be inferred. Field instruments need locations and calibration dates. Laboratory measurements need sampling protocols, preservation details and timestamps. Maintenance logs and operator observations often explain anomalies that disappear from a monthly mean. When a parameter is only an estimate, calculate sensitivity before arguing for a larger structure or a more powerful pump. A high flow multiplier cannot rescue a mislabeled unit, and an efficient machine cannot remove contaminants that its process is not designed to target.

If 7,000 m³/day carries 210 mg/L TSS, its influent solids load is 1,470 kg/day. A 55% removal gives about 808.5 kg/day captured in this simplified balance.

§6.5 Nitrification, denitrification and phosphorus removal

By the end of this lesson, explain nutrient transformations using a defensible system boundary; identify the measurements needed to test a conceptual calculation; and distinguish an illustrative formula from an engineering acceptance decision.

Core reasoning

Nitrogen and phosphorus processes respond to dissolved oxygen, anoxic volume, carbon availability, temperature, alkalinity and solids retention. Nitrification consumes oxygen and alkalinity, while denitrification can recover alkalinity.
