§2.1 Ohm’s Law and Linear Resistance

U02-L01 · Ohm’s Law and Linear Resistance

Unit: 2 — Resistive Networks Core relation: v=Ri Essential ideas: resistance, conductance, V–I characteristics, and material limits.

Learning objectives

After this lesson, explain ohm’s law and linear resistance and its circuit context, assign reference polarities and directions, derive or use v=Ri with SI units, solve a numerical example, and evaluate its validity using conservation laws and a practical limiting case.

Physical and mathematical foundation

Ohm’s law describes an ideal linear resistor at a specified operating condition. The selected current arrow and voltage polarity give meaning to the signs in an equation; they do not force a physical quantity to flow in that direction. Nodes define equal-potential connection points in the lumped model. Branches define component connections, and a chosen ground provides a mathematical reference rather than a physical energy sink. Interpreting the equation v=Ri requires knowing both the connected terminals and the applicable ideal-component assumptions.

Within resistive networks, distinguish a source-imposed quantity from a circuit response. A voltage source constrains one terminal difference, while a current source constrains a branch current. An element's constitutive relation describes its response but cannot replace Kirchhoff's laws. If a diagram changes, the electrical topology may change even when the drawn symbols and their numerical values remain the same.

Concept 1 — Resistance. Identify the branch or terminals to which this idea applies, state its units, and connect it with v=Ri. Explain what changes when the source or component parameter increases; predict the direction of the change before doing arithmetic. Consider whether reference directions, instrument loading, or tolerance could distort the interpretation.

Concept 2 — Conductance. Identify the branch or terminals to which this idea applies, state its units, and connect it with v=Ri. Explain what changes when the source or component parameter increases; predict the direction of the change before doing arithmetic. Consider whether reference directions, instrument loading, or tolerance could distort the interpretation.

Concept 3 — V–i characteristics. Identify the branch or terminals to which this idea applies, state its units, and connect it with v=Ri. Explain what changes when the source or component parameter increases; predict the direction of the change before doing arithmetic. Consider whether reference directions, instrument loading, or tolerance could distort the interpretation.

Result. The current is 0.050 A, and the resistor dissipates 0.55 W. First translate all prefixes into SI units and state the formula before substituting. Verify that the dimensions of the answer agree with the quantity being requested. Reverse the calculation, or test a meaningful limiting case, rather than trusting the displayed calculator result alone.

Interpretation. Ohm’s law describes an ideal linear resistor at a specified operating condition. A negative signed answer may only mean that a current or voltage points opposite to its reference. It does not indicate an impossible circuit. When multiple elements appear, check their node-current balance, the signed sum of drops around a loop, and the sum of absorbed and delivered powers.

A reliable solution method

  1. Draw the topology, identify nodes, and choose polarities and branch-current arrows; write down which source and element quantities are known.
  2. Combine the component relation v=Ri with KCL, KVL, and state continuity where relevant; solve symbolically before substituting numbers.
  3. Convert units, calculate, and cross-check using a conservation equation or limiting case. Explain why the result is physically plausible.
  4. Identify the dominant idealization. A real resistor can change value with temperature and can exceed its power rating. If any predicted value approaches a component rating, the analysis is only a starting point for a safety-qualified design review.
