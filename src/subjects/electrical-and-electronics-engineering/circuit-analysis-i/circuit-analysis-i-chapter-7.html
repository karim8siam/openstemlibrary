§7.1 RLC State Equations

U07-L01 · RLC State Equations

Unit: 7 — Second-Order Circuits Core relation: L q¨+R q˙+q/C=0 Essential ideas: second-order ODEs, energy exchange, and state selection.

Learning objectives

After this lesson, explain rlc state equations and its circuit context, assign reference polarities and directions, derive or use L q¨+R q˙+q/C=0 with SI units, solve a numerical example, and evaluate its validity using conservation laws and a practical limiting case.

Physical and mathematical foundation

Capacitor voltage and inductor current are natural states for a lumped second-order model. The selected current arrow and voltage polarity give meaning to the signs in an equation; they do not force a physical quantity to flow in that direction. Nodes define equal-potential connection points in the lumped model. Branches define component connections, and a chosen ground provides a mathematical reference rather than a physical energy sink. Interpreting the equation L q¨+R q˙+q/C=0 requires knowing both the connected terminals and the applicable ideal-component assumptions.

Within second-order circuits, distinguish a source-imposed quantity from a circuit response. A voltage source constrains one terminal difference, while a current source constrains a branch current. An element's constitutive relation describes its response but cannot replace Kirchhoff's laws. If a diagram changes, the electrical topology may change even when the drawn symbols and their numerical values remain the same.
