Unit 4 capstone — Low-power IIR architecture challenge

Engineering problem

Design a two-stage causal IIR smoother, show exact-real-number pole stability and compare 16-bit section ordering and saturation effects. The capstone intentionally uses invented signals, not field measurements. The unit provides the mathematical concepts: Z-plane poles and causal stability, First-order recursive smoothing, Second-order biquad resonators, Analog prototype mapping and warping, Second-order sections and numerical robustness. Write a concise problem statement and choose consistent notation for time, sample index, physical units and the transform convention. Keep raw input parameters in a table separate from derived outputs.

Required submissions

  1. A mathematical model listing all assumptions, independent variables and initial conditions or finite record lengths.
  2. A hand-verifiable result with the steps from governing equation to value or functional expression.
  3. One independent computation, using a different method from the hand calculation, with an explicit discrepancy tolerance.
  4. A plot that labels units, sample rate, window and phase/frequency conventions when relevant.
  5. Two stress tests: one boundary case and one deliberate model violation with the expected failure symptoms.
  6. A short engineering report distinguishing verification of code from validation against physical data.

Evaluation rubric (100 points)

  • Mathematical and physical specification: 20.
  • Correct derivations and units: 25.
  • Independently reproducible numerical result: 20.
  • Boundary and violation tests: 20.
  • Clear interpretation and data provenance: 15.

Discussion prompts

Explain why your assumptions are necessary; demonstrate whether changing the record length or excitation changes the result; discuss which error is numerical and which is modeling error; and propose a minimal real-world test to determine whether the simplified model is appropriate.
