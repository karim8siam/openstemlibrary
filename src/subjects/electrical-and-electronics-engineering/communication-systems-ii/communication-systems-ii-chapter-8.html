§8.1 Theoretical Overview: Convolutional Codes, Trellis Diagrams, Viterbi Decoding Algorithm, and Modern FEC

Unit 8 — Practical link and network engineering

Why this unit exists

The unit centers on emergency communications service, where a regional network carries mixed urgent control traffic and bulk data. Its controlling difficulty is that latency tails and observed delivery reliability are contractual requirements. The four lessons isolate different parts of the mechanism, yet the delivered system must satisfy all of them at once. You will link equations to implementation, design measurements and deliberately test situations in which a convenient idealization becomes misleading.

Concept sequence

Lesson 8.1 — Adaptive modulation and coding

The most aggressive modulation is rarely optimal for delivered payload. Higher spectral efficiency increases raw bits per symbol but also raises the required signal quality for a specified error target. The scheduler must weigh payload goodput, retransmission cost, interference exposure and channel-estimation delay. A threshold table can become unstable if it switches schemes on noisy near-threshold measurements. Hysteresis and smoothing help, but slow adaptation may lag a rapidly changing link. An outer-loop controller can adjust thresholds based on observed acknowledgments rather than assuming perfect channel calibration.

Key relation: A link-adaptation controller selects a modulation/coding scheme using estimated channel quality, predicted packet error and implementation-specific throughput tables.

Design boundary: Goodput figures are dependent on traffic pattern, failure correlation, headers and retransmission details. A controller optimized using mean SNR can fail severely during rare fades.

Lesson 8.2 — ARQ, hybrid ARQ and retransmission

Automatic repeat request uses error detection and feedback to resend missing or corrupted frames. Stop-and-wait schemes are simple but can waste a long round-trip time. Sliding windows allow several frames in flight; sequence numbers and buffering manage reordering. Hybrid ARQ combines forward-error correction with retransmission and may soft-combine repeated observations or transmit incremental redundancy. Receiver memory, feedback error and delay budgets constrain practical designs. Stronger FEC reduces retries but may reduce raw throughput, so end-to-end goodput is a coupled design problem.

Key relation: For independent packet attempts with success probability p, expected attempts to success are 1/p; HARQ may combine failed transmissions so attempts are not statistically identical.

Design boundary: A geometric retry model is wrong when fading remains correlated across attempts or incremental redundancy changes conditional success probability after each transmission.

Lesson 8.3 — Queueing, latency and quality of service

A communication system must deliver useful data on time, not merely maximize peak physical-layer rate. Packet queues arise when bursts arrive faster than a scheduler can transmit them. Average service rate exceeding average arrival rate is necessary for long-term stability in basic models but says little about tail latency during bursts. Real packet service times are not always exponential, so the M/M/1 formula is an instructive benchmark rather than a blanket guarantee. Prioritization helps urgent traffic but can starve other flows; admission control and shaping influence both reliability and fairness.

Key relation: For a stable M/M/1 queue with arrival rate λ and service rate μ>λ, mean system time is W=1/(μ-λ), and mean number in system L=λW.

Design boundary: Little's law is broadly valid for steady systems with consistent counting, but the exact M/M/1 mean waiting-time formula depends on its arrival and service assumptions. Report the 95th percentile rather than only mean latency for interactive services.

Lesson 8.4 — Validation, reproducibility and deployment boundaries

A communication study should separate physical model assumptions from claims about deployed hardware or real traffic. Use a fixed seed to reproduce experiments, but repeat across seeds to measure uncertainty. Log rate, coding parameters, received power reference, noise bandwidth, channel model, packetization and error policy. Establish unit tests for basic identities, numerical conservation checks and negative controls for failure cases. Report confidence intervals when estimating rare error rates. Regulatory compliance and product certification need actual measurements and applicable jurisdiction-specific requirements, not a synthetic chart or a clean source-code build.

Key relation: An end-to-end measured goodput G is accepted user payload divided by elapsed observation time; simulated symbol BER, packet error rate, EVM and goodput answer different questions.

Design boundary: A visually attractive plot can hide a normalization bug; in simulation, independent recomputation of selected numerical points is essential. Correlation across trials invalidates common binomial uncertainty calculations.

Integrated case study

An engineer reviews the architecture for emergency communications service. The first review identifies the performance or integrity quantity at the receiver. The second asks which subsystem can change that quantity. The third checks the impact on user payload, energy consumption, delay and operating cost. A favorable conclusion requires comparable conditions; changing both the channel model and signal power while comparing decoders makes it impossible to attribute improvement to one change.

Three-stage assessment plan

1. Analytical baseline. Derive a transparent relation using one of the four listed key models. Describe the allowed parameter region, units and what was held constant in the comparison. For emergency communications service, decide which model variable is realistically measurable and which must be inferred.

2. Synthetic controlled test. Isolate one impairment, vary its magnitude and compare to the baseline. Re-run with a different seed and a second numerical implementation. Build a plot with labeled axes and, for error probabilities, uncertainty intervals.

3. Engineering hand-off. Replace the idealized assumption with a more plausible impairment relevant to a regional network carries mixed urgent control traffic and bulk data. State which earlier conclusion remains reliable and which needs further data. Record what must be checked before a prototype is trusted outside the laboratory.

Unit questions

  • Which of the four mechanisms is most likely to control acceptance for emergency communications service, and what measured evidence would establish that claim?
  • How would the design fail if the assumption “Goodput figures are dependent on traffic pattern, failure correlation, headers and retransmission details. A controller optimized using mean SNR can fail severely during rare fades.” were ignored?
  • Identify a numerical sanity check, an implementation test, and a separate real-world validation requirement.
  • Where would additional redundancy help and where might it merely increase latency or occupy already-limited bandwidth?

Suggested learning order

Read the four lessons in order; complete the simulation, then submit the project with a result table and the associated limitations.

Fully explained example and verification

If zero errors appear in 1000 independent trials, the observed rate is zero but the one-sided approximate 95% upper bound is about 3/1000=0.003, not zero. A claim that BER is below 10^-6 cannot be supported by such a run. For very low BER, use analytically justified importance sampling or much larger experiments with explicit error bars.

Boundary conditions and common mistakes

A visually attractive plot can hide a normalization bug; in simulation, independent recomputation of selected numerical points is essential. Correlation across trials invalidates common binomial uncertainty calculations.

An additional failure mode in emergency communications service arises when the local mechanism is interpreted as an end-to-end guarantee. For example, packets may be rejected by framing or replay checks even after an error-correcting decoder outputs a plausible bit sequence; conversely, a successful frame may be too late to serve a time-critical application. To separate causes, log the inputs and outputs of each major subsystem, retain the raw observation when possible, and name the exact stage at which a failure is declared.
