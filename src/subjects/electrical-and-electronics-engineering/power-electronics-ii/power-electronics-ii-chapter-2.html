§2.1 Foundational Principles & Curriculum Scope: Bridge Topologies: Half-Bridge and Full-Bridge Isolated Conversion

Unit 2: Three-phase voltage-source converters

Engineering situation

Three-phase modulation uses three coordinated switching legs. A commanded set of phase voltages can be represented as a rotating vector in a stationary plane; the vector description simplifies modulation analysis but does not remove semiconductor constraints or common-mode voltages.

Shared numerical scenario

The shared case is a 300 V educational DC-link model supplying a balanced three-phase passive load. This is a mathematical exercise: the corresponding hardware would be hazardous. Phase-to-neutral and line-to-line voltages differ by a factor of square-root three for balanced sinusoids, and the distinction matters in every motor-drive specification.

Intended competence

Learners will identify active and zero states, distinguish carrier-based PWM from space-vector PWM, interpret common-mode voltage, and estimate dead-time distortion. All waveform plots represent deterministic switching models with no grid synchronization or safety claims.

Assumptions and model boundary

This unit uses specific equations at specific operating points; a model must not be extended merely because it produces a number outside the stated range. In particular, the switching network may be averaged over a PWM period for a slow state calculation, while magnetic reset, terminal-voltage stress and semiconductor switching energy demand individual switching intervals. Keep the mechanical, magnetic and electrical energy variables separate where appropriate. Every diagram should identify a reference node, current direction and whether the axes show peak, average, or RMS quantities. Where an external network or motor is discussed, the waveform is software-generated for learning and does not authorize connection to an energized system.

Unit reading route

Read Lessons 2.1 through 2.4 in sequence, then use the [unit simulation](../simulations/model-02.html). Complete the unit project before the assessment. Preserve the original scenario as a baseline so parameter changes are attributable; do not change source, loading and control law simultaneously. When citing a numerical result, write the values, units and limit cases, then label it as ideal analytical, simplified dynamic model, computed simulation or actual measurement. No measurement claims are made here.

Technical review checkpoint

The algebra of switching states is only reliable when terminal references remain consistent. A floating load neutral, a DC negative terminal, a protective-earth conductor and a mathematical zero-sequence component are not equivalent. Changing the reference silently can change every reported phase voltage without changing the actual line voltage.

Reconstruct one stated switching interval from the upper-device binary state, compute all three line-to-line voltages, then verify that Vab+Vbc+Vca=0. A failed sum is a strong indicator of a sign or node-label error.

A good explanation specifies the assumptions under which a relation is true and names an experiment or simulation capable of detecting when it fails. A numerical waveform can agree with its own programmed equations and still be incapable of predicting a real apparatus. Use the following assessment to distinguish those two forms of evidence.

§2.2 Three-phase bridge switching states

Lesson 2.1: Three-phase bridge switching states

Position: Unit 2, Three-phase voltage-source converters. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

A conventional two-level three-phase bridge has eight ideal switching combinations, represented as three binary upper-device states. Two are zero vectors because all three phase terminals are tied to the same DC rail; six active states create nonzero line-to-line voltage patterns. This is a switching-state description, not an independent neutral connection.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

For phase state bits Sa,Sb,Sc ∈{0,1}, ideal pole voltages relative to the negative DC rail equal Sa Vdc, Sb Vdc, Sc Vdc. Line voltage Vab=(Sa−Sb)Vdc. Phase-to-neutral values require a specified load neutral or a balanced floating-neutral assumption.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

With Vdc=300 V and state (1,0,0), Vab=+300 V, Vbc=0 V and Vca=−300 V. For a balanced floating star load, mean pole voltage is 100 V, giving instantaneous phase potentials (200,−100,−100) V relative to the floating star point.

A credible numerical answer includes both a displayed number and its physical meaning. If a computed voltage is a peak, it cannot be directly used as an RMS rating. If a frequency is a natural resonance, it cannot be presented as a measured closed-loop bandwidth. If a result is an ideal output voltage, it cannot be equated with the real part's dielectric withstand requirement. Use at least three significant digits in the intermediate steps and round the final display to the precision actually supported by input parameters. A second independent check, such as energy conservation, a complementary voltage, or the expected response as one variable approaches zero, should be written beside the arithmetic.

Derivation workflow

Start from a physical definition and then introduce any simplification. Before calculating, sketch or describe the states that make the claimed equation meaningful. A derived relation is usually an operating-point statement, not a guarantee across startup, overload and faulted conditions. Distinguish independent inputs from outputs: a duty ratio may be commanded by the regulator, but a peak current is determined jointly by source voltage, load, storage and event timing.

Check dimensions at every substitution. In particular, microseconds and nanoseconds differ by a factor of one thousand; degrees and radians require explicit conversion before inserting a frequency; and a quantity in electrical radians per second is not a shaft speed in revolutions per minute. The direction of energy flow should be consistent with the current-arrow convention shown beside the circuit or graph.

Important failure modes and nonideal boundaries

A common error is to call +300 V the phase-to-neutral voltage without establishing the neutral reference. A real inverter cannot change device state instantaneously: gate-driver delays and blanking times alter the observed waveform.

These limitations are not a minor footnote: they are the conditions under which this chapter's conclusion could reverse. Separate the design intent from the semiconductor's transient stress, and the requested controller action from the physical switching state. A controller display can be numerically smooth while its switching node is overshooting; a motor speed command can be well behaved while the DC bus is absorbing excessive regenerative energy. A waveform generated by a hand-written program is only as complete as the modeled parasitics and fault behavior.

Engineering judgment

Build the full eight-row switching-state table and use it to check every line-voltage sign. Include both (0,0,0) and (1,1,1) as distinct device states even though their ideal line voltages are zero.

An engineering comparison should use a common reference case and explicitly report one metric that improves and one that could worsen. For example, increasing switching frequency may reduce some storage ripple yet increase switching heat and radiated emissions; adding capacitance may smooth a voltage while raising surge energy and discharge requirements; increasing control bandwidth may improve tracking while reducing delay tolerance. The tradeoff depends on this lesson's topology and assumptions. Where the text omits a datasheet value, test configuration or certification criterion, the correct output is 'not established' rather than an invented margin.

§2.3 Space-vector modulation geometry

Lesson 2.2: Space-vector modulation geometry

Position: Unit 2, Three-phase voltage-source converters. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

Space-vector modulation represents three-phase reference commands using a two-axis voltage vector and approximates it over a PWM period by spending calculated fractions of time in nearby active states and zero states. The vector concept can reduce redundant calculations while allowing different zero-state arrangements. It does not imply continuously variable semiconductor voltage.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

Within a 60° sector, ideal normalized dwell fractions are T1/Ts=m sin(π/3−θ) and T2/Ts=m sin θ for a particular modulation normalization. The remaining fraction is zero-vector dwell; the formula is valid only when that remainder is nonnegative and the normalization is defined.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

For m=0.8 and θ=30°, T1/Ts=T2/Ts=0.8×0.5=0.4, leaving T0/Ts=0.2. If the switching period is 100 µs the two active times are 40 µs each and the combined zero time is 20 µs.

Model comparison workflow

A second model is useful only if it changes an identifiable assumption. Compare an ideal lossless expression with a bounded nonideal version, or a fixed load with a changed load; do not modify several unrelated quantities and attribute the entire difference to a single mechanism. Make a table showing which equations survive the changed operating regime and which must be derived again.

Include the neglected process in plain language even when it cannot be computed: leakage inductance, dead time, load-angle error, winding temperature or non-sinusoidal current can change a conclusion. Giving an uncalibrated guessed correction factor to hide that omission is less useful than stating that the quantity remains unknown.

Alternative texts define m differently, sometimes including √3 and bus scaling. Copying dwell formulas between conventions without conversion can falsely produce a negative zero-vector time and an apparent overmodulation event.

§2.4 Common-mode voltage and parasitic paths

Lesson 2.3: Common-mode voltage and parasitic paths

Position: Unit 2, Three-phase voltage-source converters. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

The common-mode pole voltage is the average of the three inverter pole voltages relative to a chosen DC reference. Rapid changes in this voltage excite parasitic capacitive current paths through motor winding insulation, bearings, heatsinks and enclosures. A balanced line-to-line sinusoid does not eliminate common-mode excitation.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

For pole voltages Va0,Vb0,Vc0, define Vcm=(Va0+Vb0+Vc0)/3 relative to the negative rail. The capacitive current relation i=Cpar dvcm/dt provides a first approximation of fast common-mode current through a parasitic capacitance.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

With Vdc=300 V, state (1,0,0) yields Vcm=100 V; state (1,1,1) yields 300 V. For an illustrative 2 nF path and a 100 V change over 100 ns, C ΔV/Δt gives 2 A transient current. This represents a rough edge estimate, not a constant 2 A load.

Measurement and inference workflow

Define what an instrument would actually observe, even though this exercise is software only. A spectrum analyzer reports a frequency-domain estimate, a current sensor reports a bandwidth-limited sample, and a thermal measurement reports temperature at a location and time. None directly measures every term in an analytical state equation. Design an independent cross-check using conservation of energy or symmetry before trusting a displayed result.

Record the intended sample rate, window, triggering event and reference node. For transient switching analyses distinguish a 10 ns peak from a 1 ms average and identify whether the probe itself could perturb the observed circuit. If a claim needs actual bench data, mark it explicitly as not yet established.

Important failure modes and nonideal boundaries

A very small parasitic capacitance can support significant transient current under fast edges. However, the equivalent lumped capacitance ignores distributed cables and frequency-dependent impedances, so the peak magnitude needs measured validation.

§2.5 Dead-time voltage error and compensation

Lesson 2.4: Dead-time voltage error and compensation

Position: Unit 2, Three-phase voltage-source converters. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

In a half bridge, the deliberately inserted nonoverlap interval causes the output pole to freewheel according to the instantaneous current direction. This creates a systematic voltage error that often changes sign when load current reverses, contributing to low-speed torque ripple and distorted current zero-crossings.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

A first-order magnitude estimate for an average pole-voltage error is |ΔV|≈Vdc td fs, where td is nonoverlap time and fs is switching frequency. Actual polarity depends on current, device and diode conduction. Device turn-on and turn-off delays may not be symmetric.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

For Vdc=300 V, td=1 µs and fs=10 kHz, the approximate per-leg error magnitude is 300×10^-6×10^4=3 V. This value is not an additional line-to-line RMS voltage and must not be inserted directly into a motor-voltage rating.

Design-release workflow

A mathematical design is complete only relative to its declared requirements. Produce a decision matrix connecting efficiency, voltage stress, current stress, temperature, dynamics, isolation where applicable, EMC and fault response. Each column has a different pass criterion; a high numerical efficiency cannot compensate for a failed voltage rating or missing fault isolation.

Audit the design under the worst credible input, the largest and smallest loads, startup, shutdown and a sensor fault. State which cases have been derived, which have only been simulated, and which demand independent qualified testing. A student solution can be rigorous while acknowledging that specific manufacturers' datasheets and safety standards have not been checked.

Important failure modes and nonideal boundaries

Compensation based solely on the sign of a noisy current sample can oscillate around zero crossing. The minimum pulse width and hardware interlocks are independent from the software correction strategy.

For Exercise A, the key is a consistent sign convention and correct categorization of the calculated quantity. In Exercise B the arithmetic must start from the same stated units, and a legitimate answer must note when a changed parameter would require a new operating regime. Bridge to the unit model

Reference-frame and topology integrity: The algebra of switching states is only reliable when terminal references remain consistent. A floating load neutral, a DC negative terminal, a protective-earth conductor and a mathematical zero-sequence component are not equivalent. Changing the reference silently can change every reported phase voltage without changing the actual line voltage.

Reconstruct one stated switching interval from the upper-device binary state, compute all three line-to-line voltages, then verify that Vab+Vbc+Vca=0. A failed sum is a strong indicator of a sign or node-label error.
