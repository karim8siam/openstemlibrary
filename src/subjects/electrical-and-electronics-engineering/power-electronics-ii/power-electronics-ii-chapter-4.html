§4.1 Foundational Principles & Curriculum Scope: Single-Phase Voltage-Source Inverters and Sinusoidal Pulse-Width Modulation

Unit 4: Isolated converters and transformer constraints

Engineering situation

Galvanic isolation changes the available circuit architecture but does not make an energized output automatically safe. A flyback device stores energy in a coupled magnetic element; a forward or bridge converter transfers energy during a different interval. Transformer turns ratios alone cannot determine the operating point without the switch timing and reset conditions.

Shared numerical scenario

Our case is a conceptual 24 V source powering a low-voltage isolated sensor supply. We examine energy per cycle, peak flux density, winding copper loss, insulation assumptions and reset constraints. Hardware construction details and safety isolation certification are outside the scope of this book.

Intended competence

Learners will explain magnetizing current, distinguish energy-storage and transformer-action topologies, identify output rectifier timing and produce a safety review that lists required but unavailable material properties and manufacturing evidence.

Assumptions and model boundary

This unit uses specific equations at specific operating points; a model must not be extended merely because it produces a number outside the stated range. In particular, the switching network may be averaged over a PWM period for a slow state calculation, while magnetic reset, terminal-voltage stress and semiconductor switching energy demand individual switching intervals. Keep the mechanical, magnetic and electrical energy variables separate where appropriate. Every diagram should identify a reference node, current direction and whether the axes show peak, average, or RMS quantities. Where an external network or motor is discussed, the waveform is software-generated for learning and does not authorize connection to an energized system.

Unit reading route

Read Lessons 4.1 through 4.4 in sequence, then use the [unit simulation](../simulations/model-04.html). Complete the unit project before the assessment. Preserve the original scenario as a baseline so parameter changes are attributable; do not change source, loading and control law simultaneously. When citing a numerical result, write the values, units and limit cases, then label it as ideal analytical, simplified dynamic model, computed simulation or actual measurement. No measurement claims are made here.

Technical review checkpoint

A coupled magnetic device has at least three different duties: transfer or store magnetic energy, prevent flux walk, and maintain physical separation between conductors. A calculated volts-per-turn value may address the first two but cannot establish that winding insulation withstands the required service environment.

For every switch interval mark applied primary voltage, magnetizing-current direction, secondary-diode condition and stored energy change. Then make an additional, intentionally incomplete insulation checklist. Do not fill missing material or test data with an assumed pass result.

A good explanation specifies the assumptions under which a relation is true and names an experiment or simulation capable of detecting when it fails. A numerical waveform can agree with its own programmed equations and still be incapable of predicting a real apparatus. Use the following assessment to distinguish those two forms of evidence.

§4.2 Flyback energy per switching cycle

Lesson 4.1: Flyback energy per switching cycle

Position: Unit 4, Isolated converters and transformer constraints. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

A flyback converter stores energy in the magnetizing inductance while the primary switch conducts and releases it through the secondary during the off interval. The secondary polarity and diode orientation must reflect this sequence. Flyback equations are particularly sensitive to whether operation is discontinuous, continuous or boundary conduction.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

In ideal discontinuous conduction mode, delivered energy per cycle is approximately E=½Lm Ipk², with average transferred output power approximately E fs times an efficiency factor. In continuous mode, inductor energy never returns to zero and that simplified energy formula must be replaced by an energy increment.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

With Lm=150 µH, primary peak current 2 A and fs=50 kHz, stored energy each cycle is 0.5×150e−6×4=0.0003 J; ideal cycle power is 15 W. At an assumed 80% energy-transfer efficiency, illustrative output power is 12 W, subject to DCM and leakage-energy limits.

A credible numerical answer includes both a displayed number and its physical meaning. If a computed voltage is a peak, it cannot be directly used as an RMS rating. If a frequency is a natural resonance, it cannot be presented as a measured closed-loop bandwidth. If a result is an ideal output voltage, it cannot be equated with the real part's dielectric withstand requirement. Use at least three significant digits in the intermediate steps and round the final display to the precision actually supported by input parameters. A second independent check, such as energy conservation, a complementary voltage, or the expected response as one variable approaches zero, should be written beside the arithmetic.

Derivation workflow

Start from a physical definition and then introduce any simplification. Before calculating, sketch or describe the states that make the claimed equation meaningful. A derived relation is usually an operating-point statement, not a guarantee across startup, overload and faulted conditions. Distinguish independent inputs from outputs: a duty ratio may be commanded by the regulator, but a peak current is determined jointly by source voltage, load, storage and event timing.

Check dimensions at every substitution. In particular, microseconds and nanoseconds differ by a factor of one thousand; degrees and radians require explicit conversion before inserting a frequency; and a quantity in electrical radians per second is not a shaft speed in revolutions per minute. The direction of energy flow should be consistent with the current-arrow convention shown beside the circuit or graph.

Important failure modes and nonideal boundaries

Leakage inductance energy can produce a drain-voltage spike and must not be confused with useful magnetizing energy. A 150 µH small-signal measurement says nothing about core saturation at the selected current.

These limitations are not a minor footnote: they are the conditions under which this chapter's conclusion could reverse. Separate the design intent from the semiconductor's transient stress, and the requested controller action from the physical switching state. A controller display can be numerically smooth while its switching node is overshooting; a motor speed command can be well behaved while the DC bus is absorbing excessive regenerative energy. A waveform generated by a hand-written program is only as complete as the modeled parasitics and fault behavior.

Engineering judgment

Create a switching-interval table with winding polarity, diode state and current flow. Check that the magnetizing energy can be fully reset during the available off-time before using the DCM power expression.

An engineering comparison should use a common reference case and explicitly report one metric that improves and one that could worsen. For example, increasing switching frequency may reduce some storage ripple yet increase switching heat and radiated emissions; adding capacitance may smooth a voltage while raising surge energy and discharge requirements; increasing control bandwidth may improve tracking while reducing delay tolerance. The tradeoff depends on this lesson's topology and assumptions. Where the text omits a datasheet value, test configuration or certification criterion, the correct output is 'not established' rather than an invented margin.

§4.3 Forward conversion and core reset

Lesson 4.2: Forward conversion and core reset

Position: Unit 4, Isolated converters and transformer constraints. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

In a forward converter, the transformer transfers energy while the primary switch is on and an output inductor smooths the load current. The magnetizing flux still requires a mechanism to reset; failing to recover sufficient negative volt-seconds can accumulate flux across successive cycles.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

For equal-magnitude primary magnetizing voltages in on and reset intervals, volt-second balance implies Von ton+Vreset treset≈0 with signed values. If reset magnitude equals applied magnitude, reset duration must be at least the on-duration; that imposes a duty-ratio constraint in the simplest single-switch arrangement.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

If Vin=24 V and on-time=4 µs, an ideal −24 V reset winding needs at least 4 µs to undo the volt-seconds. For a 10 µs switching period, this arrangement leaves at most 2 µs for any remaining interval. Other topologies use different reset paths and limits.

If a frequency is a natural resonance, it cannot be presented as a measured closed-loop bandwidth. Model comparison workflow

A second model is useful only if it changes an identifiable assumption. Compare an ideal lossless expression with a bounded nonideal version, or a fixed load with a changed load; do not modify several unrelated quantities and attribute the entire difference to a single mechanism. Make a table showing which equations survive the changed operating regime and which must be derived again.

Include the neglected process in plain language even when it cannot be computed: leakage inductance, dead time, load-angle error, winding temperature or non-sinusoidal current can change a conclusion. Giving an uncalibrated guessed correction factor to hide that omission is less useful than stating that the quantity remains unknown.

An unexplained 50% maximum duty statement is specific to certain reset configurations and cannot be applied universally to two-switch forward or active-clamp converters. Flux density needs core area and turn count, not only turns ratio.

§4.4 Full-bridge isolation and phase-shift control

Lesson 4.3: Full-bridge isolation and phase-shift control

Position: Unit 4, Isolated converters and transformer constraints. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

An isolated full-bridge converter drives a transformer with alternating primary voltage polarities. Phase-shift control changes the time intervals in which effective voltage is applied while using device output capacitance and circulating current to support soft-switching transitions. Zero-voltage switching depends strongly on load and device parasitics.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

For an ideal bridge with bus Vdc and transformer primary square-wave duty fraction Deff, a simple full-wave rectified secondary approximation gives Vout≈Deff (Ns/Np) Vdc before diode drops and filter dynamics. The exact definition of Deff depends on timing arrangement and dead intervals.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

With Vdc=48 V, Ns/Np=0.5 and Deff=0.4, an ideal averaged output estimate is 9.6 V. Increasing Deff to 0.5 predicts 12 V only in the lossless model, and leakage inductance plus rectifier commutation introduce real differences.

Measurement and inference workflow

Define what an instrument would actually observe, even though this exercise is software only. A spectrum analyzer reports a frequency-domain estimate, a current sensor reports a bandwidth-limited sample, and a thermal measurement reports temperature at a location and time. None directly measures every term in an analytical state equation. Design an independent cross-check using conservation of energy or symmetry before trusting a displayed result.

Record the intended sample rate, window, triggering event and reference node. For transient switching analyses distinguish a 10 ns peak from a 1 ms average and identify whether the probe itself could perturb the observed circuit. If a claim needs actual bench data, mark it explicitly as not yet established.

Important failure modes and nonideal boundaries

Light-load ZVS failure may increase switching losses and heating even when current is low. The simple voltage law cannot certify safe magnetics, zero-current commutation or semiconductor voltage stress.

§4.5 Isolation, insulation and transformer specification

Lesson 4.4: Isolation, insulation and transformer specification

Position: Unit 4, Isolated converters and transformer constraints. Reading level: undergraduate circuit analysis, switching converters and elementary calculus. Learning claim: distinguish the mathematical approximation from the physical device-level consequences. Status: unreviewed teaching chapter.

Physical picture and mechanism

Electrical isolation is a physical and regulatory design problem, not just a schematic symbol. Required creepage, clearance, dielectric withstand, protective earth strategy, bobbin material and production tests depend on voltage class, pollution conditions and the actual applicable standard.

The practical boundary of the system matters. Draw where energy enters, where useful energy leaves, what storage changes within the process and where heat or field energy is likely to be lost. Define all voltage polarities before changing reference frames; a reported sign is physically meaningful only relative to a declared node or direction. The natural interval for averaging must also be stated: a switching-period equation, a line-frequency RMS result and a slowly varying thermal estimate describe different aspects of the same apparatus. One cannot simply average all three over an arbitrary interval and call the result a design guarantee.

Governing mathematical relation

An ideal turns-ratio relation gives Vs/Vp≈Ns/Np for an ideal coupled transformer at a specified instant, but actual winding copper, leakage inductance and parasitic capacitance alter behavior. Peak core flux change is approximately ΔB=V ton/(Np Ae), assuming constant applied voltage and correct core area definition.

Write the baseline relation symbolically first, then test it against a dimensional check and an elementary limiting case. For a variable expressed as a rate, verify what time base is implicit: per switching period, per electrical cycle, per unit mechanical rotation or per second. If the equation assumes continuous conduction, a linear magnetic inductance, an ideal waveform or a balanced three-phase input, say so before substituting numerical values. For a measured value, specify sensor position and measurement bandwidth; for a computed value, identify the exact source equation.

Worked numerical example with interpretation

For 24 V applied over 4 µs to a 20-turn primary with Ae=100 mm², ΔB=24×4e−6/(20×100e−6)=0.048 T. This is a flux swing, not a claim of acceptable total peak flux; initial bias and temperature still matter.

Design-release workflow

A mathematical design is complete only relative to its declared requirements. Produce a decision matrix connecting efficiency, voltage stress, current stress, temperature, dynamics, isolation where applicable, EMC and fault response. Each column has a different pass criterion; a high numerical efficiency cannot compensate for a failed voltage rating or missing fault isolation.

Audit the design under the worst credible input, the largest and smallest loads, startup, shutdown and a sensor fault. State which cases have been derived, which have only been simulated, and which demand independent qualified testing. A student solution can be rigorous while acknowledging that specific manufacturers' datasheets and safety standards have not been checked.

Magnetic reset and insulation evidence: A coupled magnetic device has at least three different duties: transfer or store magnetic energy, prevent flux walk, and maintain physical separation between conductors.
