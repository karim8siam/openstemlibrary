§6.1 Theoretical Overview: Sampling Theorem, Pulse Amplitude Modulation (PAM), and Time-Division Multiplexing

Unit 6: Random processes, noise and communication channels

Learning objective: Model random voltage, PSD and receiver noise with consistent probabilistic and bandwidth conventions.

Physical perspective

Real signals may be stochastic in source content or channel disturbance. Expectation describes an ensemble and does not guarantee the outcome of a short observation. A wide-sense stationary model uses constant mean and lag-dependent autocorrelation; physical channels often violate that assumption over long intervals. The receiver must translate an SNR hypothesis into a specified detector performance under a stated noise model.

Extended engineering case: how to reason from requirements

Noise calculations are full of hidden reference choices. A density expressed in watts per hertz becomes a power only after integration over the filter response. Converting to decibels before integrating is generally wrong: integrate linear power first. A two-sided PSD over negative and positive frequencies differs numerically from a one-sided PSD defined only for nonnegative frequencies, but both predict the same total noise power when used consistently. A noise figure convention adds further assumptions about source temperature and a matched input. A simulation may omit amplifier gain compression, quantization and adjacent-channel interference; note these omissions in every result.

Matched filtering is a decision-oriented use of signal knowledge. If a known pulse occupies a finite interval, the receiver integrates its energy with a filter matched to its shape. This raises the sample SNR compared with observing one instantaneous noisy amplitude. When the interference spectrum is colored, weighting all frequencies equally is no longer optimal. A whitening filter followed by a matched filter can improve detection, but increases reliance on a correct noise model. Channel estimation uncertainty also matters: a receiver using the wrong pulse shape may suffer mismatch loss.

An apparently excellent simulated bit-error result deserves a statistical check. If only one thousand bits are sent and zero errors occur, it is not legitimate to report zero underlying error probability. The result supplies an upper statistical bound whose size depends on confidence. Approximately three expected-error opportunities for a zero-error trial gives the familiar rough 95% upper bound near 3/N for independent rare errors. Fading and burst interference violate independence, so interleaving or repeated independent trials may be needed to interpret data. Report trial count, random seed, confidence interval and Eb/N0 normalization alongside the headline BER.

Connections and study order

The next four lessons move from the simplest tractable model to a broader engineering treatment of this unit. Solve their worked cases separately first, then assemble the assumptions in a single coherent link design. For every step, distinguish data representing the actual message from a signal generated by the transceiver and from noise introduced by the environment or receiver. Use the model's limiting cases to decide whether parameters can be safely neglected. Finally, specify how any simulation could be validated through an independent analytic calculation and, if qualified laboratory access exists, through a supervised physical measurement.

§6.2 Probability, autocorrelation and power spectra

Lesson 6.1 Probability, autocorrelation and power spectra

Unit: 6 — Random processes, noise and communication channels Core model: Rx(τ)=E[x(t)x*(t-τ)]; Sx(f)=F{Rx(τ)} for WSS processes Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

A random waveform is described by many possible outcomes, not by one deterministic plot. Autocorrelation describes similarity as a function of delay, while power spectral density describes the distribution of mean power over frequency. The Wiener–Khinchin relationship connects these descriptions under appropriate stationarity assumptions. White noise has ideal flat two-sided PSD and infinite total power if extended across all frequencies, which is a model abstraction rather than a realizable source. Noise observed after a filter has shaped PSD and potentially correlated samples.

Derivation and interpretation

Begin by writing the input, output and channel reference planes for probability, autocorrelation and power spectra. The governing relationship in this lesson is Rx(τ)=E[x(t)x*(t-τ)]; Sx(f)=F{Rx(τ)} for WSS processes. Read each symbol according to its definition in the accompanying discussion, not as a context-free calculator instruction. Where the formula uses decibels, convert back to linear power before adding contributions. Where it describes a spectrum, identify whether positive and negative frequencies, peak or RMS amplitudes and window normalization are being used. If a formula describes probability, specify the random variable and statistical model. These conditions are substantive mathematical requirements: using the right arithmetic on the wrong reference conditions does not produce a correct engineering result.

Worked engineering case

If independent zero-mean samples have variance 0.04 V², their lag-zero correlation equals 0.04 V² and off-zero discrete-time correlation approaches zero in the ensemble model. Individual finite records still show accidental nonzero estimates.

Failure mode and model boundary

Never interpret a periodogram estimate as the exact underlying PSD. Window choice, averaging, record length and one-sided/two-sided scaling affect numerical values.

Investigation and design transfer

Simulate multiple short Gaussian-noise records; compare empirical variance and autocorrelation with theoretical targets, then average more trials.

Connection to the rest of the chain

A communication receiver does not experience this mechanism in isolation. Source statistics influence coding; waveform choice influences occupied bandwidth; filtering changes the noise entering the decision statistic; and synchronization transforms signal geometry into errors. When using the result from probability, autocorrelation and power spectra inside an end-to-end budget, name its upstream inputs and downstream consequences. Avoid adding an improvement twice: reducing noise bandwidth and improving SNR through a filter may describe the same physical change. Conversely, an unmodeled impairment can create a gap between a correct local calculation and actual delivered payload. A full design review therefore asks for both local correctness and end-to-end error or outage evidence.

§6.3 Thermal noise, matched filtering and equivalent noise bandwidth

Lesson 6.2 Thermal noise, matched filtering and equivalent noise bandwidth

Unit: 6 — Random processes, noise and communication channels Core model: Nout=∫S_n(f)|H(f)|²df using compatible two-sided conventions Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

When noise passes through an LTI filter, its output variance is the integral of input PSD weighted by squared filter magnitude. Equivalent noise bandwidth compares the actual filter’s integrated noise passage to a rectangular reference filter with the same peak gain. For a fixed signal pulse in additive white Gaussian noise, a matched filter maximizes output SNR at the nominated sampling instant, assuming the noise is white and timing and waveform are known. Colored noise calls for whitening or an equivalent generalized detector.

§6.4 AWGN, Eb/N0 and probability of error

Lesson 6.3 AWGN, Eb/N0 and probability of error

Unit: 6 — Random processes, noise and communication channels Core model: for coherent BPSK in AWGN, Pb=Q(sqrt(2Eb/N0)) Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

AWGN adds a random Gaussian voltage vector with a flat spectral model within the bandwidth of interest. It provides a tractable baseline for detector design and approximate link budgeting. Eb/N0 normalizes received energy per information bit against noise spectral density; it is not the same as broadband SNR unless the relevant bit rate and receiver bandwidth are specified. Uncoded coherent BPSK in AWGN has a simple Q-function bit-error expression with perfect synchronization. Applying that formula to fading, coding, differential detection or phase ambiguity without adjustments is incorrect.

§6.5 Multipath, fading and channel coherence

Lesson 6.4 Multipath, fading and channel coherence

Unit: 6 — Random processes, noise and communication channels Core model: r(t)=∑k ak x(t-τk)+n(t) for a tapped delay-line channel Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

A physical receiver often collects delayed copies of the same transmitted waveform. Their complex phasors may reinforce or cancel at different frequencies, producing frequency-selective fading. Channel coherence bandwidth is related to delay spread, while coherence time is related to Doppler spread; both relationships require a precise definition and statistical channel model. A flat-fading approximation is plausible only if the occupied signal bandwidth is small relative to channel variation in frequency. Equalization, diversity and adaptive coding address distinct risks.

Worked engineering case

Two equal-amplitude paths with a 1 microsecond relative delay interfere constructively at some frequencies and destructively where phase difference equals odd multiples of π. The approximate notch spacing between equivalent destructive conditions is 1/τ=1 MHz.

Failure mode and model boundary

A deterministic two-tap example is not a Rayleigh or Rician statistical fading model unless a distribution for the path coefficients has been declared.
