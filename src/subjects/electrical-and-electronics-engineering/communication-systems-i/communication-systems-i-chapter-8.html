§8.1 Theoretical Overview: Digital Baseband Transmission, Inter-Symbol Interference (ISI), and Nyquist Criterion

Unit 8: Coherent digital modulation and detection foundations

Learning objective: Introduce coherent ASK/BPSK/QPSK, constellation decisions, pulse shaping and timing recovery.

Physical perspective

A digital receiver decides which symbol most likely generated a noisy observation. A constellation plots a selected pair of sufficient-statistic coordinates, not the raw traveling radio wave. The distance between candidates and the noise covariance control detection reliability. Receiver timing, pulse shape and carrier phase errors can be as important as noise magnitude.

Extended engineering case: how to reason from requirements

A constellation is not a picture of electromagnetic radiation traveling through space. It is the receiver's collection of noisy coordinates after filtering, frequency translation and timing selection. The ideal points are deliberately separated so the detector can use geometry. In AWGN with equal noise variance on orthogonal axes, nearest-point detection is maximum likelihood for equal priors. A carrier-phase error rotates the point cloud; an I/Q gain imbalance stretches it, a DC offset shifts it and timing errors smear the clusters. Correcting only SNR cannot repair those systematic effects.

BPSK and QPSK offer a useful comparison. At equal energy per bit and coherent detection in ideal AWGN, Gray-coded QPSK does not inherently have a worse BER than BPSK. Its benefit is higher bits per symbol rather than a free reduction in needed Eb/N0. Increasing constellation order without increasing power places points closer together and ordinarily demands better SNR at a given error target. Pulse shaping further complicates the link: the transmitted waveform is a sum of overlapping pulses, and a matched receiver uses the expected pulse shape to recover clean sampling instants. Choosing a smaller raised-cosine rolloff reduces theoretical spectral occupancy but increases filter length and timing sensitivity.

An engineering review should show at least four displays: time waveform, frequency spectrum, constellation and eye diagram. Each reveals a different failure mode. A clean-looking constellation at correct timing says little about adjacent-channel emissions; a narrow spectrum does not guarantee accurate clock recovery. The eight-unit course culminates in a disciplined link assessment that states known assumptions, separates synthetic models from hardware results and reserves space for uncertain channel conditions.

Connections and study order

The next four lessons move from the simplest tractable model to a broader engineering treatment of this unit. Solve their worked cases separately first, then assemble the assumptions in a single coherent link design. For every step, distinguish data representing the actual message from a signal generated by the transceiver and from noise introduced by the environment or receiver. Use the model's limiting cases to decide whether parameters can be safely neglected. Finally, specify how any simulation could be validated through an independent analytic calculation and, if qualified laboratory access exists, through a supervised physical measurement.

§8.2 Binary ASK, OOK and energy detection

Lesson 8.1 Binary ASK, OOK and energy detection

Unit: 8 — Coherent digital modulation and detection foundations Core model: OOK symbols s0(t)=0 and s1(t)=A p(t) under the chosen mapping Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

On–off keying maps one bit to transmitted energy and the other to nominal silence. Coherent and noncoherent receivers use different decision variables and have different performance under phase uncertainty. An optical direct-detection system has additional shot-noise and intensity constraints beyond a simple bipolar AWGN voltage model. Threshold selection must reflect prior probabilities, noise variance and relative costs of false alarms versus misses. A receiver built only for equal symbol probabilities may be unsuitable for a rare-event telemetry link.

Derivation and interpretation

Begin by writing the input, output and channel reference planes for binary ask, ook and energy detection. The governing relationship in this lesson is OOK symbols s0(t)=0 and s1(t)=A p(t) under the chosen mapping. Read each symbol according to its definition in the accompanying discussion, not as a context-free calculator instruction. Where the formula uses decibels, convert back to linear power before adding contributions. Where it describes a spectrum, identify whether positive and negative frequencies, peak or RMS amplitudes and window normalization are being used. If a formula describes probability, specify the random variable and statistical model. These conditions are substantive mathematical requirements: using the right arithmetic on the wrong reference conditions does not produce a correct engineering result.

Worked engineering case

If the zero-symbol decision statistic is distributed N(0,σ²) and one-symbol statistic is N(A,σ²) with equal priors and costs, the optimal midpoint threshold is A/2. Unequal priors shift the likelihood-ratio threshold.

Failure mode and model boundary

Electrical OOK transmit amplitude is not the same as optical field amplitude. Do not mix squared amplitude conventions when computing received optical power.

Investigation and design transfer

Plot false-alarm and missed-detection probabilities as threshold moves across two Gaussian distributions. Find a threshold for equal priors and discuss the effect of asymmetric costs.

§8.3 BPSK and matched-filter decisions

Lesson 8.2 BPSK and matched-filter decisions

Unit: 8 — Coherent digital modulation and detection foundations Core model: BPSK symbols ±sqrt(Eb) in normalized signal space Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

BPSK uses opposite phases of the same carrier or equivalent baseband pulse. With perfect coherent phase reference and symmetric AWGN, the maximum-likelihood decision is simply the sign of the matched-filter projection. Normalizing signal-space coordinates makes geometric distance explicit: the points lie 2√Eb apart. An unknown 180-degree phase ambiguity swaps every bit, which motivates pilots, differential encoding or a code structure capable of detecting inversion. The BER function assumes the receiver samples at the intended pulse time.

Worked engineering case

At Eb=1 normalized unit and N0=0.5, Eb/N0=2 (3.01 dB). Ideal coherent BPSK bit-error rate is Q(2)≈0.02275. Raising energy by 3 dB improves performance nonlinearly, not by a fixed additive BER amount.

Failure mode and model boundary

A hard-decision detector discards reliability information that a soft-input decoder could exploit. The simple sign rule also fails with nonzero decision bias or correlated interference.

§8.4 QPSK, quadrature carrier mixing and symbol mapping

Lesson 8.3 QPSK, quadrature carrier mixing and symbol mapping

Unit: 8 — Coherent digital modulation and detection foundations Core model: QPSK symbols (±1±j)/sqrt(2); Es=2Eb for uncoded equal-probability mapping Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

QPSK conveys two coded bits per symbol using orthogonal I and Q components. With a Gray mapping, nearest-neighbor symbol errors usually change one bit, improving BER relative to arbitrary bit assignments at moderate noise. Under equal energy per bit, ideal coherent Gray-coded QPSK in AWGN has the same bit-error probability as BPSK, despite transmitting twice as many bits per symbol. Spectral occupation also depends on pulse shaping, not only the number of constellation points.

§8.5 Pulse shaping, Nyquist ISI criterion and eye diagrams

Lesson 8.4 Pulse shaping, Nyquist ISI criterion and eye diagrams

Unit: 8 — Coherent digital modulation and detection foundations Core model: ideal raised-cosine passband width ≈Rs(1+α) with rolloff α in [0,1] Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

A zero-ISI pulse has the property that its sampled values at symbol-spaced offsets vanish except at the origin, under ideal synchronization. Raised-cosine pulses are common examples, with α trading spectral compactness against sharper time-domain sidelobes. In many transmitters a root-raised-cosine filter is used, and a matching receiver filter produces the overall raised-cosine response. An eye diagram superimposes symbol intervals to reveal timing margin and interference. The eye is a diagnostic, not a direct absolute BER certificate.
