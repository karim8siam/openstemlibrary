§5.1 Theoretical Overview: Noise in Analog Continuous-Wave Communication Systems (SNR, Noise Figure)

Unit 5: Pulse modulation, PCM and quantization

Learning objective: Connect finite-rate digital sources to sampling, quantization and framing and distinguish quantizer noise from channel errors.

Physical perspective

Digitizing an analog source is not the same as transmitting a digital waveform over a physical channel. Sampling selects times, quantization assigns values, binary labeling assigns bits and line coding maps those bits to an electrical or optical signal. These stages introduce different failure modes. A network can deliver all bits without error while the reconstructed waveform still suffers aliasing or quantization distortion.

Extended engineering case: how to reason from requirements

Digitization should be audited as a cascade of distinct irreversible decisions. The anti-alias filter is chosen before sampling; it rejects analog content that the sample rate cannot uniquely represent. Sampling then produces time-indexed amplitudes. Quantization reduces the amplitude alphabet to a finite set, creating a controlled but irreversible error. Binary labeling selects how those levels are represented in bits. Framing establishes how a receiver finds the sample boundaries and the channel identity. Bit errors introduced later by the communication channel are different from quantizer error introduced at the source.

Consider a biomedical-type sensor in a safe offline mathematical model. Its high-frequency artifact may be outside the desired physiological band but still large enough to alias if the front-end filter is inadequate. Increasing the number of ADC bits does not remove that aliased content. Doubling sample rate may move an alias to a different location without reducing it, unless filtering is also revised. Increasing input gain can improve effective use of quantizer levels for a quiet signal but can also produce overload when a transient arrives. A real clinical device requires independent safety and regulatory engineering; the textbook model is only an educational arithmetic exercise.

Companding reveals another tradeoff: when many samples cluster near zero, uniform code spacing is wasteful under some distortion goals. A nonlinear map can spread low-amplitude values across more codewords. The receiver's inverse transformation recovers an approximation but not the quantized detail that was removed. For delta modulation, the rate of slope change matters more than absolute amplitude. Choose a step and sampling rate based on the steepest permissible input slope, then examine granular error on nearly constant signals. Record whether the design objective is mean-square error, peak error, perceptual weighting or bit rate.

Connections and study order

The next four lessons move from the simplest tractable model to a broader engineering treatment of this unit. Solve their worked cases separately first, then assemble the assumptions in a single coherent link design. For every step, distinguish data representing the actual message from a signal generated by the transceiver and from noise introduced by the environment or receiver. Use the model's limiting cases to decide whether parameters can be safely neglected. Finally, specify how any simulation could be validated through an independent analytic calculation and, if qualified laboratory access exists, through a supervised physical measurement.

§5.2 Pulse amplitude modulation and time-domain reconstruction

Lesson 5.1 Pulse amplitude modulation and time-domain reconstruction

Unit: 5 — Pulse modulation, PCM and quantization Core model: x(t)=∑n a[n] p(t-nT) for pulse shaping p(t) Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

Pulse amplitude modulation carries discrete-valued symbols on a waveform basis function. The pulse shape determines how adjacent transmitted symbols overlap and therefore what a receiver must undo. Sampling a continuous waveform and using its samples as PAM amplitudes are different operations, though they often appear consecutively in a digitization chain. The receiver should observe the matched or otherwise appropriate filtered output at specified sampling instants. A zero-order hold produces a staircase-shaped reconstruction, not an ideal bandlimited interpolation.

Worked engineering case

For symbol spacing 1 ms and pulse p(t) with support 2 ms, adjacent symbols can overlap in continuous time. If the pulse is designed with zero crossings at all nonzero multiples of T, the ideal sampling instants can still be free of intersymbol interference.

Failure mode and model boundary

Bandwidth and timing recovery cannot be designed independently when pulses change sharply. A narrower pulse in time generally carries broader frequency content.

§5.3 Uniform quantization and SQNR

Lesson 5.2 Uniform quantization and SQNR

Unit: 5 — Pulse modulation, PCM and quantization Core model: Δ=(Vmax-Vmin)/2^N; high-resolution sine SQNR≈6.02N+1.76 dB under specified assumptions Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

A uniform quantizer maps each analog input in an allowed range into a finite reproduction level. The maximum granular error for a nearest-neighbor midrise rule is roughly half a step, provided the signal stays within range. Treating quantization error as a uniform, independent random process is an approximation that can fail for tiny coherent inputs or overload. The common 6.02N+1.76 dB sine-wave expression assumes a full-scale ideal sinusoid, high resolution, no overload and sufficiently decorrelated error. It is not a general ADC SNR guarantee.

A quiet signal far below full scale has worse relative SQNR unless gain, companding or noise shaping is used. Dither can linearize distortion at the cost of extra noise.

§5.4 Pulse-code modulation rate and framing

Lesson 5.3 Pulse-code modulation rate and framing

Unit: 5 — Pulse modulation, PCM and quantization Core model: Rb=fs Nbits nchannels before compression or transport overhead Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

PCM typically means sampling, quantizing and labeling a waveform in binary. The raw source rate follows directly from samples per second, bits per sample and channel count. An actual stream also needs timing, frame boundaries, error checks and possibly multiplexing. A designer should distinguish aggregate bit rate from net application payload rate; compression can reduce one while introducing processing delay and potentially distortion. Time-division multiplexing interleaves channel data and must preserve channel identity and timing.

§5.5 Companding, delta modulation and overload

Lesson 5.4 Companding, delta modulation and overload

Unit: 5 — Pulse modulation, PCM and quantization Core model: μ-law companding y=sgn(x)ln(1+μ|x|)/ln(1+μ) for |x|≤1 Suggested study: derive the model, solve its worked case, compare one limiting case, then attempt the open questions.

What is being described?

A nonlinear compressor redistributes quantization levels toward weak input amplitudes, improving perceived or weighted resolution in some source distributions. A complementary expander at the receiver restores approximate amplitude scaling. Companding does not create information: it sacrifices some high-amplitude precision for lower-amplitude detail. Delta modulation instead sends a stepwise difference decision and can fail through slope overload when the step rate is too small to track rapid changes, or show granular noise when steps are unnecessarily large for a flat source.
