§3.1 BJT terminal currents and active-region physics

Unit 3, Lesson 1: BJT terminal currents and active-region physics

Model: Ie=Ic+Ib; Ic≈β Ib Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

In a bipolar transistor, base-emitter injection controls the collector current by transporting minority carriers through a thin base. The conventional β description is a local operating approximation, not a universal constant. Kirchhoff current conservation holds regardless of the transistor operating region. The VBE junction is diode-like, so temperature and current change the operating point.

Draw the relevant circuit or band diagram and name the source of energy, the signal that controls the result, the return path and the reference directions. A model of an individual element must be used with conservation of current and voltage around the complete circuit. This distinction is critical because a device characteristic measured under one set of bias conditions may not hold with a different load. Describe which parameters are external controls and which are intrinsic or empirical device parameters. In bipolar transistor bias and operating regions, an especially important issue is bias feasibility; make it explicit before calculating anything.

Derivation and calculation

Start from Ie=Ic+Ib; Ic≈β Ib. Explain the assumptions required to use the expression and distinguish DC current, incremental gain, instantaneous waveform, average power and RMS quantities wherever they arise. Write the associated Kirchhoff equations and translate all input data to compatible SI units. Verify that the final expression has the dimensions of the required output. If the expression is nonlinear, first identify the valid region; if it is an incremental result, identify the linearization point. An elegant equation is not proof that the circuit's bias or signal swing permits the result.

Worked example. If Ic=2 mA and β=100 in a forward-active approximation, Ib≈20 μA and Ie≈2.02 mA. A 30% beta variation changes base current requirements even if feedback holds Ic nearly fixed.

Limitations and expected departures

Saturation invalidates the simple active relation; transistor β is device- and current-dependent.

This is the boundary of the teaching model, not evidence that all equations are unreliable. §3.2 Operating regions and load lines

Unit 3, Lesson 2: Operating regions and load lines

Model: VCE=VCC−Ic Rc−Ie Re Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

A resistor load line gives the possible collector-emitter voltage versus current combinations imposed by supply and passive resistors. The transistor characteristic intersects this line at the operating point. The line sets limits before a detailed device equation is used. Saturation produces low VCE with both junctions forward-biased; cutoff means negligible collector conduction in the first approximation.

§3.3 Fixed bias versus emitter stabilization

Unit 3, Lesson 3: Fixed bias versus emitter stabilization

Model: Ib≈(Vth−VBE)/(Rth+(β+1)Re) Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

A voltage divider can be represented by a Thevenin supply, but transistor base current loads the divider. An emitter resistor develops a rising voltage as emitter current increases. This subtracts from the effective junction drive and produces negative feedback. Choosing a very large divider resistance saves idle power but makes the bias more beta sensitive.

§3.4 BJT thermal stability and resistor spread

Unit 3, Lesson 4: BJT thermal stability and resistor spread

Model: VBE(T)≈VBE(T0)−2 mV/K (roughly) Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

A bias point can shift with temperature because the semiconductor transport coefficients and the base-emitter junction voltage change. The common −2 mV/K rule is only an approximate local trend for certain fixed-current conditions. A resistor network, emitter feedback and thermal coupling determine whether the shift is tolerable. Avoid interpreting a temperature coefficient as an absolute device specification.

§3.5 Bias design with headroom and dissipation

Unit 3, Lesson 5: Bias design with headroom and dissipation

Model: Ptransistor≈VCE Ic Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

Bias design is a constraint problem: place the operating point where desired signal swing is possible and power limits are satisfied across device spread. A collector voltage that looks centered without a load may not be centered with a finite load. Emitter degeneration improves robustness but consumes DC voltage headroom. Check the device current, collector-emitter voltage and resistor wattages separately.
