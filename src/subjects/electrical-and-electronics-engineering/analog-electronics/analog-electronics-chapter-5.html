§5.1 MOSFET threshold and transfer characteristics

Unit 5, Lesson 1: MOSFET threshold and transfer characteristics

Model: ID≈(k/2)(VGS−VT)² for saturation Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

An enhancement-mode NMOS channel begins conducting progressively as gate voltage increases. In a simplified long-channel strong-inversion model, drain current is proportional to squared overdrive. The threshold parameter is model dependent; subthreshold conduction is not zero. A gate is insulated and has extremely small steady input current, but finite dynamic charge and leakage.

Draw the relevant circuit or band diagram and name the source of energy, the signal that controls the result, the return path and the reference directions. A model of an individual element must be used with conservation of current and voltage around the complete circuit. This distinction is critical because a device characteristic measured under one set of bias conditions may not hold with a different load. Describe which parameters are external controls and which are intrinsic or empirical device parameters. In mos transistors and amplifier topologies, an especially important issue is headroom; make it explicit before calculating anything.

Derivation and calculation

Start from ID≈(k/2)(VGS−VT)² for saturation. Explain the assumptions required to use the expression and distinguish DC current, incremental gain, instantaneous waveform, average power and RMS quantities wherever they arise. Write the associated Kirchhoff equations and translate all input data to compatible SI units. Verify that the final expression has the dimensions of the required output. If the expression is nonlinear, first identify the valid region; if it is an incremental result, identify the linearization point. An elegant equation is not proof that the circuit's bias or signal swing permits the result.

Worked example. For k=2 mA/V², VT=1 V and VGS=2 V in ideal saturation, ID≈1 mA. Raising VGS to 3 V gives ID≈4 mA, provided supply and compliance allow.

§5.2 MOSFET triode and saturation operation

Unit 5, Lesson 2: MOSFET triode and saturation operation

Model: ID=k[(VGS−VT)VDS−VDS²/2] in triode Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

The gradual-channel approximation gives two mathematical regions. For VDS below overdrive, an NMOS behaves approximately as a voltage-controlled resistance, with drain current depending on VDS. For VDS at or above overdrive, a long-channel square-law idealization gives a saturating current. These boundaries are conventions of an approximate model, not abrupt physical changes in real devices.

§5.3 Common-source amplification

Unit 5, Lesson 3: Common-source amplification

Model: Av≈−gm(RD∥RL) Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

A common-source amplifier creates an inverted output because increasing the gate voltage increases drain current and the drain resistor drop. Gain must be computed after finding a DC bias that places the device in the desired region. The MOS gate can present high DC input resistance but not infinite high-frequency impedance due to capacitances. Channel-length modulation produces a finite ro that reduces gain.

§5.4 Source followers and buffering

Unit 5, Lesson 4: Source followers and buffering

Model: Av≈gm RS/(1+gm RS) Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

The common-drain or source-follower topology responds to gate changes with a source change in nearly the same direction. The resulting local feedback limits voltage gain to less than one in the elementary small-signal model but lowers output impedance. Source followers are valuable for driving a moderately resistive load without loading a fragile signal source as severely.

§5.5 MOS differential pairs and bias mirrors

Unit 5, Lesson 5: MOS differential pairs and bias mirrors

Model: ID1+ID2≈Itail Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

A differential pair steers current between branches as the input voltage difference varies. The sum remains near the tail-source current when the transistors stay properly biased. A current mirror supplies a reference-controlled current but introduces compliance voltage, mismatch and finite output resistance. Differential operation rejects an ideal common-mode input only under matched components and sufficient headroom.
