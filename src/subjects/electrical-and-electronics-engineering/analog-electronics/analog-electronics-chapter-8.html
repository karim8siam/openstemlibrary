§8.1 Comparator switching and hysteresis

Unit 8, Lesson 1: Comparator switching and hysteresis

Model: VTH+ > VTH− Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

A comparator reports which of two signals is larger, usually through a saturated or digital output. Positive feedback can establish separate thresholds for rising and falling input, preventing chatter around a noisy switching point. Threshold separation is hysteresis; it is not equivalent to low-pass filtering. The output interface may require pull-up wiring if the comparator has an open-collector or open-drain output.

Draw the relevant circuit or band diagram and name the source of energy, the signal that controls the result, the return path and the reference directions. A model of an individual element must be used with conservation of current and voltage around the complete circuit. This distinction is critical because a device characteristic measured under one set of bias conditions may not hold with a different load. Describe which parameters are external controls and which are intrinsic or empirical device parameters. In power stages, interfaces and robust analog design, an especially important issue is thermal and safety margins; make it explicit before calculating anything.

Derivation and calculation

Start from VTH+ > VTH−. Explain the assumptions required to use the expression and distinguish DC current, incremental gain, instantaneous waveform, average power and RMS quantities wherever they arise. Write the associated Kirchhoff equations and translate all input data to compatible SI units. Verify that the final expression has the dimensions of the required output. If the expression is nonlinear, first identify the valid region; if it is an incremental result, identify the linearization point. An elegant equation is not proof that the circuit's bias or signal swing permits the result.

Worked example. If upper and lower thresholds are +0.2 V and −0.2 V, a rising ramp switches high at +0.2 V and a falling ramp returns low at −0.2 V.

Two independent checks. First examine the zero-drive or zero-load limit where physically applicable. Second change one stated parameter by 10% while holding the others fixed. Limitations and expected departures

Propagation delay, overdrive recovery and input common-mode limits are device specific.

§8.2 Oscillators and startup conditions

Unit 8, Lesson 2: Oscillators and startup conditions

Model: f0=1/(2πRC) for equal-RC Wien network Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

An oscillator converts DC energy into sustained periodic motion through a frequency-selective feedback path and a nonlinear amplitude-setting mechanism. The Barkhausen magnitude and phase conditions describe a candidate steady oscillation but do not alone prove startup or stable amplitude. A Wien-bridge arrangement gives a familiar frequency relationship when its resistors and capacitors are matched.

§8.3 Class-A, B and AB output stages

Unit 8, Lesson 3: Class-A, B and AB output stages

Model: Pout=Vpk²/(2RL) Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

An output stage must provide enough current to drive the load. Class A conducts throughout the cycle and typically dissipates significant idle power. Ideal push-pull class B uses alternate conduction halves and can be more efficient, but devices crossing between conduction states introduce dead-zone distortion. Class AB establishes small quiescent conduction to reduce the dead zone.

§8.4 Output-stage efficiency and thermal path

Unit 8, Lesson 4: Output-stage efficiency and thermal path

Model: ηB,ideal=π Vpk/(4Vs) Outcome: Analyze the operating principle, calculate a reference case, identify the limit of the approximation, and plan an independent check.

Physical mechanism

For an ideal class-B complementary push-pull stage on symmetric rails, average DC supply current follows the rectified load current and efficiency approaches π/4 at full allowable sinusoidal swing. At lower amplitude, the theoretical efficiency is proportionally smaller. Heat is the difference between electrical input and delivered signal power in the simplified model.

Physical mechanism

Sensitivity analysis connects component variation to output variation. Worst-case stacking differs from statistical Monte Carlo assumptions; uniform or Gaussian component distributions must be justified rather than invented. A good design review reports test conditions, power limits, thermal limits, failure modes and methods for reproducing results. An attractive simulated waveform is insufficient evidence of a safe product.
