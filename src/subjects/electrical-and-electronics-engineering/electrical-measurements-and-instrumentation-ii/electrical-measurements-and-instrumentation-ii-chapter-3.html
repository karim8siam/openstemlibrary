§3.1 Foundational Principles & Curriculum Scope: Displacement and Motion Transducers: LVDT, Capacitive, Inductive, and Piezoelectric

Unit 3 — Multichannel and distributed data acquisition

Conceptual thread: When channels are not sampled at the same instant. Application scenario: Synchronous voltage-current estimation using low-energy synthetic waveforms.

Why this topic matters

Sampling, triggers and channel ordering affect derived parameters as much as ADC resolution does. Simultaneous sampling, multiplexing, inter-channel phase calibration and packet timestamping should be evaluated at the system level. Robust acquisition retains both samples and the conditions that produced them. The engineering goal is a measurement whose physical interpretation can survive an independent check. The activity is intentionally model-based: any numbers in assignments, datasets or simulator plots are invented for educational purposes. In actual equipment, frequency-dependent calibration, laboratory safety and environmental ratings would require external evidence.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this unit, you should be able to construct the principal measurement relation and explain its limits; reproduce two independent numerical results; identify at least one coherent alternative explanation for an apparent trend; select the data and metadata needed for a reproducible report; and design a measurement assurance check that does not assume the result it intends to confirm. The last outcome is central: a collection of accurate-looking digital values is not itself a valid measurement.

Suggested sequence

Read the first lesson as a model-building exercise, the second as an instrument or method comparison, the third as an error-analysis problem, and the fourth as a verification and decision exercise. Keep an equation notebook with a units check, an uncertainty table and a record of any assumptions you changed. Then solve the [unit assessment](../assessments/unit-03.html), complete the [integrated project](../projects/unit-03.html) and explore the [interactive model](../simulations/model-03.html).

Model interpretation

The simulator for this unit is Sequential channel phase skew. Compare aperture delay with a sinusoidal period; physical phase defined modulo 360°. It varies only the mathematical quantities visible in its controls; a plotted result does not constitute an equipment qualification or measured sample. For a meaningful parameter study, save the default values, change one input at a time, reproduce a point independently and then look for a counterexample outside the ideal model. The derivative of the curve, not merely its appearance, is often the best clue about sensitivity and where resolution becomes inadequate.

§3.2 Simultaneous versus multiplexed sampling

3.1 — Simultaneous versus multiplexed sampling

Unit: Multichannel and distributed data acquisition Prerequisites: introductory electronics, calculus, signals and probability. Type of evidence: mathematical and synthetic; no physical hardware was tested. Estimated study time: 75–110 minutes including the calculation and verification challenge.

Engineering question and underlying phenomenon

A multiplexed ADC visits channels sequentially, so voltages labeled with the same scan index may refer to different physical instants. If channels are slowly varying, the offset may be negligible; for power, vibrations or phase measurements it becomes a systematic error. Simultaneous sample-and-hold architectures reduce that skew but can retain channel-to-channel filter delays.

Within this unit, the example application is synchronous voltage-current estimation using low-energy synthetic waveforms. A useful outcome is not merely a number: the reader must be able to decide what electrical or physical quantity is inferred, how the inference depends on a reference, and which alternative mechanisms might reproduce the observation. A calibrated indication is still conditional on the quantity being measured, reference range, environmental state and acquisition protocol. Consider whether the experiment will distinguish the input phenomenon from the measurement chain itself. If not, a second observable or controlled intervention is required.

Governing model

Central relation: Interchannel phase skew φ=360 f Δt (degrees).

Interpret every mathematical symbol before substitution. Express frequencies in hertz, durations in seconds, temperatures in the convention required by the equation, and angles in degrees or radians as specified. When an inverse operation is needed, verify that it is unique over the operational range. For ratio measurements, verify that the denominator cannot approach zero; for logarithmic levels, identify the reference quantity, detection bandwidth and impedance convention. More reported digits cannot compensate for an undefined physical variable.

Model development

Classify the relation as a direct physical law, an approximation derived under controlled assumptions, or an empirical calibration curve. Write the independent and dependent variables and check the direction of the physical mechanism. In particular, look for a relevant limiting case: no input, very large impedance, long observation, or an ideal reference.

Specific context: The acquisition record is best understood as a map from each channel and sample index to a physical aperture time. Sorting received packets cannot restore information destroyed by undersampling or dropped observations. Even when the equation is correct, it is valid only under its stated physical model. A good laboratory report differentiates assumptions adopted to make a model solvable, experimentally checked operating limits and parameters that remain completely unknown. The first two can be defended; the third must be stated as an unresolved source of uncertainty rather than assigned an attractive but unjustified small value.

Detailed worked calculation

With four channels scanned 50 µs apart, channel 1 and channel 4 have 150 µs separation. At 500 Hz this equals 27° phase shift, large enough to distort a calculated phase difference or power factor.

Interpretation. This calculation is a model prediction under the stated conditions, not a newly observed experimental result. To audit it, write the substituted numbers next to their units, calculate without premature rounding, and estimate the order of magnitude before accepting the final digits. Distinguish a predicted change caused by a controlled parameter from an apparent change caused by sensor gain, instrument timing, filtering or normalization. If the expected sign of a correction is reversed, review the signal flow and physical reference before changing the equation to match the desired answer.

Checks that should accompany the numerical result

First, change one input while keeping the remaining assumptions fixed and confirm the output moves in the physically expected direction. Second, calculate the result through a different arrangement of the same governing law or a limiting-case approximation. Third, state the span over which the model has been calibrated or independently verified. Fourth, document whether the result is meant to represent an instantaneous value, an RMS estimate, an average over a defined interval or a statistical bound. These distinctions affect what can legitimately be compared with a manufacturer's specification.

Important departures from the ideal model

The configured scan rate alone does not specify actual interchannel aperture times. Input settling after multiplexer switching may create additional amplitude-dependent errors.

It is useful to classify the departures by where they enter. Errors before conversion can distort the physical input; errors in amplification or digitization change the measured indication; timebase and software errors corrupt event ordering; calibration errors convert an otherwise faithful electrical reading to an incorrect physical quantity. The appropriate check differs across these cases. Repeated acquisition on the same unmodified chain can measure repeatability but cannot expose every persistent bias. In practice one should prioritize a small number of physically plausible error sources over a long, unweighted list of hypothetical effects.

Practical verification strategy

Write a timing diagram showing the aperture instant of every channel, not just the time when software received a sample block. Use a second method or reference only when its systematic errors are sufficiently independent. Record conditions that make the check reproducible: model revision, input data, ranges, reference conditions, clock and sample rate, software version, arithmetic precision and the date of the hypothetical experiment. If equipment were involved, the methods would also need actual safety ratings and personnel authorization. The offline simulations in this book test formulas, not equipment compliance.

§3.3 Sample clocks, triggers and record alignment

3.2 — Sample clocks, triggers and record alignment

Unit: Multichannel and distributed data acquisition Prerequisites: introductory electronics, calculus, signals and probability. Type of evidence: mathematical and synthetic; no physical hardware was tested. Estimated study time: 75–110 minutes including the calculation and verification challenge.

Engineering question and underlying phenomenon

Sharing a start trigger is not equivalent to sharing a sample clock. Two devices can begin together and gradually diverge if their oscillators differ. Clock routing, synchronization domains, routing delays, trigger propagation, and ADC aperture jitter all belong in the error budget. Resampling can align data numerically but cannot replace high-frequency content lost to inadequate acquisition bandwidth.

Within this unit, the example application is synchronous voltage-current estimation using low-energy synthetic waveforms. §3.4 Anti-aliasing, channel bandwidth and group delay

3.3 — Anti-aliasing, channel bandwidth and group delay

Unit: Multichannel and distributed data acquisition Prerequisites: introductory electronics, calculus, signals and probability. Type of evidence: mathematical and synthetic; no physical hardware was tested. Estimated study time: 75–110 minutes including the calculation and verification challenge.

Engineering question and underlying phenomenon

An analog front end limits the spectral content arriving at the sampler. A steep anti-alias filter attenuates unwanted high frequencies, but its phase and group delay may be important when comparing channels. A digital low-pass filter after aliasing cannot distinguish a folded high-frequency tone from a genuine in-band tone. Filter validation must therefore precede sampling.

Within this unit, the example application is synchronous voltage-current estimation using low-energy synthetic waveforms. §3.5 Data integrity, timestamps and missing samples

3.4 — Data integrity, timestamps and missing samples

Unit: Multichannel and distributed data acquisition Prerequisites: introductory electronics, calculus, signals and probability. Type of evidence: mathematical and synthetic; no physical hardware was tested. Estimated study time: 75–110 minutes including the calculation and verification challenge.

Engineering question and underlying phenomenon

Acquisition software often introduces hidden assumptions: blocks may be reordered, buffers may overflow, samples may be silently dropped, and time fields may represent transfer time rather than sampling time. A robust record includes source ID, per-channel calibration, clock domain, expected rate, sequence index, condition flags and corruption checks. Missing samples should be recorded as missing rather than interpolated without disclosure.
