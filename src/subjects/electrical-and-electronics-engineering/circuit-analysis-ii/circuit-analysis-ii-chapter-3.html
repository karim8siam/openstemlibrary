§3.1 Balanced three-phase generation

Unit 3, Lesson 1: Balanced three-phase generation

Primary relation: va+vb+vc=0 Unit outcome: Relate balanced phase quantities to line measurements and identify what changes under unbalanced conditions.

What the relation means

Ideal balanced phase voltages have identical magnitudes and 120° separation. Their instantaneous sum is zero and total three-phase power is constant for a balanced sinusoidal load. The expression va+vb+vc=0 is meaningful only when the input, output, reference arrows, and component idealizations are stated. Draw one simple circuit or signal diagram, label every referenced terminal, and identify which terms can be measured and which are inferred from the model. Distinguish an algebraic sign from a physical polarity; a sign reversal after changing an arrow is usually a coordinate choice, not a violation of energy conservation.

Detailed engineering reasoning

For a balanced three-phase source, select a sequence and hold it consistently throughout the drawing. Positive sequence often uses 0°, −120°, and +120° phase order in phasor analysis. The line-to-line voltage is a phasor difference, not merely a magnified phase-to-neutral voltage. Derive Vab=Van−Vbn before using the √3 relationship.

For balanced three-phase generation, begin with a simplest-case prediction before substituting values. If an excitation vanishes, a passive zero-initial-state model must not invent an output. If one parameter becomes very large or small, interpret the corresponding limit with the relevant source and load held fixed. For an AC circuit, a magnitude-only answer is incomplete whenever phase matters, and for a transient circuit the initial state changes the waveform even if the final DC steady state is the same.

Worked example and interpretation

With a 230 V RMS phase voltage, ideal phases can be 230∠0°, 230∠−120°, 230∠120° V. Start from the stated equation, convert any milli-, micro- or kilo- prefixes to SI units, and substitute only quantities that share a consistent peak/RMS convention. Report the numerical quantity with physical units. Then explain what the sign or phase of the result tells an observer. Use another representation—for example rectangular versus polar complex notation or time versus frequency response—to check the result without simply repeating the first arithmetic.

The model predicts a conditional answer. It does not predict tolerance drift, insulation integrity, wiring parasitics, thermal runaway, bandwidth of an instrument, or safe operation unless those effects have been explicitly included. A nominal value should therefore be reported alongside its assumed operating conditions.

Failure mode to detect

Three equal magnitudes with arbitrary phase angles are not a balanced three-phase set. To diagnose this error, ask which assumption was silently changed. Trace the computation back to the definition of the source, the selected terminals, and the units of the constitutive law. A useful check is to repeat the calculation with a parameter changed by a factor of two and decide whether the direction of the output change is consistent with physical intuition. Where the network has several branches, apply KCL, KVL, and a power balance rather than trusting a single number.

§3.2 Wye loads and line quantities

Unit 3, Lesson 2: Wye loads and line quantities

Primary relation: VL=√3 Vphase; IL=Iphase Unit outcome: Relate balanced phase quantities to line measurements and identify what changes under unbalanced conditions.

What the relation means

For a balanced positive-sequence wye system, line voltage magnitude is √3 times phase voltage; line current equals branch current. The line-to-line phasor also has an additional ±30° angle. The expression VL=√3 Vphase; IL=Iphase is meaningful only when the input, output, reference arrows, and component idealizations are stated. Draw one simple circuit or signal diagram, label every referenced terminal, and identify which terms can be measured and which are inferred from the model. Distinguish an algebraic sign from a physical polarity; a sign reversal after changing an arrow is usually a coordinate choice, not a violation of energy conservation.

Detailed engineering reasoning

A balanced resistive three-phase load can deliver nearly constant aggregate instantaneous power even though each phase pulsates individually. This helps explain smooth electromechanical operation without implying that mechanical torque ripple disappears in every real motor. Three-phase power equations use RMS line voltage and line current; a 400 V line specification is not 400 V on every wye phase.

For wye loads and line quantities, begin with a simplest-case prediction before substituting values. If an excitation vanishes, a passive zero-initial-state model must not invent an output. If one parameter becomes very large or small, interpret the corresponding limit with the relevant source and load held fixed. For an AC circuit, a magnitude-only answer is incomplete whenever phase matters, and for a transient circuit the initial state changes the waveform even if the final DC steady state is the same.

§3.3 Delta loads and branch currents

Unit 3, Lesson 3: Delta loads and branch currents

Primary relation: VL=Vphase; IL=√3 Iphase Unit outcome: Relate balanced phase quantities to line measurements and identify what changes under unbalanced conditions.

What the relation means

In a balanced delta connection, each branch is across a pair of lines and its current combines vectorially at each line terminal. The √3 factor belongs to current, not branch voltage. The expression VL=Vphase; IL=√3 Iphase is meaningful only when the input, output, reference arrows, and component idealizations are stated. Draw one simple circuit or signal diagram, label every referenced terminal, and identify which terms can be measured and which are inferred from the model. Distinguish an algebraic sign from a physical polarity; a sign reversal after changing an arrow is usually a coordinate choice, not a violation of energy conservation.

Detailed engineering reasoning

An unbalanced neutral-current calculation is an excellent vector check. Write all three phase currents as rectangular complex numbers; sum; then reverse sign for the defined neutral reference. With a missing or disconnected neutral, the load star point can shift, requiring a complete node-voltage solution. Never propose physical work on energized three-phase distribution: the exercises are software-only.

For delta loads and branch currents, begin with a simplest-case prediction before substituting values. If an excitation vanishes, a passive zero-initial-state model must not invent an output. If one parameter becomes very large or small, interpret the corresponding limit with the relevant source and load held fixed. For an AC circuit, a magnitude-only answer is incomplete whenever phase matters, and for a transient circuit the initial state changes the waveform even if the final DC steady state is the same.
