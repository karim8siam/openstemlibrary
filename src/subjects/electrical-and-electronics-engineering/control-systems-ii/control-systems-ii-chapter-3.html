§3.1 Observability and reconstructible state

Lesson 3.1 — Observability and reconstructible state

Unit: State estimation and Kalman filtering. Prerequisites: Book 11 feedback and state-space fundamentals; preceding lessons in this unit. Learning objectives: explain the model, reproduce the worked case, identify an important failure condition, and independently solve the problems.

Concept and mechanism

A linear system may have internal modes not visible from one sensor. The observability matrix stacks C, CA and higher powers; full column rank allows state recovery for a finite-dimensional LTI system in principle. An observable pair may still be poorly conditioned, making numerical estimates sensitive to measurement noise. The test describes a model, not whether a cheap sensor can achieve a required physical accuracy.

Governing relation: O=[C; C A; ...; C A^(n-1)]; rank(O)=n for observable n-state model

To interpret this expression, mark each symbol as a state, command, observation, uncertainty, noise statistic or design weight. Determine whether the result is continuous-time, sampled-time, a static mapping, or a bound. The statement may require nonsingular matrices, bounded variation, a linear model or known timing. List the specific hypotheses before drawing an engineering conclusion.

Worked example

For A=[[0,1],[-4,-3]] and C=[1,0], the two-state observability matrix is [[1,0],[0,1]], so rank=2. A position signal contains information about velocity through time evolution. If C=[0,0], the matrix is zero and no state reconstruction can follow from output observations.

Independent check: Write the same example as an equation whose two sides must agree after substitution. Check the sign and limiting cases; then change only one number and predict whether the outcome increases, decreases, or requires a full recalculation. Do not mistake numerical agreement under synthetic data for physical validation. A result describing a covariance is conditional on the noise model; one describing stability is conditional on the model and its defined perturbations; one describing an optimum is conditional on both the objective and the feasible set.

Interpretation and decision

Observability is an information question: it is neither a demand to measure all states directly nor a proof that the estimate remains accurate during a sensor fault. Modeling assumptions about A and timing matter greatly.

The design choice should identify the goal being improved—tracking, robustness, estimation, control effort or timing—and a quantity that may become worse. Distinguish a sufficient theorem condition from a necessary condition. For optimization explicitly report actuator limits; for estimation report which measurements support the inference. A calculated number is useful only when its assumptions and units are available to the reader.

When the argument fails

Two eigenmodes with nearly identical sensor signatures can make a model mathematically observable but practically difficult. Singular values and noise sensitivity give information beyond a binary rank calculation.

Indicate which measured variable or computed check would reveal the discrepancy. Repeating the original calculation at the same nominal point cannot test a modeling assumption that never appeared in the equation.

§3.2 Luenberger observer and error dynamics

Lesson 3.2 — Luenberger observer and error dynamics

Unit: State estimation and Kalman filtering. Prerequisites: Book 11 feedback and state-space fundamentals; preceding lessons in this unit. Learning objectives: explain the model, reproduce the worked case, identify an important failure condition, and independently solve the problems.

Concept and mechanism

A deterministic observer evolves a model state and corrects its trajectory when a measurement differs from prediction. The correction term L(y−C xhat) drives estimation error according to A−LC in the ideal model. Observer gain is chosen for adequate convergence without excessive sensitivity to noise. Controller and observer poles serve different purposes and need not coincide.

Governing relation: xhat_dot=A xhat+B u+L(y-C xhat); e_dot=(A-LC)e

To interpret this expression, mark each symbol as a state, command, observation, uncertainty, noise statistic or design weight. Determine whether the result is continuous-time, sampled-time, a static mapping, or a bound. The statement may require nonsingular matrices, bounded variation, a linear model or known timing. List the specific hypotheses before drawing an engineering conclusion.

Worked example

Consider a scalar plant x_dot=−x+u with measurement y=x and observer gain L=4. Estimation error follows e_dot=−5e, so a starting error e(0)=2 becomes 2e^(−5t). At t=0.2 s it is approximately 0.7358. If sensor noise v enters y, the observer correction includes Lv, which can amplify high-frequency noise when L is increased.

Interpretation and decision

Faster observer poles are attractive in an ideal noiseless model; they are not free. A modest observer may provide a better noise-versus-delay tradeoff for a quantized instrument. The appropriate bandwidth depends on the controller dynamics and sample rate.

The design choice should identify the goal being improved—tracking, robustness, estimation, control effort or timing—and a quantity that may become worse. When the argument fails

If the actual plant has bias or differs from A, the clean homogeneous error equation no longer holds. An observer can converge to a biased estimate rather than the true state.

§3.3 Kalman prediction and measurement update

Lesson 3.3 — Kalman prediction and measurement update

Unit: State estimation and Kalman filtering. Prerequisites: Book 11 feedback and state-space fundamentals; preceding lessons in this unit. Learning objectives: explain the model, reproduce the worked case, identify an important failure condition, and independently solve the problems.

Concept and mechanism

The Kalman filter forms a minimum-variance linear estimator under specified linear-Gaussian assumptions; even without Gaussian noise, the covariance recursion still follows for the linear minimum mean-square formulation under appropriate second moments. Process covariance Q describes uncertainty in model evolution and measurement covariance R describes sensor uncertainty. A larger R reduces trust in an individual noisy measurement, all else equal.

Governing relation: Pminus=A P A^T+Q; K=Pminus H^T(H Pminus H^T+R)^(-1); Pplus=(I-KH)Pminus

To interpret this expression, mark each symbol as a state, command, observation, uncertainty, noise statistic or design weight. Determine whether the result is continuous-time, sampled-time, a static mapping, or a bound. The statement may require nonsingular matrices, bounded variation, a linear model or known timing. List the specific hypotheses before drawing an engineering conclusion.

Worked example

For a scalar random-walk model A=H=1, prior covariance P=1, process covariance Q=0.25, measurement covariance R=0.75, the prediction covariance is Pminus=1.25. Kalman gain is 1.25/(1.25+0.75)=0.625 and posterior covariance is (1−0.625)×1.25=0.46875. If predicted state is 4 and measured value is 6, the corrected state is 4+0.625(6−4)=5.25.

Check the sign and limiting cases; then change only one number and predict whether the outcome increases, decreases, or requires a full recalculation. Do not mistake numerical agreement under synthetic data for physical validation. A result describing a covariance is conditional on the noise model; one describing stability is conditional on the model and its defined perturbations; one describing an optimum is conditional on both the objective and the feasible set.

Interpretation and decision

The covariance reduction measures uncertainty under the model assumptions, not guaranteed physical error. For numerical implementations the Joseph covariance update form can better preserve symmetry and positive semidefiniteness when rounding or matrix conditioning matters.

The design choice should identify the goal being improved—tracking, robustness, estimation, control effort or timing—and a quantity that may become worse. Distinguish a sufficient theorem condition from a necessary condition. For optimization explicitly report actuator limits; for estimation report which measurements support the inference. A calculated number is useful only when its assumptions and units are available to the reader.

When the argument fails

If sensor noise has an unknown constant offset, treating it as independent zero-mean Gaussian noise is mis-specified. The filter may show low internal covariance while producing a biased physical estimate.

§3.4 Innovations, noise tuning and estimator validation

Lesson 3.4 — Innovations, noise tuning and estimator validation

Unit: State estimation and Kalman filtering. Prerequisites: Book 11 feedback and state-space fundamentals; preceding lessons in this unit. Learning objectives: explain the model, reproduce the worked case, identify an important failure condition, and independently solve the problems.

Concept and mechanism

The innovation is the difference between observed and predicted measurement. Its expected covariance can be derived from the prediction covariance and sensor model. Innovations should be examined over time for autocorrelation, changing variance, sign bias and outliers; a single small innovation says little. Estimator validation also compares reconstructed states against an independent reference when possible.

Governing relation: nu_k=y_k-H xhatminus_k; S_k=H Pminus_k H^T+R; NIS=nu^T S^(-1)nu

To interpret this expression, mark each symbol as a state, command, observation, uncertainty, noise statistic or design weight. Determine whether the result is continuous-time, sampled-time, a static mapping, or a bound. The statement may require nonsingular matrices, bounded variation, a linear model or known timing. List the specific hypotheses before drawing an engineering conclusion.

Worked example

Suppose a scalar innovation is ν=2 with predicted variance S=4. Its normalized innovation squared is 2²/4=1. For another innovation ν=6 with the same variance, NIS=9, signaling a much larger discrepancy relative to modeled noise. This statistic should be interpreted using a specified distribution and repeated data, not by applying an arbitrary alarm threshold to one sample.

Independent check: Write the same example as an equation whose two sides must agree after substitution. Check the sign and limiting cases; then change only one number and predict whether the outcome increases, decreases, or requires a full recalculation. Do not mistake numerical agreement under synthetic data for physical validation. A result describing a covariance is conditional on the noise model; one describing stability is conditional on the model and its defined perturbations; one describing an optimum is conditional on both the objective and the feasible set.

Interpretation and decision

An innovation can be small because the filter follows a faulty sensor too closely, especially under a mis-tuned R. Independent physical checks are essential for critical signals. Changing Q and R simply until a plot looks smooth risks hiding a model defect.

The design choice should identify the goal being improved—tracking, robustness, estimation, control effort or timing—and a quantity that may become worse. Distinguish a sufficient theorem condition from a necessary condition. For optimization explicitly report actuator limits; for estimation report which measurements support the inference. A calculated number is useful only when its assumptions and units are available to the reader.

When the argument fails

An intermittent delay can make innovations temporally correlated even when the logged covariance looks reasonable. Stale timestamps need to be represented in the model or rejected by a verified data-handling rule.

§3.5 Engineering Design Project: Controllability, Observability, and Kalman Canonical Decompositions

Unit 3 applied project — Estimator consistency and bias detection

Engineering question

Simulate a scalar random walk with Q=0.1, R=0.4, a deterministic measurement perturbation, and a late injected sensor bias of +1. Compare innovation, estimated state and covariance before and after bias. Test a second filter with R=4 and describe how innovation and responsiveness differ. The validation report must distinguish assumed covariance from observed model mismatch.

This is an offline design and evidence exercise. Its output is a reproducible mathematical study and a bounded conclusion, not permission to commission a physical controller. Write the study question in a sentence that could be falsified by a measured trajectory or a computed counterexample. Explain why the chosen input variation contains more information than an arbitrary random curve.

Setup, model and data protocol

Specify each variable, unit and initial state. The baseline model must be documented independently of the controller. List any linearization point, noise covariance, uncertainty interval, constraints or bounded disturbance. If the project contains a numerical experiment, provide either a short script or a CSV data table, and log the equation and numerical integration interval that produced it. Avoid a test in which the same output is used both to choose every model parameter and to certify accuracy. If this project cannot be evaluated without a missing sensor or unsupported assumption, make the gap an explicit result.

Required analytical work

  1. Derive the governing state equation, transfer equation, cost, covariance update or stability inequality appropriate to this unit. Where possible, independently derive a special case without a library solver.
  2. Calculate a baseline numerical value and check it using a distinct method: direct substitution, a matrix identity, a simulated recurrence or an analytically known limit. Include the expected dimensions.
  3. Declare a testable range for one key parameter. Sample several values and describe what is and is not proven by that sample. For a mathematical worst-case guarantee, state why the chosen analytic bound applies to the full nominated uncertainty set.
  4. Check performance and feasibility separately. A low cost does not imply constraints are met; a stable trajectory does not prove that an actuator command is allowed. Where the optimizer or model predicts an impossible demand, report infeasibility plainly.
  5. Record at least one deliberately adverse scenario: sensor bias, delayed packet, perturbation outside the fit range, insufficient excitation, a saturated actuator, or a change in the model’s region of validity.

Measurement and critical interpretation

Information audit: Draw the causal arrows between state, input and measured output. Compare a measured trace with the estimator prediction without using that same trace to retune every unknown coefficient. Record where unobservable modes, unmeasured bias or timing uncertainty can enter.

A technically useful report separates mathematical truths under explicit assumptions, simulations of those assumptions, and empirical claims that require physical evidence. Explain which additional variable would most improve confidence. A compelling graph should show labeled axes and a model-versus-reference comparison, not just a beautiful output trace. If a proposed controller improves response time but worsens effort, the tradeoff belongs in the conclusion. Do not discard a failed scenario merely because it disrupts the nominal result.

Required report package

  • Model card: signals and dimensions, operating range, nominal and uncertain parameters, initial state and constraints.
  • One derivation checked by a second independent calculation, with all rounding disclosed.
  • A baseline numeric table and an adverse-case numeric table, each with reproducible values.
  • At least two plots with captions explaining what the curves demonstrate and what they do not.
  • A decision paragraph about the next cheapest test that could reject an important assumption.
  • A separate limitations note identifying implementation, sensor, timing, actuator and safety evidence not supplied by this exercise.

Assessment rubric

25% analytical correctness means the mathematical object and its conditions are identified precisely. 20% numerical traceability means the results can be recomputed from the stated data. 20% comparison requires a meaningful alternative, sensitivity or failure scenario. 20% engineering interpretation requires an explicit distinction between a passed model test and an unverified deployment assumption. 15% communication covers units, graph labels, repeatability and clear identification of the limits of conclusions. Strong work may conclude that a proposed design is not justified by the evidence; that is not a failure of the assignment.

Solved Problem Example 3.3: Controllability, Observability, and Kalman Canonical Decompositions Case Analysis 3

For a scalar random-walk model A=H=1, prior covariance P=1, process covariance Q=0.25, measurement covariance R=0.75, the prediction covariance is Pminus=1.25. Kalman gain is 1.25/(1.25+0.75)=0.625 and posterior covariance is (1−0.625)×1.25=0.46875. If predicted state is 4 and measured value is 6, the corrected state is 4+0.625(6−4)=5.25.
