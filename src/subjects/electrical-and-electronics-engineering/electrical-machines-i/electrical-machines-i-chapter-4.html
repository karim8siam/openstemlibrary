§4.1 Three-phase transformer connections

4.1 Three-phase transformer connections

Physical model and mathematical relationship

Wye and delta winding arrangements set line-to-phase voltage/current relations. For balanced wye, V_line=sqrt(3)V_phase; for delta, I_line=sqrt(3)I_phase. Vector group controls phase shift. The core procedure is to select a reference polarity and sign convention, distinguish an ideal relation from an experimentally calibrated one, and account for quantities whose units differ. Electromagnetic quantities must be checked against the associated conservation law: electric current and flux linkage describe excitation; voltages and torques describe observable exchange; and losses reduce usable output. In rotating or time-varying equipment, steady-state expressions must not be extrapolated to startup without tracking stored energy.

Fully worked numerical example

A balanced 400 V line-to-line wye secondary has phase voltage 400/sqrt(3)=230.94 V. A phase load of 10 A gives apparent power sqrt(3)40010=6.93 kVA. Write the given quantities in SI units before substitution, then compute the predicted response and its physical meaning. If an ideal result conflicts with a device rating, the correct engineering action is to question the simplifying assumptions rather than force the physical device to satisfy the equation. For a measured system, use its nameplate, empirical characteristic curves and operating limits to refine the estimate.

Failure modes, units, and boundary conditions

Parallel three-phase transformers need compatible voltage ratio, phase displacement, polarity and impedance characteristics. A useful independent verification is dimensional analysis, followed by one limiting case: for example, zero excitation, zero frequency, or no mechanical load. In magnetic systems, material saturation invalidates proportional field laws; in electric machines, parasitic resistance and friction introduce heat; and in measurements, uncertainty prevents an exact empirical conclusion.

Comprehensive Theoretical Derivations & Analytical Modeling

In electrical machinery analysis, quantitative formulation begins from Maxwell's integral laws applied to ferromagnetic geometries. For 4.1 Three-phase transformer connections, we define the magnetic circuit relationship:

$$\mathcal{F} = N I = \oint \vec{H} \cdot d\vec{l} = \Phi \left( \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} + \mathcal{R}_{\text{gap}} \right)$$

where the reluctance $\mathcal{R}$ is governed by path length $l$, cross-sectional area $A$, and effective permeability $\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r$:

$$\mathcal{R} = \frac{l}{\mu A}$$

When analyzing electromechanical energy conversion in Unit 4, the differential mechanical force developed along coordinate $x$ is derived rigorously from the magnetic coenergy $W_f'(i, x)$:

$$F_e = \left. \frac{\partial W_f'(i, x)}{\partial x} \right|_{i = \text{const}} = \frac{1}{2} i^2 \frac{dL(x)}{dx}$$

For rotational devices, the developed electromagnetic torque becomes:

$$T_e = \left. \frac{\partial W_f'(i, \theta)}{\partial \theta} \right|_{i = \text{const}} = \frac{1}{2} i^2 \frac{dL(\theta)}{d\theta}$$

Under steady-state machine operation, terminal voltage balances internal induced electromotive force $E_a$ and winding impedance drops:

$$V_t = E_a \pm I_a R_a$$

where the sign reflects generator (+ for internal EMF) or motor (- for terminal supply) operating quadrants. Core loss segregation accounts for hysteresis loss $P_h = k_h f B_{\max}^n$ and eddy current loss $P_e = k_e f^2 B_{\max}^2$, providing complete physical fidelity.

§4.2 Autotransformers and isolation

4.2 Autotransformers and isolation

Physical model and mathematical relationship

An autotransformer shares part of its winding between input and output; it can reduce copper and size when ratios are near unity but generally lacks galvanic isolation. The core procedure is to select a reference polarity and sign convention, distinguish an ideal relation from an experimentally calibrated one, and account for quantities whose units differ. Electromagnetic quantities must be checked against the associated conservation law: electric current and flux linkage describe excitation; voltages and torques describe observable exchange; and losses reduce usable output. In rotating or time-varying equipment, steady-state expressions must not be extrapolated to startup without tracking stored energy.

§4.4 Load sharing and parallel operation

4.4 Load sharing and parallel operation

Physical model and mathematical relationship

Two transformers operating in parallel share load roughly inversely with their per-unit impedances if ratios and phase angles match. Different X/R ratios cause unequal real/reactive division and possible circulating currents. The core procedure is to select a reference polarity and sign convention, distinguish an ideal relation from an experimentally calibrated one, and account for quantities whose units differ. Electromagnetic quantities must be checked against the associated conservation law: electric current and flux linkage describe excitation; voltages and torques describe observable exchange; and losses reduce usable output. In rotating or time-varying equipment, steady-state expressions must not be extrapolated to startup without tracking stored energy.

Fully worked numerical example

For equal-rated transformers with 4% and 5% impedance magnitudes and approximately equal impedance angles, a 900 kVA load splits approximately 500 kVA and 400 kVA.
