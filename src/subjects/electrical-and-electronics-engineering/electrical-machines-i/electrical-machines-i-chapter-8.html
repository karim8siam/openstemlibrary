§8.1 Loss segregation and efficiency tests

8.1 Loss segregation and efficiency tests

Physical model and mathematical relationship

Evaluate copper, field, core, friction and stray-load losses separately when possible. Input-output measurement requires synchronized, calibrated electrical and mechanical instruments. The core procedure is to select a reference polarity and sign convention, distinguish an ideal relation from an experimentally calibrated one, and account for quantities whose units differ. Electromagnetic quantities must be checked against the associated conservation law: electric current and flux linkage describe excitation; voltages and torques describe observable exchange; and losses reduce usable output. In rotating or time-varying equipment, steady-state expressions must not be extrapolated to startup without tracking stored energy.

Fully worked numerical example

For 2 kW shaft output at 2.4 kW electrical input, efficiency is 83.33%; 400 W total loss remains to be apportioned through targeted tests. Write the given quantities in SI units before substitution, then compute the predicted response and its physical meaning. If an ideal result conflicts with a device rating, the correct engineering action is to question the simplifying assumptions rather than force the physical device to satisfy the equation. For a measured system, use its nameplate, empirical characteristic curves and operating limits to refine the estimate.

Failure modes, units, and boundary conditions

A single operating point does not establish peak efficiency or suitable operating envelope. A useful independent verification is dimensional analysis, followed by one limiting case: for example, zero excitation, zero frequency, or no mechanical load. In magnetic systems, material saturation invalidates proportional field laws; in electric machines, parasitic resistance and friction introduce heat; and in measurements, uncertainty prevents an exact empirical conclusion.

Comprehensive Theoretical Derivations & Analytical Modeling

In electrical machinery analysis, quantitative formulation begins from Maxwell's integral laws applied to ferromagnetic geometries. For 8.1 Loss segregation and efficiency tests, we define the magnetic circuit relationship:

$$\mathcal{F} = N I = \oint \vec{H} \cdot d\vec{l} = \Phi \left( \mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} + \mathcal{R}_{\text{gap}} \right)$$

where the reluctance $\mathcal{R}$ is governed by path length $l$, cross-sectional area $A$, and effective permeability $\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r$:

$$\mathcal{R} = \frac{l}{\mu A}$$

When analyzing electromechanical energy conversion in Unit 8, the differential mechanical force developed along coordinate $x$ is derived rigorously from the magnetic coenergy $W_f'(i, x)$:

$$F_e = \left. \frac{\partial W_f'(i, x)}{\partial x} \right|_{i = \text{const}} = \frac{1}{2} i^2 \frac{dL(x)}{dx}$$

For rotational devices, the developed electromagnetic torque becomes:

$$T_e = \left. \frac{\partial W_f'(i, \theta)}{\partial \theta} \right|_{i = \text{const}} = \frac{1}{2} i^2 \frac{dL(\theta)}{d\theta}$$

Under steady-state machine operation, terminal voltage balances internal induced electromotive force $E_a$ and winding impedance drops:

$$V_t = E_a \pm I_a R_a$$

where the sign reflects generator (+ for internal EMF) or motor (- for terminal supply) operating quadrants. Core loss segregation accounts for hysteresis loss $P_h = k_h f B_{\max}^n$ and eddy current loss $P_e = k_e f^2 B_{\max}^2$, providing complete physical fidelity.

Unit 8 assessment

1. Calculation: Recompute the first worked example in this unit after increasing its central excitation (current, voltage, flux, speed or load) by 10%. Declare whether linear scaling is justified and show units.

2. Model critique: Select a different lesson within this unit and identify two mechanisms absent from its ideal equation. Explain how each mechanism would alter a specific measurement.

3. Design decision: Develop a one-paragraph specification for a laboratory device from this unit, including a rated quantity, a safety boundary, and a verification test.

4. Interpretation: Describe one conservation law that constrains the unit's numerical answers, and show its corresponding terms.

Marking guide: For question 1, the numeric answer depends on the worked example and whether its equations are linear; give credit for correctly substituting and rechecking assumptions. For questions 2–4, require explicit physical mechanisms, units, realistic protection and an objective acceptance criterion.

Integrated capstone

Select a 230 V, 50 Hz single-phase transformer feeding a regulated DC drive and a separately excited DC motor. Establish a rated load, transformer equivalent impedance, DC bus model and motor torque-speed requirement. Compute an energy balance at two load points. Predict how copper loss changes at 50% current, and discuss why magnetic core loss need not follow the same scaling. Give a test plan (open-circuit and short-circuit transformer tests only under supervised lab procedure, low-voltage motor measurement with protected drive), error budget and two fail-safe shutdown conditions. Do not energize a high-voltage system based on this exercise.
