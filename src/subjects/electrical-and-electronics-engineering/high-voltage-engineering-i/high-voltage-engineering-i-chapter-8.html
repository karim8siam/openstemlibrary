§8.1 Foundational Principles & Curriculum Scope: Generation of Switching Impulses and High-Current Impulse Waveforms

Unit 8: Qualification, condition, and engineering decisions

Why this subject matters

High-voltage engineering uses layered evidence rather than a single magic number. Material procurement, installation, environmental duty, measurement uncertainty, safety programs, inspections and acceptance criteria must align with the intended equipment application.

Common reference case and assumptions

Prepare a software-only insulation system dossier covering stress, ageing, faults, measurement credibility, repairability and documents a reviewer would request. Do not substitute a simulated breakdown value for standardized withstand qualification.

Outcomes and verification

Evaluate failure first, document uncertainties explicitly, and write go/no-go decisions whose acceptance evidence is testable by a qualified organization.

A researcher's reading protocol

Construct a requirement-by-requirement evidence table with status: analytical, simulated, measured, independently tested, or missing. Trace each claim to a specific standard and edition when relevant. Prioritize irreversible consequences and repair access before optimizing a single numerical margin. The model is intentionally limited to analytical and offline-computation exercises. A student should identify the quantity being predicted and ask what observational evidence would be needed to validate it. The location of the maximum physical field is especially significant: an average stress may conceal a sharp local concentration at a boundary, edge, void, accessory or transient discontinuity.

When reading Lessons 8.1–8.4, first reconstruct the derivation, then change one variable in the supplied worked example. Carry the physical units consistently and ask whether a plotted curve can be traced back to the same symbol definitions as the equation. Use the [interactive software model](../simulations/model-08.html) after the hand calculation so the plot serves as a diagnostic rather than an opaque answer generator.

What counts as evidence

The unit challenge is making a defensible decision even when the correct answer is not established. Distinguish proof of code execution, numerical agreement with a mathematical model, calibration evidence, dielectric qualification, and safe operation. These are five separate accomplishments; none implies the next automatically. All practice in this book is software-only. Do not build test apparatus, charge capacitors or obtain energized equipment for these exercises.

Suggested learning route

Read all four lessons and reproduce the arithmetic in a notebook. Complete the project with a baseline case and three documented variations. Attempt the unit assessment before consulting its solution notes. Record a clear status for each conclusion: derived, numerically checked, simulated, empirically supported or unresolved. There are no fabricated laboratory observations in this book.

§8.2 Insulation stress envelopes and fault scenarios

Lesson 8.1: Insulation stress envelopes and fault scenarios

Unit: Qualification, condition, and engineering decisions. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

Equipment experiences continuous voltage, periodic variation and rare stress events. A stress envelope must include polarity, rise time, repetition, location and environmental conditions. Equating a maximum terminal voltage with every internal field maximum can conceal interface stress.

Governing equations and interpretation

A schematic stress ratio S=Epeak/Ereference is only interpretable if the same field measure, temperature and environmental state are used; it is not a failure probability.

Fully stated worked example

Suppose a software-only model predicts 3 MV/m nominal peak field and 4.2 MV/m in a geometric tolerance case. The ratio is 1.4, demanding investigation of the deviation rather than an invented pass/fail threshold.

Reconstructing the model

Define every boundary condition and symbol before calculating. Derive the supplied relation from a recognizable physical law: Gauss’s law, conservation of charge, electromagnetic-wave continuity, equivalent-circuit impedance or probability bookkeeping as appropriate. If a derivative or spatial gradient appears, write what is being held constant and what geometric symmetry is assumed. The derivation must connect to an experimentally meaningful quantity rather than stop at formal algebra.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

No trustworthy withstand verdict is possible without material qualification, geometry validation, tolerance control and the appropriate application standard.

§8.3 Failure mode, detection and consequence analysis

Lesson 8.2: Failure mode, detection and consequence analysis

Unit: Qualification, condition, and engineering decisions. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

An FMEA separates physical failure mechanisms from their consequences and observability. A ranked risk priority number may help order discussion, but multiplying ordinal scores is not a physical probability. Safety-critical modes may deserve immediate action even with uncertain occurrence rates.

Governing equations and interpretation

An illustrative RPN=S×O×D combines ordinal severity, occurrence and detectability scales. The multiplication is a prioritization convention, not a measured expected loss.

Fully stated worked example

If S=9, O=3 and D=6, RPN=162, while S=5,O=6,D=5 gives 150. One should not conclude the first is exactly 8% riskier; the scales are ordinal.

Dimensional and limiting checks

Check dimensions at each intermediate step. In a relationship containing logarithms or exponentials, ensure their arguments are dimensionless: empirical constants may carry units precisely to make this possible. Then push one variable toward a simple limiting case. Ask whether the outcome is consistent with the physical arrangement, and identify a parameter range where the equation would cease to describe the real world.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

Real decisions require hazard controls, validated failure rates where available, and explicit treatment of low-frequency catastrophic events.

Physical mechanism

A design calculation, type test, routine production test, site acceptance record and surveillance result serve different purposes. Formal requirements vary with asset category, jurisdiction and the standard edition incorporated into a contract. A textbook cannot issue certification.

Governing equations and interpretation

An evidence matrix maps each claim to a test method, responsible lab, calibration traceability, sample identification, environment and acceptance criterion. A blank field means UNVERIFIED, not assumed compliant.

Fully stated worked example

For a proposed withstand claim, write: claim unknown; specimen identification missing; waveform definition requires IEC-standard test plan; measuring-system approval absent; environmental correction absent. The rational status is pending, not pass.

Comparing alternative mechanisms

Write down at least two competing explanations for the same observation. One can be the intended low-order model, and the other can be an omitted physical process such as moisture, a floating conductor, spatial inhomogeneity, instrument loading, dielectric ageing, or waveform bandwidth. Explain what additional observation would discriminate the hypotheses. A falsifiable comparison is more useful than repeating a likely answer.

Physical mechanism

Condition-based maintenance combines inspections, operational events, diagnostic tests and deterioration mechanisms. A single trending signal cannot quantify remaining life unless failure physics and thresholds are validated. Engineering decisions need a clear route for reversibility and investigation.

Governing equations and interpretation

A basic expected-cost model is E[C]=Σpi Ci only if event probabilities pi are justified and outcomes are defined consistently. Qualitative scenarios should remain scenarios rather than disguised probability estimates.

Fully stated worked example

Consider independent illustrative scenarios with annual probabilities 0.01 and 0.001 and repair impacts $10k and $200k. The expected annual cost is $300 under those assumed inputs; this is a demonstration, not a validated reliability forecast.

Making a controlled decision

Set a baseline, change only one controlled variable, and recalculate the metric using the stated formula. Report the change numerically, including units. Then ask whether the change also introduces an adverse effect: higher stored energy, field enhancement, leakage current, uncertainty, ageing stress, or loss of a valid approximation. End with an evidence request that a qualified technical reviewer could act on.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

Correlated events, human harm, environmental consequences and incomplete probability knowledge can dominate a simplistic financial model.
