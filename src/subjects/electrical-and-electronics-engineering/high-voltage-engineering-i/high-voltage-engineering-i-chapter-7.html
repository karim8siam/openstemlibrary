§7.1 Foundational Principles & Curriculum Scope: Generation of Impulse Voltages: Single-Stage and Multi-Stage Marx Generators

Unit 7: Partial discharge and insulation diagnostics

Why this subject matters

Partial discharge is a localized event that does not completely bridge the electrode separation. The measurable terminal pulse depends on circuit coupling, instrument bandwidth and interference; apparent charge is not necessarily the actual charge transferred inside a void.

Common reference case and assumptions

Analyze a synthetic phase-resolved PD display with known injected pulse times. Use charge-based concepts to explain why calibration, noise rejection and a measurement window are necessary before interpreting a pattern.

Outcomes and verification

Interpret synthetic pulses and their limitations, distinguish diagnostic hypotheses, and design a defensible measurement-evidence report.

A researcher's reading protocol

Describe how a calibrated equivalent injection would be interpreted, without building the system. Separate the event detection rule, false-positive prevalence, phase reference and hypothesis about the source of the pulses. Beware of using an attractive phase-resolved image as proof of a unique defect. The model is intentionally limited to analytical and offline-computation exercises. A student should identify the quantity being predicted and ask what observational evidence would be needed to validate it. The location of the maximum physical field is especially significant: an average stress may conceal a sharp local concentration at a boundary, edge, void, accessory or transient discontinuity.

When reading Lessons 7.1–7.4, first reconstruct the derivation, then change one variable in the supplied worked example. Carry the physical units consistently and ask whether a plotted curve can be traced back to the same symbol definitions as the equation. Use the [interactive software model](../simulations/model-07.html) after the hand calculation so the plot serves as a diagnostic rather than an opaque answer generator.

What counts as evidence

The unit challenge is resisting overconfident condition diagnosis from a short synthetic sample. Distinguish proof of code execution, numerical agreement with a mathematical model, calibration evidence, dielectric qualification, and safe operation. These are five separate accomplishments; none implies the next automatically. All practice in this book is software-only. Do not build test apparatus, charge capacitors or obtain energized equipment for these exercises.

Suggested learning route

Read all four lessons and reproduce the arithmetic in a notebook. Complete the project with a baseline case and three documented variations. Attempt the unit assessment before consulting its solution notes. Record a clear status for each conclusion: derived, numerically checked, simulated, empirically supported or unresolved. There are no fabricated laboratory observations in this book.

§7.2 Partial discharge versus complete breakdown

Lesson 7.1: Partial discharge versus complete breakdown

Unit: Partial discharge and insulation diagnostics. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

Localized discharge within or along insulation does not necessarily form a full electrode-to-electrode conductive path. Apparent charge is a circuit-dependent measured quantity, not directly the microscopic charge in every void. Inception and extinction may depend on previous cycles.

Governing equations and interpretation

Charge from a calibrated equivalent injection is q=Cinj ΔV in a simple capacitive calibration model. State that this is an injected reference charge in the calibration circuit.

Fully stated worked example

For Cinj=10 pF and ΔV=10 V, the mathematical injected charge is 100 pC. This does not establish the size or damage rate of an internal void discharge.

Derive the supplied relation from a recognizable physical law: Gauss’s law, conservation of charge, electromagnetic-wave continuity, equivalent-circuit impedance or probability bookkeeping as appropriate. If a derivative or spatial gradient appears, write what is being held constant and what geometric symmetry is assumed. The derivation must connect to an experimentally meaningful quantity rather than stop at formal algebra.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

Instrument coupling, bandwidth, grounding, PD pulse overlap and electrical noise affect the displayed apparent charge.

§7.3 Phase-resolved PD pattern interpretation

Lesson 7.2: Phase-resolved PD pattern interpretation

Unit: Partial discharge and insulation diagnostics. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

A phase-resolved view maps pulse amplitude or apparent charge against the instantaneous AC phase and repetition. Patterns can suggest possible discharge mechanisms but are not unique signatures; multiple physical faults and external interference can create similar images.

Governing equations and interpretation

If t is a pulse timestamp and excitation frequency f, the nominal electrical phase is φ=(360° f t) mod 360°, provided the phase reference and synchronization are correct.

Fully stated worked example

At 50 Hz, a pulse 5 ms after a reference crossing occurs at 90°, while one 15 ms after occurs at 270°. A 1 ms timing offset shifts phase by 18°.

Dimensional and limiting checks

Check dimensions at each intermediate step. In a relationship containing logarithms or exponentials, ensure their arguments are dimensionless: empirical constants may carry units precisely to make this possible. Then push one variable toward a simple limiting case. Ask whether the outcome is consistent with the physical arrangement, and identify a parameter range where the equation would cease to describe the real world.

§7.4 Noise, pulse discrimination, and frequency effects

Lesson 7.3: Noise, pulse discrimination, and frequency effects

Unit: Partial discharge and insulation diagnostics. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

PD sensing competes with switching transients, radio interference and sensor noise. A threshold can reject noise while also deleting small true pulses, creating a biased count. Choosing bandwidth or filtering requires a signal-and-noise tradeoff.

Governing equations and interpretation

If a threshold is T and observed amplitude is A, the simple decision rule |A|>T classifies a recorded event, but it does not prove the event is PD. False-positive and false-negative rates require labeled evidence.

Fully stated worked example

In a synthetic batch of 100 true pulses and 200 noise pulses, suppose a threshold captures 80 true pulses and 10 noise pulses: precision=80/90≈88.9%, recall=80/100=80%.

Comparing alternative mechanisms

Write down at least two competing explanations for the same observation. One can be the intended low-order model, and the other can be an omitted physical process such as moisture, a floating conductor, spatial inhomogeneity, instrument loading, dielectric ageing, or waveform bandwidth. Explain what additional observation would discriminate the hypotheses. A falsifiable comparison is more useful than repeating a likely answer.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

These rates describe only the specified synthetic dataset. Field performance can be very different due to site-specific interference.

§7.5 Condition monitoring and trends without false certainty

Lesson 7.4: Condition monitoring and trends without false certainty

Unit: Partial discharge and insulation diagnostics. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

A diagnostic trend is meaningful only if the measurement process, operating conditions and normalization are sufficiently comparable. One apparently lower PD amplitude does not prove self-healing; the stimulus might have changed or the sensor might have drifted.

Governing equations and interpretation

For repeated apparent-charge readings qi, a sample mean qbar=Σqi/n and sample deviation s=√[Σ(qi−qbar)²/(n−1)] summarize a dataset but do not quantify failure probability.

Fully stated worked example

Synthetic readings 10, 12 and 14 pC have mean 12 pC and sample standard deviation 2 pC. A later single reading of 9 pC is inadequate evidence of an improving asset.

Making a controlled decision

Set a baseline, change only one controlled variable, and recalculate the metric using the stated formula. Report the change numerically, including units. Then ask whether the change also introduces an adverse effect: higher stored energy, field enhancement, leakage current, uncertainty, ageing stress, or loss of a valid approximation. End with an evidence request that a qualified technical reviewer could act on.

Service-life forecasting needs validated material-specific models, operating history, confounder control and appropriate physical inspections.
