§6.1 Foundational Principles & Curriculum Scope: Generation of High Alternating-Current Voltages: Cascaded and Series-Resonant Transformers

Unit 6: High-voltage measurement systems and uncertainty

Why this subject matters

A measurement chain contributes loading, stray coupling, frequency response, calibration uncertainty and data-acquisition errors. Simply dividing a scope trace by a nominal ratio cannot verify a test voltage. Rated voltage alone does not imply impulse bandwidth.

Common reference case and assumptions

Use low-energy equivalent circuits, uncertainty budgets and synthetic impulse samples to analyze measurement credibility. Physical dividers and energized probes are not assembled. The goal is competence in interpreting certificates and synthetic measurements, not a test-lab construction guide.

Outcomes and verification

Propagate uncertainty, distinguish random and systematic effects, assess sensor loading and determine when traceable calibration evidence is required.

A researcher's reading protocol

Distinguish an instrument input from a calibrated complete measurement system. Work through loading, transfer function, resolution, systematic error and uncertainty of the inferred measurand. An instrument display with six decimals need not carry six decimals of credibility. The model is intentionally limited to analytical and offline-computation exercises. A student should identify the quantity being predicted and ask what observational evidence would be needed to validate it. The location of the maximum physical field is especially significant: an average stress may conceal a sharp local concentration at a boundary, edge, void, accessory or transient discontinuity.

When reading Lessons 6.1–6.4, first reconstruct the derivation, then change one variable in the supplied worked example. Carry the physical units consistently and ask whether a plotted curve can be traced back to the same symbol definitions as the equation. Use the [interactive software model](../simulations/model-06.html) after the hand calculation so the plot serves as a diagnostic rather than an opaque answer generator.

What counts as evidence

The unit challenge is reporting what an independently calibrated chain can support, not what its screen is capable of rendering. Distinguish proof of code execution, numerical agreement with a mathematical model, calibration evidence, dielectric qualification, and safe operation. These are five separate accomplishments; none implies the next automatically. All practice in this book is software-only. Do not build test apparatus, charge capacitors or obtain energized equipment for these exercises.

Suggested learning route

Read all four lessons and reproduce the arithmetic in a notebook. Complete the project with a baseline case and three documented variations. Attempt the unit assessment before consulting its solution notes. Record a clear status for each conclusion: derived, numerically checked, simulated, empirically supported or unresolved. There are no fabricated laboratory observations in this book.

§6.2 Voltage-dividing ratios and loading

Lesson 6.1: Voltage-dividing ratios and loading

Unit: High-voltage measurement systems and uncertainty. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

An ideal resistive divider ratio is determined by two resistances, but finite instrument impedance loads the lower element. Frequency behavior adds stray capacitance and inductance, requiring more than a DC ratio for impulse measurement. This chapter analyzes low-energy math only.

Governing equations and interpretation

For R1 in series with R2 and measured output across R2, k=R2/(R1+R2). With instrument resistance Rin, replace R2 by R2∥Rin.

Fully stated worked example

For R1=9 MΩ and R2=100 kΩ, ideal k≈0.010989. With Rin=1 MΩ, the effective low side is 90.91 kΩ and k≈0.010000; ignoring loading would bias the inferred input.

Reconstructing the model

Define every boundary condition and symbol before calculating. Derive the supplied relation from a recognizable physical law: Gauss’s law, conservation of charge, electromagnetic-wave continuity, equivalent-circuit impedance or probability bookkeeping as appropriate. If a derivative or spatial gradient appears, write what is being held constant and what geometric symmetry is assumed. The derivation must connect to an experimentally meaningful quantity rather than stop at formal algebra.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

A divider that is correct at DC can be grossly wrong for fast impulses; no physical assembly or energized test is described.

Physical mechanism

Capacitive measurement chains are sensitive to parasitic capacitances, grounding arrangement and frequency. Their transfer functions often require calibrated dynamic response rather than a nominal capacitance ratio. The voltage delivered to an instrument can change with cable length.

Governing equations and interpretation

§6.4 Uncertainty budgets and calibration chains

Lesson 6.3: Uncertainty budgets and calibration chains

Unit: High-voltage measurement systems and uncertainty. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

Measurement uncertainty is a statement about the dispersion of values reasonably attributable to a measurand. Independent components combine differently from correlated systematic terms. A reported calibrated ratio without conditions, traceability and a coverage factor is not enough for high-voltage qualification.

Governing equations and interpretation

For independent standard uncertainties u1 and u2, uc=√(u1²+u2²). An expanded uncertainty may be U=k uc when the chosen coverage factor is justified; it is not automatically a universal tolerance.

Fully stated worked example

Independent relative standard uncertainties of 1% and 2% combine to 2.236%. Using k=2 illustratively yields 4.472% expanded uncertainty, conditional on the distribution and coverage assumptions.

Comparing alternative mechanisms

Write down at least two competing explanations for the same observation. One can be the intended low-order model, and the other can be an omitted physical process such as moisture, a floating conductor, spatial inhomogeneity, instrument loading, dielectric ageing, or waveform bandwidth. Explain what additional observation would discriminate the hypotheses. A falsifiable comparison is more useful than repeating a likely answer.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

Correlated offsets cannot be combined as though independent; repeatability is not the same as calibration accuracy.

§6.5 Impulse parameter extraction and reporting

Lesson 6.4: Impulse parameter extraction and reporting

Unit: High-voltage measurement systems and uncertainty. Mode: theoretical analysis and offline simulation. Prerequisites: elementary electromagnetism, circuits, differential equations, logarithms and unit conversion. Review status: teaching draft.

Physical mechanism

A peak number from a digitizer requires a declared processing method. Filtering can smooth noise but also shift peak value and front time. Test reports should distinguish raw captured samples from corrected parameters and state method and uncertainty.

Governing equations and interpretation

For a sampled signal vi, the raw sampled maximum is max(vi). It is not necessarily the continuous-waveform peak. A local quadratic interpolation may improve some estimates, provided the sampling and noise assumptions are disclosed.

Fully stated worked example

A 100 kV synthetic pulse sampled at only 1 µs intervals can miss a narrow true peak between samples. Two traces with identical reported sample maxima need not have the same front-time dynamics.

Making a controlled decision

Set a baseline, change only one controlled variable, and recalculate the metric using the stated formula. Report the change numerically, including units. Then ask whether the change also introduces an adverse effect: higher stored energy, field enhancement, leakage current, uncertainty, ageing stress, or loss of a valid approximation. End with an evidence request that a qualified technical reviewer could act on.

Failure modes, model exclusions, and hazard boundary

Use IEC 60060-1:2025 and IEC 60060-2:2025 for the applicable formal definitions and measurement-system requirements; this lesson is not a substitute.
