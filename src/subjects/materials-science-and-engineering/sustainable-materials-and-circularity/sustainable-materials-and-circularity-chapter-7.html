Process improvement, supply chains and justice

**Guiding question:** Can cleaner processing reduce harm without merely exporting it to another location?

About this textbook. Original English teaching material mapped to University of Dhaka Statistics B.S. Honours 2023–24, Stat H-307 (Statistical Simulation and Monte Carlo Methods). Eight units and exactly three solved questions per unit. Interactive simulations illustrate linear congruential generators, rejection sampling envelopes, rare-event importance sampling, and Metropolis-Hastings random walks. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition; it has not received independent academic peer review. Curriculum mapping does not imply university endorsement.

§7.1 The system before the material label

Guiding question: Can cleaner processing reduce harm without merely exporting it to another location?

A manufacturing business switches from solvent-intensive cleaning to a water-based process. It reports reduced direct emissions but must investigate energy, wastewater, worker exposure and upstream sourcing. A product comparison only becomes meaningful after its required service, physical boundaries, time and mandatory safety specifications are written down. The system may contain long-lived inventories, poorly measured return streams, process losses, upstream impacts and financial constraints. The engineering objective is to find the particular intervention that actually improves useful output per resource and reduces meaningful harms. The unit combines an explicit material mechanism with a data and decision workflow, rather than presenting recycling, repair, substitution or efficiency as universally superior.

An evidence-ready project in this unit starts with site-specific metered resource and hazard baselines. Its indispensable records include record process heat, water balance, wastewater, exposure pathways and suppliers, and important counterevidence comes from pre/post operational data, workplace controls and third-party sampling. Before reading any calculated result, consider whether reduced on-site burdens are shifted to another facility or community. If the answer can be changed by a reasonable assumption about material quality or service life, the proper result is a bounded comparison and a plan to collect missing evidence, not a confident environmental winner.

§7.2 Unit project: challenge an appealing sustainability claim

Write an initial one-page decision specification for the case above. State the material in/out boundaries, the functional unit and a physically meaningful reference flow. Identify at least one mass-balance constraint and two competing mechanisms that might explain an observed difference. Then list the measurement owner, instrument or transaction log, geographic and temporal applicability, uncertainty and a specific control. Revisit this specification after each lesson. The five topics form parts of one investigation, not five disconnected sustainable-material slogans.

§7.3 Lesson 7.1 — Process energy and heat integration

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Sustainable processing starts with a measured energy baseline at the correct output quality and production scale. Furnaces, dryers, separation columns and chillers have different temperature levels; recovering low-grade heat cannot always replace high-temperature process input. Heat integration needs matching duty, timing, pinch constraints and safety. Changes to equipment utilization or product yield can alter the apparent per-tonne benefit.

Working relationship: Net purchased process energy = gross input − useful recovered heat on a matching time and temperature basis. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A ceramics kiln produces recoverable exhaust heat but adjacent dryers do not operate during its hottest period. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Claiming the entire exhaust enthalpy as usable recovery ignores heat-transfer driving force and synchronization. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§7.4 Lesson 7.2 — Water use, recycling and water stress

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Water withdrawals, consumption, recirculation and discharge describe different things. An industrial closed loop can reduce withdrawals but concentrate salts and require additional treatment energy. Freshwater burdens depend on geography and scarcity: one cubic meter in a water-stressed basin is not equivalent to the same volume in a low-stress region. Wastewater quality, hazardous species and receiving-water ecology belong in the assessment.

Working relationship: Freshwater consumption = withdrawal − returned flow adjusted for defined quality and basin conditions. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A plating line recycles process water but discharges a smaller, saltier purge. Compare actual regional water impacts. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: A high internal reuse percentage can coexist with rising total evaporation or polluted discharge. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§7.5 Lesson 7.3 — Safer chemistry and material substitutions

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Process substitutions should examine intrinsic hazard and actual exposure pathways rather than treat a material as safe because it is marketed as green. Solvents, catalysts, additives and coatings need compatibility, durability and lifecycle hazard evaluation. Worker exposure, combustion byproducts, wastewater and disposal conditions can dominate practical risks. A replacement chemical with lower immediate toxicity may have higher persistence or require greater application frequency.

Working relationship: Risk screening = hazard severity × credible exposure likelihood, with explicit categories rather than a universal physical law. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A factory replaces a volatile cleaner with a water-based detergent and reports zero solvent emissions. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Eliminating one solvent does not guarantee lower total health or environmental harm. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§7.6 Lesson 7.4 — Supplier provenance, human rights and local impacts

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

A materials footprint aggregated to a global indicator can conceal local land damage, labor abuse or water scarcity. Supply-chain due diligence needs traceable supplier tiers, audits and independent grievance mechanisms. Certifications can lower search cost but do not prove every purchase is conflict-free or every mine has identical practices. Procurement should define exclusion criteria, remedial timelines and evidence requirements rather than a single vague sustainable-material score.

Working relationship: Supply-chain coverage = verified spend or mass with traceable origin / relevant total spend or mass. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A component supplier reports low factory carbon emissions while refusing disclosure of a high-risk upstream mineral source. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: A high percentage of audited direct suppliers cannot establish traceability of unobserved upstream extraction. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§7.7 Lesson 7.5 — Durability, protection and preventive maintenance

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Corrosion resistance, surface treatments and accessible maintenance can extend component life and reduce virgin replacement demand. A coating also carries production and removal impacts, and failed coatings may require hazardous surface preparation. Preventive maintenance can pay back environmentally when failures are frequent and expensive, but frequent interventions may impose avoidable burdens. Reliability, inspection accessibility and actual maintenance compliance determine outcomes.

Working relationship: Lifetime avoided-impact benefit = avoided replacements − cumulative maintenance burdens, with consistent function and period. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A coastal bridge needs recoating. Compare frequent light maintenance with less frequent extensive renewal. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Assuming a nominal coating life without climate and defect histories may overstate savings. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§7.8 Unit 7 capstone — challenge, calculation, evidence and choice

Integrated case: A manufacturing business switches from solvent-intensive cleaning to a water-based process. It reports reduced direct emissions but must investigate energy, wastewater, worker exposure and upstream sourcing.

1. Service definition and counterfactual. Define the actual useful function and service horizon, identify the current material or process, and explain what product/service alternative is realistically available in the decision location. List safety and legal criteria that must be met before comparing optional environmental benefits.

2. Mass, energy and quality ledger. Construct a table of incoming material, recovered output, contaminants, rejects and inventory changes. Add the largest energy and water flows. Any unaccounted balance residual must be visibly labeled instead of silently treated as saleable recovered material.

3. Two quantitative sensitivities. Recalculate the unit's two interactive models. Change the default assumptions to two defensible adverse scenarios. Explain what is synthetic and what measurements would be needed to convert the question into a supplier-specific evaluation.

4. Independent audit. Design a sampling plan using site-specific metered resource and hazard baselines, recording record process heat, water balance, wastewater, exposure pathways and suppliers and challenging the decision with pre/post operational data, workplace controls and third-party sampling. Explain how many independent production batches, assets or user cycles are needed before generalization and how the result could be biased by rejected output.

5. Decision and falsification. Present a one-page recommendation with a go/no-go pilot, responsible decision-maker, estimated economic and environmental trade-offs, and a measurement that would overturn your recommendation. Specifically challenge whether reduced on-site burdens are shifted to another facility or community. No safety-critical release or environmental certification is authorized by the classroom exercises.

After the capstone

Discuss whether a reduction in impact per service is likely to translate into lower total resource use. A more efficient or more recyclable product can still be produced in expanding quantities, and a durable product can still create local harms if its manufacturing is poorly controlled. Document the supply chain and user context in which the conclusion holds. A coherent sustainability argument preserves engineering functionality, mass conservation, traceability and the limits of the available evidence at the same time.
