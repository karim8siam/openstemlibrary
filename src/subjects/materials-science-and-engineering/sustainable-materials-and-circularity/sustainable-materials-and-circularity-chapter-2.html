Life-cycle assessment and evidence quality

**Guiding question:** How can a defensible environmental comparison survive different assumptions and data sources?

About this textbook. Original English teaching material mapped to University of Dhaka Statistics B.S. Honours 2023–24, Stat H-307 (Statistical Simulation and Monte Carlo Methods). Eight units and exactly three solved questions per unit. Interactive simulations illustrate linear congruential generators, rejection sampling envelopes, rare-event importance sampling, and Metropolis-Hastings random walks. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition; it has not received independent academic peer review. Curriculum mapping does not imply university endorsement.

§2.1 The system before the material label

Guiding question: How can a defensible environmental comparison survive different assumptions and data sources?

Two suppliers independently market a low-carbon façade panel. One uses plant-average electricity and the other selects renewable contractual factors, while their service lives and replacement rates differ. A product comparison only becomes meaningful after its required service, physical boundaries, time and mandatory safety specifications are written down. The system may contain long-lived inventories, poorly measured return streams, process losses, upstream impacts and financial constraints. The engineering objective is to find the particular intervention that actually improves useful output per resource and reduces meaningful harms. The unit combines an explicit material mechanism with a data and decision workflow, rather than presenting recycling, repair, substitution or efficiency as universally superior.

An evidence-ready project in this unit starts with locked goal/scope sheet and versioned inventory database. Its indispensable records include record factor sources, geographic era, unit basis, allocation rules, co-products and use phase, and important counterevidence comes from alternative boundary cases and independent data-quality audit. Before reading any calculated result, consider whether a change in method, rather than manufacture, explains the claimed improvement. If the answer can be changed by a reasonable assumption about material quality or service life, the proper result is a bounded comparison and a plan to collect missing evidence, not a confident environmental winner.

§2.2 Unit project: challenge an appealing sustainability claim

Write an initial one-page decision specification for the case above. State the material in/out boundaries, the functional unit and a physically meaningful reference flow. Identify at least one mass-balance constraint and two competing mechanisms that might explain an observed difference. Then list the measurement owner, instrument or transaction log, geographic and temporal applicability, uncertainty and a specific control. Revisit this specification after each lesson. The five topics form parts of one investigation, not five disconnected sustainable-material slogans.

§2.3 LCA goal, scope and system boundaries

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

A life-cycle assessment starts by stating why a comparison is made, its audience and whether results will support a public comparative assertion. The functional unit, reference flows, geographical and temporal coverage, cut-off rules and impact categories should be decided before the desired outcome is known. Cradle-to-gate and cradle-to-grave results answer different questions. Including or excluding maintenance and end-of-life can reverse a ranking even if manufacturing inventories are identical.

Working relationship: Life-cycle impact per functional unit is the sum of inventory flows multiplied by category-specific characterization factors, subject to defined methods. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: Two flooring types show opposite rankings depending on whether seven years of replacement are included. Prepare a transparent comparison protocol. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: A truncated boundary can hide electricity, transport, installation or replacement burdens; impact categories are not interchangeable. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

3. Transparent synthetic calculation and sensitivity

The paired interactive card for this lesson is Manufacturing plus transport inventory. Its computational expression is 50+0.08*d, with adjustable Delivery distance for one tonne in km. At 200 km the deterministic output is 66 kg CO₂e; at 620 km it is 99.6 kg CO₂e. The two-point sensitivity is 0.08 kg CO₂e per km. These are synthetic teaching calculations, not measurements. 4. Evidence protocol, sampling and falsification

Specific investigation: Write a registered scope sheet and challenge each cut-off with a high-impact sensitivity case. Define independent samples, record rejected outputs and capture instrument calibration and field-relevant service conditions.

Verify both mass-balance closure and functionally qualified output; publish observations that contradict the preferred mechanism.

5. Design trade-off and conditions of adoption

Trade-off: A low cradle-to-gate footprint may be outweighed by energy, cleaning or premature replacement during use. Evaluate the trade-off only after minimum safety, quality and legal requirements are satisfied.

§2.4 Inventory data, provenance and representativeness

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Life-cycle inventory links measured operations, purchased inputs, transport modes and waste outcomes to a reproducible flow table. Primary process data are preferable when available, but secondary regional databases can fill gaps only with their uncertainty made visible. Grid mixes, recycled material allocations, furnace technologies and production years change environmental intensity. A data source that is geographically convenient may be technically wrong for an individual supplier.

Working relationship: Impact = Σ(activity quantity × emission or impact factor); every factor needs compatible units and traceable provenance. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A factory averages production electricity for a product whose high-temperature step operates mainly at night. Decide the significance. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Using an obsolete grid factor or mismatched steelmaking route can dominate precision gained by more decimal places. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§2.5 Impact categories and trade-off interpretation

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Climate change expressed in carbon-dioxide equivalents is only one impact category. Eutrophication, acidification, toxicity, freshwater use, land use and resource-related indicators can lead to different rankings. Characterization models carry uncertainties and assumptions; results should not be arbitrarily added together unless normalization or weighting choices are explicit. An increase in electricity for advanced separation can lower toxic releases or improve material recovery while increasing climate impacts under a high-carbon grid.

Working relationship: For category k, I_k = Σ_j Q_j CF_(j,k) with explicit flow quantities and characterization factors. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A recycling solvent eliminates contamination but raises water use. Assess climate, water stress, worker safety and local wastewater constraints. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: A single weighted environmental score can hide unacceptable local harms or uncertain toxicity factors. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§2.6 Allocation, co-products and recycled content

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Manufacturing operations often produce several saleable products or recovered streams. Attributional models require allocation or system partition rules when processes cannot be subdivided. Mass and economic allocation distribute burdens differently. Recycled-content and end-of-life approaches can also double count credit if both parties claim the same avoided virgin production. Circular systems should report allocation assumptions and avoid presenting a methodological convention as an observed physical fact.

Working relationship: Allocated burden_i = total joint burden × allocation share_i, with all shares summing to one. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A copper smelter produces cathode copper, precious-metal byproducts and sulfuric acid. Two studies use incompatible burden allocations. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Market price volatility can change economic allocation while physics stays constant. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§2.7 Uncertainty, scenarios and review

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Parameter uncertainty includes measurement error and variation in process conditions; model uncertainty includes boundary choices and assumed future recovery pathways. Monte Carlo sampling quantifies only the uncertainty encoded in its inputs and dependence structure. Correlated electricity intensity and operating temperature cannot be treated as unrelated if they move together. Review should separately check data traceability, arithmetic, scope consistency and whether conclusions exceed the evidence.

Working relationship: For independent first-order inputs, σ_y² ≈ Σ(∂y/∂x_i)²σ_i²; correlations add covariance terms. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A published footprint has a narrow interval because transport and end-of-life were treated as exact despite missing records. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Precise software outputs cannot rescue biased or unrepresentative source inventories. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§2.8 Unit 2 capstone — challenge, calculation, evidence and choice

Integrated case: Two suppliers independently market a low-carbon façade panel. One uses plant-average electricity and the other selects renewable contractual factors, while their service lives and replacement rates differ.

1. Service definition and counterfactual. Define the actual useful function and service horizon, identify the current material or process, and explain what product/service alternative is realistically available in the decision location. List safety and legal criteria that must be met before comparing optional environmental benefits.

2. Mass, energy and quality ledger. Construct a table of incoming material, recovered output, contaminants, rejects and inventory changes. Add the largest energy and water flows. Any unaccounted balance residual must be visibly labeled instead of silently treated as saleable recovered material.

3. Two quantitative sensitivities. Recalculate the unit's two interactive models. Change the default assumptions to two defensible adverse scenarios. Explain what is synthetic and what measurements would be needed to convert the question into a supplier-specific evaluation.

4. Independent audit. Design a sampling plan using locked goal/scope sheet and versioned inventory database, recording record factor sources, geographic era, unit basis, allocation rules, co-products and use phase and challenging the decision with alternative boundary cases and independent data-quality audit. Explain how many independent production batches, assets or user cycles are needed before generalization and how the result could be biased by rejected output.

5. Decision and falsification. Present a one-page recommendation with a go/no-go pilot, responsible decision-maker, estimated economic and environmental trade-offs, and a measurement that would overturn your recommendation. Specifically challenge whether a change in method, rather than manufacture, explains the claimed improvement. No safety-critical release or environmental certification is authorized by the classroom exercises.

After the capstone

Discuss whether a reduction in impact per service is likely to translate into lower total resource use. A more efficient or more recyclable product can still be produced in expanding quantities, and a durable product can still create local harms if its manufacturing is poorly controlled. Document the supply chain and user context in which the conclusion holds. A coherent sustainability argument preserves engineering functionality, mass conservation, traceability and the limits of the available evidence at the same time.
