Batteries, electronics and critical material loops

**Guiding question:** Can recovered critical materials supply credible new products without compromising safety?

About this textbook. Original English teaching material mapped to University of Dhaka Statistics B.S. Honours 2023–24, Stat H-307 (Statistical Simulation and Monte Carlo Methods). Eight units and exactly three solved questions per unit. Interactive simulations illustrate linear congruential generators, rejection sampling envelopes, rare-event importance sampling, and Metropolis-Hastings random walks. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition; it has not received independent academic peer review. Curriculum mapping does not imply university endorsement.

§6.1 The system before the material label

Guiding question: Can recovered critical materials supply credible new products without compromising safety?

A producer takes back consumer electronics and battery packs. Cells differ in history and chemistry; the organization needs safe screening, traceability and a recovery route without claiming every collected device becomes new battery material. A product comparison only becomes meaningful after its required service, physical boundaries, time and mandatory safety specifications are written down. The system may contain long-lived inventories, poorly measured return streams, process losses, upstream impacts and financial constraints. The engineering objective is to find the particular intervention that actually improves useful output per resource and reduces meaningful harms. The unit combines an explicit material mechanism with a data and decision workflow, rather than presenting recycling, repair, substitution or efficiency as universally superior.

An evidence-ready project in this unit starts with chemistry-specific, safety-reviewed recovered products. Its indispensable records include log origin, pack chemistry, sample purity, return rates and independent release testing, and important counterevidence comes from batch mass balances and qualified downstream acceptance certificates. Before reading any calculated result, consider whether high collection creates saleable materials at required safety level. If the answer can be changed by a reasonable assumption about material quality or service life, the proper result is a bounded comparison and a plan to collect missing evidence, not a confident environmental winner.

§6.2 Unit project: challenge an appealing sustainability claim

Write an initial one-page decision specification for the case above. State the material in/out boundaries, the functional unit and a physically meaningful reference flow. Identify at least one mass-balance constraint and two competing mechanisms that might explain an observed difference. Then list the measurement owner, instrument or transaction log, geographic and temporal applicability, uncertainty and a specific control. Revisit this specification after each lesson. The five topics form parts of one investigation, not five disconnected sustainable-material slogans.

§6.3 Lesson 6.1 — Battery material compositions and collection safety

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Lithium-ion batteries span multiple cathode chemistries, anodes, solvents and pack architectures. Collection pathways must handle state-of-charge, physical damage, uncertain history and fire risk under qualified safety protocols. Black mass composition is a variable intermediate, not a standardized metal content. Recovered lithium, nickel and cobalt yields differ by technology; products must satisfy new manufacturing specifications before closed-loop claims are made.

Working relationship: Recovered element mass = incoming active-material mass × element fraction × recovery yield for a specified process. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A recycler receives damaged battery modules from mixed vehicle fleets and consumer devices, with inconsistent records. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Assuming constant composition and full metal recovery across product generations overstates output. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

3. Transparent synthetic calculation and sensitivity

The paired interactive card for this lesson is Recovered lithium in sorted battery stream. Its computational expression is 60*p/100, with adjustable Element recovery yield in %. At 36 % the deterministic output is 21.6 kg Li; at 70 % it is 42 kg Li. The two-point sensitivity is 0.6 kg Li per %. These are synthetic teaching calculations, not measurements. Independently verify units and a third input before interpretation.

Why the numerical output has limits: 1000 kg active material at an illustrative fixed 6 mass% lithium; recovery and sorting ideal except variable yield. For a real decision, independently measure the variables that this illustrative formula holds constant.

4. Evidence protocol, sampling and falsification

Specific investigation: Document chemistry classes, qualified handling and independently assay all incoming and outgoing streams. Define independent samples, record rejected outputs and capture instrument calibration and field-relevant service conditions.

Verify both mass-balance closure and functionally qualified output; publish observations that contradict the preferred mechanism.

5. Design trade-off and conditions of adoption

Trade-off: High-value selective recovery may be economical while lower-value chemistries demand different financing or policy arrangements. Evaluate the trade-off only after minimum safety, quality and legal requirements are satisfied.

§6.4 Lesson 6.2 — Hydrometallurgy, pyrometallurgy and direct recycling

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Pyrometallurgical routes use high temperature to transform battery feed and often concentrate selected metals; hydrometallurgical routes use aqueous chemistry to selectively extract and refine constituents. Direct recycling aims to preserve active cathode structure where feasible, but feed quality and contamination strongly constrain reuse. Each route incurs energy, reagents, wastewater, solids, capital and yield costs. A complete comparison requires final product specifications, not merely dissolved-metal recoveries.

Working relationship: Saleable value per input tonne = Σ(product mass_i × realized price_i) − treatment and compliance costs. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: Three plants claim superior battery recycling based respectively on metal yield, total mass recovery and recovered cathode capacity. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: A high recovery percentage based on total battery mass can hide low recovery of target elements. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§6.5 Lesson 6.3 — Electronic-waste disassembly and component reuse

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Electronics contain precious and base metals alongside plastics, flame retardants, glass and hazardous components. Recovering a functional circuit board or module can preserve more manufacturing value than shredding, but functionality, software support, cybersecurity and safety must be verified. Informal dismantling can expose workers to unacceptable harm. A component recovered from scrap is only a reusable product when its performance and reliability are established.

Working relationship: Qualified reuse yield = modules that pass documented functional criteria / modules recovered for assessment. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A repair center can reuse 40% of returned laptop displays but must test brightness, dead pixels and electrical safety. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Counting harvested parts as reused without acceptance testing overstates circular performance. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§6.6 Lesson 6.4 — Rare-earth magnets and separation constraints

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Permanent magnets are often embedded in motors and electronics where adhesives, coatings and geometries impede recovery. Feed varies in rare-earth composition; contamination by oxidation or steel can disrupt remanufacturing. Reuse of intact magnets, hydrogen-assisted processing and chemical separation have different inputs, yields and quality constraints. Magnetic strength, coercivity and thermal stability must be tested for a target duty cycle.

Working relationship: Qualified magnetic-material yield = remanufacturable magnet alloy mass / collected magnet-containing assemblies. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A dismantler collects motor magnets and markets them for high-temperature industrial motors without testing. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: Recovered magnet mass does not guarantee coercivity, remanence or safe service performance. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§6.7 Lesson 6.5 — Traceability, passports and verification

1. Physics, chemistry and the function actually supplied

Material declarations and product passports organize identity, composition, repair instructions, disassembly and environmental attributes. Their reliability rests on identifier continuity, documented measurement methods, supplier controls and versioning; a QR code is merely an access mechanism. Regulatory coverage varies by product group and phase-in date. A truthful passport distinguishes measured content, calculated estimates, supplier declarations and unknowns.

Working relationship: Verified recycled-content share = eligible recovered input mass / total declared mass on an explicitly stated chain-of-custody basis. Keep the system boundary, physical units and quality specification visible before asserting an avoided impact.

2. A real engineering dilemma with rival explanations

Problem statement: A manufacturer wants one recycled-content percentage on every device despite different supplier batches and changing materials. Propose two rival mechanisms and one observation that differentiates them. Record timing, composition and functional performance before ranking either explanation.

Constraint that can reverse the answer: A digital identifier without batch-level evidence can give false precision and facilitate double counting. Estimate which service, quality or environmental condition could reverse the conclusion.

§6.8 Unit 6 capstone — challenge, calculation, evidence and choice

Integrated case: A producer takes back consumer electronics and battery packs. Cells differ in history and chemistry; the organization needs safe screening, traceability and a recovery route without claiming every collected device becomes new battery material.

1. Service definition and counterfactual. Define the actual useful function and service horizon, identify the current material or process, and explain what product/service alternative is realistically available in the decision location. List safety and legal criteria that must be met before comparing optional environmental benefits.

2. Mass, energy and quality ledger. Construct a table of incoming material, recovered output, contaminants, rejects and inventory changes. Add the largest energy and water flows. Any unaccounted balance residual must be visibly labeled instead of silently treated as saleable recovered material.

3. Two quantitative sensitivities. Recalculate the unit's two interactive models. Change the default assumptions to two defensible adverse scenarios. Explain what is synthetic and what measurements would be needed to convert the question into a supplier-specific evaluation.

4. Independent audit. Design a sampling plan using chemistry-specific, safety-reviewed recovered products, recording log origin, pack chemistry, sample purity, return rates and independent release testing and challenging the decision with batch mass balances and qualified downstream acceptance certificates. Explain how many independent production batches, assets or user cycles are needed before generalization and how the result could be biased by rejected output.

5. Decision and falsification. Present a one-page recommendation with a go/no-go pilot, responsible decision-maker, estimated economic and environmental trade-offs, and a measurement that would overturn your recommendation. Specifically challenge whether high collection creates saleable materials at required safety level. No safety-critical release or environmental certification is authorized by the classroom exercises.

After the capstone

Discuss whether a reduction in impact per service is likely to translate into lower total resource use. A more efficient or more recyclable product can still be produced in expanding quantities, and a durable product can still create local harms if its manufacturing is poorly controlled. Document the supply chain and user context in which the conclusion holds. A coherent sustainability argument preserves engineering functionality, mass conservation, traceability and the limits of the available evidence at the same time.
