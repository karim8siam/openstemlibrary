Unit 3 — Sedimentary environments and facies

Rivers, shorelines and deserts leave overlapping signatures.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university sedimentology and stratigraphy courses. Eight units with five solved questions per unit and 16 interactive simulations illustrating grain settling velocities, cross-bedding paleoflow vectors, porosity loss compaction curves, accommodation-to-sediment supply ratios, and subsidence curves. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§3.1 Learning outcomes

Infer depositional settings from facies associations, spatial architecture and temporal evolution.

§3.2 Extended unit perspective

Depositional environments cannot be assigned from one diagnostic-looking structure in isolation. A channel sandstone may appear in a river, delta, estuary or submarine system; a mud drape can follow either tides or episodic river slack water. Facies analysis gains power from repeated associations and their lateral connections. Students should draw a three-dimensional conceptual block diagram and trace how a sand body thins, migrates or connects to neighbouring muddy facies. A vertical succession is a single path through a changing spatial landscape and can be modified by avulsion, erosion and changes in accommodation. This unit uses environment labels as hypotheses about process and geography, not immutable rock identities. Field tests focus on geometry, current directions, contact relations, biogenic structures and a plausible sediment-routing pathway. Statements about ancient climate require evidence beyond the general presence of sand or mud.

§3.3 Alluvial fans and braided rivers

What is being explained?

High sediment supply, fluctuating discharge and channel mobility produce conglomeratic bars, sheetflood beds and laterally shifting channel fills. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Amalgamated channel bodies, scour surfaces, grain-size trends and palaeocurrent dispersal indicate active sediment routing. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

A coarse sheet may represent fan flooding, braided stream gravel or marine reworking on a high-energy coast. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Map grain size and thickness downfan and relate palaeoflow data to mapped channel belts and basin margin geometry. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Field notebook and facies recognition. Write a two-column field record headed observation and inference. Enter grains, laminae, colour, contact shape and any measurable geometry in the observation column; reserve terms describing ancient rivers, shorelines or deep water for the inference column. Choose an outcrop reference scale suitable for the problem: centimetres for a ripple foreset, metres for a channel body, and kilometres for a basin map. Mark beds that cannot be observed because of cover. The same feature may be meaningful at one scale and misleading at another. Draw the simplest facies association consistent with the visible relationships and list one expected lateral equivalent. In your report do not call an exposure representative merely because it is accessible; accessibility itself may produce sampling bias.

Worked quantitative exercise 11: Downfan gravel reduction

Hypothetical inputs: median grain sizes of 38 mm proximally and 23 mm distally; ratio of medians. The transparent classroom expression is 100*(38-23)/38. Evaluating this expression produces 39.4737 % decrease. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Exposure may omit fine overbank deposits and give an exaggerated picture of coarse-grained dominance. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Assign a fan or braided interpretation only when geometry and facies association support it.

§3.4 Meandering rivers and floodplains

What is being explained?

Lateral channel migration deposits point-bar successions; overbank flooding and soil formation create contrasting floodplain facies. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Lateral-accretion surfaces, fining-upward channel fills, levee silts, crevasse splays and palaeosols create a coupled record. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

A fining-upward log can form in a tidal channel or delta distributary as well as a meandering river. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Trace accretion surfaces laterally and use floodplain indicators to distinguish alluvial from tidally influenced packages. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Measurement and dimensional reasoning. For the numerical component, write the governing relation and then translate every quantity into a unit consistent with that relation. State whether thickness is true stratigraphic thickness or apparent width, whether grain size is a mass-weighted or number-weighted distribution, and whether a flux represents sediment delivered or rock preserved. A point estimate may hide a large physical uncertainty. Recalculate after changing one assumed input by ten percent, and distinguish the mathematical sensitivity from the real variability of that input. Present units in the plotted axis titles rather than only in figure captions. A sensible arithmetic result is not sufficient evidence that the equation is valid at a particular outcrop. Discuss whether equilibrium, uniform flow, conserved grain mass or constant deposition has been assumed.

Avulsion can abandon a channel without the ideal lateral-migration sequence seen in textbooks. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Interpret channel bodies in relation to floodplain preservation rather than treating every fining-upward cycle as identical.

§3.5 Aeolian dunes and interdunes

What is being explained?

Wind transports dry sand by saltation and creep; dune migration deposits large cross-sets while wet interdunes preserve distinct laminae. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Very well-sorted sand, high-angle cross-strata, wind-ripple lamination and evaporitic interdune layers establish environmental context. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

High-angle cross-bedding also occurs in subaqueous deltas and bars, so angle alone is insufficient. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Map cross-set dip directions and compare grain-size/roundness with interdune desiccation or evaporite evidence. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Hypothesis competition and counterexample. Begin with a pair of incompatible explanations that both seem plausible from the first observation. Instead of collecting more features that merely illustrate the preferred story, identify the measurement on which the predictions disagree. Consider that one hypothesis may involve sediment transport while the other involves later erosion or cementation; these are not mutually exclusive unless the timing is explicitly constrained. Record the expected observation under each explanation before seeing any additional dataset. Evidence that fails to discriminate is still descriptive evidence, but it does not rank the competing models. Where a third process could imitate both, give that possibility a separate row in the reasoning table. The final verdict may be that present evidence supports only a family of environments or ages.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Dune migration directions can vary seasonally and compaction changes preserved angles. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Use both dune deposits and associated low-energy facies to reconstruct an erg, not an isolated cross-set.

§3.6 Deltas and distributary systems

What is being explained?

Sediment supply meets standing water at a shoreline, producing mouth bars, distributary channels, prodelta mud and changing clinoforms. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Prograding coarsening-upward packages, river-mouth bar geometries and basinward facies changes constrain shoreline advance. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

Shoreface progradation or a shallow-lake margin can create upward-coarsening sections with no fluvial delta present. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Construct a dip-oriented correlation panel and seek distributary channels landward of prodelta or offshore deposits. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Sampling design and data integrity. Prepare an explicit sampling protocol: what interval is the population of interest, how will representative sites be selected, which fraction of the sediment is included, and what precision is possible? Replicate critical counts because sedimentary deposits are patchy in both space and time. A sieve sample, thin section and borehole log observe different volumes and may not sample equivalent properties. Record locations and orientation without inventing precision that was not measured. For destructive sampling, respect collection permits and institution rules. Store the original readings separately from any normalized percentages so that the transformation can be inspected later. Treat missing data as missing, not as zeros, and write down every exclusion criterion before producing an attractive final plot.

Wave and tidal processes redistribute sediment and can erase the classic river-dominated delta pattern. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Describe the balance of river, wave and tidal processes rather than insisting that all deltas look alike.

§3.7 Beaches, tidal flats and estuaries

What is being explained?

Waves, tides and river discharge operate on overlapping time scales and preserve mixed sand/mud packages. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Herringbone cross-strata, mud drapes, tidal bundles, wave ripples and bioturbation can indicate flow reversal and salinity gradients. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

Bidirectional cross-sets may reflect shifting river channels rather than true tides, and individual mud drapes occur in nonmarine settings. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Measure repeated lamina thicknesses and compare bundles and ichnofacies with a shoreline-to-basin transect. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Comparative synthesis and communication. Move from a single log to a lateral comparison. Propose which properties ought to remain similar between neighbouring sections and which may change as environments migrate. A marker bed is only a reliable time line when evidence establishes its correlation; similar sandstone colour is not such evidence. Choose a visual representation that distinguishes observed boundaries from extrapolated ones, perhaps using solid versus dashed lines and a brief uncertainty legend. Present at least one alternative explanation in the figure caption rather than burying disagreement in the final paragraph. A coherent story should preserve contradictions instead of quietly removing inconvenient beds. In the conclusion identify the smallest claim that would remain valid if your preferred process model were overturned.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Neap–spring cyclicity is often incompletely preserved and biological reworking blurs hydrodynamic signals. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Give a tidal interpretation when several independent indicators agree, not solely a picturesque ripple pattern.

§3.8 Integrated research studio: A changing coastal plain

Imagine a mapped valley with basal braided-channel gravel, meandering-channel sand and upper estuarine mud. Test both a relative sea-level rise and an avulsion-driven explanation. Sketch cross-sections that distinguish tidal channels from abandoned floodplain wetlands. Identify the facies you would expect laterally beside each channel body and the consequences of an erosional gap between two logs.

Practical workflow

A teaching dataset is a deliberately simplified set of observations with enough metadata to support a reproducible calculation. It is not evidence from a named real basin. Before graphing the numbers, decide which independent variable you think orders the processes and which observations might instead reflect changes in sampling position. Draw uncertainty markers or ranges where possible, even when the figures are hypothetical. A single good-fitting curve should be treated as a question: Which physical assumptions created it, and what independent data would disprove it? The submission must contain both a physical interpretation and a statement of one competing explanation. If a student uses a generative model or plotting program, they should preserve the original measurements and equations so the result can be independently reproduced.

The first deliverable is a clean measured-section or experimental-data table with a metadata line defining measurement units. The second is a graph or annotated cross-section that does not confuse elevation and stratigraphic thickness. The third is a two-model comparison in which each model must make at least one concrete additional prediction. The fourth is a brief calculation checked by an independent method and accompanied by a description of uncertainty. The fifth is a research note that states what cannot be resolved without additional evidence. Assign confidence to individual inferences, not to the diagram as a whole.

Data critique and transfer

A reproducible student submission must preserve a copy of the raw values, retain any derived normalized quantities separately and state which values were inferred rather than measured. Consider the possibility that the most accessible outcrop or easiest-to-analyse core was systematically unrepresentative. Plan an independent observation that would most sharply reduce uncertainty. If the best test is not feasible, present the honest limit of the inference rather than substituting a plausible-looking numerical value. This is especially important when educational methods are adapted to real land-use or subsurface questions.

§3.9 Unit 3 synthesis and assessment

Across these five topics, the central scientific skill is to distinguish a physically possible depositional interpretation from one that is specifically supported by evidence. Submit all five numerical exercises with units, the integrated research studio, and a correlation or process diagram that uses line styles to separate observed boundaries from inferred ones. Cite the source of external classification terminology when appropriate. A brief oral defence should explain which sedimentary observation would best falsify the preferred interpretation, how a missing interval affects the reconstruction, and whether the strongest evidence is geometric, compositional, chronological or process-based.

Self-assessment questions

  • Which observations in this unit are primary depositional properties, and which could be modified by burial or structural deformation?
  • Which quantities are measured directly and which are model-dependent estimates?
  • Where would another measured section most effectively distinguish competing hypotheses?
  • Does the proposed correlation connect similar rocks, synchronous time markers or a hypothesized environmental boundary?
  • What remains valid if the preferred interpretation is wrong?
