Unit 2 — Fluid motion, transport and deposition

From force balance to preserved fabric.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university sedimentology and stratigraphy courses. Eight units with five solved questions per unit and 16 interactive simulations illustrating grain settling velocities, cross-bedding paleoflow vectors, porosity loss compaction curves, accommodation-to-sediment supply ratios, and subsidence curves. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§2.1 Learning outcomes

Relate ideal transport equations to field evidence without presenting simplified models as universal thresholds.

§2.2 Extended unit perspective

Water or air carries sediment only because moving fluid exerts forces on particles. The tempting shortcut is to use one threshold graph or one settling equation to explain every deposit. Instead, ask which forces the equation represents. A Reynolds number based on channel depth describes a different ratio from particle Reynolds number; a Shields parameter estimates incipient motion under defined assumptions; a Stokes calculation presumes laminar flow around an isolated sphere. Changes in viscosity with temperature, intergranular cohesion, turbulent fluctuations and grain packing change the result. A preserved ripple can show a past transport process while saying little about the precise velocity or discharge. Teaching models in this unit demonstrate relations rather than reconstruct real floods or predict channel engineering outcomes. Each exercise should include a dimensional check, a sensitivity test and a statement identifying which parts of nature are missing.

§2.3 Reynolds number and flow regime

What is being explained?

The relative importance of inertial and viscous forces influences turbulence but does not by itself determine deposition style in natural rivers. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Velocity profiles, water depth, viscosity, roughness and mobile-bed observations establish the context of a measured discharge. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

An observed ripple set can persist under transient flows even when a single estimated Reynolds number suggests another regime. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Calculate Re using appropriate length and velocity scales, then test sensitivity to chosen hydraulic radius or grain dimension. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Field notebook and facies recognition. Write a two-column field record headed observation and inference. Enter grains, laminae, colour, contact shape and any measurable geometry in the observation column; reserve terms describing ancient rivers, shorelines or deep water for the inference column. Choose an outcrop reference scale suitable for the problem: centimetres for a ripple foreset, metres for a channel body, and kilometres for a basin map. Mark beds that cannot be observed because of cover. The same feature may be meaningful at one scale and misleading at another. Draw the simplest facies association consistent with the visible relationships and list one expected lateral equivalent. In your report do not call an exposure representative merely because it is accessible; accessibility itself may produce sampling bias.

Worked quantitative exercise 6: Channel Reynolds number

Hypothetical inputs: mean flow 0.75 m/s, representative depth 1.2 m and water kinematic viscosity 10^-6 m2/s. The transparent classroom expression is 0.75*1.2/1.0e-6. Evaluating this expression produces 900,000.0 dimensionless. Identify the numerator and denominator when the calculation produces a percentage, and write each conversion between metres, kilometres, days, years or millions of years explicitly. If the result is a transport rate, do not describe it as an observed sedimentation rate unless storage and bypass are accounted for; if it is a thickness, identify whether erosion and compaction are represented.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Bed roughness, nonuniform flow and variable sediment concentration break the assumptions behind an idealized single-number diagnosis. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Explain exactly which forces and length scale the calculated dimensionless ratio compares.

  2. State the physical reason the proposed mechanism would produce one of those observations, then identify a contrary observation that would count against it.
  3. Recalculate Exercise 6 after modifying one stated parameter, document units and explain sensitivity.
  4. Design an independent test using the protocol above; state a clear predicted outcome for the leading model and for its rival.
  5. Write an 80-word report that explains what is known, what remains uncertain and why the distinction matters when interpreting preserved strata.

§2.4 Competence, capacity and Shields stress

What is being explained?

Entrainment depends on hydrodynamic stress relative to submerged particle weight, with grain packing and cohesion modifying thresholds. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Bed grain distributions, critical motion in a flume and bedload measurements relate transport onset to material and boundary conditions. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

A large grain may be deposited by a brief high-stress flood, a debris flow, ice transport or subsequent erosion of finer surrounding material. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Calculate an approximate Shields parameter and bracket the result under uncertain bed slope and hydraulic radius. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Measurement and dimensional reasoning. For the numerical component, write the governing relation and then translate every quantity into a unit consistent with that relation. State whether thickness is true stratigraphic thickness or apparent width, whether grain size is a mass-weighted or number-weighted distribution, and whether a flux represents sediment delivered or rock preserved. A point estimate may hide a large physical uncertainty. Recalculate after changing one assumed input by ten percent, and distinguish the mathematical sensitivity from the real variability of that input. Present units in the plotted axis titles rather than only in figure captions. A sensible arithmetic result is not sufficient evidence that the equation is valid at a particular outcrop. Discuss whether equilibrium, uniform flow, conserved grain mass or constant deposition has been assumed.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Natural cohesive beds, hiding/exposure effects and particle shape mean there is no single universal motion threshold. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Distinguish the ability to move a particular particle from the total mass that a flow can carry.

§2.5 Settling, flocculation and suspension

What is being explained?

Gravity-driven settling competes with turbulent diffusion; Stokes settling is a narrow low-Reynolds-number approximation for isolated spheres. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Settling columns, concentration profiles and measured size distributions reveal whether particles remain suspended during a flow. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

Clay flocs can settle faster than primary clay platelets, while platy particles violate the ideal sphere calculation. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Calculate settling time for an ideal fine-grained sphere; compare its assumptions with a flocculated saline-water sample. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Hypothesis competition and counterexample. Begin with a pair of incompatible explanations that both seem plausible from the first observation. Instead of collecting more features that merely illustrate the preferred story, identify the measurement on which the predictions disagree. Consider that one hypothesis may involve sediment transport while the other involves later erosion or cementation; these are not mutually exclusive unless the timing is explicitly constrained. Record the expected observation under each explanation before seeing any additional dataset. Evidence that fails to discriminate is still descriptive evidence, but it does not rank the competing models. Where a third process could imitate both, give that possibility a separate row in the reasoning table. The final verdict may be that present evidence supports only a family of environments or ages.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Hindered settling, particle shape, turbulence and aggregation create uncertainty much larger than arithmetic rounding. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Use a settling estimate to frame a test, not to assign precise deposition age or water depth.

§2.6 Bedforms, dunes and migration

What is being explained?

Sediment transport and bed morphology interact as ripples or dunes migrate and create cross-stratification. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Bedform wavelength, crest orientation, stoss/lee geometry and time-lapse observations record migration direction and changing hydraulics. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

Climbing-ripple lamination records net aggradation and can occur under changing suspended-sediment supply, not simply high velocity. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Measure bedform displacement between observations and compute migration speed; compare with cross-set thickness in preserved strata. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Sampling design and data integrity. Prepare an explicit sampling protocol: what interval is the population of interest, how will representative sites be selected, which fraction of the sediment is included, and what precision is possible? Replicate critical counts because sedimentary deposits are patchy in both space and time. A sieve sample, thin section and borehole log observe different volumes and may not sample equivalent properties. Record locations and orientation without inventing precision that was not measured. For destructive sampling, respect collection permits and institution rules. Store the original readings separately from any normalized percentages so that the transformation can be inspected later. Treat missing data as missing, not as zeros, and write down every exclusion criterion before producing an attractive final plot.

Evidence limitations and a qualified conclusion

Preserved cross-set thickness is typically smaller than original bedform height, and erosional truncation produces selection bias. Categorize the dominant uncertainty as sampling, analytical, chronological, mechanistic or preservation-related, and name the measurement that would reduce it.

Link a measured sedimentary structure to a plausible bedform only after considering preservation potential.

§2.7 Event beds and graded deposition

What is being explained?

Rapid changes in flow competence, supply and settling can generate normally graded or inverse-graded layers by different mechanisms. Explain which visible feature would distinguish this process from later sedimentary reworking or burial modification.

The observational record

Turbidite divisions, traction carpets, debris-flow fabrics and flood couplets provide associations stronger than one grading direction. Record the scale and instrument behind each observation; do not convert a field description into a claim about ancient environment without additional evidence.

Competing interpretations

Normal grading does not automatically prove a deep-marine turbidity current; river floods and volcanic density currents also grade. Describe at least one predicted feature that differs between the rival and leading model, and identify a realistic observation that would distinguish them.

A discriminating investigation

Compare vertical grading, sole marks, lateral continuity and entrained mud clasts across several beds. Specify the comparison material and observable decision rule so that a negative test outcome could change your preferred interpretation.

Comparative synthesis and communication. Move from a single log to a lateral comparison. Propose which properties ought to remain similar between neighbouring sections and which may change as environments migrate. A marker bed is only a reliable time line when evidence establishes its correlation; similar sandstone colour is not such evidence. Choose a visual representation that distinguishes observed boundaries from extrapolated ones, perhaps using solid versus dashed lines and a brief uncertainty legend. Present at least one alternative explanation in the figure caption rather than burying disagreement in the final paragraph. A coherent story should preserve contradictions instead of quietly removing inconvenient beds. In the conclusion identify the smallest claim that would remain valid if your preferred process model were overturned.

Interpret event beds using process assemblages rather than a one-feature label.

§2.8 Integrated research studio: From grain to bed

Use supplied synthetic water depths and sediment sizes to estimate Reynolds number and bed shear under consistent assumptions. Explain why a Stokes settling result for fine sand may violate its own low-particle-Re condition. Design a flume study that records ripple migration and sediment supply simultaneously. Compare the predicted bedform record of steady current against a short flood pulse, and state what may be lost during preservation.

Practical workflow

A teaching dataset is a deliberately simplified set of observations with enough metadata to support a reproducible calculation. It is not evidence from a named real basin. Before graphing the numbers, decide which independent variable you think orders the processes and which observations might instead reflect changes in sampling position. Draw uncertainty markers or ranges where possible, even when the figures are hypothetical. A single good-fitting curve should be treated as a question: Which physical assumptions created it, and what independent data would disprove it? The submission must contain both a physical interpretation and a statement of one competing explanation. If a student uses a generative model or plotting program, they should preserve the original measurements and equations so the result can be independently reproduced.

The first deliverable is a clean measured-section or experimental-data table with a metadata line defining measurement units. The second is a graph or annotated cross-section that does not confuse elevation and stratigraphic thickness. The third is a two-model comparison in which each model must make at least one concrete additional prediction. The fourth is a brief calculation checked by an independent method and accompanied by a description of uncertainty. The fifth is a research note that states what cannot be resolved without additional evidence. Assign confidence to individual inferences, not to the diagram as a whole.

Data critique and transfer

A reproducible student submission must preserve a copy of the raw values, retain any derived normalized quantities separately and state which values were inferred rather than measured. Consider the possibility that the most accessible outcrop or easiest-to-analyse core was systematically unrepresentative. Plan an independent observation that would most sharply reduce uncertainty. If the best test is not feasible, present the honest limit of the inference rather than substituting a plausible-looking numerical value. This is especially important when educational methods are adapted to real land-use or subsurface questions.

§2.9 Unit 2 synthesis and assessment

Across these five topics, the central scientific skill is to distinguish a physically possible depositional interpretation from one that is specifically supported by evidence. Submit all five numerical exercises with units, the integrated research studio, and a correlation or process diagram that uses line styles to separate observed boundaries from inferred ones. Cite the source of external classification terminology when appropriate. A brief oral defence should explain which sedimentary observation would best falsify the preferred interpretation, how a missing interval affects the reconstruction, and whether the strongest evidence is geometric, compositional, chronological or process-based.

Self-assessment questions

  • Which observations in this unit are primary depositional properties, and which could be modified by burial or structural deformation?
  • Which quantities are measured directly and which are model-dependent estimates?
  • Where would another measured section most effectively distinguish competing hypotheses?
  • Does the proposed correlation connect similar rocks, synchronous time markers or a hypothesized environmental boundary?
  • What remains valid if the preferred interpretation is wrong?
