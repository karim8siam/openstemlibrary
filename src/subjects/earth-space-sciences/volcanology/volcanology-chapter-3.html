Volatiles and Magma Ascent

**Why can similar magmas erupt quietly at one time and explosively at another?**

About this textbook. Original English teaching material mapped to University of Dhaka Statistics B.S. Honours 2023–24, Stat H-307 (Statistical Simulation and Monte Carlo Methods). Eight units and exactly three solved questions per unit. Interactive simulations illustrate linear congruential generators, rejection sampling envelopes, rare-event importance sampling, and Metropolis-Hastings random walks. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition; it has not received independent academic peer review. Curriculum mapping does not imply university endorsement.

§3.1 Central question

Why can similar magmas erupt quietly at one time and explosively at another?

Synthetic scenario. A fictional conduit transports magma with dissolved water and carbon dioxide, evolving pressure, bubble populations, permeability and shear stresses during ascent. This is an imagined classroom landscape; no measured eruption or real safety recommendation is implied.

§3.2 Concepts and study approach

The unit brings together volatile solubility, bubble nucleation, fragmentation, permeability and conduit dynamics. Physical processes can be reconstructed from deposits, rock composition, geodetic observations or measured signals, but different materials survive and different instruments observe different parts of the system. The same observable feature may arise from distinct combinations of source conditions and later changes. The aim is to identify the evidence most capable of separating interpretations, not merely to attach a name to the most striking landform. The included case measurements and graphs were designed solely for teaching.

Problem framing. A fictional conduit transports magma with dissolved water and carbon dioxide, evolving pressure, bubble populations, permeability and shear stresses during ascent. Imagine that students must present a short technical brief. Before making a numerical model, determine the time span, which observations are independent, and which boundaries are directly mapped rather than estimated. Data may have unequal completeness and calibration. Distinguish an interpretation based on direct field material from one inferred from numerical fitting or a remote sensor. Where the evidence allows more than one answer, explain what added observation would be valuable.

Lesson 3.1: Dissolved gases and solubility

Learning objective. Explain the physical reasoning behind dissolved gases and solubility, design a suitable observation, evaluate a counterexample, and distinguish classroom arithmetic from an empirical volcanic conclusion.

Scientific foundation. Water, carbon dioxide and sulfur species may be dissolved in magma at depth; lower pressure during ascent generally favors volatile exsolution depending on composition and temperature. The issue is not resolved by knowing a label alone: observations may be indirect, sampled at different scales or altered after the event. A defensible explanation specifies the process, its boundary conditions and a plausible competing mechanism.

Evidence and method. Compare schematic solubility curves at two compositions and discuss a gas-rich recharge pulse. Define the instrument or mapping method, the measured physical quantity, the sampling interval and the possible biases. State what could be directly checked by another observer, without requiring them to accept your interpretation first.

Common interpretive trap. The gas measured above a vent is not an unmodified sample of the initial dissolved volatile inventory. The corresponding methodological safeguard is to compare observations that should differ between explanations. A failure to collect discriminating data is a limitation, not proof that the working mechanism is right.

Measurements and evidence quality

Implement the following discipline-specific measurement plan: Compare schematic solubility curves at two compositions and discuss a gas-rich recharge pulse. Make sampling and calibration explicit. If an instrument observes several correlated points, avoid reporting them as fully independent replications. Carry the raw data and a record of exclusions into the final analysis. If a signal is close to the detection limit, show an interval or a qualified statement rather than an extra decimal place. Ask whether the same observational conclusion would emerge if the most uncertain record were removed or processed using another defensible method.

Model validation protocol

Write assumptions beside the model, not in a remote appendix. Confirm dimensional consistency and limiting behavior; then evaluate at the smallest, intermediate and largest plausible classroom inputs. Validate arithmetic against an independent calculation. The model remains provisional if there are no external observations that test the mechanism. Apply it specifically to dissolved gases and solubility using the supplied scenario. Lesson 3.2: Bubble nucleation and growth

Learning objective. Explain the physical reasoning behind bubble nucleation and growth, design a suitable observation, evaluate a counterexample, and distinguish classroom arithmetic from an empirical volcanic conclusion.

Scientific foundation. Bubbles nucleate, expand and coalesce as magma decompresses; kinetics and nucleation-site availability influence how close the system stays to equilibrium. The issue is not resolved by knowing a label alone: observations may be indirect, sampled at different scales or altered after the event. A defensible explanation specifies the process, its boundary conditions and a plausible competing mechanism.

Evidence and method. Record bubble-size distributions and vesicle number densities on fictional thin-section imagery using a declared image-analysis threshold. Define the instrument or mapping method, the measured physical quantity, the sampling interval and the possible biases. State what could be directly checked by another observer, without requiring them to accept your interpretation first.

Common interpretive trap. A vesicularity value without bubble connectivity cannot by itself reveal whether the magma was permeable before eruption. The corresponding methodological safeguard is to compare observations that should differ between explanations. A failure to collect discriminating data is a limitation, not proof that the working mechanism is right.

Physical scale and boundary conditions in practice

Physical interpretation changes with scale. A hand specimen, a ten-metre outcrop, a volcanic edifice and a satellite pixel do not offer interchangeable constraints on bubble nucleation and growth. State the spatial support of each observation and the time averaging implicit in the selected method. Compare the variables held fixed in the worked exercise with the quantities that vary naturally. If your calculation assumes a homogeneous material or a steady flow, explain which observations could reveal heterogeneity, a discontinuity, or a shift in regime.

Designing an investigation in practice

An actionable class exercise is: Compare two volume-fraction scenarios while distinguishing closed and connected pores. Describe two observations that support the preferred inference and one that would substantially weaken it. Provide a sketch or table with a data dictionary and include both an expected result and a plausible unexpected result. Distinguish data gathered at the same site from independently sampled sites. Even if the teaching exercise is entirely desk-based, include metadata on coordinate systems, measurement precision, instrument or image source, and whether values are synthetic.

Communication protocol

Write a technical paragraph and a public-facing paragraph using exactly the same evidence. In the public version, preserve uncertainty and cite the relevant observatory rather than presenting a student result as advice. Keep hazard, risk, exposure, vulnerability and uncertainty as separate concepts. The meaning of a forecast depends on the time window and the outcome that is counted. Apply it specifically to bubble nucleation and growth using the supplied scenario. An effective report should make the observer’s original question visible and keep all artificial data marked as invented.

Lesson 3.3: Magma ascent and conduit flow

Learning objective. Explain the physical reasoning behind magma ascent and conduit flow, design a suitable observation, evaluate a counterexample, and distinguish classroom arithmetic from an empirical volcanic conclusion.

Scientific foundation. Ascent rate depends on pressure gradients, frictional resistance, conduit geometry, changing magma density and evolving multiphase flow. The issue is not resolved by knowing a label alone: observations may be indirect, sampled at different scales or altered after the event. A defensible explanation specifies the process, its boundary conditions and a plausible competing mechanism.

Evidence and method. Calculate travel times under an explicitly constant-rise-speed assumption and discuss how conduit narrowing would change them. Define the instrument or mapping method, the measured physical quantity, the sampling interval and the possible biases. State what could be directly checked by another observer, without requiring them to accept your interpretation first.

Common interpretive trap. A single average velocity cannot resolve acceleration, wall rock failure, recirculation or heterogeneous magma mixtures. The corresponding methodological safeguard is to compare observations that should differ between explanations. A failure to collect discriminating data is a limitation, not proof that the working mechanism is right.

Contradictions worth testing

A scientific explanation is strengthened when it survives serious attempts at falsification. Here, a specific danger is: A single average velocity cannot resolve acceleration, wall rock failure, recirculation or heterogeneous magma mixtures. Write the interpretation that a skeptical colleague would advance from the same field notebook. Then identify what the two explanations predict differently in rock fabric, chemistry, geophysical time series or deposit distribution. Record the proposed test before examining the answer so it cannot be adjusted after the fact. Where more than one hypothesis survives, report that ambiguity instead of choosing the version that tells the most coherent story.

Interpretation, uncertainty, and safe limits

When presenting a result, separate the observation, the calculation and the implication in three sentences. An output that matches a conceptual model does not necessarily predict a real eruptive event. In this topic, A single average velocity cannot resolve acceleration, wall rock failure, recirculation or heterogeneous magma mixtures. remains an important constraint even when the exercise appears numerically neat. Volcanic hazards affect communities and field researchers; classroom values must never become evacuation zones, thresholds, aircraft routing or claims of safety. The scientific report should end by naming what would have to be independently observed before any consequential application.

Competing hypothesis protocol

Begin with three columns: observable feature, explanation A, explanation B. For each column decide whether an observation is expected, merely compatible or contradictory. The exercise should not put a weight of one on every observation: multiple measurements from the same camera, sample or process can be correlated. Where the evidence is ambiguous, propose a new measurement rather than manufacturing a definitive choice. Apply it specifically to magma ascent and conduit flow using the supplied scenario. An effective report should make the observer’s original question visible and keep all artificial data marked as invented.

Lesson 3.4: Permeability, degassing and fragmentation

Learning objective. Explain the physical reasoning behind permeability, degassing and fragmentation, design a suitable observation, evaluate a counterexample, and distinguish classroom arithmetic from an empirical volcanic conclusion.

Scientific foundation. Connected bubble pathways can permit gas escape, potentially reducing overpressure, while rapid ascent and inhibited degassing can promote fragmentation. The issue is not resolved by knowing a label alone: observations may be indirect, sampled at different scales or altered after the event. A defensible explanation specifies the process, its boundary conditions and a plausible competing mechanism.

Evidence and method. Compare gas discharge, pore connectivity, ascent speed and particle-size indicators for two hypothetical eruption stages. Define the instrument or mapping method, the measured physical quantity, the sampling interval and the possible biases. State what could be directly checked by another observer, without requiring them to accept your interpretation first.

Common interpretive trap. High vesicularity alone is not a direct measurement of gas permeability or a guaranteed indication of explosivity. The corresponding methodological safeguard is to compare observations that should differ between explanations. A failure to collect discriminating data is a limitation, not proof that the working mechanism is right.

Lesson 3.5: Hydrothermal systems and phreatic explosions

Learning objective. Explain the physical reasoning behind hydrothermal systems and phreatic explosions, design a suitable observation, evaluate a counterexample, and distinguish classroom arithmetic from an empirical volcanic conclusion.

Scientific foundation. Steam-driven explosions can occur when pressurized hydrothermal fluids flash or rupture overlying material; these events may not involve freshly erupted juvenile magma. The issue is not resolved by knowing a label alone: observations may be indirect, sampled at different scales or altered after the event. A defensible explanation specifies the process, its boundary conditions and a plausible competing mechanism.

Evidence and method. Compare alteration minerals, steam chemistry, thermal imaging and seismic signals while explicitly noting non-unique precursors. Define the instrument or mapping method, the measured physical quantity, the sampling interval and the possible biases. State what could be directly checked by another observer, without requiring them to accept your interpretation first.

Common interpretive trap. A phreatic explosion does not necessarily require a newly ascending magma batch, and some events have few detectable warning signs. The corresponding methodological safeguard is to compare observations that should differ between explanations. A failure to collect discriminating data is a limitation, not proof that the working mechanism is right.

Mechanisms and competing explanations

A causal model should connect source conditions, transport, transformation and the observable product. The central mechanism is: Steam-driven explosions can occur when pressurized hydrothermal fluids flash or rupture overlying material; these events may not involve freshly erupted juvenile magma. This description predicts particular textures or signals, yet alternate processes might also produce parts of the same pattern. Use A phreatic explosion does not necessarily require a newly ascending magma batch, and some events have few detectable warning signs. as a test of an attractive but inadequate story. Set out two hypotheses with predictions that diverge at a practical sampling location. Retain the less dramatic hypothesis in the record until some observation actually rules it out; familiarity is not a substitute for comparison.

Designing an investigation in practice

An actionable class exercise is: Construct a cautious interpretation of a synthetic crater deposit with abundant altered lithic fragments. Describe two observations that support the preferred inference and one that would substantially weaken it. Provide a sketch or table with a data dictionary and include both an expected result and a plausible unexpected result. Distinguish data gathered at the same site from independently sampled sites. Even if the teaching exercise is entirely desk-based, include metadata on coordinate systems, measurement precision, instrument or image source, and whether values are synthetic.

Time-series protocol

State acquisition times, the local-to-UTC conversion, instrument uptime, rate of sampling and any changes to detection limits. Calculate the effect of one missing observation on a simple mean, then compare this change with any claimed physical trend. Multiple channels may indicate the same phenomenon and should not automatically be interpreted as independent evidence. §3.3 Integrated research studio: Unit 3

Scenario. A fictional conduit transports magma with dissolved water and carbon dioxide, evolving pressure, bubble populations, permeability and shear stresses during ascent. Assemble an evidence ledger with a row for each observation, its provenance, its measured units, and an explicit distinction between original observation and interpreted process. Add a third column for alternative hypotheses. Decide which items could be affected by the same source of error or sampling gap before computing averages, rates or correlations. A synthetic observation should be documented as such both in the file name and in any figure legend, because a decontextualized number can appear real after it is copied into another report.

Research sequence. First define a narrowly stated question. Second draw the relevant physical geometry or time axis. Third identify process variables that could be measured independently. Fourth check dimensional consistency of a simple model. Fifth create two alternative explanations and an observation capable of distinguishing them. Sixth record an uncertainty or sensitivity check. Seventh communicate the conclusion in a short paragraph aimed at technically informed readers and in another paragraph suited to nonspecialists, preserving the uncertainty in both.

Practical assessment. Use the explicitly synthetic CSV file for this unit to construct a two-panel visualization: one panel shows the fictional input series, and the other shows the result of a transparently specified numerical transformation. Add labeled axes and units; do not connect samples with a line if the observation units are unrelated. Explain whether outliers represent legitimate processes, data errors or unknowns. Describe how results change if the least complete rows are excluded, and archive the transformation steps.

Scope and responsibility. The educational concepts here do not generate official volcano warnings, physical access permissions or operational models of lava inundation, ash dispersal or lahar travel. Where a problem resembles an applied hazard calculation, the student must state why it lacks sufficient measurements for public-safety use. Official observatories and civil authorities are responsible for operational interpretations and decisions. For reproducible research, preserve the original dataset, all transformations, software environment information and the model assumptions.
