Unit 5 — Joints, fractures and veins

Distinguish opening, shear and mineral precipitation in fractured rock masses. Use structural observations and reproducible geometric tests to evaluate competing interpretations.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Structural Geology study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§5.1 Central question

Distinguish opening, shear and mineral precipitation in fractured rock masses. Use structural observations and reproducible geometric tests to evaluate competing interpretations.

§5.2 Extended unit perspective

Fractures and veins carry information about mechanics, fluid flow and mineral precipitation. Their orientation alone does not identify the event that formed them. A joint may have opened without detectable shear, a mineral vein may preserve repeated opening increments, and a fault-related fracture may show both opening and sliding. It is therefore valuable to record whether fracture walls match, whether filling minerals grow inward from walls, and whether later veins cut or terminate against an earlier structure. Measured fracture density also depends heavily on the line's orientation relative to the fracture set. One scanline parallel to joints will undercount them, even if the outcrop contains many. Topological network models offer one way to investigate connectivity, but a connected line drawing is not proof that a rock mass transmits fluids: apertures may be sealed, stress dependent or discontinuous through the third dimension. The elementary fracture exercises in this book deliberately ignore these complications in order to show what is being calculated, then invite students to design better measurements. Real fracture networks require statistical sampling, a three-dimensional conceptual model and independent hydraulic evidence before permeability inferences can be defended.

Field and scientific objectives

Describe relevant structural objects with location and orientation, translate them between map and section, construct a physically and chronologically consistent interpretation, and test at least one alternative. The aim is to communicate exactly which information is observed, which result follows by geometric calculation and which regional mechanism is inferred. Avoid interpreting one structural indicator as proof of a single tectonic event where earlier deformation, reactivation or anisotropic rock properties remain plausible.

§5.3 Joint sets and spacing distributions

Mechanism and observational scale

Joints are mechanical discontinuities with no confidently measurable shear displacement; their spacing depends on layer thickness, material and stress history. Draw a basic geometric sketch and define the coordinate system, sample orientation and reference state if one is needed.

Evidence and documentation

Systematic orientations, termination patterns and bed-confined spacing may reveal fracture-set families. Document the exposure, instrument or measurement technique, scale and uncertainty before discussing tectonic implications. Maintain an observation/inference table with scanline orientation, surface exposure window and relative age of mineral fill and illustrate joint set frequency, intersection topology and hydraulic uncertainty. If your evidence is a two-dimensional trace of a three-dimensional surface, state exactly what its projection does and does not resolve.

Competing interpretation

Cooling joints, unloading fractures and tectonic joints can share orientations but differ in context. Record expected results for each explanation before carrying out the test. A model requiring hidden geometry must mark that geometry as inferred.

Test and scientific protocol

Count fractures along scanlines of multiple orientation, record censoring and convert counts to spacing with clear sampling definitions. State the predicted direction, location, quantity or material signature under both models. Use at least one measurement not previously employed to construct the model. For joint sets and spacing distributions, define sample selection criteria, coordinate conventions and an acceptable range of observation error.

Limitation, cross-check and synthesis

Scanline angle and truncated exposure lengths bias estimated fracture density. Consider the alternative that changes the interpretation most while retaining the measured observations. Make a two-column field record. Put verifiable geometric and material facts in the left column and causal or historical interpretation in the right. For each interpreted event, cite the specific measured feature and its location. An interpretation without such a link is a hypothesis to test rather than a supported result.

Describe spacing statistics alongside bed thickness and sampling geometry. A defensible conclusion therefore has five components: observed feature, ideal relation used, leading interpretation, decisive contrary test and residual uncertainty. Indicate whether each is local to the measured exposure or can legitimately be extended across the mapped domain.

§5.4 Mode-I fractures and hybrid opening

Mechanism and observational scale

Opening-mode extension fractures separate walls normal to the fracture surface, while hybrid fractures may also include shear displacement. Draw a basic geometric sketch and define the coordinate system, sample orientation and reference state if one is needed.

Evidence and documentation

Wall-matching textures and crack-seal increments can show repeated opening; microscopic mineral fibres may track growth directions. Document the exposure, instrument or measurement technique, scale and uncertainty before discussing tectonic implications. Maintain an observation/inference table with scanline orientation, surface exposure window and relative age of mineral fill and illustrate joint set frequency, intersection topology and hydraulic uncertainty. If your evidence is a two-dimensional trace of a three-dimensional surface, state exactly what its projection does and does not resolve.

Competing interpretation

Veins may fill older joints reopened by a later stress field; a mineralized plane is not automatically the initial fracture. Record expected results for each explanation before carrying out the test. A model requiring hidden geometry must mark that geometry as inferred.

Test and scientific protocol

Map matching walls and mineral fibres, then assess whether growth increments are parallel to a plausible opening direction. State the predicted direction, location, quantity or material signature under both models. Use at least one measurement not previously employed to construct the model. For mode-i fractures and hybrid opening, define sample selection criteria, coordinate conventions and an acceptable range of observation error.

§5.5 Vein arrays and crack-seal chronology

Mechanism and observational scale

Mineral precipitation can fill fractures during successive episodes of opening, fluid flow and sealing, generating layered or fibrous veins. Draw a basic geometric sketch and define the coordinate system, sample orientation and reference state if one is needed.

Evidence and documentation

Cross-cutting veins, inclusion trails and growth zoning provide relative timing even without numerical ages. Document the exposure, instrument or measurement technique, scale and uncertainty before discussing tectonic implications. Maintain an observation/inference table with scanline orientation, surface exposure window and relative age of mineral fill and illustrate joint set frequency, intersection topology and hydraulic uncertainty. If your evidence is a two-dimensional trace of a three-dimensional surface, state exactly what its projection does and does not resolve.

Competing interpretation

A broad vein may form by one opening pulse instead of many, and intersecting veins may reactivate. Record expected results for each explanation before carrying out the test. A model requiring hidden geometry must mark that geometry as inferred.

Test and scientific protocol

Build a paragenetic order from inclusion bands and multiple intersections and test whether it is internally transitive. State the predicted direction, location, quantity or material signature under both models. Use at least one measurement not previously employed to construct the model. For vein arrays and crack-seal chronology, define sample selection criteria, coordinate conventions and an acceptable range of observation error.

Different minerals may nucleate at different times and temperature regimes; age of host rock is not age of vein. §5.6 Fracture connectivity and permeability

Mechanism and observational scale

A fracture network transmits fluid according to aperture, connectivity, stress state and sealing; fracture count alone does not specify permeability. Draw a basic geometric sketch and define the coordinate system, sample orientation and reference state if one is needed.

Evidence and documentation

Intersections, terminations, mineral fill and directional hydraulic responses help identify connected pathways. Document the exposure, instrument or measurement technique, scale and uncertainty before discussing tectonic implications. Maintain an observation/inference table with scanline orientation, surface exposure window and relative age of mineral fill and illustrate joint set frequency, intersection topology and hydraulic uncertainty. If your evidence is a two-dimensional trace of a three-dimensional surface, state exactly what its projection does and does not resolve.

Competing interpretation

Two rocks with identical fracture density can have radically different transport if one is sealed or poorly connected. Record expected results for each explanation before carrying out the test. A model requiring hidden geometry must mark that geometry as inferred.

Test and scientific protocol

Build a graph of fracture intersections and test sensitivity when short discontinuous branches are omitted. State the predicted direction, location, quantity or material signature under both models. Use at least one measurement not previously employed to construct the model. For fracture connectivity and permeability, define sample selection criteria, coordinate conventions and an acceptable range of observation error.

A model that works in one section but fails in map view needs revision.

Treat network geometry and hydraulic transmissivity as distinct measurements. A defensible conclusion therefore has five components: observed feature, ideal relation used, leading interpretation, decisive contrary test and residual uncertainty. §5.7 Fracture mechanics and scaling

Mechanism and observational scale

Fracture-tip stress concentration, material toughness and existing flaw geometry influence propagation; natural joints span multiple orders of scale. Draw a basic geometric sketch and define the coordinate system, sample orientation and reference state if one is needed.

Evidence and documentation

Arrest at layer boundaries, interaction with other cracks and systematic spacing can reveal mechanical contrasts. Document the exposure, instrument or measurement technique, scale and uncertainty before discussing tectonic implications. Maintain an observation/inference table with scanline orientation, surface exposure window and relative age of mineral fill and illustrate joint set frequency, intersection topology and hydraulic uncertainty. If your evidence is a two-dimensional trace of a three-dimensional surface, state exactly what its projection does and does not resolve.

Competing interpretation

Regular spacing may arise from sequential saturation or from a joint set preorganized by layering rather than one simple stress event. Record expected results for each explanation before carrying out the test. A model requiring hidden geometry must mark that geometry as inferred.

Test and scientific protocol

Compare crack-tip termination frequencies across layer interfaces and distinguish size-dependent observation completeness. State the predicted direction, location, quantity or material signature under both models. Use at least one measurement not previously employed to construct the model. For fracture mechanics and scaling, define sample selection criteria, coordinate conventions and an acceptable range of observation error.

Limitation, cross-check and synthesis

An elastic crack model ignores inelastic process zones, pore fluids and anisotropic material. Consider the alternative that changes the interpretation most while retaining the measured observations. List which surfaces or fabrics predate which others using direct cut, fold, overprint or growth relations. Leave pairs unordered where no observation establishes priority. Reactivation means a single surface may occur more than once in the event graph.

Use idealized fracture scaling to formulate tests rather than engineering predictions without calibration. A defensible conclusion therefore has five components: observed feature, ideal relation used, leading interpretation, decisive contrary test and residual uncertainty. Indicate whether each is local to the measured exposure or can legitimately be extended across the mapped domain.

§5.8 Integrated investigation 5 — Fracture networks from scanlines

A classroom pavement map depicts three fracture sets, mineral-sealed veins and zones of systematic spacing. Design perpendicular and oblique scanlines, count fracture intersections and estimate linear density with appropriate censoring notes. Draw a connectivity graph but leave sealed segments marked as uncertain conductors. Build two mineral-vein chronologies that agree with the intersections and determine the one extra observation required to distinguish them. Explain why a two-dimensional graph is not a permeability measurement.

Analysis protocol and deliverables

The following integrated investigation uses fictional measurements expressly manufactured to demonstrate methods. It is not a published field survey or a substitute for collecting data. Begin with original measurements and metadata; present interpreted structures in separate layers. Compute at least one simple quantity with its units, make two competing predictions, and decide which additional piece of evidence would be most informative. Report any failure of the data to distinguish models. Submit a map or diagram, annotated calculations, a brief uncertainty table and a paragraph explaining whether the result has any practical limitations. The first panel of the submitted figure should contain observations only. The second may show an inferred geometry, but include a legend differentiating solid observation lines from dashed extrapolations. The third should compare an alternative model and identify the measurement that would select between them. A model should be judged against the data it did not use for fitting whenever possible.

Interpreting the synthetic dataset

Open datasets/unit-05.csv from the offline reader. All station names, orientations, distances and categories in this CSV are fictional. Plot the values and report which trend, if any, is robust against removal of two observations. Where the data do not contain enough directional or temporal context to support a tectonic claim, write that limitation explicitly. Do not claim a physical trend is established just because a graphing tool draws a smooth line.

Independent review

Exchange interpretations with another student. Ask them to identify one term or symbol whose meaning is unclear, one unstated convention and one observation that could disprove the preferred reconstruction. Revise the final diagram to make these items visible. Grade the work on measurement transparency and falsifiability, not on whether every student selected the same visually attractive fault or fold model.
