Elasticity and Seismic Wave Propagation

Seismic waves are vector disturbances traveling through heterogeneous elastic media. Coordinate systems, phase conventions and the validity of linear elasticity matter when comparing a simple model with measured records. A wave-speed estimate is conditional on the medium and on which phase was actually picked.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Seismology study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§2.1 Guiding question

What determines the speed, mode, direction and amplitude of seismic waves?

Fictional unit investigation. A fictional layered crustal section provides densities, elastic moduli and interface depths. Different wavefront sketches are supplied and students diagnose which inferences depend on homogeneity.

§2.2 Learning workflow

Before beginning, distinguish the scientific question, the measured quantity and the latent parameter that the student hopes to infer. Assign physical units, name a plausible alternate hypothesis, and state what data are fictional. For wave propagation, distinguish phase, group speed, mode conversion and energy transport. State the isotropic homogeneous approximations before using scalar wave speeds; compare the consequence of a layered model. The experiments are deliberately simplified so their calculations can be independently checked. They are not validated field instruments, real-time forecasts, or site-specific hazard assessments.

§2.3 Stress, strain and elastic moduli

Learning goals. Explain the mechanism, translate the problem into measurable quantities, test an alternative interpretation and defend an uncertainty statement.

Physical foundation. Small recoverable deformation can be described using stress and strain tensors and elastic parameters. Bulk modulus concerns compression; shear modulus concerns shape change; the moduli cannot be substituted casually. In the broader unit, Seismic waves are vector disturbances traveling through heterogeneous elastic media. Coordinate systems, phase conventions and the validity of linear elasticity matter when comparing a simple model with measured records. A wave-speed estimate is conditional on the medium and on which phase was actually picked.

Observational method. Translate simple uniaxial tests into stress–strain points and check whether a linear elastic approximation fits at small strain. Specify the coordinate frame, observation period, relevant units, acquisition and calibration limitations before interpreting the resulting figure.

Common interpretive trap. Elastic deformation of intact rock in a laboratory does not reproduce fracture, anisotropy or nonlinear soil response during intense shaking. A neat numeric solution does not remove this interpretive ambiguity. Ask which further independent measurement could reveal whether the preferred explanation is incorrect.

Applied investigation. Estimate a toy shear strain under a prescribed stress and explain why a failure criterion is additional information. Provide a labeled sketch, a short reproducible table and a paragraph separating observed evidence from inferred process.

§2.4 Compressional and shear waves

Learning goals. Explain the mechanism, translate the problem into measurable quantities, test an alternative interpretation and defend an uncertainty statement.

Physical foundation. An isotropic elastic solid supports P waves involving volumetric and shear deformation, and S waves involving shear distortion. Their speeds depend on density and moduli, and fluid media lack propagating bulk shear waves. In the broader unit, Seismic waves are vector disturbances traveling through heterogeneous elastic media. Coordinate systems, phase conventions and the validity of linear elasticity matter when comparing a simple model with measured records. A wave-speed estimate is conditional on the medium and on which phase was actually picked.

Observational method. Use synthetic density, bulk modulus and rigidity values to compute the two velocities, and check if the resulting ratio is physically consistent. Specify the coordinate frame, observation period, relevant units, acquisition and calibration limitations before interpreting the resulting figure.

Common interpretive trap. An arrival labelled P or S can reflect conversions and path complexity; raw travel time is not a direct rock property. A neat numeric solution does not remove this interpretive ambiguity. Ask which further independent measurement could reveal whether the preferred explanation is incorrect.

§2.5 Reflection, refraction, and Snell's law

Learning goals. Explain the mechanism, translate the problem into measurable quantities, test an alternative interpretation and defend an uncertainty statement.

Physical foundation. At material boundaries, wave energy partitions among reflected, transmitted and converted modes. Ray directions approximately follow slowness relations at wavelengths small compared with heterogeneity scales. In the broader unit, Seismic waves are vector disturbances traveling through heterogeneous elastic media. Coordinate systems, phase conventions and the validity of linear elasticity matter when comparing a simple model with measured records. A wave-speed estimate is conditional on the medium and on which phase was actually picked.

Observational method. Trace a set of incident and refracted rays for a layered velocity section and document the convention used for angles. Specify the coordinate frame, observation period, relevant units, acquisition and calibration limitations before interpreting the resulting figure.

Common interpretive trap. A single sharp arrival does not imply one interface: multiples, converted phases or processing artifacts can appear similar. A neat numeric solution does not remove this interpretive ambiguity. Ask which further independent measurement could reveal whether the preferred explanation is incorrect.

Applied investigation. Construct alternative layered profiles that yield similar first-arrival times. Provide a labeled sketch, a short reproducible table and a paragraph separating observed evidence from inferred process.

§2.6 Surface waves, dispersion, and polarization

Learning goals. Explain the mechanism, translate the problem into measurable quantities, test an alternative interpretation and defend an uncertainty statement.

Physical foundation. Rayleigh and Love waves propagate along a free surface and commonly sample depths dependent on their wavelengths. Dispersion reflects stratification, not a single universal surface-wave speed. In the broader unit, Seismic waves are vector disturbances traveling through heterogeneous elastic media. Coordinate systems, phase conventions and the validity of linear elasticity matter when comparing a simple model with measured records. A wave-speed estimate is conditional on the medium and on which phase was actually picked.

Observational method. Make a synthetic phase-velocity-versus-period plot and compare its inferred depth sensitivity with the survey dimensions. Specify the coordinate frame, observation period, relevant units, acquisition and calibration limitations before interpreting the resulting figure.

Common interpretive trap. Surface-wave amplitude or ellipticity depends on path, source and local structure; polarization alone is not an absolute depth profile. A neat numeric solution does not remove this interpretive ambiguity. Ask which further independent measurement could reveal whether the preferred explanation is incorrect.

§2.7 Attenuation, scattering, and geometric spreading

Learning goals. Explain the mechanism, translate the problem into measurable quantities, test an alternative interpretation and defend an uncertainty statement.

Physical foundation. Amplitude generally diminishes through geometric spreading and anelastic loss; scattering redistributes energy rather than necessarily removing it from the seismogram. Quality factor Q parameterizes attenuation in an approximation. In the broader unit, Seismic waves are vector disturbances traveling through heterogeneous elastic media. Coordinate systems, phase conventions and the validity of linear elasticity matter when comparing a simple model with measured records. A wave-speed estimate is conditional on the medium and on which phase was actually picked.

Observational method. Plot expected amplitude versus distance for a fixed frequency while varying Q in a labeled hypothetical velocity model. Specify the coordinate frame, observation period, relevant units, acquisition and calibration limitations before interpreting the resulting figure.

Common interpretive trap. Observed damping cannot be assigned entirely to intrinsic attenuation without examining radiation, focusing and station response. A neat numeric solution does not remove this interpretive ambiguity. Ask which further independent measurement could reveal whether the preferred explanation is incorrect.
