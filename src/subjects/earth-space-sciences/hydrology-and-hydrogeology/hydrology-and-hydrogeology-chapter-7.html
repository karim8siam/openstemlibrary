§7.1 Scientific problem for this unit

What can responsible planning infer from finite records under changing human and climatic pressures? The case-study setting is: Compare demand and supply policies under uncertain recharge.. The central difficulty is that the same observed water level, hydrograph, concentration or land-surface change can arise from multiple physical causes. A course in hydrology becomes useful when students learn to infer processes cautiously rather than replacing a missing measurement with a convenient story. This unit integrates conservation laws, field observables and independent checks in a coherent investigation.

Background and field context

A growing coastal district with dry-season withdrawals and uneven water service. The investigation asks students to define a measurement support, write the governing equation with correct units, and make a prediction before inspecting hypothetical data. Water availability depends on seasonality, water quality, cost, conveyance reliability, ecosystems and rights. A city may report a positive annual water balance while aquifers decline during the dry season or an upstream village has no safe supply. Students should keep a concise field ledger indicating which variables were measured, which were derived, and which were assigned because the problem did not provide enough data.

The unifying tools are dimensional reasoning, boundary diagrams and comparison of rival conceptual models. Reservoirs, drought, uncertainty, recharge, abstraction, coastal aquifers and sustainability all become difficult when scales are mixed, when a sensor's datum is omitted, or when statistical fit is confused with causal evidence. Draw one section or plan view that labels land surface, relevant geological horizons, the stream or well network, and the water-transfer pathways under discussion. Give a sign convention to every flux. For quantities reported as volumes, identify the integration interval and contributing area. Explain why the chosen model domain includes or excludes neighboring exchanges.

Reading and learning outcomes

Students should be able to calculate a conditional result, describe at least two competing processes, identify limitations of measurement, and write a short reproducible report. Alongside the five lessons, the unit includes a synthetic laboratory dataset and two illustrative slider-driven equations. These models are deliberately simple: use them to see parameter sensitivity, not to recommend operational changes. Any inference about actual water quality, flood hazard or well yield requires site data, qualified review and the relevant authorities.

§7.2 Lesson 7.1 — Renewable water, demand and seasonal deficit

How the process works

Annual renewable supply obscures dry-season shortages, conveyance losses and ecological flow needs. Groundwater pumping may temporarily cover deficits while depleting reserves or capturing streamflow. Begin with a drawing of the physical stores and fluxes relevant to renewable water, demand and seasonal deficit, showing both the water-bearing materials and the chosen observation boundary. Explain which forces move the water, solute or energy; identify the reference datum if hydraulic head or elevation is involved. A catchment outlet, soil column, well screen and river reach measure different domains. Never describe the value at one instrument as a universal property without a scaling argument. Distinguish a conservation relationship, which must hold for a correctly bounded system, from a constitutive equation, whose coefficients require calibration and whose validity is conditional on site conditions.

Evidence, ambiguity and falsifiable alternatives

Seasonal supply estimates, withdrawals, meter records, river environment indicators and aquifer heads are needed. For every proposed observation, record units, location, date, time step, instrument support and an independent quality check. Sketch what is measured directly and what must be inferred. Estimate an error or detection limit if the result will be compared across sites or dates. Changes that are smaller than the relevant error should not be described as robust physical trends.

A stable annual pumping volume may cause declining heads if recharge shrinks or stream capture evolves. A scientifically serious explanation must accommodate rival mechanisms. Identify what the preferred mechanism would predict at another time, depth or location. Choose a second type of evidence—geological context, temperature, chemical tracers, parallel reference catchments or independently surveyed elevations—rather than only refitting the same data. An observation that both models predict equally well is not a discriminating test.

Answer guidance: a sound answer includes the governing relationship, a physically appropriate measurement boundary, checks on units and uncertainty, and one genuinely differentiating field test. Numerical precision alone does not strengthen an unsupported hydrologic interpretation.

§7.7 Integrated laboratory: Compare demand and supply policies under uncertain recharge.

Research task. A growing coastal district with dry-season withdrawals and uneven water service Start from the attached datasets/unit-07-synthetic.csv. The records are deliberately fictional and provide no local environmental observations. The learner's deliverable is a short technical notebook, a table of measurements and conversions, a controlled comparison between two interpretations, and a one-page statement of residual uncertainty.

Phase A — conceptual diagram. Water availability depends on seasonality, water quality, cost, conveyance reliability, ecosystems and rights. A city may report a positive annual water balance while aquifers decline during the dry season or an upstream village has no safe supply. Each observation has a spatial footprint; annotate which terms would require a point sample, area-average estimate or volumetric balance. For groundwater, show screened intervals and vertical datum. For rivers, mark the measurement section and route. For water chemistry, identify concentration, flow and analyte units. A diagram that omits the boundary often hides the missing flux that later produces an apparently anomalous numerical result.

Phase B — independent calculation. A drought index can signal atmospheric shortage before groundwater responds; the delay is a storage property rather than inconsistency. A coastal salinity trend might have several groundwater and surface-water explanations. Use at least two independent calculation approaches where possible. Compare the result against a sanity bound: storage cannot be below a declared physically admissible minimum, chemical mixing fractions should lie between zero and one under the assumed ideal endmembers, and a probability cannot exceed unity. Round only after combining inputs; retain raw values and intermediate results in a reproducible notebook.

Phase C — evaluate the alternative. Planning under uncertainty should compare a baseline, a dry scenario and an intervention scenario with declared demands and ecological minimum flows. Calibrated models are not automatic guarantees when forcing changes. Replace one assumption at a time. Include a small scenario table showing baseline, lower plausible input and upper plausible input, then a separate table for structural alternatives where equations themselves differ. Explain whether the compared outcomes cross a chosen decision threshold. If no real-world decision threshold has been justified, label the result descriptive only rather than declaring a policy winner.

Phase D — submit a traceable report. Include the following in one coherent narrative: (a) research question and ethical scope, (b) boundary and major water or solute pathways, (c) original invented records, (d) exact equation and dimensional checks, (e) rival mechanisms and discriminating observations, (f) sensitivity or uncertainty table, and (g) a short conclusion distinguishing the measurement from its interpretation. State why the data cannot justify a real-world site decision.

Unit synthesis

Every lesson above contributes a different constraint to the integrated investigation. Cross-check a mass balance against a physically plausible flow path; cross-check a statistical summary against a documented measurement interval; and cross-check a model-derived conclusion against an independent observation wherever feasible. Report what evidence would overturn the preferred conclusion. The appropriate scientific endpoint is not a graph that looks convincing, but an interpretation that remains defensible when another researcher can inspect its data, assumptions and alternatives.

State why the inputs have compatible units, and explain which water store, flux, property, probability or statistic the output actually represents. For mixed units, write intermediate conversions explicitly. Distinguish the numerical answer from a claim about the real-world process: the answer follows from the invented inputs and the chosen equation, not from measurements of an actual catchment or aquifer.

Step

Sensitivity and limits: An endpoint slope is sensitive to anomalies and a short record. If an input is changed by 20%, calculate the new output and describe whether the response is linear or nonlinear. Where appropriate, identify an excluded term or an assumption about spatial uniformity, stationarity, constant flow, conservative chemistry, or homogeneous porous media. This calculation cannot establish the excluded term is zero merely because it produces a tidy result.
