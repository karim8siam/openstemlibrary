Solved Problem Example 2.1: Calculation & Verification: Pump head from pressure and velocity

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Pump head from pressure and velocity** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** For water at 998 kg/m3, 200 kPa pressure rise and negligible elevation or velocity difference give H≈20.43 m. **Explicit substitution:** H = 200,000/(998×9.81); therefore **20.43 m**. This is pressure head only; add velocity and elevation terms if nonzero. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 2.2: Calculation & Verification: System curves and operating-point intersection

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **System curves and operating-point intersection** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** A test loop with 12 m static head and K=8000 s2/m5 requires 32 m at Q=0.050 m3/s. **Explicit substitution:** H=12+8000×(0.050)²; therefore **32.0 m**. Square law here assumes the fixed system resistance coefficient remains appropriate. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 2.3: Calculation & Verification: Pump efficiency and power

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Pump efficiency and power** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** Water at 1000 kg/m3 and Q=0.035 m3/s over 25 m, with pump eta=0.76 and motor eta=0.93, uses about 12.13 kW. **Explicit substitution:** P = 1000×9.81×0.035×25/(0.76×0.93); therefore **12.13 kW**. Fluid power is 8.584 kW, less than electrical input. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 2.4: Calculation & Verification: Cavitation and NPSH margin

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Cavitation and NPSH margin** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** At suction absolute pressure 65 kPa, vapor pressure 3 kPa, rho=1000 kg/m3 and negligible velocity correction, NPSHa≈6.32 m. **Explicit substitution:** NPSHa = (65,000−3,000)/(1000×9.81); therefore **6.32 m**. Absolute suction and vapor pressures must share the same reference. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 2.5: Calculation & Verification: Fans and compressible ventilation flow

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Fans and compressible ventilation flow** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** A fan delivering 2.5 m3/s against 800 Pa total-pressure rise gives 2.0 kW air power and 3.08 kW input at overall eta=0.65. **Explicit substitution:** P_air=800×2.5=2000 W; P_input=2000/0.65; therefore **2.00 kW useful; 3.08 kW input**. Static pressure must not be substituted for total pressure without correction. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.
