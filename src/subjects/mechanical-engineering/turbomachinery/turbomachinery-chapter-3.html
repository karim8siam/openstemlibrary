Solved Problem Example 3.1: Calculation & Verification: Axial compressor flow passages

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Axial compressor flow passages** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** With density 1.1 kg/m3, annulus area 0.12 m2 and axial velocity 115 m/s, the flow is 15.18 kg/s. **Explicit substitution:** m_dot = 1.1×0.12×115; therefore **15.18 kg/s**. The area is the annulus, not the full casing disk. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 3.2: Calculation & Verification: Incidence, turning and cascade losses

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Incidence, turning and cascade losses** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** A relative flow angle of -41 degrees at the leading edge and a metal angle of -45 degrees implies +4 degrees incidence. **Explicit substitution:** incidence = (−41°)−(−45°); therefore **+4°**. Opposite sign convention would need a corresponding interpretation change. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 3.3: Calculation & Verification: Degree of reaction in an axial stage

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Degree of reaction in an axial stage** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** If rotor static enthalpy increases 10 kJ/kg and the whole stage increases 22 kJ/kg, the reaction is about 0.455. **Explicit substitution:** R = 10/22; therefore **0.4545**. Rotor and stage static enthalpy stations must be stated. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 3.4: Calculation & Verification: Compressor pressure ratio and isentropic efficiency

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Compressor pressure ratio and isentropic efficiency** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** Air with pi=2.5, gamma=1.4, T01=300 K and T02=415 K has eta_c≈0.780. **Explicit substitution:** eta_c = [2.5^(0.4/1.4)−1]/(415/300−1); therefore **0.7807**. T temperatures are in kelvin and actual rise exceeds isentropic rise. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 3.5: Calculation & Verification: Stall and surge as distinct instabilities

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Stall and surge as distinct instabilities** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** At common corrected flow, PR_surge=3.1 and PR_operating=2.8 give a nominal 10.7% ratio margin. **Explicit substitution:** (3.1−2.8)/2.8×100; therefore **10.71% pressure-ratio margin**. A static scalar margin does not resolve transient surge risk. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.
