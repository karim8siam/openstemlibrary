Solved Problem Example 4.1: Calculation & Verification: Radial impeller energy rise

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Radial impeller energy rise** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** At U2=300 m/s, Vtheta2=210 m/s and negligible inlet swirl, the ideal input is 63 kJ/kg. **Explicit substitution:** Delta h0 =300×210−0; therefore **63,000 J/kg**. Inlet whirl was explicitly assumed negligible. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 4.2: Calculation & Verification: Slip factor and backswept blades

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Slip factor and backswept blades** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** If an ideal exit whirl is 235 m/s and measured or modeled whirl is 205 m/s, sigma≈0.872. **Explicit substitution:** sigma =205/235; therefore **0.8723**. Defined slip factor depends on the specified ideal whirl reference. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 4.3: Calculation & Verification: Diffuser velocity recovery

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Diffuser velocity recovery** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** With rho=1.2 kg/m3, V1=160 m/s and V2=100 m/s, the ideal static recovery is 9.36 kPa. **Explicit substitution:** Delta p≈(1.2/2)×(160²−100²); therefore **9360 Pa**. Valid only as a constant-density inviscid recovery idealization. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 4.4: Calculation & Verification: Corrected compressor flow and speed

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Corrected compressor flow and speed** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** At m_dot=8 kg/s, T01=330 K, Tref=288.15 K, p01=95 kPa and pref=101.325 kPa, corrected flow is about 9.12 kg/s. **Explicit substitution:** m_corr=8×sqrt(330/288.15)/(95/101.325); therefore **9.12 kg/s corrected**. This is a coordinate for a specified reference state, not physical mass flow. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.

Solved Problem Example 4.5: Calculation & Verification: Choke and impeller mechanical limits

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Choke and impeller mechanical limits** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Teaching case:** A local absolute flow speed of 340 m/s at 300 K air gives Mach≈0.98 using gamma=1.4, R=287 J/(kg K). **Explicit substitution:** a=sqrt(1.4×287×300); M=340/a; therefore **a≈347.2 m/s; M≈0.979**. Relative Mach may exceed the absolute Mach and matters at the blade throat. Check arithmetic independently. Then perturb one input by 10%: linear, quadratic and cubic factors become 1.1, 1.21 and 1.331, respectively. Recompute nonlinear property relationships. An algebraic change is not evidence of a new real-machine operating point.
