Solved Problem Example 6.1: Calculation & Verification: Internal Combustion Engines — U06-L01

Problem Statement: The smallest useful model is `Steady coolant heat rate Q_dot = m_dot_c * cp_c * (T_out-T_in).`. Write it first in symbols because symbols expose which quantities are inputs and which are derived. Conduct a dimensional check. For example, an energy rate must have dimensions of power, and a dimensionless efficiency must not quietly carry a pressure unit. Then inspect the limiting cases. A vanishing input, a zero flow or an impossible temperature ratio should trigger a validity warning rather than a numerically polished answer. The equation belongs to the assumptions stated with it, not to every engine. **Assumed numerical case:** At 0.5 kg/s water, cp=4.18 kJ/kg K and DeltaT=8 K, coolant heat is 16.7 kW.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Calculation walkthrough:** For a 0.5 kg/s coolant flow, specific heat 4.18 kJ/(kg·K), and measured 8 K rise, Q_dot = 0.5×4.18×8 = 16.72 kW. If flow is not measured accurately or temperature probes are separated from the control volume, the energy-balance figure can shift. The result is a coolant heat rate, not useful shaft work. The stated values form a hypothetical teaching example and should be recalculated by hand. First write the governing relation, then translate the inputs to SI, then perform substitution before rounding the displayed output. Check the sign and order of magnitude against a physical limiting case. Finally, ask which measured input would dominate an uncertainty interval. A large discrepancy from an independently computed check is grounds to revise the work, not to adjust the accepted answer without explanation. ### 3. Interpret the result, including failures **Critical interpretation:** This estimate excludes transient storage and errors from temperature probe placement. A misleading numerical result can arise even when the arithmetic is perfect: the problem may be a wrong system boundary, an inappropriate reference property, a time-averaged signal used for a crank-angle event, or an unrecorded controller response. State which of those failure mechanisms is most likely here. Think about the change that would be observed in cylinder pressure, exhaust temperature, manifold state, or brake output if the explanatory mechanism were genuinely active rather than merely correlated with an unrelated change. **Conservation audit:** Start by marking the system boundary on a sketch. Separate fuel energy entering the cylinder from indicated work on the piston and brake work at the shaft. A computed improvement that violates energy conservation is a mistake, not a new engine phenomenon. Where an experimental result seems to contradict the calculation, investigate calibration, hidden storage, auxiliary loads and the interpretation of reference conditions before adding complexity to the model. Applied to **combustion heat transfer and coolant rejection**, this discipline helps decide whether the model is a prediction with independent evidence or merely a restatement of its input measurements. Solved Problem Example 6.3: Calculation & Verification: Internal Combustion Engines — U06-L03

Problem Statement: The working relation used here is `FMEP = IMEP - BMEP with a consistent four-stroke-cycle definition.`. Define every variable before substitution, keep a single system of units, and check whether its result should increase or decrease when an input changes. Ask which assumptions make the expression possible: steady conditions, a perfect gas, constant properties, a lumped state, negligible pressure loss or an ideal mechanical linkage may be necessary. If an assumption does not hold in the intended application, identify the direction of the expected deviation when physics permits. When that direction is not clear, do not guess; schedule a measurement or use a higher-fidelity calculation. **Assumed numerical case:** If IMEP=12 bar and BMEP=9.5 bar, FMEP is 2.5 bar.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Calculation walkthrough:** With IMEP=12 bar and BMEP=9.5 bar on matching cycles and displacement, the estimated friction mean effective pressure is 2.5 bar. If the pressure analysis and shaft test refer to different load conditions, subtracting them would be meaningless. Also specify whether accessory loads are included in the brake measurement. Solved Problem Example 6.5: Calculation & Verification: Internal Combustion Engines — U06-L05

Problem Statement: Use `A simple Miner sum D = sum(n_i/N_i) is an empirical screening tool for fatigue.` as the initial closure relation. It is tempting to interpret the number as a design guarantee; instead define a reference operating point, perturb each independent input separately and observe the output response. Check whether apparently independent variables are actually correlated by conservation or control logic. Treat a high-sensitivity variable as a candidate for better measurement. If the result requires a parameter measured by the same output that is being predicted, the model is partly circular and its validation requires an independent observation. **Assumed numerical case:** A block with 10000 cycles at a nominal endurance of 100000 gives damage fraction 0.1.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Calculation walkthrough:** For a load block with 10,000 cycles applied and a nominal constant-amplitude life of 100,000 cycles, the linear damage fraction is 0.10. Ten identical such blocks would sum to one within the model. Sequence effects, lubricating film changes and corrosion can invalidate this simple addition, so it is a screening estimate rather than a certified life prediction.
