Braking Systems, Tires and Regenerative Braking

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Braking Systems, Tires and Regenerative Braking.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Automotive Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§4.1 Tire slip and force generation

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **contact-patch mechanics and traction envelope**, then evaluate whether set conservative validation limits. The illustrative field case is **instrumented hard braking on variable-grip pavement**. The primary relation is `Slip_brake = (v-R*omega_wheel)/max(v,epsilon)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§4.2 Deceleration force and stopping distance

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **friction-limited constant-deceleration idealization**, then evaluate whether define a safe test envelope. The illustrative field case is **braking on a straight level surface**. The primary relation is `d = v0^2/(2*mu*g)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§4.3 Brake torque, hydraulic systems and thermal energy

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **friction brake heat and thermal stress**, then evaluate whether size ventilation and thermal test requirements. The illustrative field case is **repeated descents with heavy payload**. The primary relation is `Delta_Ek = 0.5*m*(v1^2-v2^2)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§4.4 Anti-lock braking and wheel-speed estimation

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **feedback modulation of brake pressure to manage slip**, then evaluate whether validate sensing and fault-handling. The illustrative field case is **emergency stop over a surface transition**. The primary relation is `Wheel angular acceleration = d(omega_wheel)/dt`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§4.5 Regenerative and friction brake coordination

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **recoverable braking energy with power and battery limits**, then evaluate whether design consistent pedal response. The illustrative field case is **hybrid braking with a nearly full battery**. The primary relation is `E_recovered <= eta_regen*Delta_Ek`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.
