Steering, Handling and Vehicle Stability

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Steering, Handling and Vehicle Stability.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Automotive Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§5.1 Bicycle model and lateral acceleration

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **kinematic curvature and tire-force requirement**, then evaluate whether choose speed during tests. The illustrative field case is **a constant-radius corner at moderate speed**. The primary relation is `a_y = v^2/R`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§5.2 Steering geometry and turning radius

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **low-speed steering kinematics**, then evaluate whether compare geometry and driver effort. The illustrative field case is **vehicle maneuvering through a narrow turn**. The primary relation is `delta = atan(L/R)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§5.3 Understeer, oversteer and yaw response

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **yaw rate and cornering balance**, then evaluate whether set stable handling targets. The illustrative field case is **steering step and constant-radius tests**. The primary relation is `r_ideal = v/R`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§5.4 Load transfer, roll and stability margins

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **simplified lateral axle load transfer**, then evaluate whether set a stability test plan. The illustrative field case is **a tall vehicle entering a sharp bend**. The primary relation is `Delta_Fz_axle ≈ m*a_y*h/t`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§5.5 Steering tests and handling model validation

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **model residuals versus measured yaw and speed**, then evaluate whether qualify operating envelope. The illustrative field case is **double lane-change test with sensor logs**. The primary relation is `Error(t) = r_measured(t) - r_model(t)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.
