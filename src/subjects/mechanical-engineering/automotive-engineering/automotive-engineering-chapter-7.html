Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Energy Systems

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Energy Systems.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Automotive Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§7.1 Electric traction motor torque and inverter limits

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **motor torque-speed envelope and electrical losses**, then evaluate whether map continuous versus short-duration output. The illustrative field case is **EV acceleration during a battery-limited maneuver**. The primary relation is `P_mech = T*omega`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§7.2 Battery capacity, usable window and vehicle range

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **energy content, auxiliary use and road demand**, then evaluate whether evaluate route-based reserve energy. The illustrative field case is **cold-weather range planning**. The primary relation is `Range_km = usable_kWh/(kWh_per_km)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§7.3 Battery internal resistance and thermal management

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **electrothermal coupling and cell protection**, then evaluate whether design a safe cooling evaluation. The illustrative field case is **high-current acceleration in warm ambient air**. The primary relation is `P_heat = I^2*R`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§7.4 Charging power, efficiency and taper

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **energy delivery boundary and current limits**, then evaluate whether plan fleet charging turnaround. The illustrative field case is **fast charging near high state of charge**. The primary relation is `t_ideal = Delta_E/(P_charger*eta_charge)`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.

§7.5 Hybrid and EV system-level comparison

**Learning objective.** Build a reproducible analysis of **boundaries for efficiency and life-cycle assessment**, then evaluate whether select evidence-based technology for a service. The illustrative field case is **comparing EV and hybrid on a mixed-duty fleet**. The primary relation is `E_wall = E_pack/eta_charging`; it is a starting approximation, not a complete calibration or certification method.
