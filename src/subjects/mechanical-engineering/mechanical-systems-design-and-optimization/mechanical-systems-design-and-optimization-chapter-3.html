Solved Problem Example 3.1: Calculation & Verification: Optimization problem formulation

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Optimization problem formulation** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Core relation or design rule:** `min_x f(x), subject to g(x) ≤ 0` **Worked case.** Choose pipe diameter to minimize installed and energy cost while respecting a maximum allowable pressure drop. Distinguish continuous diameters used for exploration from manufactured nominal sizes used in purchase. The first check is dimensional: if the symbols represent different physical dimensions, convert to a common SI basis before evaluating. The second check is one limiting case: set a load to zero, make the design very stiff, or let an efficiency approach unity—whichever is physically relevant—and ask if the predicted direction makes sense. The third check is to estimate the size of an ignored term. A compact hand calculation is valuable because it can expose a reversed ratio or a misplaced conversion factor that would otherwise pass through a spreadsheet unnoticed. Convert the worked case into a design memo: state the known inputs, unknown output, assumptions, calculation, units and consequence of crossing the limit. Make two alternative estimates, one at a favorable operating point and one at a credible adverse point. Explain why the apparent improvement may not translate into a better total system. The correct comparison uses the same function, service life and boundary for each alternative rather than comparing one design at peak efficiency with another at average duty. ## 3. Scope, counterexample and failure conditions An optimizer can choose a physically impossible tube wall thickness if minimum gauge, buckling and manufacturing constraints are missing. A calculation is only as good as its assumed configuration. Ask whether the actual mechanism is linear, whether geometry remains unchanged, whether a temperature-dependent property was treated as fixed, and whether contact, vibration or actuator saturation could introduce a discontinuity. If the model ignores an effect because it appears small, report the evidence supporting that decision. Quantification is not a substitute for identifying hazards: an attractive weighted objective cannot excuse an unverified guard, protection feature, pressure boundary or emergency stop. A counterexample helps establish the scope. Imagine the original design moved from a controlled teaching bench to a dirty or wet industrial location. The incoming load spectrum, corrosion exposure, calibration frequency and repair resources may be different. Name at least one parameter from the equation that remains meaningful and one omitted mechanism that becomes important. Explain how the design review would change if that mechanism is plausible. Record any consequence whose acceptability must be judged by a competent safety professional rather than inferred from a classroom equation.

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Pareto fronts and multiobjective decisions** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Core relation or design rule:** `A dominates B if no worse in every objective and better in ≥1` **Worked case.** Three alternatives cost 80, 100 and 120 units and weigh 12, 9 and 11 kg respectively. The last is dominated by the middle because it is both heavier and more expensive. The other two express a real tradeoff. Solved Problem Example 3.5: Calculation & Verification: Design of experiments and optimization validation

Problem Statement: An engineering practitioner analyzes a mechanical system governed by **Design of experiments and optimization validation** under steady operational loading.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Core relation or design rule:** `Response = β0 + βA A + βB B + βAB AB + ε` **Worked case.** For a cooling plate, vary channel width and flow rate at two levels each, repeat selected runs and examine whether the effect of width depends on the flow rate. Four corner runs alone give little information about curvature. The first check is dimensional: if the symbols represent different physical dimensions, convert to a common SI basis before evaluating.
