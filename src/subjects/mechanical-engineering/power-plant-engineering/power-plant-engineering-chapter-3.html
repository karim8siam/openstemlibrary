Fuels, Combustion and Gas-Turbine Cycles

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Fuels, Combustion and Gas-Turbine Cycles.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Power Plant Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§3.1 Fuel characterization and stoichiometry

**Core topic:** air requirement and excess air. **Representative relation:** `AFR_st = m_air_st / m_fuel`. **Case context:** boiler burner with varying fuel composition.

§3.2 Steam-generator heat balance

**Core topic:** boiler efficiency and steam production. **Representative relation:** `Q_to_steam = m_dot_s*(h_out-h_in)`. **Case context:** feedwater-to-superheated steam balance.

§3.3 Ideal Brayton pressure-ratio trends

**Core topic:** pressure ratio under constant-property ideal assumptions. **Representative relation:** `eta_B = 1-r_p**(-(gamma-1)/gamma)`. **Case context:** gas-turbine performance thought experiment.

§3.4 Compressor and turbine specific work

**Core topic:** gas-path shaft balance. **Representative relation:** `w_net = cp*(T3-T4)-cp*(T2-T1)`. **Case context:** simple open Brayton cycle.

§3.5 Combustion emissions and operational trade-offs

**Core topic:** emissions intensity and denominators. **Representative relation:** `e_CO2 = m_CO2 / E_net`. **Case context:** plant output versus fuel-carbon accounting.

Solved Problem Example 3.1: Calculation & Verification: Fuel characterization and stoichiometry

Problem Statement: For ideal pure methane, CH4 + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O. One mole of methane has mass about 16 g and needs 64 g oxygen; hence **4 kg O2/kg CH4**. If dry air has 23.2% oxygen by mass, the stoichiometric air requirement is 4/0.232 = **17.24 kg air/kg methane**. This ignores real fuel mixtures and moisture.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Using the wrong oxygen mass fraction or an LHV/HHV basis changes a combustion calculation materially. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 3.2: Calculation & Verification: Steam-generator heat balance

Problem Statement: Feedwater at 850 kJ/kg is heated to steam at 3200 kJ/kg at a mass flow of 20 kg/s. Gross steam-side duty is 20(3200 − 850) = **47 MW(th)**. This is not identical to fuel input, because the boiler has losses, blowdown and possibly attemperation or other mass-flow branches.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** A measured flue-gas stack temperature is not enough to compute complete boiler efficiency without a fuel and mass balance. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 3.3: Calculation & Verification: Ideal Brayton pressure-ratio trends

Problem Statement: Assume an ideal reversible Brayton cycle with a constant heat-capacity ratio γ = 1.4 and compressor pressure ratio 10. The ideal expression 1 − 10^(−0.4/1.4) gives **48.21%**. Real simple-cycle efficiency will generally differ because of compressor/turbine isentropic losses, combustor pressure drop and turbine inlet temperature limits.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Do not interpret the simple air-standard value as a vendor map or predicted guaranteed turbine performance. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 3.4: Calculation & Verification: Compressor and turbine specific work

Problem Statement: With cp = 1.05 kJ/(kg K), compressor inlet/outlet temperatures 300 and 610 K, and turbine inlet/outlet temperatures 1400 and 900 K, net gas-path specific work is 1.05[(1400 − 900) − (610 − 300)] = **199.5 kJ/kg**. Turbine work alone would be 525 kJ/kg and would badly overstate shaft output.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** The example assumes identical gas and air mass flows and constant cp solely for introductory comparison. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 3.5: Calculation & Verification: Combustion emissions and operational trade-offs

Problem Statement: An illustrative emissions inventory gives 16 tonnes CO2 for 80 MWh(e) net export in the same hour. The intensity is (16,000 kg)/(80 MWh) = **200 kg CO2/MWh**. This is a synthetic arithmetic case, not an emissions factor for a fuel or technology. Boundary choice must state whether the metric covers direct combustion, upstream supply or full lifecycle.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Emissions accounting must not conflate greenhouse-gas intensity with NOx or air-quality compliance. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.
