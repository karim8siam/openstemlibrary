Steam-Cycle Power Generation

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Steam-Cycle Power Generation.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Power Plant Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§2.1 Rankine states and thermal conversion

**Core topic:** net cycle work and heat input. **Representative relation:** `eta_R = (w_t - w_p) / q_in`. **Case context:** four-state saturated cycle.

§2.2 Steam turbine expansion and extraction

**Core topic:** enthalpy work and internal efficiency. **Representative relation:** `W_t = m_dot*(h_in-h_out)`. **Case context:** high-pressure turbine with a bleed stream.

§2.3 Condenser duty and backpressure

**Core topic:** heat rejection from wet exhaust. **Representative relation:** `Q_cond = m_dot*(h_in-h_out)`. **Case context:** surface condenser in a humid climate.

§2.4 Feedwater pumps and boiler input

**Core topic:** liquid-pump work and steam-generator duty. **Representative relation:** `w_p ≈ v*(p_out-p_in)`. **Case context:** multistage feedwater train.

§2.5 Reheat and regenerative feedwater heating

**Core topic:** cycle efficiency and moisture management. **Representative relation:** `Q_added = sum(m_dot*delta_h) across heated streams`. **Case context:** one reheat stage with closed feedwater heater.

Solved Problem Example 2.1: Calculation & Verification: Rankine states and thermal conversion

Problem Statement: Take idealized cycle enthalpies of 192 kJ/kg at pump inlet, 202 after pumping, 3450 after the boiler, and 2300 at turbine exit. Turbine work is 1150 kJ/kg, pump work is 10 kJ/kg, and heat supplied is 3248 kJ/kg; ideal thermal efficiency is (1150 − 10)/3248 = **35.10%**. These are a synthetic set of enthalpies, not a verified steam-table operating point.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Detailed state selection must satisfy actual pressure and entropy constraints; arbitrary enthalpies cannot validate a real cycle. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 2.2: Calculation & Verification: Steam turbine expansion and extraction

Problem Statement: With steam flow of 30 kg/s and inlet/outlet enthalpies 3410 and 2550 kJ/kg, an ideal steady turbine power estimate is 30(3410 − 2550) = **25,800 kW**, or 25.8 MW. A real extraction turbine must apply this balance separately to every branch rather than using the full inlet flow across all stages.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** The estimate excludes mechanical losses, leakage and the small kinetic-energy terms until their significance is checked. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 2.3: Calculation & Verification: Condenser duty and backpressure

Problem Statement: A condenser receives 42 kg/s of wet exhaust at specific enthalpy 2400 kJ/kg and discharges condensate at 220 kJ/kg. Condenser thermal duty is 42(2400 − 220) = **91,560 kW**. This heat must reach a cooling medium, subject to nonzero temperature approach and finite transfer area.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** A lower condensing temperature may improve ideal cycle output but can increase cooling demand or air-leakage sensitivity. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 2.4: Calculation & Verification: Feedwater pumps and boiler input

Problem Statement: Using near-incompressible liquid specific volume 0.0011 m³/kg and pressure increase 11 MPa, ideal pump work per kilogram is approximately 0.0011 × 11,000,000 = **12,100 J/kg**, or 12.1 kJ/kg. A 75%-efficient pump requires approximately 16.1 kJ/kg shaft input, excluding other losses.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Convert pressure to pascals before multiplying by m³/kg; this dimensional check prevents a thousand-fold error. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 2.5: Calculation & Verification: Reheat and regenerative feedwater heating

Problem Statement: A reheat section raises specific enthalpy of 45 kg/s of steam by 580 kJ/kg. Assuming negligible external heat loss, reheat duty is 45 × 580 = **26.1 MW(th)**. Whether reheat improves overall cycle efficiency depends on the resulting turbine expansion and heat-supply conditions, not duty alone.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** For regenerative systems, a steam bleed lowers the downstream mass flow; use branch-by-branch energy balances. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.
