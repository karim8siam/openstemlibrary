Cooling Systems, Water and Environmental Constraints

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Cooling Systems, Water and Environmental Constraints.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Power Plant Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§5.1 Condenser heat-rejection accounting

**Core topic:** water-side calorimetry. **Representative relation:** `Q_reject = m_dot_cw*cp*DeltaT`. **Case context:** condenser cooling-water loop.

§5.2 Cooling towers and ambient boundaries

**Core topic:** approach, range and wet-bulb influence. **Representative relation:** `Range = T_hot-T_cold`. **Case context:** forced-draft evaporative cooling tower.

§5.3 Water consumption and blowdown

**Core topic:** cycles of concentration and water budgets. **Representative relation:** `COC = C_circulating/C_makeup`. **Case context:** cooling tower makeup, drift and blowdown records.

§5.4 Dry and hybrid cooling

**Core topic:** heat-exchanger effectiveness and climate effects. **Representative relation:** `Q = UA*LMTD`. **Case context:** air-cooled condenser under hot weather.

§5.5 Environmental evaluation and monitoring

**Core topic:** resource intensity across a reporting boundary. **Representative relation:** `I_water = V_consumed/E_net`. **Case context:** monthly plant water balance.

Solved Problem Example 5.1: Calculation & Verification: Condenser heat-rejection accounting

Problem Statement: A cooling-water loop carries 2600 kg/s with assumed cp = 4.18 kJ/(kg K) and temperature rise 7 K. Heat duty is 2600 × 4.18 × 7 = **76,076 kW**, or 76.076 MW(th). A 0.2 K sensor offset would change the inferred duty by about 2.17 MW, so calibration matters.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** This single-phase sensible estimate does not include independent condenser steam-side validation. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 5.2: Calculation & Verification: Cooling towers and ambient boundaries

Problem Statement: A cooling tower returns water at 29 °C after receiving hot water at 36 °C. Cooling range is **7 K**. If ambient wet-bulb temperature is 25 °C, the cold-water approach is **4 K**. Neither number alone specifies evaporation, electrical fan demand or biological treatment requirements.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Cooling-tower performance changes with humidity, air flow and water chemistry. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 5.3: Calculation & Verification: Water consumption and blowdown

Problem Statement: A concentration proxy is 1800 mg/L circulating-water dissolved solids divided by 300 mg/L makeup-water solids, giving **6 cycles of concentration**. This simplified ratio assumes consistent species behavior and mixing; chemical reaction and selective precipitation break the scalar approximation.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** A plant water budget also requires evaporation, blowdown, drift and leak measurements. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 5.4: Calculation & Verification: Dry and hybrid cooling

Problem Statement: A simplified air-cooled exchanger has UA = 250 kW/K and a log-mean temperature difference of 16 K. Its idealized rate is **4.0 MW(th)**. Real UA varies with fouling, air velocity, fin condition and operating temperature, so a hot-day prediction requires actual equipment data.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** An assumed fixed temperature approach can conceal a high-temperature derating risk. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 5.5: Calculation & Verification: Environmental evaluation and monitoring

Problem Statement: A plant consumes 500,000 m³ of water while exporting 700,000 MWh(e) over the same annual period. Consumptive intensity is 500,000/700,000 = **0.714 m³/MWh**. Withdrawal volume could be much larger; report the two separately and document where water is returned.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** This accounting value is not a license to withdraw water or discharge heated effluent. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.
