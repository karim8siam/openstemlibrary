Nonfossil Generation and Hybrid Power Systems

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Nonfossil Generation and Hybrid Power Systems.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Power Plant Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§6.1 Hydroelectric conversion and head

**Core topic:** hydraulic potential to electric power. **Representative relation:** `P_h = rho*g*Q*H*eta`. **Case context:** reservoir release with known head.

§6.2 Wind and variable-power capture

**Core topic:** wind-energy flux and rotor capture. **Representative relation:** `P_w = 0.5*rho_air*A*V**3*Cp`. **Case context:** wind farm performance estimate.

§6.3 Solar photovoltaics and solar thermal

**Core topic:** incident irradiance and AC derating. **Representative relation:** `P_PV = G*A*eta_module*PR`. **Case context:** rooftop module versus utility-scale array.

§6.4 Geothermal and nuclear thermal boundaries

**Core topic:** thermal-source-to-electric conversion. **Representative relation:** `eta_thermal = P_e/Q_source`. **Case context:** geothermal brine loop and nuclear steam generator comparison.

§6.5 Hybrid storage, flexibility and capacity value

**Core topic:** duration and round-trip losses. **Representative relation:** `E_store = P_discharge*t_discharge`. **Case context:** solar plus battery and dispatchable reserve.

Solved Problem Example 6.1: Calculation & Verification: Hydroelectric conversion and head

Problem Statement: Take water density 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², flow 40 m³/s, net head 75 m and total efficiency 0.90. Hydropower is 1000 × 9.81 × 40 × 75 × 0.90 = **26.487 MW(e)**. Actual output also depends on reservoir conditions, permitted releases and seasonal flow.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Using gross rather than net head causes a systematic overestimate of delivered power. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 6.2: Calculation & Verification: Wind and variable-power capture

Problem Statement: For air density 1.2 kg/m³, swept area 5000 m², wind speed 8 m/s and power coefficient 0.42, idealized captured power is 0.5 × 1.2 × 5000 × 8³ × 0.42 = **0.645 MW**. Because wind power scales as the cube of speed, using the arithmetic mean wind speed in this formula cannot generally reproduce annual energy.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Do not assume Cp constant across wind speeds or disregard cut-in and cut-out limits. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 6.3: Calculation & Verification: Solar photovoltaics and solar thermal

Problem Statement: A 5000 m² PV array receives irradiance 800 W/m², has an assumed module efficiency of 20% and an overall performance ratio of 0.85. Net illustrative output is 800 × 5000 × 0.20 × 0.85 = **680 kW**. This is an instantaneous representative condition, not an annual yield estimate.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Shading, inverter clipping, temperature and soiling require more complete modeling. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 6.4: Calculation & Verification: Geothermal and nuclear thermal boundaries

Problem Statement: A thermal system receives 90 MW(th) and exports 25 MW(e). Its thermal-to-electric ratio is 25/90 = **27.78%** under the stated boundary. This value cannot establish whether the heat came from geothermal fluid, nuclear fission, concentrated solar heat or industrial waste heat, nor can it establish any source-specific safety conclusion.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Only source-specific professional studies can establish nuclear safety or geothermal sustainability. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 6.5: Calculation & Verification: Hybrid storage, flexibility and capacity value

Problem Statement: A battery is rated 20 MW discharge power for four hours of its nominal usable energy. The ideal delivered energy is 20 × 4 = **80 MWh**. An 88% round-trip efficiency means that cycling 80 MWh of delivered energy requires approximately 80/0.88 = **90.9 MWh** charge input under the simplified model.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Energy storage shifts delivery time and has losses; it is not a generator of net energy. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.
