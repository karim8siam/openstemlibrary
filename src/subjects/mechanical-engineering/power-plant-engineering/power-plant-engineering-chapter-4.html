Combined Cycles and Cogeneration

Comprehensive university textbook unit covering fundamental principles, governing relationships, and engineering applications of Combined Cycles and Cogeneration.

About this textbook. Original English teaching material for university Power Plant Engineering study. Eight units with verified worked problems and 16 interactive simulations illustrating fundamental principles and core applications. Numerical checks and an editorial review support this edition.

§4.1 Heat-recovery steam generators

**Core topic:** gas-to-water energy recovery. **Representative relation:** `Q_HRSG = m_dot_g*cp_g*(T_g,in-T_g,out)`. **Case context:** unfired exhaust heat exchanger.

§4.2 Combined-cycle net efficiency

**Core topic:** system electric performance. **Representative relation:** `eta_CC = (P_GT+P_ST-P_aux)/Q_fuel`. **Case context:** gas and steam generator gross meters.

§4.3 CHP total fuel utilization

**Core topic:** simultaneous heat and electric services. **Representative relation:** `U_CHP = (P_e+Q_useful)/Q_fuel`. **Case context:** industrial process steam demand.

§4.4 Pinch, approach and exhaust constraints

**Core topic:** thermal contact and exchanger sizing. **Representative relation:** `DeltaT_pinch = T_g,evap_out-T_sat`. **Case context:** single-pressure HRSG temperature sketch.

§4.5 Integrated part-load operation

**Core topic:** coupled equipment at variable demand. **Representative relation:** `P_net = P_GT(load)+P_ST(load)-P_aux(load)`. **Case context:** daily dispatch and ambient-temperature variation.

Solved Problem Example 4.1: Calculation & Verification: Heat-recovery steam generators

Problem Statement: An HRSG receives 220 kg/s of exhaust gas, with assumed cp = 1.1 kJ/(kg K), entering at 560 °C and leaving at 160 °C. Sensible heat lost by gas is 220 × 1.1 × 400 = **96.8 MW(th)**. Actual heat delivered to steam is lower if there are heat losses or bypass streams.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Finite temperature approach, material limits and stack condensation rules constrain real recovery. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 4.2: Calculation & Verification: Combined-cycle net efficiency

Problem Statement: A gas turbine produces 180 MW(e), a downstream steam turbine 90 MW(e), and auxiliaries consume 12 MW(e), with 450 MW(th) primary fuel input. Net output is 258 MW(e) and overall net efficiency is 258/450 = **57.33%**. The fuel denominator already supplied the gas turbine; recovered exhaust is not counted as a second fuel source.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** This is an illustrative operating point; ambient conditions and part-load behavior change real performance. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 4.3: Calculation & Verification: CHP total fuel utilization

Problem Statement: A CHP plant produces 30 MW(e) and delivers 40 MW(th) of genuinely useful heat while consuming 100 MW(th) primary fuel. Total fuel utilization is (30 + 40)/100 = **70%**. Its electric-only ratio remains 30%; a different customer heat-demand profile could substantially reduce the annual useful-heat credit.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Measure heat at the customer export boundary rather than counting internal steam circulation. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 4.4: Calculation & Verification: Pinch, approach and exhaust constraints

Problem Statement: In a schematic HRSG, the gas temperature leaving the evaporator is 195 °C and steam saturation temperature is 175 °C at that section. Pinch temperature difference is **20 K**. Reducing this difference would generally require greater heat-transfer area or different design; it cannot be reduced to zero in a finite exchanger for finite heat transfer.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** A local pinch value does not describe the complete multi-pressure temperature profile or acid-dew-point limits. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.

Solved Problem Example 4.5: Calculation & Verification: Integrated part-load operation

Problem Statement: At a particular dispatch point, gas and steam turbines produce 150 and 70 MW(e), and station service consumes 10 MW(e). Net export is **210 MW(e)**. A 10% gas-turbine reduction need not yield an immediate 10% steam-turbine reduction because exhaust temperature, steam storage, controls and time response all matter.

Worked Engineering Derivation:

**Verification and limitations.** Do not use a fixed rated steam-to-gas power ratio to predict part-load output. The result is illustrative and does not replace equipment-specific property data, measurements or professional review.
